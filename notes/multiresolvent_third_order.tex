\title{Third-Order Extension of the Multiresolvent Representation of the Parquet Vertex}

\author{Pierre-Fran\c{c}ois \surname{Loos}}
    \email{loos@irsamc.ups-tlse.fr}
    \affiliation{\LCPQ}

\begin{abstract}
These companion notes address only the extension of the multiresolvent
representation of the parquet vertex to third order in the bare interaction.
The frequency conventions, channel decomposition, one-frequency projections,
and complete second-order construction are taken from the companion
second-order notes and are not repeated here.
At third order, the conventional projected construction contains
second-order corrections to the two coupling vertices and a first-order
correction to the central $\eh$ or $\pp$ resolvent.
An unrestricted audit of the frequency convolutions shows, however, that a
dynamic second-order irreducible vertex activates additional internal orbital
sectors whose isolated non-interacting bubbles vanish: $vv$ and $oo$ sectors
in the $\eh$ channel and analogous mixed sectors in the $\pp$ channel.
These terms are retained explicitly as complementary-sector remainders
$\Delta_{L/R}^{r,(3)}$.
Accordingly, the ordinary
$U^{(2)}R^{(0)}V^{(1)}+U^{(1)}R^{(0)}V^{(2)}+U^{(1)}R^{(1)}V^{(1)}$
expression is the complete contribution within the conventional projected
central spaces, but not the complete unrestricted third-order vertex.
The purpose of these notes is to isolate the genuinely new third-order
structures that an enlarged auxiliary-space representation must reproduce.
\end{abstract}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{Scope and conventions}
\label{sec:introduction}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

These notes begin where the second-order multiresolvent construction ends.
We adopt without rederivation its direct-$\eh$ frequency convention, parquet
channel definitions, normalized factors $Q^\pm$, channel resolvents,
coupling vertices $U^r$ and $V^r$, and explicit second-order irreducible
kernels $\Gamma^{\eh,(2)}$ and $\Gamma^{\pp,(2)}$.
The second-order primitive vertices, frequency reductions, residue tensors,
GF2 kernels, $GW$ comparison, and consistency checks are deliberately not
repeated.
The objective here is solely to identify and organize the new structures
that appear at third order.

We retain the same Hartree--Fock reference, M\o ller--Plesset partition,
real-orbital convention, and $0^+$ causal prescription as in the
second-order notes.

\section{Third-order multiresolvent extension}
\label{sec:multiresolvent_third}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

We take the complete second-order multiresolvent construction as given and
extend it by one perturbative order.
Only the generic channel factorization is needed below,
\begin{equation}
    \ii\Phi^r
    =
    U^r R^r V^r,
    \qquad
    R^r(\Omega_r)
    =
    [\Omega_r\eta^r-M^r]^{-1},
    \qquad
    r=\eh,\pp
    \label{eq:third_starting_point}
\end{equation}
with the frequency conventions, metrics, normalized one-frequency factors,
and all zeroth- and first-order quantities defined in the second-order notes.
Within the conventional central channel spaces, the third-order terms are
organized by corrections to the two coupling vertices and to the central
resolvent.
The unrestricted frequency convolution will show that additional orbital
sectors are activated at this order; these are introduced explicitly below
rather than being hidden inside the restricted ansatz.

For either primitive channel $r=\eh,\pp$, we expand
\begin{align}
	U^r
	&=
	U^{r,(1)}+U^{r,(2)}+\mathcal O(v^3)
	&
	V^r
	&=
	V^{r,(1)}+V^{r,(2)}+\mathcal O(v^3)
	\label{eq:target_UV_expansion}
	\\
	M^r
	&=
	M^{r,(0)}+M^{r,(1)}+\mathcal O(v^2)
	\label{eq:target_M_expansion}
\end{align}
The corresponding central resolvent becomes
\begin{align}
	R^r
	&=
	R^{r,(0)}
	+
	R^{r,(1)}
	+
	\mathcal O(v^2)
	\label{eq:target_R_expansion}
	\\
	R^{r,(1)}
	&=
	R^{r,(0)}M^{r,(1)}R^{r,(0)}
	\label{eq:target_R_first}
\end{align}
The third-order contribution captured by this restricted ansatz is
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\ii\Phi_{\rm proj}^{r,(3)}
	={}&
	U^{r,(2)}R^{r,(0)}V^{r,(1)}
	+
	U^{r,(1)}R^{r,(0)}V^{r,(2)}
	\\
	&+
	U^{r,(1)}R^{r,(1)}V^{r,(1)}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:target_third_order}
\end{equation}
The three terms correspond, respectively, to a second-order correction to
the left coupling vertex, a second-order correction to the right coupling
vertex, and a first-order correction to the central resolvent.
They exhaust the contribution obtained after projection onto the usual
central channel spaces, but not the unrestricted third-order convolution.
The complementary orbital-sector remainders are derived and kept explicitly
in the following sections.

\section{Direct eh vertex through third order}
\label{sec:eh_third}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The direct-$\eh$ construction uses without repetition the second-order
irreducible kernel $\Gamma^{\eh,(2)}$, the normalized factors $Q^\pm$,
the zeroth-order neutral resolvent $R^{\eh,(0)}$, and the first-order
coupling vertices $U^{\eh,(1)}$ and $V^{\eh,(1)}$ established in the
second-order notes.
The new ingredients are the second-order coupling-vertex corrections, the
first-order correction to the neutral resolvent, and the orbital sectors
that appear only in the unrestricted third-order convolution.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Perturbative BSE expansion}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

Define the reduced vertices and reduced non-interacting $\eh$ propagator
\begin{equation}
	\mathcal G^\eh
	=
	\ii\Gamma^\eh
	\qquad
	\mathcal P^\eh
	=
	\ii\Phi^\eh
	\qquad
	\mathcal L_0
	=
	-\ii L_0
	\label{eq:reduced_vertices}
\end{equation}
The last definition removes the explicit phase carried by the time-ordered
$\eh$ propagator. In the forward/backward $1h1p$ space its one-frequency
form is precisely the zeroth-order metric resolvent $R^{\eh,(0)}$.
Before integration over one fermionic frequency, products of frequency-dependent
vertices and two-particle propagators are frequency convolutions, denoted by $\circ$.
With the reduced quantities the third-order direct $\eh$ reducible vertex is
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\mathcal P^{\eh,(3)}
	={}&
	\mathcal G^{\eh,(2)}
	\circ \mathcal L_0
	\circ \mathcal G^{\eh,(1)}
	\\
	&+
	\mathcal G^{\eh,(1)}
	\circ \mathcal L_0
	\circ \mathcal G^{\eh,(2)}
	\\
	&+
	\mathcal G^{\eh,(1)}
	\circ \mathcal L_0
	\circ \mathcal G^{\eh,(1)}
	\circ \mathcal L_0
	\circ \mathcal G^{\eh,(1)}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:Phi_eh_third_convolution}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, if the unreduced $L_0$ is used instead, the first two terms
carry a prefactor $-\ii$ and the three-interaction term a prefactor $-1$.
No $\Gamma^{\eh,(3)}$ contribution appears in
$\Phi^{\eh,(3)}$ because an $\eh$ reducible diagram contains at least one
interaction on each side of the cut.

The fully irreducible vertex has no second- or third-order contribution
beyond the bare interaction. In the present convention the unambiguous
statement is
\begin{equation}
	\ii\Lambda^{(1)}_{pqrs}
	=
	\mel{pq}{}{rs}
	\qquad
	\Lambda
	=
	\Lambda^{(1)}+\mathcal O(v^4)
	\label{eq:Lambda_through_third}
\end{equation}
so that the second-order irreducible kernel entering
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Phi_eh_third_convolution} is precisely the
$\Gamma^{\eh,(2)}$ obtained in the second-order construction.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Orbital sectors in the third-order convolution}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The orbital restriction implicit in the ordinary neutral resolvent requires
special care at third order.
For a frequency-independent interaction, same-occupancy reference bubbles
vanish,
\begin{equation}
	\int\frac{dx}{2\pi}
	G_{0,a}(x+\omega)G_{0,b}(x)
	=
	0
	\qquad
	\int\frac{dx}{2\pi}
	G_{0,i}(x+\omega)G_{0,j}(x)
	=
	0
	\label{eq:same_occupancy_bubbles_zero}
\end{equation}
because the two one-particle poles lie in the same half-plane.
This is why the second-order direct $\eh$ vertex may be represented entirely
in the $1h1p/1p1h$ neutral space.

A frequency-dependent second-order vertex changes this conclusion.
For example, with $a,b,c$ virtual, $k$ occupied, and
$\Delta_{ck}=\eps_c-\eps_k$,
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	&\int\frac{dx}{2\pi}
	\frac{1}{x+\omega-\eps_a+\ii0^+}
	\frac{1}{x-\eps_b+\ii0^+}
	\frac{1}{x-\nu+\Delta_{ck}-\ii0^+}
	\\
	&\qquad=
	\frac{\ii}
	{[\nu+\omega-(\eps_a+\Delta_{ck})+\ii0^+]
	 [\nu-(\eps_b+\Delta_{ck})+\ii0^+]}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:vv_dynamic_counterexample}
\end{equation}
which is nonzero.
The analogous $oo$ contributions are also nonzero when the dynamic kernel
provides a pole in the opposite half-plane.

It is therefore useful to decompose the reduced non-interacting $\eh$ propagator as
\begin{equation}
	\mathcal L_0
	=
	\mathcal L_{0,\ph}
	+
	\mathcal L_{0,\rm so}
	\label{eq:L0_sector_decomposition}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal L_{0,\ph}$ contains the $ia$ and $ai$ sectors and
$\mathcal L_{0,\rm so}$ the same-occupancy $ab$ and $ij$ sectors.
The one-frequency projections used below evaluate the
$\mathcal L_{0,\ph}$ part exactly.
The remaining third-order contributions are
\begin{align}
	\Delta_L^{\eh,(3)}
	&=
	\mathcal G^{\eh,(2)}
	\circ\mathcal L_{0,\rm so}
	\circ\mathcal G^{\eh,(1)}
	\label{eq:Delta_eh_left_same_occ}
	\\
	\Delta_R^{\eh,(3)}
	&=
	\mathcal G^{\eh,(1)}
	\circ\mathcal L_{0,\rm so}
	\circ\mathcal G^{\eh,(2)}
	\label{eq:Delta_eh_right_same_occ}
\end{align}
The purely first-order ladder does not acquire such a remainder because the
interactions are frequency independent and the same-occupancy bubble then
vanishes before the next scattering event.
Consequently, the ordinary neutral-resolvent construction below should be
understood as the complete $\ph$-projected part of the third-order vertex;
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:Delta_eh_left_same_occ} and
\eqref{eq:Delta_eh_right_same_occ} must be retained for the unrestricted
three-frequency result.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Second-order corrections to the coupling vertices}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The left coupling-vertex correction for forward ($+$) central propagation through the pair $ia$ is obtained by integrating over one
fermionic frequency of the second-order irreducible kernel,
\begin{equation}
	U^{\eh,+,(2)}_{pr,ia}(\nu,\omega)
	=
	\frac{1}{2\pi}
	\int d\bar\nu
	\left[
	\ii\Gamma^{\eh,(2)}_{pa\,ri}
	(\nu,\bar\nu,\omega)
	\right]
	Q^+_{ia}(\bar\nu,\omega)
	\label{eq:U_eh_second_def}
\end{equation}
The explicit result is
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	U^{\eh,+,(2)}_{pr,ia}(\nu,\omega)
	={}&
	\sum_{jb}
	\frac{
	\mel{pb}{}{ij}
	\mel{aj}{}{rb}
	}
	{\nu-(\eps_i+\eps_j-\eps_b)-\ii0^+}
	\\
	&-
	\sum_{jb}
	\frac{
	\mel{pj}{}{ib}
	\mel{ab}{}{rj}
	}
	{\nu+\omega-(\eps_a+\eps_b-\eps_j)+\ii0^+}
	\\
	&-
	\frac{1}{2}
	\sum_{jk}
	\frac{
	\mel{pa}{}{jk}
	\mel{jk}{}{ri}
	}
	{\nu-(\eps_j+\eps_k-\eps_a)-\ii0^+}
	\\
	&+
	\frac{1}{2}
	\sum_{bc}
	\frac{
	\mel{pa}{}{bc}
	\mel{bc}{}{ri}
	}
	{\nu+\omega-(\eps_b+\eps_c-\eps_i)+\ii0^+}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:U_eh_second_explicit}
\end{equation}
The first two terms originate from the transverse $\eh$ channel
and the last two from the $\hh$ and $\pp$ branches.

The right coupling-vertex correction for forward ($+$) central propagation through the pair $ia$ follows from the right side of the $\eh$ BSE with its orbital ordering kept explicit,
\begin{equation}
	V^{\eh,+,(2)}_{ia,sq}(\nu',\omega)
	=
	\frac{1}{2\pi}
	\int d\bar\nu
	Q^+_{ia}(\bar\nu,\omega)
	\left[
	\ii\Gamma^{\eh,(2)}_{iq\,as}
	(\bar\nu,\nu',\omega)
	\right]
	\label{eq:V_eh_second_def}
\end{equation}
which gives
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	V^{\eh,+,(2)}_{ia,sq}(\nu',\omega)
	={}&
	\sum_{kc}
	\frac{
	\mel{sc}{}{ik}
	\mel{ak}{}{qc}
	}
	{\nu'-(\eps_i+\eps_k-\eps_c)-\ii0^+}
	\\
	&-
	\sum_{kc}
	\frac{
	\mel{sk}{}{ic}
	\mel{ac}{}{qk}
	}
	{\nu'+\omega-(\eps_a+\eps_c-\eps_k)+\ii0^+}
	\\
	&-
	\frac{1}{2}
	\sum_{kl}
	\frac{
	\mel{sa}{}{kl}
	\mel{kl}{}{qi}
	}
	{\nu'-(\eps_k+\eps_l-\eps_a)-\ii0^+}
	\\
	&+
	\frac{1}{2}
	\sum_{cd}
	\frac{
	\mel{sa}{}{cd}
	\mel{cd}{}{qi}
	}
	{\nu'+\omega-(\eps_c+\eps_d-\eps_i)+\ii0^+}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:V_eh_second_explicit}
\end{equation}
For an instantaneous first-order interaction, $\Gamma_{iq\,as}$ can be
related to alternative index groupings by ordinary integral symmetries.
For the frequency-dependent second-order kernel this replacement is not
permitted unless the corresponding frequency transformation is applied as
well; Eq.~\eqref{eq:V_eh_second_def} therefore fixes the right-side routing
used below.
The backward ($-$) coupling-vertex components follow from the same pair-reversal operation as
the corresponding second-order projections.
The explicit formulas are not required for the third-order matching.

Equations~\eqref{eq:U_eh_second_explicit} and
\eqref{eq:V_eh_second_explicit} demonstrate that the coupling vertices of the
reducible direct $\eh$ object contain the same $\hhp$ and $\pph$
resolvents as the partially integrated Hedin kernel.
The difference lies only in the grouping of the external indices.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{First-order correction to the neutral resolvent}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The first-order neutral interaction matrix is
\begin{equation}
	M^{\eh,(1)}
	=
	\begin{pmatrix}
	A^{(1)}&B^{(1)}
	\\
	B^{(1)\dagger}&A^{(1)\dagger}
	\end{pmatrix}
	\label{eq:M_eh_first}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{align}
	A^{(1)}_{ia,jb}
	&=
	\mel{aj}{}{ib}
	\label{eq:A_eh_first}
	\\
	B^{(1)}_{ia,jb}
	&=
	\mel{ab}{}{ij}
	\label{eq:B_eh_first}
\end{align}
Using the general expansion of Eq.~\eqref{eq:target_R_first}, the first-order neutral-resolvent correction is
\begin{equation}
	R^{\eh,(1)}
	=
	R^{\eh,(0)}
	M^{\eh,(1)}
	R^{\eh,(0)}
	\label{eq:R_eh_first}
\end{equation}
No additional metric matrix appears in Eq.~\eqref{eq:R_eh_first} because the
metric is already included in the definition of the resolvent.

In the Tamm--Dancoff approximation (TDA), the corresponding central third-order
contribution is
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\left[
	\ii\Phi_{\rm cent}^{\eh,(3)}
	\right]_{pqrs}
	={}&
	\sum_{ia,jb}
	\frac{
	\mel{pa}{}{ri}
	\mel{aj}{}{ib}
	\mel{qj}{}{sb}
	}
	{
	[\omega-(\eps_a-\eps_i)+\ii0^+]
	[\omega-(\eps_b-\eps_j)+\ii0^+]
	}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:Phi_eh_third_central_tda}
\end{equation}
The full forward-backward expression is generated automatically by the
first-order correction to the neutral resolvent
\begin{equation}
	U^{\eh,(1)}
	R^{\eh,(1)}
	V^{\eh,(1)}
	=
	U^{\eh,(1)}
	R^{\eh,(0)}
	M^{\eh,(1)}
	R^{\eh,(0)}
	V^{\eh,(1)}
	\label{eq:Phi_eh_third_central_matrix}
\end{equation}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Direct eh result through third order}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

After integration over one fermionic frequency on each side, the $\ph$ part of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Phi_eh_third_convolution} reduces to ordinary matrix products.
The unrestricted third-order result is therefore
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\ii\Phi^{\eh,(3)}
	={}&
	U^{\eh,(2)}R^{\eh,(0)}V^{\eh,(1)}
	+
	U^{\eh,(1)}R^{\eh,(0)}V^{\eh,(2)}
	\\
	&+
	U^{\eh,(1)}R^{\eh,(1)}V^{\eh,(1)}
	+
	\Delta_L^{\eh,(3)}
	+
	\Delta_R^{\eh,(3)}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:Phi_eh_third_final}
\end{equation}
The first three terms are the part captured by the ordinary neutral
$1h1p/1p1h$ resolvent: they correct the left coupling vertex, right coupling
vertex, and central neutral resolvent, respectively.
The last two terms contain the same-occupancy internal sectors activated by
the dynamic second-order kernel.
Thus the simple $URV$ ansatz is complete only after those sectors are either
represented in an enlarged auxiliary space or retained explicitly as the
remainders above.

The explicit products contain genuine multiresolvent structures.
For example, a correction to the left coupling vertex contains terms of the form
\begin{equation}
	\frac{1}{\nu-E^-_{I^-}-\ii0^+}
	\frac{1}{\omega-\Delta\eps_{ia}+\ii0^+}
	\label{eq:multi_resolvent_left_minus}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
	\frac{1}{\nu+\omega-E^+_{I^+}+\ii0^+}
	\frac{1}{\omega-\Delta\eps_{ia}+\ii0^+}
	\label{eq:multi_resolvent_left_plus}
\end{equation}
while the central correction contains two neutral resolvents separated by
the first-order interaction matrix.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{pp vertex through third order}
\label{sec:pp_third}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

For the $\pp$ channel we likewise take the zeroth-order metric, pair
matrix, central resolvent, and first-order coupling vertices from the
second-order construction.
The third-order extension requires the first-order correction to the pair
matrix, the second-order corrections to the coupling vertices, and the
mixed orbital sectors identified below.

The first-order pair interaction matrix is
\begin{equation}
	M^{\pp,(1)}
	=
	\begin{pmatrix}
	\mel{ab}{}{cd}&\mel{ab}{}{ij}
	\\
	\mel{ij}{}{cd}&\mel{ij}{}{kl}
	\end{pmatrix}
	\label{eq:M_pp_first}
\end{equation}
The upper-left block acts in the $N+2$ sector, the lower-right block in the
$N-2$ sector, and the off-diagonal blocks couple the two pair sectors.
Accordingly,
\begin{equation}
	R^\pp
	=
	R^{\pp,(0)}
	+
	R^{\pp,(1)}
	+
	\mathcal O(v^2)
	\label{eq:R_pp_expansion}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
	R^{\pp,(1)}
	=
	R^{\pp,(0)}
	M^{\pp,(1)}
	R^{\pp,(0)}
	\label{eq:R_pp_first}
\end{equation}

The second-order corrections to the $\pp$ coupling vertices are generated
from the previously derived $\Gamma^{\pp,(2)}$ by $\eh$ intermediate
propagation.
One-frequency integration with the non-interacting $\pp$ propagator produces
$N+1$ $\pph$ and $N-1$ $\hhp$ coupling-vertex poles.
For the two-particle central sector,
\begin{equation}
	U_{\ee}^{\pp,(2)}
	=
	\sum_{\ell}
	\sum_{I^+}
	\frac{
	\mathcal S^{+,\ell}_{I^+}
	}
	{z_\ell-E^+_{I^+}+\ii0^+}
	\label{eq:U_pp_ee_second}
\end{equation}
while for the two-hole central sector,
\begin{equation}
	U_{\hh}^{\pp,(2)}
	=
	\sum_{\ell}
	\sum_{I^-}
	\frac{
	\mathcal S^{-,\ell}_{I^-}
	}
	{z_\ell-E^-_{I^-}-\ii0^+}
	\label{eq:U_pp_hh_second}
\end{equation}
Here $z_\ell$ denotes the one-particle energy carried by either member of
the external pair and $\ell$ labels the two antisymmetrically related
attachments.
The corresponding right coupling vertices $V^{\pp,(2)}$ have the same pole spaces.
The residues $\mathcal S^{\pm,\ell}$ are not evaluated explicitly in the
present document.
They are not needed to state the formal parquet decomposition, but they are
required for an explicit third-order calculation or implementation.

The same orbital-sector issue appears in the $\pp$ channel.
The standard pair resolvent contains the $\ee$ and $\hh$ sectors, whereas a
frequency-dependent $\Gamma^{\pp,(2)}$ can activate mixed occupied--virtual
internal pairs whose isolated free pair integral vanishes.
Writing $\mathcal L_{0,\rm mix}^{\pp}$ symbolically for the mixed
occupied--virtual part of the reduced $\pp$ propagator and
$\mathcal G^{\pp}=\ii\Gamma^{\pp}$, the symbol
$\mathcal L_{0,\rm mix}^{\pp}$ is deliberately left unevaluated
here: its phase, pair normalization, and frequency routing depend on the
precise native pp BSE convention and cannot be inferred by copying the
$\eh$ definition $-\ii GG$.
Accordingly, the following equations are formal definitions of the missing
mixed-sector contributions rather than an evaluated auxiliary-space
representation.

\begin{align}
	\Delta_L^{\pp,(3)}
	&=
	\mathcal G^{\pp,(2)}
	\circ\mathcal L_{0,\rm mix}^{\pp}
	\circ\mathcal G^{\pp,(1)}
	\\
	\Delta_R^{\pp,(3)}
	&=
	\mathcal G^{\pp,(1)}
	\circ\mathcal L_{0,\rm mix}^{\pp}
	\circ\mathcal G^{\pp,(2)}
\end{align}
The unrestricted third-order result can therefore be organized as
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\ii\Phi_P^{\pp,(3)}
	={}&
	U^{\pp,(2)}R^{\pp,(0)}V^{\pp,(1)}
	+
	U^{\pp,(1)}R^{\pp,(0)}V^{\pp,(2)}
	\\
	&+
	U^{\pp,(1)}R^{\pp,(1)}V^{\pp,(1)}
	+
	\Delta_L^{\pp,(3)}
	+
	\Delta_R^{\pp,(3)}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:Phi_pp_third_final}
\end{equation}
The pure two-particle part of the central correction is the third-order
pair ladder
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\left[
	\ii\Phi_{P,\rm cent}^{\pp,(3)}
	\right]_{pqrs}^{\ee}
	={}&
	\sum_{\substack{a<b\\c<d}}
	\frac{
	\mel{pq}{}{ab}
	\mel{ab}{}{cd}
	\mel{cd}{}{rs}
	}
	{
	[\Omega-(\eps_a+\eps_b)+\ii0^+]
	[\Omega-(\eps_c+\eps_d)+\ii0^+]
	}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:Phi_pp_third_central_ee}
\end{equation}
The remaining $\ee\leftrightarrow\hh$ and $\hh\to\hh$ contributions are
generated by the other blocks of $M^{\pp,(1)}$.

The first three terms of Eq.~\eqref{eq:Phi_pp_third_final} contain the
second-order pp-irreducible kernel in the coupling vertices and one
first-order pp random-phase-approximation (ppRPA) interaction in the central
resolvent.
They give the complete contribution within the standard $\ee/\hh$ pair
space.
The mixed-sector remainders must be included, or represented by an enlarged
auxiliary space, before the unrestricted third-order $\pp$ vertex can be
called complete.
Because neither $\mathcal L_{0,\rm mix}^{\pp}$ nor the residues
$\mathcal S^{\pm,\ell}$ have been reduced to explicit orbital sums here, the
$\pp$ part of the present third-order extension should be regarded as a
formal decomposition rather than an implementable final representation.

In the direct-$\eh$ frequency convention, $\Omega_\pp=\omega+\nu+\nu'$, so the central $\pp$ resolvent generates the characteristic anti-diagonal
structure of the three-frequency vertex.
\section{Consistency checks at third order}
\label{sec:checks_third}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The third-order extension is deliberately overdetermined.
Several independent checks can therefore be applied to the new terms; the
second-order validations are not repeated here.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Status of the two-sided projection}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

At second order, the two-sided $Q^\pm$ projection is unambiguous because the
four-point kernel is mapped explicitly to pair-space blocks before the second
frequency integration.
At third order, the same shorthand cannot be used as an independent check
without first specifying the permutation that maps the external indices of
$\Phi_{pqrs}$ onto the pair-space matrix and the associated transformation of
the transfer frequency.
A literal reuse of the second-order block notation can otherwise map a
forward pole at $\omega-\Delta\eps$ onto its reversed counterpart at
$\omega+\Delta\eps$.

We therefore do not use a projected one-frequency identity as a validation of
the third-order construction in the present notes.
The algebraic resolvent identity
\begin{equation}
	R^{\eh,(1)}
	=
	R^{\eh,(0)}M^{\eh,(1)}R^{\eh,(0)}
	\label{eq:R_eh_first_repeated}
\end{equation}
remains exact for the stated resolvent definition, but a genuine two-sided
third-order check requires an explicit four-point-to-pair-space mapping for
every orientation before any projection is performed.
The complementary-sector terms $\Delta_L^{\eh,(3)}$ and
$\Delta_R^{\eh,(3)}$ must then be projected with the same mapping.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Crossing}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The transverse $\eh$ primitive is never constructed
independently.
At every perturbative order,
\begin{equation}
	\Phi^{\teh,(n)}_{pqrs}(\nu,\nu',\omega)
	=
	-
	\Phi^{\eh,(n)}_{pqsr}
	\left(
	\nu,
	\nu+\omega,
	\nu'-\nu
	\right)
	\label{eq:crossing_order_n}
\end{equation}
This maps each left, right, and central third-order direct $\eh$
contribution into the corresponding transverse topology and preserves the
fermionic crossing symmetry order by order.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Parquet reconstruction}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

Because the fully irreducible vertex has no third-order correction,
\begin{align}
	F^{(3)}
	&=
	\Phi^{\eh,(3)}+\Phi^{\teh,(3)}+\Phi^{\pp,(3)}
	\label{eq:F_n_parquet}
	\\
	\Gamma^{\eh,(3)}
	&=
	\Phi^{\teh,(3)}+\Phi^{\pp,(3)}
	\label{eq:Gamma_eh_n}
	\\
	\Gamma^{\pp,(3)}
	&=
	\Phi^{\eh,(3)}+\Phi^{\teh,(3)}
	\label{eq:Gamma_pp_n}
	\\
	F^{(3)}
	&=
	\Gamma^{\eh,(3)}+\Phi^{\eh,(3)}
	=
	\Gamma^{\pp,(3)}+\Phi^{\pp,(3)}
	\label{eq:channel_consistency_n}
\end{align}
The direct $\eh$ Hedin kernel is therefore reconstructed from the
two primitive multiresolvent objects through
$\Gamma^\eh=\Lambda+\Phi^{\teh}+\Phi^\pp$.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{High-frequency structure}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The second-order corrections to the coupling vertices decay as the inverse of their fermionic frequency,
\begin{equation}
	U^{(2)}
	=
	\mathcal O(\nu^{-1})
	\qquad
	V^{(2)}
	=
	\mathcal O({\nu'}^{-1})
	\label{eq:external_high_frequency}
\end{equation}
When both fermionic frequencies are large, only the central
$\omega$-dependent contribution survives at third order.
The resulting hierarchy is therefore consistent with the standard
parquet asymptotic decomposition into a purely bosonic part and left and
right one-fermionic-frequency corrections.

The same argument applies in the pp channel with the central pair
frequency $\Omega_\pp$ held fixed.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Diagram classes and orbital-sector completeness}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

Every third-order diagram reducible in the direct $\eh$ channel belongs to
one of three diagrammatic classes,
\begin{equation}
	\Gamma^{\eh,(2)}L_0\Gamma^{\eh,(1)}
	\qquad
	\Gamma^{\eh,(1)}L_0\Gamma^{\eh,(2)}
	\qquad
	\Gamma^{\eh,(1)}L_0\Gamma^{\eh,(1)}L_0\Gamma^{\eh,(1)}
	\label{eq:third_classes_eh}
\end{equation}
and the corresponding statement holds in the $\pp$ channel.
This topological classification avoids double counting between parquet
channels, but it does not by itself justify restricting the internal orbital
sums of the first two classes to the conventional central channel spaces.
Because $\Gamma^{(2)}$ is frequency dependent, the same-occupancy $vv/oo$
sectors in the $\eh$ channel and mixed sectors in the $\pp$ channel can be
nonzero, as demonstrated by Eq.~\eqref{eq:vv_dynamic_counterexample}.

Consequently, the $URV$ expressions derived above are the complete
contributions within the standard $\ph$ or $\ee/\hh$ central spaces, while
the unrestricted third-order vertex additionally contains the explicit
remainders $\Delta_{L/R}^{r,(3)}$.
Parquet crossing and reconstruction remain valid once these remainders are
included; they cannot recover terms that have already been discarded by an
orbital-space projection inside a primitive channel.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{Recipe through third order}
\label{sec:recipe}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

Given the second-order ingredients $U^{r,(1)}$, $V^{r,(1)}$,
$R^{r,(0)}$, and $\Gamma^{r,(2)}$, the third-order analysis proceeds in
four steps for each primitive channel $r=\eh,\pp$.

First, obtain the second-order corrections to the left and right coupling
vertices from the corresponding second-order channel-irreducible kernel.
Their poles remain confined to the $(N-1)$ $\hhp$ and $(N+1)$ $\pph$
sectors when the central pair is projected onto the conventional channel
space.

Second, construct the first-order correction to the central matrix,
$M^{r,(1)}$, and hence
\begin{equation}
	R^{r,(1)}
	=
	R^{r,(0)}M^{r,(1)}R^{r,(0)}
	\label{eq:recipe_R_first}
\end{equation}
The central resolvent describes $N$-electron propagation for $r=\eh$ and
$(N\pm2)$-electron propagation for $r=\pp$.

Third, evaluate the complementary internal orbital sectors before carrying
out any orbital-space restriction: $vv$ and $oo$ for the direct $\eh$
convolution, and mixed occupied--virtual pairs for the $\pp$ convolution.
Collect them in $\Delta_L^{r,(3)}$ and $\Delta_R^{r,(3)}$.

Fourth, assemble
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\ii\Phi^{r,(3)}
	={}&
	U^{r,(2)}R^{r,(0)}V^{r,(1)}
	+
	U^{r,(1)}R^{r,(0)}V^{r,(2)}
	\\
	&+
	U^{r,(1)}R^{r,(1)}V^{r,(1)}
	+
	\Delta_L^{r,(3)}
	+
	\Delta_R^{r,(3)}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:recipe_third_general}
\end{equation}
Generate $\Phi^{\teh,(3)}$ by crossing and reconstruct
\begin{align}
	\Gamma^{\eh,(3)}
	&=
	\Phi^{\teh,(3)}+\Phi^{\pp,(3)}
	\\
	F^{(3)}
	&=
	\Phi^{\eh,(3)}+\Phi^{\teh,(3)}+\Phi^{\pp,(3)}
\end{align}
The resulting expressions are complete only when the complementary-sector
remainders are retained or represented in an enlarged auxiliary space.

The $\Delta_{L/R}^{r,(3)}$ terms therefore identify precisely where the
central spaces of the second-order representation must be enlarged to
represent the unrestricted third-order three-frequency vertex.
