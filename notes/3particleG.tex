\title{From the three-particle Green's function to the parquet equations,
to ADC, and beyond}

\begin{abstract}
In three stages, these notes point out the connection between the parquet and the ADC
constructions of the self-energy. First, we show that the
single-particle Green's function is naturally
expressed in terms of the three-particle Green's
function through the Martin--Schwinger hierarchies of
equations of motion for $n$-th order Green's functions.
We then introduce the Faddeev decomposition of the 
three-particle vertex and show that it naturally leads to 
the parquet approximation of the self-energy. 
In a next step, finite-order Faddeev kernels are introduced, 
and it is shown that the formalism with the $n$th-order Faddeev kernel, before any resummation, is equivalent to GF($n+2$); this is worked out explicitly for $n=1$. Within the Tamm--Dancoff approximation (TDA) 
to the $\eh$ and $\pp$ Bethe--Salpeter equations, 
ADC(3)/ADC(4) are obtained. Beyond the TDA, 
to first order in the Faddeev kernel we obtain a
Hermitian version of the Faddeev random-phase approximation (FRPA), and at fourth order 
we obtain a new partial parquet approximation. 
Full parquet is significantly more involved, and includes 
a) an internally resummed infinite-order Faddeev kernel, 
b) cross-channel interactions, and c) self-consistency in the 
single-particle propagator. 
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
Via the three-particle Green's function $G_3$,
two routes to the one-particle self-energy are connected: the
parquet equations for the two-particle vertex and the
algebraic-diagrammatic construction (ADC).
Starting from the Martin--Schwinger hierarchy (Sec.~\ref{sec:MS}), we
express the correlation self-energy in terms of a 6-point response
function $R$, which obeys a Dyson equation with a three-particle kernel
(Sec.~\ref{sec:eliminating}). Dropping its 3-body irreducible part
leaves the Faddeev kernel,\cite{Faddeev1961,Winter1972,Ethofer1969} in
which two of the three lines scatter through a two-particle vertex
while the third line is a spectator. In the Faddeev form, this vertex
is the full parquet vertex $F$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:Faddeev}).\cite{Kugler2018}

Truncated at fixed order in $v$, the Faddeev series gives GF$n$, and
Schirmer's matching gives ADC($n$) (App.~\ref{app:ADC}). The connection
becomes useful once the ladders in the pairs are resummed beyond the
Tamm--Dancoff ladders contained in ADC (Sec.~\ref{sec:parquet_Heff}).
With the RPA ladders of the first-order Faddeev kernel in every pair,
and without overlaps between the ladders of different pairs, a
Hermitian effective Hamiltonian of the size of ADC(3) is obtained, that
describes the coupling of a single particle to $\pp$ and $\eh$
phonons. This is essentially the Faddeev random-phase approximation
(FRPA) of Barbieri and co-workers.\cite{Barbieri2001,
barbieri2003extension,barbieri2007quasiparticles,Degroote2011}
In FRPA, the couplings are those of ADC(3), $U^{(1)}+U^{(2)}$, added by
hand;\cite{Degroote2011} here they follow from the backward amplitudes
of the phonons, the ring-CCD ($\eh$) and ladder-CCD ($\pp$) amplitudes
of the channels.\cite{Scuseria2013} The hierarchy is
$\text{ADC(3)}\subset\text{FRPA}\subset\text{parquet}$ for the dynamic
self-energy. At fourth order, the crossed channels, the overlaps and the
line dressing are added in the $\ppphh$ space
(Sec.~\ref{sec:overlap}).

Three approximations are made by construction. The parquet
approximation, $\Lambda=\Lambda^{(1)}$, keeps $F$ exact through third
order, since $\Lambda=\Lambda^{(1)}+O(v^4)$,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:crossing_Lambda}. With a static HF reference in all lines
of the vertex, every diagram of the vertex is a product of
instantaneous interactions and $G_{\rm HF}$ lines, whose frequency
integrals turn into resolvents of configuration spaces. This makes the
effective-Hamiltonian form possible. Finally, the Faddeev approximation
affects the self-energy from fifth order on. The orders through which
the resulting self-energies are exact are collected in
Table~\ref{tab:orders}; the constructions derived in these notes are exact through fourth order at most.

\section{Martin--Schwinger Equations}
\label{sec:MS}
Let us define the $n$-particle Green's function as 
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:Gn_definition}
\begin{aligned}
G_n(1\ldots n;1'\ldots n')
={}&
(-\ii)^n
\big\langle
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{C}}\,
\hat{\psi}(1)\cdots\hat{\psi}(n)
\\
&\times
\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}(n')\cdots\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}(1')
\big\rangle \;,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with $\hat{\psi}$ in the Heisenberg picture.
$G_n$ might be expressed in terms of $G_{n-1}$ and
$G_{n+1}$ through the
Martin--Schwinger hierarchy,
\begin{align}
&
\left[
\ii\partial_{t_1}
-
h_0(1)
\right]
G_n(1\ldots n;1'\ldots n')
\nonumber\\
={}&
\sum_{j=1}^{n}
(-1)^{j-1}
\delta(1j')\,
G_{n-1}
\bigl(
2\ldots n;
1'\ldots \widehat{j'}\ldots n'
\bigr)
\nonumber\\
&+
(-1)^{n}\,\ii\,
v(1\bar2;\bar3\bar4)\,
G_{n+1}
\bigl(
\bar3\bar4\,2\ldots n;
1'\ldots n'\bar2^{+}
\bigr) \;.
\label{eq:Martin_Schwinger_general}
\end{align}
We define 
\begin{equation}
G_0^{-1}(11')
=
\left[
\ii\frac{\partial}{\partial t_1}
-
h_0(1)
\right]
\delta(11') \;,
\label{eq:G0_inverse_def}
\end{equation}
and its inverse via
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:G0_inverse}
G_0^{-1}(1\bar{1})\,
G_0(\bar{1}1')
=
\delta(11') \;.
\end{equation}
Using both relations, $G_1$ through $G_3$ become
\begin{align}
G(11')
= & 
G_0(11')
\nonumber\\
& -\ii \,
G_0(1\bar3)\,
v(\bar3\bar4;\bar5\bar6)\,
G_2(\bar5\bar6;1'\bar4^+) \;,
\label{eq:Martin_Schwinger_G1} \\
G_2(12;1'2')
= & 
G_0(11')G(22')
-
G_0(12')G(21')
\nonumber\\
& +
\ii \,
G_0(1\bar3)\,
v(\bar3\bar4;\bar5\bar6)
\nonumber\\
&\times
G_3(2\bar5\bar6;1'2'\bar4^+) \;,
\label{eq:Martin_Schwinger_G2}\\
G_3(123;1'2'3')
= &
G_0(11')\,
G_2(23;2'3') \nonumber \\
& -
G_0(12')\,
G_2(23;1'3')
\nonumber\\
& +
G_0(13')\,
G_2(23;1'2')
\nonumber\\
& -
\ii\,
G_0(1\bar4)\,
v(\bar4\bar5;\bar6\bar7)
\nonumber\\
&\times
G_4(23\bar6\bar7;1'2'3'\bar5^+) \;,
\label{eq:Martin_Schwinger_G3}
\end{align}
Through Eq.~\eqref{eq:Martin_Schwinger_G1}, we can define the single-particle
self-energy according to 
\begin{equation}
	\Sigma(11')
	=
	-\ii
	v(1\bar2;\bar3'\bar2')
	G_2(\bar3'\bar2';\bar3\bar2^+)
	G^{-1}(\bar3 1') \;.
	\label{eq:SDE_G2}
\end{equation}
\section{Eliminating $G_2$}
\label{sec:eliminating}
We can now use Eq.~\eqref{eq:Martin_Schwinger_G2}
to eliminate the dependence of 
Eq.~\eqref{eq:SDE_G2} in favour of $G_3$. 
$G_4$ might be eliminated as well, 
and Eq.~\eqref{eq:SDE_G2} can be expressed in terms 
of $G_3$ and $G_5$, or, more generally, in terms 
of odd-order Green's functions. Alternatively,
combining
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Martin_Schwinger_G2} with 
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Martin_Schwinger_G3}, one can express 
Eq.~\eqref{eq:SDE_G2} in terms of $G_2$ and $G_4$,
or, more generally, in terms 
of even-order Green's functions.

Here, we take the odd-order route. To do so, following 
\citet{Winter1972} and \citet{Ethofer1969}, 
we introduce the 6-point response function
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
    R(123;1'2'3')
    &=
    G_3(123;1'2'3')
    \\
    &\quad
    -
    G_2(23;3'\bar4)\,
    G^{-1}(\bar4\bar4')\,
    G_2(\bar4'1;1'2') \;,
\end{split}
\label{eq:response_function}
\end{equation}
so that the EOM for $G_2$ becomes
\begin{align}
G_2(12;1'2')
&=
G(11')G(22')
-
G(12')G(21')
\nonumber\\
&\quad+
\ii\,
G(1\bar3)\,
v(\bar3\bar4;\bar5'\bar6')\,
R(2\bar5'\bar6';1'2'\bar4^+).
\label{eq:SDE_G2_R}
\end{align}
Plugging Eq.~\eqref{eq:SDE_G2_R}
into Eq.~\eqref{eq:SDE_G2} and
using Eq.~\eqref{eq:G0_inverse} gives
\begin{equation}
\Sigma(11')
=
\Sigma_{\rm HF}(11')
+
\Sigma^{c}(11')
\label{eq:Sigma_split}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{align}
\Sigma_{\rm HF}(11')
= &
-\ii\,
\big[
v(1\bar2;1'\bar2')
-
v(1\bar2;\bar2'1')
\big]\,
G(\bar2'\bar2^+),
\label{eq:Sigma_HF}
\\[0.5em]
\Sigma^{c}(11')
= &
v(1\bar2;\bar3'\bar2')\,
G(\bar3'\bar4)\,
v(\bar4\bar5;\bar6'\bar7') \nonumber \\
& \times
R(\bar2'\bar6'\bar7';\bar3\bar2\bar5^+) 
G^{-1}(\bar3 1').
\label{eq:Sigma_3}
\end{align}
The response function $R$ obeys a Dyson equation with the
$\pph$-irreducible three-particle kernel $\Xi$, whose free propagator is
the $\pph$ propagator of three independent (dressed) lines,
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:D_pph}
\mathcal G(312;1'2'3')
=
G(33')
\left[
G(11')\,G(22')
-
G(12')\,G(21')
\right] \;.
\end{equation}
As in $G_3$, all
three lines run from the second to the first triple. From here on, the
first triple of $R$ is ordered as in Eq.~\eqref{eq:D_pph}, with the line 3 of $\mathcal G$ in the first slot. The second triple keeps the order $(1'2'3')$, so that line 3 runs from the last slot of the second triple to the first slot of the first.
Which parts of $G_3$ are contained in $R$ is shown in App.~\ref{app:R_content}.

\section{Faddeev Decomposition}
\label{sec:Faddeev}
The kernel $\Xi$ of the Dyson equation for $R$ is split into the Faddeev
(pairwise-reducible) part and the 3-body irreducible kernel
$\Xi_{\rm 3B}$, which is dropped from now on. In the Faddeev part, one pair
of lines scatters through a two-particle vertex while the third line
propagates freely.\cite{Faddeev1961,Ethofer1969,Winter1972,Barbieri2001}
Since the two lines of every pair run in the same direction, each pair
scatters in its $\pp$ channel, with the $\pp$-irreducible vertex
$\Gamma^{\pp}$ (exact at first order, see App.~\ref{app:parquet_vertices}).
As in Refs.~\citenum{Ethofer1969,Winter1972}, the Dyson equation for $R$ then reads
\begin{equation}
R=\mathcal G+\cK R \;,
\qquad
\cK
=
\cK_{12}\big[\Gamma^{\pp}\big]
+\cK_{31}\big[\Gamma^{\pp}\big]
+\cK_{32}\big[\Gamma^{\pp}\big] \;,
\label{eq:K_pairs}
\end{equation}
where the pair operators act, for any 4-point $Y$ and any $\chi$
antisymmetric in its first pair, as
\begin{align}
\big[\cK_{12}[Y]\chi\big](312;1'2'3')
={}&
-\frac14\big[G(1\bar1)G(2\bar2)-G(1\bar2)G(2\bar1)\big]
\nonumber\\
&\times
Y(\bar1\bar2;\bar1'\bar2')\,\chi(3\bar1'\bar2';1'2'3'),
\nonumber\\
\big[\cK_{31}[Y]\chi\big](312;1'2'3')
={}&
G(3\bar3)\,G(1\bar1)\,Y(\bar3\bar1;\bar1'\bar3')
\nonumber\\
&\times
\chi(\bar3'\bar1'2;1'2'3'),
\nonumber\\
\big[\cK_{32}[Y]\chi\big](312;1'2'3')
={}&
G(3\bar3)\,G(2\bar2)\,Y(\bar3\bar2;\bar2'\bar3')
\nonumber\\
&\times
\chi(\bar3'1\bar2';1'2'3').
\label{eq:pair_ops}
\end{align}

Cutting the two lines of pair $\alpha$ separates the outgoing from the
incoming legs of $Y$ in $\cK_\alpha[Y]$, \ie\ all three pairs scatter
in the $\pp$ channel of $R$.
In $\Sigma^{c}$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:Sigma_3}, line 3 becomes the hole for
$t_{1'}<t_1$ (block II) or the particle for $t_1<t_{1'}$ (block I). The
pairs $(31)$ and $(32)$ then give the ring-type self-energy diagrams,
labelled $\eh$ and $\teh$ below.

Eq.~\eqref{eq:K_pairs} is exact at first order, where all
vertices are $\Gamma^{(1)}$. Beyond first order, iterating $\cK_{3x}[\Gamma^{\pp}]$ alone would
count the rungs of the ladders in the pairs $(31)$ and $(32)$ twice,
since $\mathcal G$ is not antisymmetrized in the lines 3 and $x$. The $\pp$ channel of $R$ in the pair $(3x)$ is the $\eh$ channel of $F$, whose ladder $\Phi^{\eh}$ is already contained in $\Gamma^{\pp}$.\cite{Degroote2011} We therefore
replace $\Gamma^{\pp}$ in $\cK_\alpha$ by a pair vertex $\Gamma_\alpha$,
fixed such that the pair $\alpha$ alone reproduces its full two-body
scattering $F$. For the pair $(12)$, $\Gamma_{12}=\Gamma^{\pp}$, and at
first order $\Gamma_\alpha=\Gamma^{(1)}$ for all pairs. Their explicit form is not needed,
since only $F$ appears in the Faddeev form derived next.

\subsection{\label{sec:Faddeev_form}Faddeev form of Eq.~\eqref{eq:Sigma_3}: the ADC--parquet link}
Eq.~\eqref{eq:K_pairs} contains the pair-irreducible vertex
$\Gamma^{\pp}$ in all three pairs.
The aim of this subsection is to rewrite $R$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:Sigma_3} such
that only the full vertex $F$ appears in all three pairs.
This is the Faddeev form of the three-body problem.\cite{Faddeev1961}

Based on the Faddeev construction, we will then show that 
GF$n$ is the truncation of the Faddeev series,
at total order $n-2$ in $v$. 
The ADC($n$) matrices then
follow from Schirmer's matching,\cite{Schirmer1983} in which the
resolvent form of ADC($n$) is imposed and matched to the self-energy
order by order. ADC(3) is worked out as an
illustrative case in App.~\ref{app:ADC}.

With the pair vertices $\Gamma_\alpha$ in place of $\Gamma^{\pp}$,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:K_pairs} reads
\begin{equation}
R=\mathcal G+\sum_{\alpha}\cK_\alpha\big[\Gamma_\alpha\big] R \;,
\label{eq:Dyson_pairs}
\end{equation}
where $\cK_\alpha\big[\Gamma_\alpha\big]$ acts only on the pair $\alpha$, with the
third line as a free spectator [Fig.~\ref{fig:Faddeev_pairs}(b), with
the thick bars standing for $\Gamma_\alpha$].
We collect the terms of $R$ in which the last interaction, \ie\ the
one closest to the outgoing triple, acts on the pair $\alpha$ into
$R_\alpha\equiv\cK_\alpha\big[\Gamma_\alpha\big] R$. Solving
$R_\alpha=\cK_\alpha\big[\Gamma_\alpha\big]\big(\mathcal G+R_\alpha+\sum_{\beta\neq\alpha}R_\beta\big)$
for $R_\alpha$ gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Faddeev_rearr}
\begin{aligned}
R
= &
\mathcal G+R_{12}+R_{31}+R_{32} \;,
\\
R_\alpha
= &
\big(\bI-\cK_\alpha\big[\Gamma_\alpha\big]\big)^{-1}\cK_\alpha\big[\Gamma_\alpha\big]
\Big(\mathcal G+\sum_{\beta\neq\alpha}R_\beta\Big) \;,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $(\bI-\cK_\alpha\big[\Gamma_\alpha\big])^{-1}\cK_\alpha\big[\Gamma_\alpha\big]$ sums all
consecutive interactions in the pair $\alpha$.

Acting on $\mathcal G$, it gives all
diagrams of $R$ in which only the pair $\alpha$ interacts, while the
third line remains a free spectator. By the definition of $\Gamma_\alpha$,
this is the full two-body scattering of the pair,
$\cK_\alpha[F]=\cK_\alpha\big[\Gamma_\alpha\big]+\cK_\alpha\big[\Gamma_\alpha\big]\cK_\alpha[F]$,
so that
\begin{equation}
\big(\bI-\cK_\alpha\big[\Gamma_\alpha\big]\big)^{-1}\cK_\alpha\big[\Gamma_\alpha\big]
=
\cK_\alpha[F] \;.
\label{eq:V_to_F}
\end{equation}
Inserting
Eq.~\eqref{eq:V_to_F} into
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Faddeev_rearr} gives the Faddeev
equations\cite{Barbieri2001,Degroote2011}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Faddeev_F}
R_\alpha
=
\cK_\alpha[F]\Big(\mathcal G+\sum_{\beta\neq\alpha}R_\beta\Big) \;.
\end{equation}
Iterating Eq.~\eqref{eq:Faddeev_F}, \ie\ inserting the $R_\beta$ on
the right-hand side again and again, gives the Faddeev series
\cite{watson1953multiple,Faddeev1961,Barbieri2001}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Faddeev_series}
\begin{aligned}
R
= &
\mathcal G+\sum_{\alpha}\cK_\alpha[F]\mathcal G
+\sum_{\alpha\neq\beta}\cK_\alpha[F]\cK_\beta[F]\mathcal G
\\
& +\sum_{\substack{\alpha\neq\beta\\\beta\neq\gamma}}
\cK_\alpha[F]\cK_\beta[F]\cK_\gamma[F]\mathcal G+\cdots \;,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
in which consecutive pairs are always different (Fig.~\ref{fig:Faddeev_runs}).
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Faddeev_series} is the starting point for two
approximations. Truncated at fixed order in $v$, it gives GF$n$ and, with Schirmer's matching, ADC($n$), which we
need for the fourth-order completion in Sec.~\ref{sec:overlap}. Keeping
only the own-channel ladders without overlaps, but to all orders, gives
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Faddeev_series_oc}, from which an effective Hamiltonian is 
derived in Sec.~\ref{sec:parquet_Heff}.

\subsubsection{Truncation at fixed order: ADC}
To relate Eq.~\eqref{eq:Faddeev_series} to ADC, we expand $F$ in powers
of $v$. By Eqs.~\eqref{eq:parquet_channels} and
\eqref{eq:crossing_Lambda}, $\Lambda$ has no second-order term,
so that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:F_orders}
\begin{aligned}
F^{(1)}
= &
\Gamma^{(1)} \;,
\\
F^{(2)}
= &
\Phi^{\pp,(2)}+\Phi^{\eh,(2)}+\Phi^{\teh,(2)} \;.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The self-energy, Eq.~\eqref{eq:Sigma_3}, contains two explicit
interactions $v$. A term of order $m$ in $v$ of $R$ therefore contributes to
$\Sigma$ at order $m+2$. ADC($n$) is complete through order $n$, so
that $R$ is needed through order $n-2$ in $v$. Collecting the terms of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Faddeev_series} up to this order,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ADC_Faddeev}
\begin{aligned}
R_{\rm ADC(2)}
= &
\mathcal G \;,
\\
R_{\rm ADC(3)}
= &
\mathcal G+\sum_{\alpha}\cK_\alpha\big[F^{(1)}\big]\mathcal G_{\rm HF} \;,
\\
R_{\rm ADC(4)}
= &
\mathcal G+\sum_{\alpha}\cK_\alpha\big[F^{(1)}+F^{(2)}\big]\mathcal G_{\rm HF}
\\
& +\sum_{\alpha\neq\beta}
\cK_\alpha\big[F^{(1)}\big]\cK_\beta\big[F^{(1)}\big]\mathcal G_{\rm HF} \;.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here, $\mathcal G$ in the first term, $G(\bar3'\bar4)$ and the static part
$\Sigma_{\rm HF}$ are evaluated with $G$ expanded to the corresponding
order, while in all terms with at least one $F$, all lines can be taken at the HF level; $\mathcal G_{\rm HF}$ denotes Eq.~\eqref{eq:D_pph} with $G_{\rm HF}$ lines.

\subsubsection{Own-Channel and No-Overlap Approximations}

We now introduce the own-channel approximation
to Eq.~\eqref{eq:Faddeev_series}.
For this, we approximate the BSEs Eq.~\eqref{eq:parquet_BSE}
in the own-channel approximation, for the special case of 
the first-order Faddeev kernel, so that 
$\Gamma^{\lambda}\to\Gamma^{(1)}$.
The two BSEs then define two separate vertices
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
F^{\pp}_{\rm oc}
= &
\Gamma^{(1)}-\frac12\,\Gamma^{(1)}\circ_{\pp}F^{\pp}_{\rm oc} \;,
\\
F^{\eh}_{\rm oc}
= &
\Gamma^{(1)}+\Gamma^{(1)}\circ_{\eh}F^{\eh}_{\rm oc} \;.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:BSE_oc}
\end{equation}
This is also done in the FLEX approximation.\cite{Bickers1989,Bickers2004}

In the own-channel approximation to Eq.~\eqref{eq:Faddeev_series},
each pair scatters only through the
ladder of its own channel with the bare kernel. 
The own-channel approximation amounts to 
neglecting the channel crossings of
parquet (and of coupled cluster) inside a pair.\cite{Barbieri2001}
\citet{Barbieri2001} combined this approximation additionally with
a no-overlap approximation that we denote here by 
$[\cdots]_{\pph}$. It neglects the time orderings in which the ladders of
different pairs overlap in time, \ie\ consecutive pairs are separated by
a $\pph$ cut, a subinterval spanned only by the three lines of a $\pph$
state (Sec.~\ref{sec:depth1}). 
Due to this restriction, at first-order Faddeev the effective
Hamiltonian only contains $\pph$ ($\hhp$) configurations.
At fourth order, the neglected terms are
added perturbatively together with the $\ppphh$
($\hhhpp$) space.
Each pair scatters with the own-channel vertex of its channel,
\begin{equation}
F_{12}=F^{\pp}_{\rm oc} \;,
\qquad
F_{31}=F_{32}=F^{\eh}_{\rm oc} \;.
\label{eq:F_alpha}
\end{equation}
With these approximations, Eq.~\eqref{eq:Faddeev_series} becomes 
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Faddeev_series_oc}
\begin{aligned}
R_{\rm oc}
= &
\mathcal G+\sum_{\alpha}\cK_\alpha[F_\alpha]\mathcal G
+\sum_{\alpha\neq\beta}\Big[\cK_\alpha[F_\alpha]\cK_\beta[F_\beta]\mathcal G\Big]_{\pph}
\\
& +\sum_{\substack{\alpha\neq\beta\\\beta\neq\gamma}}
\Big[\cK_\alpha[F_\alpha]\cK_\beta[F_\beta]\cK_\gamma[F_\gamma]\mathcal G\Big]_{\pph}
+\cdots \;.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Expanded in $v$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:Faddeev_series_oc} reproduces
Eq.~\eqref{eq:ADC_Faddeev} through $R_{\rm ADC(3)}$. At the next order,
it misses the crossed-channel parts of $F^{(2)}$ in each pair,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:F_orders}, and the overlapping orderings of
$\cK_\alpha[F^{(1)}]\cK_\beta[F^{(1)}]\mathcal G$.

\section{\label{sec:parquet_Heff}Parquet in effective-Hamiltonian form}
In this section, we insert the own-channel and no-overlap approximation,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Faddeev_series_oc}, into Eq.~\eqref{eq:Sigma_3}, 
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Sigma^{c}(11')
={}&
v(1\bar2;\bar3'\bar2')\,
G(\bar3'\bar4)\,
v(\bar4\bar5;\bar6'\bar7')
\\
&\times
R_{\rm oc}(\bar2'\bar6'\bar7';\bar3\bar2\bar5^+)\,
G^{-1}(\bar3 1') \;.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:Sigma_oc}
\end{equation}
We then show, that, with $G_{\rm HF}$ lines, the self-energy becomes
\begin{equation}
	\begin{aligned}
	\Sigma(\omega) = &
\Sigma(\infty)
+\mathcal U^{\rm II}\big(\omega\mathcal N^{\rm II}-\mathcal H^{\rm II}\big)^{-1}\mathcal U^{{\rm II}\dagger} \\
&
+\mathcal U^{\rm I}\big(\omega\mathcal N^{\rm I}+\mathcal H^{\rm I}\big)^{-1}\mathcal U^{{\rm I}\dagger} \;,
	\end{aligned}
\label{eq:Sigma_goal}
\end{equation}
with Hermitian $\mathcal H$ and $\mathcal N$, and the static self-energy
$\Sigma(\infty)=\Sigma_{\rm HF}[G]$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:Sigma_HF}.
At the level of the first-order Faddeev kernel, this is 
essentially the Faddeev random-phase approximation
(FRPA) of Barbieri and co-workers.
\cite{Barbieri2001,
barbieri2003extension,
barbieri2007quasiparticles,
Degroote2011}
The difference is that the effective Hamiltonian derived here
remains Hermitian as long as its metric is positive definite,
whereas the Faddeev eigenvalue problem of FRPA is non-Hermitian
and contains spurious solutions.\cite{Degroote2011}

Evaluating Eq.~\eqref{eq:Sigma_oc} requires the
propagation of the pairs, free and interacting
(Sec.~\ref{sec:propagators}), the sequences of
pairs (Sec.~\ref{sec:depth1}), and the coupling to the external
vertices (Sec.~\ref{sec:ext_vertices}).

We first introduce some notation.
We use real canonical spin-orbitals and map numbers to orbitals
with $1,2,1',2'\to p,q,r,s$.
Since $\bar v$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:Gamma1_bare}, is instantaneous,
\begin{equation}
\bar v(12;1'2')
\to
\mel{pq}{}{rs}\,
\delta(t_1-t_2)\,\delta(t_1-t_{1'})\,\delta(t_2-t_{2'}),
\label{eq:vbar_orbital}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
\mel{pq}{}{rs} =
\braket{pq}{rs}-\braket{pq}{sr} \;.
\label{eq:mel_def}
\end{equation}
The antisymmetrizer of a pair of indices is
\begin{equation}
\hat P^-_{ab}=1-\hat P_{ab} \;,
\label{eq:Pminus}
\end{equation}
where $\hat P_{ab}$ exchanges $a$ and $b$.
In canonical spin-orbitals $p$ with energies $\eps_p$, occupations
$n_p$ ($n_i=1$, $n_a=0$, $\bar n_p=1-n_p$), and
$\sigma_p=\bar n_p-n_p$, the HF Green's function is
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:GHF_omega}
G_{{\rm HF},p}(\omega)
=
\frac{\bar n_p}{\omega-\eps_p+\ii0^+}
+
\frac{n_p}{\omega-\eps_p-\ii0^+}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Pair propagators}
\label{sec:propagators}
In Eq.~\eqref{eq:Faddeev_series_oc}, each term
$\cK_\alpha\mathcal G$ lets the pair $\alpha$ scatter through its
own-channel ladder, Eq.~\eqref{eq:BSE_oc}, while the third line
propagates freely. This means, key to the evaluation of this expression 
are the pair propagators. 

The non-interacting particle-particle propagator is 
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:L_pp}
	L^{\pp}_0(12;1'2')=G(11')G(22')-G(12')G(21') \;,
\end{equation}
and performing the frequency integrations,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:L0pp_omega}
\begin{aligned}
L^{\pp}_{0,xy}(\Omega)
= &
\int\frac{\dd\nu}{2\pi}\,
G_x(\nu)\,G_y(\Omega-\nu)
\\
= &
\frac{-\ii\sigma_x\,\delta_{\sigma_x\sigma_y}}
{\Omega-(\eps_x+\eps_y)+\ii\sigma_x0^+} \;,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with its indices restricted to 
$(xy)\in\big\{(cd),\,c<d\big\}\cup\big\{(kl),\,k<l\big\}$.
The particle-hole propagator is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:L_eh}
L^{\eh}_0(31;1'3')=G(33')G(11') \;.
\end{equation}
After performing the frequency integrations, we obtain
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:L0eh_omega}
\begin{aligned}
L^{\eh}_{0,xr}(\Omega)
= & 
\int\frac{\dd\nu}{2\pi}\,
G_x(\nu)\,G_r(\nu+\Omega)
\\
= &
\frac{\ii\sigma_r\,\delta_{\sigma_x,-\sigma_r}}
{\Omega-(\eps_r-\eps_x)+\ii\sigma_r0^+} \;,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with its indices restricted to 
$(xr)\in\big\{(ia)\big\}\cup\big\{(ai)\big\}$.

The interacting propagators follow from the own-channel BSEs,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:BSE_oc}. Restricting the $\pp$ pairs to $c<d$, $k<l$
absorbs the $\tfrac12$ of the $\pp$ BSE, and
$\ii\Gamma^{(1)}_{pqrs}=\mel{pq}{}{rs}$ by Eqs.~\eqref{eq:Gamma1_bare}
and \eqref{eq:vbar_orbital}. With the metric
$\eta^{\lambda}={\rm diag}(\bI,-\bI)$, the $\eh$ propagator is
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:TDHF}
L^{\eh}(\Omega)
= 
\ii\big(\Omega\,\eta^{\eh}-\mathbf H^{\eh}\big)^{-1} \;,
\end{equation}
with 
\begin{equation}
\mathbf H^{\eh}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
A^{\eh} & B^{\eh}\\ B^{\eh} & A^{\eh}
\end{pmatrix} \;,
\end{equation}
and the matrix elements 
\begin{align}
A^{\eh}_{ia,jb}
= & 
(\eps_a-\eps_i)\,\delta_{ab}\delta_{ij}+\mel{aj}{}{ib},
\nonumber\\
B^{\eh}_{ia,jb}
= &
\mel{ab}{}{ij} \;.
\end{align}
The eigenvalues of $\eta^{\eh}\mathbf H^{\eh}$ come in pairs
$\pm\Omega_n$, with eigenvectors $Z_n=(X_n,Y_n)$ and
$\tilde Z_n=(Y_n,X_n)$.
The spectral representation 
of the interacting first-order propagator is therefore
\begin{equation}
L^{\eh}(\Omega)
=
\ii\sum_{\Omega_n>0}
\bigg[
\frac{Z_nZ_n^\dagger}{\Omega-\Omega_n+\ii0^+}
-
\frac{\tilde Z_n\tilde Z_n^\dagger}{\Omega+\Omega_n-\ii0^+}
\bigg].
\label{eq:L_eh_phonons}
\end{equation}
In the $\pp$ channel,
\begin{equation}
L^{\pp}(\Omega)
=  
-\ii\big(\Omega\,\eta^{\pp}-\mathbf H^{\pp}\big)^{-1} \;,
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
\mathbf H^{\pp}
= 
\begin{pmatrix}
A^{\pp} & B^{\pp}\\ B^{\pp\dagger} & D^{\pp}
\end{pmatrix}
\end{equation}
and 
\begin{align}
A^{\pp}_{cd,ef}
= &
(\eps_c+\eps_d)\,\delta_{ce}\delta_{df}+\mel{cd}{}{ef},
\nonumber\\
B^{\pp}_{cd,kl}
= &
\mel{cd}{}{kl},
\nonumber\\
D^{\pp}_{kl,mn}
= &
-(\eps_k+\eps_l)\,\delta_{km}\delta_{ln}+\mel{kl}{}{mn} \;.
\label{eq:ppRPA}
\end{align}
Unlike in the $\eh$-case, the spectrum of 
$\eta^{\pp}\mathbf H^{\pp}$ is not symmetric. With the eigenvectors
$Z_n=(X_n,Y_n)$, the positive-norm eigenvectors are the
$N+2$ states ($\Omega^+_n$), the negative-norm ones the $N-2$ states
($\Omega^-_m$). The interacting particle-particle propagator is 
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:L_pp_phonons}
L^{\pp}(\Omega)
= 
-\ii\sum_{n\in N+2}
\frac{Z_nZ_n^\dagger}{\Omega-\Omega^{+}_n+\ii0^+}
+
\ii\sum_{m\in N-2}
\frac{Z_mZ_m^\dagger}{\Omega-\Omega^{-}_m-\ii0^+} \;.
\end{equation}

Let us now turn to the three-particle propagators.
First, we note that the terms 
of Eq.~\eqref{eq:Faddeev_series_oc} with one interacting pair
can be written as
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:one_pair_split}
\mathcal G+\sum_\alpha\cK_\alpha[F_\alpha]\,\mathcal G
=
\sum_\alpha\Big(\mathcal G+\cK_\alpha[F_\alpha]\,\mathcal G\Big)
-2\,\mathcal G \;.
\end{equation}
This means that we can focus on a single pair at a time.
For the pair $(12)$,
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:D_pph}, \eqref{eq:pair_ops} and \eqref{eq:L_pp} 
give
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal G+\cK_{12}\big[F^{\pp}_{\rm oc}\big]\mathcal G
={}&
G(33')\Big[L^{\pp}_0
-\tfrac14\,L^{\pp}_0F^{\pp}_{\rm oc}L^{\pp}_0\Big](12;1'2')
\\
={}&
G(33')\,L^{\pp}(12;1'2') \;,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:one_pair_pp}
\end{equation}
Working out the frequency integral with
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:GHF_omega} and \eqref{eq:L_pp_phonons}, we obtain
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
Q^{\pp}_{xyr,uwr}(\omega)
\equiv{}&
\int\frac{\dd\Omega}{2\pi}\,
L^{\pp}_{xy,uw}(\Omega)\,G_r(\Omega-\omega)
\\
={}&
n_r\sum_{n\in N+2}
\frac{Z^n_{xy}Z^{n}_{uw}}{\omega+\eps_r-\Omega^{+}_n+\ii0^+}
\\
&+
\bar n_r\sum_{m\in N-2}
\frac{Z^m_{xy}Z^{m}_{uw}}{\omega+\eps_r-\Omega^{-}_m-\ii0^+} \;.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:Q12_full}
\end{equation}
For a hole spectator, $n_r=1$, the pole of $G_r$ lies in the upper
half-plane together with the $N-2$ poles of $L^{\pp}$, and only the
$N+2$ solutions survive. For a particle spectator, only the $N-2$
solutions survive.
For the pair $(31)$, Eqs.~\eqref{eq:D_pph}, \eqref{eq:pair_ops} and \eqref{eq:L_eh} give
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal G+\cK_{31}\big[F^{\eh}_{\rm oc}\big]\mathcal G
={}&
G(22')\Big[L^{\eh}_0
+L^{\eh}_0F^{\eh}_{\rm oc}L^{\eh}_0\Big](31;1'3')
\\
&-
G(21')\Big[L^{\eh}_0
+L^{\eh}_0F^{\eh}_{\rm oc}L^{\eh}_0\Big](31;2'3')
\\
={}&
G(22')\,L^{\eh}(31;1'3')
-
G(21')\,L^{\eh}(31;2'3') \;,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:one_pair_eh}
\end{equation}
Unlike the pair
$(12)$, the pair $(31)$ is not antisymmetrized in $\mathcal G$, so that
the exchange of the lines 1 and 2 appears as the second term. The pair
$(32)$ follows with $1\leftrightarrow2$.
Eq.~\eqref{eq:one_pair_eh} defines the three-particle $\eh$ propagator.
Working out the frequency integral with Eqs.~\eqref{eq:GHF_omega} and
\eqref{eq:L_eh_phonons},
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
Q^{\eh}_{xrs,yus}(\omega)
\equiv{}&
\int\frac{\dd\Omega}{2\pi}\,
L^{\eh}_{xr,yu}(\Omega)\,G_s(\omega-\Omega)
\\
={}&
\bar n_s\sum_{\Omega_n>0}
\frac{Z^n_{xr}Z^{n}_{yu}}{\omega-\eps_s-\Omega_n+\ii0^+}
\\
&+
n_s\sum_{\Omega_n>0}
\frac{\tilde Z^n_{xr}\tilde Z^{n}_{yu}}{\omega-\eps_s+\Omega_n-\ii0^+} \;.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:Q31_full}
\end{equation}
For a particle spectator, only the forward phonons $Z_n$ survive; for a
hole spectator, only the backward ones, $\tilde Z_n$.

\subsubsection{Forward and backward blocks}
With the shorthand
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:denom_triple}
\Delta_{xyz}
=
\eps_x+\eps_y-\eps_z \;,
\end{equation}
the zeroth-order matrices and first-order couplings in the restricted
$\pph$ and $\hhp$ spaces ($a<b$, $i<j$) are
\begin{align}
K^{\rm II}_{abi,cdj}
={}&
\Delta_{abi}\,\delta_{ac}\delta_{bd}\delta_{ij},
\qquad
K^{\rm I}_{ija,klb}
=
-\Delta_{ija}\,\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl}\delta_{ab},
\nonumber\\
U^{{\rm II},(1)}_{p;abi}
={}&
\mel{pi}{}{ab},
\qquad
U^{{\rm I},(1)}_{p;ija}
=
\mel{pa}{}{ij}.
\label{eq:K_U1}
\end{align}
We treat both blocks at once, in the space $\pph\oplus\hhp$ of the
equal-time three-line states, with
\begin{equation}
K=K^{\rm II}\oplus\big(-K^{\rm I}\big) \;,
\qquad
U^{(1)}=\big(U^{{\rm II},(1)},\,U^{{\rm I},(1)}\big) \;.
\label{eq:K_combined}
\end{equation}
With $L^{\pp}_0$ in place of $L^{\pp}$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:Q12_full} gives
the free propagation between two equal-time three-line states,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal G(\omega)=\big(\omega-K\big)^{-1} \;,
\label{eq:G_free}
\end{equation}
with $\omega+\ii0^+$ in the $\pph$ and $\omega-\ii0^+$ in the $\hhp$ block. Since the particle number of a
three-line state is fixed by the order of $t_1$ and $t_{1'}$, all
propagators below are block-diagonal in $\pph\oplus\hhp$, and the two
blocks are connected only through the one-particle space.

In the $\pph$ block (block II), the spectator of the pair $(12)$ is the
hole $i$, and the spectators of the pairs $(31)$ and $(32)$ are the
particles $b$ and $a$. In the $\hhp$ block (block I), the spectator of
the pair $(12)$ is the particle $a$, and the spectators of the pairs
$(31)$ and $(32)$ are the holes $j$ and $i$. The occupation factors of
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:Q12_full} and \eqref{eq:Q31_full} select the first terms
in block II and the second terms in block I. Their forward blocks,
between the configurations $(cd;i)$, $(ia;b)$ in block II and $(kl;a)$,
$(ai;j)$ in block I, are the propagators of one interacting pair between
two equal-time three-line states,
\begin{equation}
Q_{12}(\omega)
=
X^{\pp}\big(\omega+\eps_i-\boldsymbol\Omega^{+}\big)^{-1}X^{\pp\dagger}
\;\oplus\;
Y^{\pp}\big(\omega+\eps_a-\boldsymbol\Omega^{-}\big)^{-1}Y^{\pp\dagger} \;,
\label{eq:Q12}
\end{equation}
with the $N+2$ solutions in the first and the $N-2$ solutions in the
second term, and
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
Q_{31}(\omega)
={}&
X^{\eh}\big(\omega-\eps_b-\boldsymbol\Omega\big)^{-1}X^{\eh\dagger}
\;\oplus\;
X^{\eh}\big(\omega-\eps_j+\boldsymbol\Omega\big)^{-1}X^{\eh\dagger} \;,
\\
Q_{32}(\omega)
={}&
X^{\eh}\big(\omega-\eps_a-\boldsymbol\Omega\big)^{-1}X^{\eh\dagger}
\;\oplus\;
X^{\eh}\big(\omega-\eps_i+\boldsymbol\Omega\big)^{-1}X^{\eh\dagger} \;,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:Q31}
\end{equation}
so that
\begin{equation}
\cK_\alpha[F_\alpha]\mathcal G\;\to\;Q_\alpha(\omega)-\mathcal G(\omega) \;.
\label{eq:KF_to_Q}
\end{equation}
In block I, the forward component of the $N-2$ solutions is $Y^{\pp}$,
and that of the backward phonons $\tilde Z_n=(Y_n,X_n)$ on $(ai)$ is
$X^{\eh}$. The backward blocks connect the forward configurations to the
backward configurations of the pair, $(kl;i)$, $(ai;b)$ in block II and
$(cd;a)$, $(ia;j)$ in block I. They enter only at the external
vertices, Sec.~\ref{sec:ext_vertices}.

The pair propagators act on labelled lines. In block II, $a$ is on
line 1, $b$ on line 2 and $i$ on line 3, with $a$ and $b$ unrestricted,
so that the configurations $(ia;b)$ of $Q_{31}$ and $(ib;a)$ of
$Q_{32}$ are distinct. In this labelled space, $\mathcal G$ and
$\sum_\alpha Q_\alpha$ are symmetric under $\hat P_{ab}$, but a single
pair is not,
\begin{equation}
\hat P_{ab}\,Q_{31}\,\hat P_{ab}
=
Q_{32} \;.
\nonumber
\end{equation}
The restricted space $a<b$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:K_U1} is the antisymmetric
part of the labelled space. For $\hat P_{ab}$-symmetric $R$ and a
coupling $\mathcal U$ antisymmetric in $a$, $b$, the projection onto
$a<b$ and Eq.~\eqref{eq:pair_identities} give
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&\sum_{a<b,i}\sum_{c<d,j}
\mathcal U_{p;abi}\,R^{a<b}_{abi,cdj}\,\mathcal U^{*}_{q;cdj}
\\
&\qquad=
\frac12\sum_{abi}\sum_{cdj}
\mathcal U_{p;abi}\,R_{abi,cdj}\,\mathcal U^{*}_{q;cdj} \;,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:labelled_space}
\end{equation}
with $R^{a<b}$ the matrix of $R$ between normalized antisymmetric
states, and block I in the same way. For approximations that keep only
some of the pairs, $R$ and $\mathcal U$ are no longer symmetric, and we
define the self-energy by the right-hand side of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:labelled_space}.

\subsubsection{Amplitude Form of Propagators}
In each channel, the RPA eigenvalue problem
can equivalently be expressed as
the Riccati equation (see Appendix~\ref{app:Riccati})
\begin{equation}
B_\alpha^\dagger+D_\alpha T_\alpha+T_\alpha A_\alpha
+T_\alpha B_\alpha T_\alpha
=0 \;.
\label{eq:Riccati}
\end{equation}
Here, $A_\alpha$, $B_\alpha$ and $D_\alpha$ are the blocks of the RPA
matrix of the channel of the pair,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\big(A_{12},B_{12},D_{12}\big)
={}&
\big(A^{\pp},B^{\pp},D^{\pp}\big) \;,
\\
\big(A_{3x},B_{3x},D_{3x}\big)
={}&
\big(A^{\eh},B^{\eh},A^{\eh}\big) \;.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:RPA_blocks}
\end{equation}
The Riccati amplitudes map the forward onto the backward components
(See Ref.~\citenum{tolle2026connection}),
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:T_YX}
Y_{\alpha} =
T_{\alpha}X_{\alpha} \;,
\end{equation}
so that the backward blocks are $T_\alpha Q_\alpha(\omega)$.
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Riccati} is
the ring-CCD ($\eh$) and ladder-CCD ($\pp$) amplitude equation of the
channel.\cite{Scuseria2013} Each
channel introduces its own set of 
amplitudes, but they remain decoupled.
Full CCD couples them through the
crossed-ring and mosaic terms of the amplitude
equation.\cite{Shepherd2014} 
These couplings play the role of the crossed channels
that the own-channel approximation neglects.
The same amplitudes serve both blocks. For the $\eh$ pairs, the forward
and backward components in block I are again $X^{\eh}$ and $Y^{\eh}$.
For the $\pp$ pair, the forward component in block I is $Y_-$, and the
$N+2$ and $N-2$ solutions are $\eta$-orthogonal,
\begin{equation}
X_+^\dagger X_-=Y_+^\dagger Y_- \;.
\nonumber
\end{equation}
The block-I amplitudes are therefore
\begin{equation}
T^{\rm I}_{3x}=T^{\eh} \;,
\qquad
T^{\rm I}_{12}=X_-Y_-^{-1}=T^{\pp\dagger} \;.
\label{eq:T_blockI}
\end{equation}
In each block, the forward components are normalized as
\begin{equation}
\big(X_\alpha X_\alpha^\dagger\big)^{-1}=\bI-T_\alpha^\dagger T_\alpha \;,
\label{eq:X_norm}
\end{equation}
and Eqs.~\eqref{eq:Q12} and \eqref{eq:Q31} become
\begin{equation}
Q_\alpha(\omega)
=
\big(\omega-K-C_\alpha-B_\alpha T_\alpha\big)^{-1}
\big(\bI-T_\alpha^\dagger T_\alpha\big)^{-1} \;,
\label{eq:Q_alpha_inverse}
\end{equation}
where $C_\alpha$ is the bare vertex of the pair in the three-line space, with $C_{12}+C_{31}+C_{32}$ given by Eqs.~\eqref{eq:C_II} and \eqref{eq:C_I}, and $B_\alpha$ is its RPA coupling block. As $K$, all block-I quantities
enter with a minus sign,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
C_\alpha
={}&
C^{\rm II}_\alpha\oplus\big(-C^{\rm I}_\alpha\big) \;,
\\
B_\alpha T_\alpha
={}&
B_\alpha^{\rm II}T_\alpha^{\rm II}\oplus
\big(-B_\alpha^{\rm I}T_\alpha^{\rm I}\big) \;,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:CB_blocks}
\end{equation}
with $B^{\rm I}_{12}=B^{\pp\dagger}$ and $B^{\rm I}_{3x}=B^{\eh}$. Block I
of Eq.~\eqref{eq:Q_alpha_inverse} thus has the form of ADC,
\begin{equation}
Q^{\rm I}_\alpha(\omega)
=
\big(\omega+K^{\rm I}+C^{\rm I}_\alpha+B^{\rm I}_\alpha T^{\rm I}_\alpha\big)^{-1}
\big(\bI-T^{{\rm I}\dagger}_\alpha T^{\rm I}_\alpha\big)^{-1} \;.
\nonumber
\end{equation}
In the Tamm--Dancoff limit, $B_\alpha=0$ and $T_\alpha=0$, and
\begin{equation}
Q_\alpha(\omega)
=
\big(\omega-K-C_\alpha\big)^{-1} \;,
\label{eq:Q_TDA}
\end{equation}
where $K+C_\alpha$ is the Tamm--Dancoff Hamiltonian of the pair,
shifted by the spectator energy.

\subsection{Sequences of pairs}
\label{sec:depth1}
In the no-overlap brackets of Eq.~\eqref{eq:Faddeev_series_oc},
consecutive pairs meet at an equal-time three-line state. Its free
propagation, $\mathcal G(\omega)$, is contained in both neighbouring factors and
is divided out once,
\begin{equation}
\Big[\cK_\alpha\cK_\beta \mathcal G\Big]_{\pph}
\;\to\;
\big(Q_\alpha-\mathcal G\big)\mathcal G^{-1}\big(Q_\beta-\mathcal G\big) \;.
\nonumber
\end{equation}
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Faddeev_series_oc} then becomes
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
R_{\rm oc}(\omega)
={}&
\mathcal G+\sum_{\alpha}\big(Q_\alpha-\mathcal G\big)
+\sum_{\alpha\neq\beta}\big(Q_\alpha-\mathcal G\big)\mathcal G^{-1}\big(Q_\beta-\mathcal G\big)
\\
&+\sum_{\substack{\alpha\neq\beta\\\beta\neq\gamma}}
\big(Q_\alpha-\mathcal G\big)\mathcal G^{-1}\big(Q_\beta-\mathcal G\big)
\mathcal G^{-1}\big(Q_\gamma-\mathcal G\big)+\cdots \;.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:R_oc_omega}
\end{equation}
We define $\mathcal C_\alpha(\omega)$ by
\begin{equation}
Q_\alpha^{-1}
=
\mathcal G^{-1}-\mathcal C_\alpha(\omega) \;.
\nonumber
\end{equation}
In the TDA, $\mathcal C_\alpha=C_\alpha$. Then
\begin{equation}
Q_\alpha-\mathcal G
=
\mathcal G\,\mathcal C_\alpha\big(\bI-\mathcal G\,\mathcal C_\alpha\big)^{-1}\mathcal G
\nonumber
\end{equation}
is a run of $\mathcal C_\alpha$ of any length, and the sequences of Eq.~\eqref{eq:R_oc_omega} are all products of $\mathcal C_{12}$, $\mathcal C_{31}$ and $\mathcal C_{32}$,
\begin{equation}
R_{\rm oc}(\omega)
=
\big(\mathcal G^{-1}-\mathcal C_{12}-\mathcal C_{31}-\mathcal C_{32}\big)^{-1} \;,
\nonumber
\end{equation}
or
\begin{equation}
R_{\rm oc}^{-1}
=
Q_{12}^{-1}+Q_{31}^{-1}+Q_{32}^{-1}-2\,\mathcal G^{-1} \;.
\label{eq:Faddeev_inverse}
\end{equation}
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Faddeev_inverse} holds for any $Q_\alpha$, and in both
blocks. With the TDA propagators, Eq.~\eqref{eq:Q_TDA}, it is the
ADC(3) resolvent,
\begin{equation}
R_{\rm oc}^{-1}
=
\omega-K-C
=
\big(\omega-K^{\rm II}-C^{\rm II}\big)\oplus
\big(\omega+K^{\rm I}+C^{\rm I}\big) \;,
\nonumber
\end{equation}
with $C=C_{12}+C_{31}+C_{32}$.

Inserting the amplitude form of $Q_\alpha$,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Q_alpha_inverse}, into Eq.~\eqref{eq:Faddeev_inverse},
the frequency dependence of the phonons reduces to a metric,
\begin{equation}
	\begin{aligned}
R_{\rm oc}^{-1}(\omega)
= &
\omega\, \mathcal N -\mathcal H \;, \\
\mathcal H 
= &
K+C
+\sum_\alpha B_\alpha T_\alpha
 \\
&-\sum_\alpha T_\alpha^\dagger T_\alpha
\big(K+C_\alpha+B_\alpha T_\alpha\big) \;,
\\
\mathcal N
= &
\bI-\sum_\alpha T_\alpha^\dagger T_\alpha \;,
\label{eq:depth1_amplitudes}
\end{aligned} 
\end{equation}
in both blocks. $\mathcal H$ and the metric $\mathcal N$ are Hermitian
and block-diagonal,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal N=\mathcal N^{\rm II}\oplus\mathcal N^{\rm I} \;,
\qquad
\mathcal H=\mathcal H^{\rm II}\oplus\big(-\mathcal H^{\rm I}\big) \;.
\nonumber
\end{equation}

\subsection{External vertices and effective Hamiltonian}
\label{sec:ext_vertices}
For $R_{\rm oc}=\mathcal G$, the external vertices couple to the
three-line states through $U^{(1)}$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:K_combined}. With
interacting pairs, the pair next to $t_1$ (or $t_{1'}$) can also be in a
backward configuration, to which the external vertex couples through
$U^{(1)}_{Y,\alpha}$. By the backward blocks of
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:Q12_full} and \eqref{eq:Q31_full} and Eq.~\eqref{eq:T_YX}, its amplitude
is $T_\alpha$ times the forward one. The external vertices therefore
couple to $R_{\rm oc}$ through $\mathcal U$ instead of $U^{(1)}$.

The resulting effective Hamiltonian, Eq.~\eqref{eq:Heff_depth1}, is
the central result of this derivation.
In contrast to ADC(3), its couplings
contain the channel-restricted CCD amplitudes instead of the
first-order doubles,
\begin{align}
\mathcal U
={}&
\big(\mathcal U^{\rm II},\,\mathcal U^{\rm I}\big)
=
U^{(1)}+\sum_\alpha U^{(1)}_{Y,\alpha}\,T_\alpha \;,
\nonumber\\
\mathcal U^{\rm II}_{p;abi}
={}&
\mel{pi}{}{ab}
+ \hat P^-_{ab}\sum_{cj}\mel{pc}{}{jb}\,T^{\eh}_{jc,ia}
+\sum_{k<l}\mel{pi}{}{kl}\,T^{\pp}_{kl,ab} \;,
\nonumber\\
\mathcal U^{\rm I}_{p;ija}
={}&
\mel{pa}{}{ij}
-\hat P^-_{ij}\sum_{kb}\mel{pk}{}{bj}\,T^{\eh}_{ib,ka}
+\sum_{b<c}\mel{pa}{}{bc}\,T^{\pp}_{ij,bc} \;.
\label{eq:Ueff_depth1}
\end{align}
Here, $U^{(1)}_{Y,\alpha}$ couples to the backward configurations of the
pair $\alpha$ (all but the first terms). To first order, with the first-order doubles of Eq.~\eqref{eq:mp2_double},
\begin{equation}
T^{\eh}_{jc,ia}\to t^{ac}_{ij} \;,
\qquad
T^{\pp}_{kl,ab}\to t^{ab}_{kl} \;,
\nonumber
\end{equation}
and Eq.~\eqref{eq:Ueff_depth1} becomes $U^{(1)}+U^{(2)}$ of
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:U2_II} and \eqref{eq:U2_I}. Projected onto $a<b$ and
$i<j$, and with
\begin{equation}
\omega\mathcal N-\mathcal H
=
\mathcal N^{1/2}\big(\omega-H_{\rm eff}\big)\mathcal N^{1/2}
\nonumber
\end{equation}
for positive definite $\mathcal N$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Sigma^{c}(\omega)
={}&
\mathcal U\,R_{\rm oc}(\omega)\,\mathcal U^{\dagger}
=
\mathcal U\big(\omega\,\mathcal N-\mathcal H\big)^{-1}
\mathcal U^{\dagger}
\\
={}&
\left(\mathcal U\,\mathcal N^{-1/2}\right)
\big(\omega-H_{\rm eff}\big)^{-1}
\left(\mathcal U\,\mathcal N^{-1/2}\right)^\dagger \;,
\\
H_{\rm eff}
={}&
\mathcal N^{-1/2}\mathcal H\,\mathcal N^{-1/2} \;,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:Heff_depth1}
\end{equation}
which, written out block by block, is Eq.~\eqref{eq:Sigma_goal}.
As in ADC, the quasiparticle energies are the eigenvalues of the
Hermitian Dyson supermatrix
\begin{equation}
\begin{pmatrix}
h_0+\Sigma(\infty) & \mathcal U\,\mathcal N^{-1/2}
\\
\mathcal N^{-1/2}\,\mathcal U^\dagger & H_{\rm eff}
\end{pmatrix} ,
\nonumber
\end{equation}
with $h_0$ the one-body Hamiltonian of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Martin_Schwinger_general}.


The CCD-ADC(3) from Ref.~\citenum{forster2026third} 
uses exactly Eq.~\eqref{eq:Ueff_depth1}, but solves the 
full CCD equations to couple all channel amplitudes. However,
the effective Hamiltonian is kept at the TDA level. Their 
EN-ADC(3) is the linearized, diagonal version of the ladder-CCD couplings.
Also Coveney\cite{Coveney2025} uses the full CCD amplitudes,
but his Hamiltonian is constructed in the CC similarity transform structure.
It does not contain any metric and is non-Hermitian due to the similarity transformation. It also contains a line dressing that
ADC(4) and Eq.~\eqref{eq:d1_ADC4} contain 
only at 4th order.

In block II, the matrix elements of Eq.~\eqref{eq:depth1_amplitudes} are
\begin{align}
\mathcal H_{abi,cdj}
={}&
K^{\rm II}_{abi,cdj}+C^{\rm II}_{abi,cdj}
+\delta_{ij}\,h^{\pp}_{ab,cd}
\nonumber\\
&+\hat P^-_{ab}\hat P^-_{cd}\,\delta_{bd}\,h^{\eh}_{ia,jc} \;,
\nonumber\\
\mathcal N_{abi,cdj}
={}&
\delta_{ac}\delta_{bd}\delta_{ij}
-\delta_{ij}\sum_{k<l}T^{\pp}_{kl,ab}T^{\pp}_{kl,cd}
\nonumber\\
&-\hat P^-_{ab}\hat P^-_{cd}\,\delta_{bd}\sum_{ke}T^{\eh}_{ke,ia}T^{\eh}_{ke,jc} \;,
\label{eq:HN_explicit}
\end{align}
with
\begin{align}
h^{\pp}_{ab,cd}
={}&
\sum_{k<l}\Big[\mel{ab}{}{kl}\,T^{\pp}_{kl,cd}+T^{\pp}_{kl,ab}\,\mel{kl}{}{cd}\Big]
\nonumber\\
&+\sum_{k<l}\sum_{m<n}T^{\pp}_{kl,ab}
\big(D^{\pp}_{kl,mn}+\eps_i\,\delta_{km}\delta_{ln}\big)T^{\pp}_{mn,cd} \;,
\nonumber\\
h^{\eh}_{ia,jc}
={}&
\sum_{ke}\Big[\mel{ae}{}{ik}\,T^{\eh}_{ke,jc}+T^{\eh}_{ke,ia}\,\mel{ce}{}{jk}\Big]
\nonumber\\
&+\sum_{ke}\sum_{lf}T^{\eh}_{ke,ia}
\big(A^{\eh}_{ke,lf}-\eps_b\,\delta_{kl}\delta_{ef}\big)T^{\eh}_{lf,jc} \;.
\label{eq:h_pair}
\end{align}

\subsection{Phonon form}
\label{sec:phonon_form}
In the own-channel approximation, the reducible vertices $\Phi^{\lambda}$
follow from the channel propagators of Sec.~\ref{sec:propagators}.
In the $\eh$~channel, the reducible vertex, the resummed counterpart of
$\Phi^{\eh,(2)}$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:Phi2_explicit}, is a sum over $\eh$
phonons,
\begin{align}
\ii\Phi^{\eh}_{pqrs}(\Omega)
={}&
\sum_{\Omega_n>0}
\bigg[
\frac{\rho^n_{pr}\,\rho^n_{qs}}{\Omega-\Omega_n+\ii0^+}
-
\frac{\tilde\rho^n_{pr}\,\tilde\rho^n_{qs}}{\Omega+\Omega_n-\ii0^+}
\bigg],
\nonumber\\
\rho^n_{pr}
={}&
\sum_{ia}\Big[\mel{pa}{}{ri}\,X^n_{ia}+\mel{pi}{}{ra}\,Y^n_{ia}\Big],
\nonumber\\
\tilde\rho^n_{pr}
={}&
\sum_{ia}\Big[\mel{pa}{}{ri}\,Y^n_{ia}+\mel{pi}{}{ra}\,X^n_{ia}\Big] \;,
\label{eq:Phi_eh_phonons}
\end{align}
with $X^n_{ia}$, $Y^n_{ia}$ the components of $Z_n$.
The same first-order vertex appears in the vertex-corrected 
$GW$ self-energies of Ref.~
\citenum{Maggio2017,Vacondio2024,Forster2024}.
In the $\eh$ variables, $\Phi^{\eh}$ depends only on the bosonic
transfer frequency $\Omega$. The crossed channel, $\teh$, follows by crossing,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:crossing}.

With Eq.~\eqref{eq:L_pp_phonons}, the reducible $\pp$-vertex is
\begin{align}
\ii\Phi^{\pp}_{pqrs}(\Omega)
= & 
\sum_{n\in N+2}
\frac{\zeta^n_{pq}\,\zeta^n_{rs}}{\Omega-\Omega^{+}_n+\ii0^+}
-
\sum_{m\in N-2}
\frac{\zeta^m_{pq}\,\zeta^m_{rs}}{\Omega-\Omega^{-}_m-\ii0^+},
\nonumber\\
\zeta^n_{pq}
= & 
\sum_{c<d}\mel{pq}{}{cd}\,X^n_{cd}
+\sum_{k<l}\mel{pq}{}{kl}\,Y^n_{kl} \;.
\label{eq:Phi_pp_phonons}
\end{align}

It is instructive to write the effective 
Hamiltonian in terms of the phonons. 
We do this for block II; block I follows in the same way with the block-I forward components of Eqs.~\eqref{eq:Q12} and \eqref{eq:Q31}. By Eqs.~\eqref{eq:Faddeev_inverse} and \eqref{eq:depth1_amplitudes},
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\omega\mathcal N-\mathcal H
={}&
\sum_\alpha Q_\alpha^{-1}-2\mathcal G^{-1} \;,
\\
Q_\alpha^{-1}
={}&
X_\alpha^{-\dagger}\big(\omega-s_\alpha-\boldsymbol\Omega_\alpha\big)X_\alpha^{-1} \;.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:Rinv_phonons}
\end{equation}
Here, $\boldsymbol\Omega_\alpha$ is the diagonal matrix of the pair
energies, $\boldsymbol\Omega^+$ for the pair $(12)$ and $\boldsymbol\Omega$
for the pairs $(3x)$, and $s_\alpha$ is the spectator energy,
\begin{equation}
s_{12}=-\eps_i \;,
\qquad
s_{31}=\eps_b \;,
\qquad
s_{32}=\eps_a \;.
\label{eq:s_alpha}
\end{equation}
Its $\omega$-linear part gives $\mathcal N$, its constant part $\mathcal H$.
With Eqs.~\eqref{eq:Q31}~and~\eqref{eq:Q12},
as well as the normalization in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Q_alpha_inverse}, the quantities 
appearing in the self-energy defined by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Heff_depth1}
are
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:HNU_phonons}
	\begin{aligned}
\mathcal N
= &
\bI+\sum_\alpha\Big
[\big(X_\alpha X_\alpha^\dagger\big)^{-1}
-\bI\Big] \;, \\
\mathcal H
= &
K^{\rm II}+C^{\rm II}
+\sum_\alpha\Big[X_\alpha^{-\dagger}\big(\boldsymbol\Omega_\alpha+s_\alpha\big)X_\alpha^{-1}
-K^{\rm II}-C_\alpha\Big] \;, \\
\mathcal U
= &
U^{(1)}+\sum_\alpha\Big[M_\alpha X_\alpha^{-1}-U^{(1)}\Big] \;,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with the phonon vertices $M_\alpha=U^{(1)}X_\alpha+U^{(1)}_{Y,\alpha}Y_\alpha$,
\ie\ $M_{31}=-\tilde\rho$ and $M_{12}=\zeta$ of
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:Phi_eh_phonons} and \eqref{eq:Phi_pp_phonons}.


\subsection{Connections to (vertex-corrected) \texorpdfstring{$GW$}{GW}}
The $GW$ approximation and other schemes derived in the framework of
Hedin's equations\cite{Hedin1965} are formulated in the $\eh$-channel only.
It is therefore 
instructive to retain only the $\eh$ pair $(31)$ in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:HNU_phonons}. The quantities entering the 
effective hamiltonian then simplify to  
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:GW_phonon_HNU}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal N^{\rm II}_{iab,jcd}
&=
\big[(X_{31}X_{31}^\dagger)^{-1}\big]_{ia,jc}\,\delta_{bd} \;,
\\
\mathcal H^{\rm II}_{iab,jcd}
&=
\big[X_{31}^{-\dagger}\big(\boldsymbol\Omega_{31}+\eps_b\big)X_{31}^{-1}\big]_{ia,jc}\,\delta_{bd} \;,
\\
\mathcal U^{\rm II}_{p;iab}
&=
\sum_n M^{\rm II}_{31,p;nb}\,\big[X_{31}^{-1}\big]_{n,ia} \;,
\\
\mathcal N^{\rm I}_{iaj,kbl}
&=
\big[(X_{31}X_{31}^\dagger)^{-1}\big]_{ia,kb}\,\delta_{jl} \;,
\\
\mathcal H^{\rm I}_{iaj,kbl}
&=
\big[X_{31}^{-\dagger}\big(\boldsymbol\Omega_{31}-\eps_j\big)X_{31}^{-1}\big]_{ia,kb}\,\delta_{jl} \;,
\\
\mathcal U^{\rm I}_{p;iaj}
&=
\sum_n M^{\rm I}_{31,p;nj}\,\big[X_{31}^{-1}\big]_{n,ia} \;,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where we have specified the indices explicitly for later reference. In
block II, the pair $(ia)$ has the spectator particle $b$, in block I
the spectator hole $j$, and the pair and its spectator are independent.
With Eq.~\eqref{eq:Sigma_goal},
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\omega\,\mathcal N^{\rm II}-\mathcal H^{\rm II}
&=
X_{31}^{-\dagger}\big(\omega-\eps_b-\boldsymbol\Omega_{31}\big)X_{31}^{-1} \;,
\\
\omega\,\mathcal N^{\rm I}+\mathcal H^{\rm I}
&=
X_{31}^{-\dagger}\big(\omega-\eps_j+\boldsymbol\Omega_{31}\big)X_{31}^{-1} \;,
\end{aligned}
\nonumber
\end{equation}
and the self-energy becomes
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:GW_phonon_selfenergy}
\Sigma_{31,pq}(\omega)
=
\sum_{nb}
\frac{M^{\rm II}_{31,p;nb}\,M^{{\rm II}*}_{31,q;nb}}{\omega-\eps_b-\Omega_n}
+\sum_{nj}
\frac{M^{\rm I}_{31,p;nj}\,M^{{\rm I}*}_{31,q;nj}}{\omega-\eps_j+\Omega_n} \;.
\end{equation}
The $\eh$-first-order reducible vertices have been
defined in Eq.~\eqref{eq:Phi_eh_phonons}, with $M_{31}=-\tilde\rho$.
The self-energy Eq.~\eqref{eq:GW_phonon_selfenergy}
is completely equivalent to the PSD completion of
the $\Sigma^{\text{TDHF}}$ self-energy\cite{Forster2024} from
Ref.~\citenum{Pavlyukh2026}, therein denoted as $\Sigma^\text{PSDII}$. The 
only difference is that they construct
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Phi_eh_phonons} with a statically
screened TDHF kernel. This also illustrates the
interpretation of the PSD self-energy of
Ref.~\citenum{Pavlyukh2026} as a ``single-channel parquet''-type
approximation.

\subsubsection{Double counting}
It has been remarked in Ref.~\citenum{Pavlyukh2026} that $\Sigma^\text{PSDII}$ 
double counts the $\Sigma^{(2)}$ contribution.
This raises the natural question how this can be the case, 
since the Faddeev-ADC(3) construction performed here is double counting-free 
by construction. 

To see how the double counting of $\Sigma^{(2)}$ arises, we take
$v\to0$. Then $X_{31}\to\bI$, $Y_{31}\to0$, $\Omega_n\to\eps_a-\eps_i$,
$\mathcal N\to\bI$, $\mathcal H^{\rm II}\to\Delta_{abi}$,
$\mathcal H^{\rm I}\to-\Delta_{ija}$, and the couplings become those of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:K_U1}, $\mathcal U^{\rm II}_{p;iab}\to\mel{pi}{}{ab}$ and
$\mathcal U^{\rm I}_{p;iaj}\to\mel{pa}{}{ij}$.
Eq.~\eqref{eq:GW_phonon_selfenergy} gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Sigma_{31,pq}
\to{}&
\sum_{abi}
\frac{\mel{pi}{}{ab}\mel{ab}{}{qi}}{\omega+\eps_i-\eps_a-\eps_b}
+\sum_{ija}
\frac{\mel{pa}{}{ij}\mel{ij}{}{qa}}{\omega+\eps_a-\eps_i-\eps_j}
\\
={}&
2\,\Sigma^{>}_{pq}+2\,\Sigma^{<}_{pq}
=
2\,\Sigma^{(2)}_{pq} \;,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:Sigma31_GF2}
\end{equation}
with $\Sigma^>$ and $\Sigma^<$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:Sigma2_gl}. Since the
pair and its spectator are independent, every $\pph$ configuration
appears twice, as the pair $(ia)$ with spectator $b$ and as the pair
$(ib)$ with spectator $a$, \ie\ once in each $\eh$ pair, $(31)$ and
$(32)$, and each with its own free propagation. The same holds for the
$\hhp$ configurations, with the pairs $(ia)$ and $(ja)$ and the
spectators $j$ and $i$.
The three ways of combining the
$\eh$ pairs differ in how often they count $\mathcal G$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
Q_{31}
={}&
\mathcal G+\mathcal G\,C_{31}\,\mathcal G+\cdots \;,
\\
Q_{31}+Q_{32}
={}&
2\,\mathcal G+\mathcal G\big(C_{31}+C_{32}\big)\mathcal G+\cdots \;,
\\
\mathcal G+\sum_{\alpha=31,32}\big(Q_\alpha-\mathcal G\big)
={}&
\mathcal G+\mathcal G\big(C_{31}+C_{32}\big)\mathcal G+\cdots \;,
\end{aligned}
\nonumber
\end{equation}
whereas the $\eh$ part of ADC(3) is
$\mathcal G+\mathcal G(C_{31}+C_{32})\mathcal G$. The single pair,
$\frac12\Sigma_{31}$ by Eq.~\eqref{eq:labelled_space}, counts $\mathcal G$ once, but has only half of
the $\eh$ interaction at third order. $\Sigma_{31}$ has all of it, but
counts $\mathcal G$, and thus $\Sigma^{(2)}$, twice. The Faddeev
series, Eq.~\eqref{eq:R_oc_omega}, truncated after the single-pair
runs, counts both correctly,
\begin{equation}
\Sigma^{c}
=
\Sigma_{31}-\Sigma^{(2)} \;.
\label{eq:eh_dc}
\end{equation}
In Eq.~\eqref{eq:Faddeev_inverse}, the term $-2\,\mathcal G^{-1}$ does
the same.
This subtraction is possible for PSDII as well, but it would defeat the 
purpose of the PSD-construction, as the resulting self-energy 
is then noa sum of squares anymore and hence not PSD.

\subsubsection{$GW$ and other Vertex Corrections from Hedin's Equations}

Constructing Eq.~\eqref{eq:Phi_eh_phonons} with the standard (direct
electron-hole) RPA, and keeping only the direct vertex, gives the $GW$
approximation. The vertex-corrected $GW\Gamma$ self-energy with TDHF screening and
the first-order vertex\cite{Maggio2017,Forster2024} replaces one of
the two vertices of Eq.~\eqref{eq:GW_phonon_selfenergy} by its direct
part $M^{\rm D}_{31}$, the $GW$ vertex,
\begin{equation}
\Sigma_{GW\Gamma}(\omega)
=
M^{\rm D}_{31}
\big[
\omega-s_{31}-\boldsymbol\Omega_{31}
\big]^{-1}
M_{31}^{\dagger} \;.
\label{eq:GWGamma}
\end{equation}
With $U^{\rm D}$ the direct part of $U^{(1)}$ and
$U^{(1)}\hat P_{ab}=-U^{(1)}$, the direct vertex acts as the factor
$\frac12$ on $\mathcal G$, which is symmetric in $a\leftrightarrow b$,
but not on $C_{31}$, which $\hat P_{ab}$ maps onto $C_{32}$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
U^{\rm D}\mathcal G\,U^{(1)\dagger}
={}&
\tfrac12\,U^{(1)}\mathcal G\,U^{(1)\dagger}
=
\Sigma^{(2)} \;,
\\
U^{(1)}\mathcal G\,C_{31}\,\mathcal G\,U^{(1)\dagger}
={}&
U^{\rm D}\mathcal G\big(C_{31}+C_{32}\big)\mathcal G\,U^{(1)\dagger} \;.
\end{aligned}
\nonumber
\end{equation}
$\Sigma_{GW\Gamma}$ is therefore double
counting free. Furthermore, the triplet phonons drop out,
since the direct vertex
couples only to singlet densities.

At third order, the PSDII self-energy
contains $C_{31}+C_{32}$, i.e. the full $\eh$ part of the self-energy 
at third order in perturbation theory.
$\Sigma_{GW\Gamma}$ however only contains half of it. It only contains
$U^{\rm D}\mathcal G\,C_{31}\,\mathcal G\,U^{(1)\dagger}$,
while 
$U^{\rm D}\mathcal G\,C_{32}\,\mathcal G\,U^{(1)\dagger}$ is missing. 
These are the third-order terms that the second-order vertex would
add to $GW\Gamma$. In addition to this part, the second-order vertex also 
adds the missing $\pp$ channel to the self-energy. It is a feature of hedin's equations, 
that it never adds diagrammatically complete sets of diagrams at an
iteration order.

\subsubsection{The FLEX Approximation}
Finally, the truncation \cite{Bickers1989,Bickers2004}
\begin{equation}
R_{\rm FLEX}
= 
\mathcal G+\sum_\alpha\big(Q_\alpha-\mathcal G\big) \;,
\end{equation}
of the Faddeev series becomes the FLEX approximation,
\begin{equation}
\Sigma_{\rm FLEX}
=
\tfrac12\,\Sigma_{12}+\Sigma_{31}-2\,\Sigma^{(2)} \;.
\label{eq:FLEX}
\end{equation}
Like
Eq.~\eqref{eq:eh_dc}, FLEX counts $\mathcal G$ once.
$\frac12\Sigma_{12}$
and $\Sigma_{31}$ contain $\Sigma^{(2)}$ once and twice, and
$-2\,\Sigma^{(2)}$ removes the excess.
FLEX and 
Faddeev-ADC(3) are both 
exact through third order. FLEX misses
the sequences of different pairs in Eq.~\eqref{eq:R_oc_omega}. Since
the subtraction is outside the resolvent, $\Sigma_{\rm FLEX}$ has
negative residues and no Hermitian effective Hamiltonian.

\subsection{Fourth-order completion}
\label{sec:overlap}
We now complete the own-channel and no-overlap approximation to fourth order. We
start from the known ADC(4) effective Hamiltonian.
In addition to extra interactions within the $\pph$ ($\hhp$) space and
their couplings to the one-particle space, ADC(4) adds the
$\ppphh$ ($\hhhpp$) space.
How can these states be created when we stay in a three-particle
picture? A $\ppphh$ state is an intermediate state with
five lines, the $\pph$ state $(ab;i)$ and one additional
particle--hole pair $(c;j)$. This pair is emitted from one line of the
$\pph$ state at a bare interaction and absorbed later. A first-order rung of
the own channel cannot do this, as it is instantaneous and connects two
lines of the $\pph$ state, so that three lines enter and leave it. 

The first mechanism is line dressing. A line emits a particle--hole
pair and absorbs it again, $a\to c'+(c;j)\to a$. In between, five
lines propagate. This is a $\Sigma^{(2)}$ insertion into a line of
$\mathcal G$.

The second mechanism are the crossed channels. The pair is emitted by
one line of a pair and absorbed by the other line of the same pair.
The two bare interactions, joined by the lines $c$ and $j$, form a
second-order vertex of this pair in a channel other than its own:
$\Phi^{\eh,(2)}$ and $\Phi^{\teh,(2)}$ in the pair $(12)$, whose own
channel is the $\pp$ ladder, and $\Phi^{\pp,(2)}$ and
$\Phi^{\teh,(2)}$ in the pairs $(3x)$. These are the crossed-channel
parts of $F^{(2)}$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:ADC_Faddeev}, \ie\ the channel
crossing inside the second-order pair vertex, which the own-channel
approximation neglects.

The last mechanism are the overlaps. The pair is emitted at the rung
of one pair and absorbed at the rung of a different pair, both at first
order. For
instance, in $\cK_{31}[\Gamma^{(1)}]\cK_{12}[\Gamma^{(1)}]\mathcal G$,
the rung of the pair $(31)$ can occur before that of the pair
$(12)$. Line 1, shared by both pairs, then runs backward in time
between the two rungs. The rung of $(31)$ then creates an
additional particle and a hole,
and the rung of $(12)$ absorbs this hole together with the
original particle. In between, five lines propagate. These are the
overlapping orderings of the last term of Eq.~\eqref{eq:ADC_Faddeev},
which the no-overlap approximation excludes.

\subsubsection{ADC(4)}
Block II of ADC(4)\cite{Schirmer1983} extends the $\pph$ space of
ADC(3) by the $\ppphh$ space,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf H^{\rm ADC(4), \pph}
={}&
\begin{pmatrix}
K^{\rm II}+C^{\rm II}+C^{{\rm II},(2)} & E_3^{\dagger}
\\
E_3 & K_3
\end{pmatrix}  \;,
\\
\mathbf U^{\rm ADC(4)}
={}&
\big(U^{(1)}+U^{(2)}+U^{{\rm II},(3)},\;U_3\big) \;.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:ADC4_II}
\end{equation}
In the $\ppphh$ space, $a<b<c$, $i<j$,
\begin{align}
K_{3;abcij}
={}&
\eps_a+\eps_b+\eps_c-\eps_i-\eps_j \;, \nonumber \\
E_{3;abcij,dek}
={}&
\hat P^-_{de}\Big[
\hat P^-_{ij}\hat P_{c/ab}\mel{di}{}{ab}\,\delta_{ec}\delta_{kj}
\nonumber\\
&\hphantom{\hat P^-_{de}\Big[}
-\hat P_{c/ab}\mel{kc}{}{ij}\,\delta_{da}\delta_{eb}
\Big] \;,
\label{eq:ADC4_3p2h}
\end{align}
with $\hat P_{x/yz}f(x,y,z)=f(x,y,z)-f(y,x,z)-f(z,y,x)$,
and couplings 
\begin{equation}
U_{3;p;abcij}
=
\hat P_{c/ab}\sum_{d}t_{ij}^{dc}\mel{pd}{}{ab}
+\hat P_{a/bc}\hat P^-_{ij}\sum_{k}t_{kj}^{bc}\mel{pi}{}{ka} \;.
\end{equation}
The
second-order $\pph$ block is
$C^{{\rm II},(2)}=\tfrac12(L+L^\dagger)$,
\begin{align}
L_{abi,cdj}
={}&
\frac12\delta_{ij}\sum_{kl}t_{kl}^{ab}\mel{kl}{}{cd}
+\hat P^-_{ab}\hat P^-_{cd}\,\delta_{bd}
\sum_{ke}t_{ik}^{ae}\mel{ec}{}{kj}
\nonumber\\
&+
\frac12\big(\delta_{ac}\delta_{bd}-\delta_{ad}\delta_{bc}\big)
\sum_{kef}t_{ik}^{ef}\mel{ef}{}{kj}
\nonumber\\
&-
\frac12\hat P^-_{ab}\hat P^-_{cd}\,\delta_{ij}\delta_{bd}
\sum_{kle}t_{kl}^{ae}\mel{kl}{}{ce} \;,
\label{eq:L_II}
\end{align}
and $U^{{\rm II},(3)}$ contains the second-order singles, doubles and
triples, products of two first-order doubles, the second-order
density matrix, and $\Sigma^{(2)}$ insertions in the lines.\cite{Schirmer1983}

\subsubsection{Own-channel and no-overlap part of ADC(4)}
The first line of Eq.~\eqref{eq:L_II} contains the leading ground-state
correlations of the phonons of Sec.~\ref{sec:propagators}. With the first-
and second-order solutions of Eq.~\eqref{eq:Riccati},
\begin{align}
D^{(0)}_\alpha T^{(1)}_\alpha+T^{(1)}_\alpha A^{(0)}_\alpha
={}&
-B_\alpha^\dagger,
\nonumber\\
D^{(0)}_\alpha T^{(2)}_\alpha+T^{(2)}_\alpha A^{(0)}_\alpha
={}&
-D^{(1)}_\alpha T^{(1)}_\alpha-T^{(1)}_\alpha A^{(1)}_\alpha,
\label{eq:Riccati_orders}
\end{align}
where $A^{(0)}$, $D^{(0)}$ are the pair energies and $A^{(1)}$,
$D^{(1)}$ the first-order pair interactions, the two terms of the first line of Eq.~\eqref{eq:L_II} are
$B_\alpha T^{(1)}_\alpha$ of the $\pp$ pair and of the $\eh$ pairs,
$T^{(1)}_{12}=t$ and $T^{(1)}_{3x}=t$. The second and third lines of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:L_II} dress the hole line and a particle line with
the second-order self-energy. Hence
for the $C$-block
\begin{equation}
C^{{\rm II},(2)}
=
C^{(2)}_{\rm oc}+\Delta C \;,
\label{eq:C2_split}
\end{equation}
and coupling block,
\begin{equation}
U^{{\rm II},(3)}
=
U^{(3)}_{\rm oc}+\Delta U \;,
\label{eq:U3_split}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
C^{(2)}_{\rm oc}
=
\frac12\sum_\alpha\big(B_\alpha T^{(1)}_\alpha+T^{(1)\dagger}_\alpha B^\dagger_\alpha\big) \;,
\label{eq:d1_second}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
U^{(3)}_{\rm oc}
=
\sum_\alpha U^{(1)}_{Y,\alpha}\,T^{(2)}_\alpha
+\tfrac12\,U^{(1)}S \;,
\label{eq:U3_oc}
\end{equation}
and, for convenience, the overlap
\begin{equation}
S
=
\sum_\alpha T^{(1)\dagger}_\alpha T^{(1)}_\alpha \;,
\label{eq:S_def}
\end{equation}
with $\Delta C$ the symmetrized second and third lines of Eq.~\eqref{eq:L_II}, \ie\
the line dressing. $T^{(2)}_{12}$ contains only the $\pp$ and $\hh$
ladder terms of the second-order doubles. $T^{(2)}_{3x}$ contains
only the ring terms of the pair $(3x)$. $\Delta U$ contains the rest of
$U^{{\rm II},(3)}$, in particular the singles, the triples, the
crossed-channel parts of the second-order doubles, the density-matrix
terms and the line dressing of the couplings.

\subsubsection{Resummation}
The own-channel and no-overlap parts, Eqs.~\eqref{eq:d1_second} and \eqref{eq:U3_oc}, are the leading terms
of the resummed quantities of Sec.~\ref{sec:depth1}. With
$\mathcal N^{-1/2}=\bI+\tfrac12S+O(v^3)$ and Eq.~\eqref{eq:Riccati_orders},
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:depth1_amplitudes}, \eqref{eq:Ueff_depth1} and
\eqref{eq:Heff_depth1} give
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
H_{\rm eff}
={}&
K^{\rm II}+C^{\rm II}+C^{(2)}_{\rm oc}+O(v^3) \;,
\\
\mathcal U\,\mathcal N^{-1/2}
={}&
U^{(1)}+U^{(2)}+U^{(3)}_{\rm oc}+O(v^4) \;.
\end{aligned}
\nonumber
\end{equation}
We therefore replace these truncated sums by their resummed forms and
keep $\Delta C$, $\Delta U$ and the $\ppphh$ space at their
ADC(4) order,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf H_{\rm oc}
={}&
\begin{pmatrix}
H_{\rm eff}+\Delta C & E_3^{\dagger}
\\
E_3 & K_3
\end{pmatrix} ,
\\
\mathbf U_{\rm oc}
={}&
\big(\mathcal U\,\mathcal N^{-1/2}+\Delta U,\;U_3\big) \;.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:d1_ADC4}
\end{equation}
The differences to Eq.~\eqref{eq:ADC4_II} are of third order in the
matrix and of fourth order in the couplings. They enter the self-energy
at fifth order, so that Eq.~\eqref{eq:d1_ADC4} is exact through fourth
order and keeps the own-channel and no-overlap resummation of the phonons to all
orders. Block I follows in the same way, and the static self-energy is
needed through fourth order, as in ADC(4).

$\Delta C$, $\Delta U$ and the $\ppphh$ space collect the
line dressing, the crossed-channel vertices inside a pair and the
orderings in which the ladders of different pairs overlap in time. The
latter open $\ppphh$ subintervals, but only their sum with
the crossed channels and the line dressing has the Hamiltonian form
$E_3^\dagger(\omega-K_3)^{-1}E_3$. A resummation of these terms would
therefore have to be carried out in the $\ppphh$
configuration space.

\section{\label{sec:conclusions}Conclusions}
Through the response function $R$, Eqs.~\eqref{eq:Sigma_3} and
\eqref{eq:K_pairs}, the self-energy is expressed in
terms of the three-particle kernel. In the
Faddeev form, Eq.~\eqref{eq:Faddeev_F}, the only two-body input is the
parquet vertex $F$. ADC($n$) is the
Schirmer matching of the Faddeev series at total order $n-2$ in $v$.
The orders through which the various
approximations are exact are collected in Table~\ref{tab:orders}.

Mapping the Faddeev form of parquet onto an effective Hamiltonian of
the $\pph$ ($\hhp$) space involves a few approximations. First, the
parquet approximation, $\Lambda=\Lambda^{(1)}$, is combined with a
static reference, $G=G_{\rm HF}$ in all lines of the vertex. Every building block of
the vertex is then an instantaneous interaction $\bar v$ or a
$G_{\rm HF}$ line, and the frequency integrals of the pair propagators
turn into resolvents of configuration spaces (Sec.~\ref{sec:propagators}).
Next, an own-channel approximation is invoked that consists in
letting each pair scatter only through the ladder of its own
channel with the bare kernel. Moreover, 
the ladders of different pairs do not
overlap in time (Sec.~\ref{sec:depth1}). 

In the own-channel and no-overlap approximation, Hermitian effective Hamiltonians
(for a positive-definite metric) are obtained that go beyond ADC by going beyond the TDA. 
At the ADC(3) level, this amounts to an effective Hamiltonian that
replaces the first-order double amplitudes in the couplings with the 
ring-CCD (for the $\eh$ channel) and the ladder-CCD amplitudes
(for the $\pp$ channel), and adds the metric $\mathcal N$ and the terms $B_\alpha T_\alpha$ to the matrix. Channel crossings
that are not already part of the Faddeev kernel itself,
as in the full CCD amplitude equations, are not considered. 
At first order, the Faddeev kernel does not contain any 
channel crossings.

Starting from the second-order Faddeev kernel, crossed channels 
already enter before any resummation, by Eq.~\eqref{eq:Phi2_explicit}
and Eq.~\eqref{eq:ADC_Faddeev}. These crossed ladders, together with the overlapping orderings of two first-order kernels and the line dressing, lead to
the presence of $\ppphh$ configurations. Obtaining 
an effective Hamiltonian requires to include this configuration space,
in the same way as in the ADC(4) approximation.

An advantage of our Faddeev construction is that 
it preserves the resolvent structure of the 
self-energy through a Hermitian effective Hamiltonian 
formalism, in the same way as in ADC. Unlike ADC, it also 
introduces a significantly larger amount of 
ground-state correlation through an 
infinite-order amplitude renormalization.

The derivations shown here allow us to make a 
precise connection to a recent PSD completion of 
a vertex-corrected self-energy,\cite{Pavlyukh2026}
suggesting its interpretation as a single-channel 
parquet-type approximation. It remains to be established 
whether similar connections can be drawn for 
other PSD-completion schemes.\cite{Bruneval2025,Marie2026}

\section{Riccati equations and decoupling conditions}
\label{app:Riccati}
Consider a space $\mathcal{P}\oplus\mathcal{Q}$, where $\mathcal{P}$ is
some active space. Let $x\in\mathcal{P}\oplus\mathcal{Q}$ be an
eigenvector of $H$ and write 
\begin{equation}
	x = 
	\begin{pmatrix}
		X \\
		Y 
	\end{pmatrix} = 
	\begin{pmatrix}
		X \\
		TX 
	\end{pmatrix}	
	\end{equation} 
which implies $Y = TX$. We then define the graph of $T$ as
\begin{equation}
	\mathcal{P}_T = \text{graph}(T) = 
	\left\{
	\begin{pmatrix}
		X \\
		TX 
	\end{pmatrix}	\;, X \in \mathcal{P}
	\right\}
\end{equation}
and require it to be invariant under the action of the partitioned Hamiltonian 
\begin{equation}
	\begin{pmatrix}
		H_{PP} & H_{PQ} \\ H_{QP} & H_{QQ}
	\end{pmatrix}
	\begin{pmatrix}
		X \\
		Y 
	\end{pmatrix}		= 
	\begin{pmatrix}
		H_{PP} & H_{PQ} \\ H_{QP} & H_{QQ}
	\end{pmatrix}
	\begin{pmatrix}
		X \\
		TX 
	\end{pmatrix} =
	\begin{pmatrix}
		\left(H_{PP} + H_{PQ}T\right)X \\
		\left(H_{QP} + H_{QQ}T \right)X
	\end{pmatrix}	\;.	
\end{equation}
Invariance under the action of $H$ then gives the condition 
\begin{equation}
	\left(H_{QP} + H_{QQ}T\right)X = T\left(H_{PP} + H_{PQ}T \right)X \;,
\end{equation}
and as this must be valid for all $X \in \mathcal{P}$, this yields the Riccati 
equation 
\begin{equation}
	H_{QP} + H_{QQ}T = TH_{PP} + T H_{PQ}T \;.
\end{equation}
The rewriting of the $\pp$ and $\eh$-RPA equations, Eqs.~\eqref{eq:ppRPA} and \eqref{eq:TDHF}, as the Riccati equations, Eq.~\eqref{eq:Riccati}, is 
easily seen to be a special case of this general transformation. 
Equivalently, with
\begin{equation}
	\Theta =
		\begin{pmatrix}
		\bI & 0 \\ T & \bI
	\end{pmatrix} \;,
	\qquad
	\Theta^{-1} =
	\begin{pmatrix}
		\bI & 0 \\ -T & \bI
	\end{pmatrix} \;,
\end{equation}
whose first block column spans $\mathcal{P}_T$,
\begin{equation}
	\Theta^{-1}H\Theta =
	\begin{pmatrix}
		H_{PP} + H_{PQ}T & H_{PQ} \\
		H_{QP} + H_{QQ}T - TH_{PP} - TH_{PQ}T & H_{QQ} - TH_{PQ}
	\end{pmatrix} \;.
	\label{eq:SHS_Riccati}
\end{equation}
The lower-left block is the Riccati equation, and the invariance of
$\mathcal{P}_T$ is the decoupling condition
\begin{equation}
	Q\,\Theta^{-1}H\Theta\,P = 0 \;,
	\label{eq:PQ_decoupling}
\end{equation}
with $P$ and $Q$ the projectors onto $\mathcal{P}$ and $\mathcal{Q}$.
$\Theta^{-1}H\Theta$ is then block upper triangular, and $H_{PP}+H_{PQ}T$ carries
the eigenvalues of $H$ on $\mathcal{P}_T$.
For $H=\eta^\lambda\mathbf H^\lambda$ of Eqs.~\eqref{eq:TDHF} and \eqref{eq:ppRPA}, \ie\
$H_{PP}=A_\alpha$, $H_{PQ}=B_\alpha$, $H_{QP}=-B_\alpha^\dagger$ and
$H_{QQ}=-D_\alpha$, the lower-left block is minus the left-hand side of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Riccati}.

\subsection{Generalized Bloch Equations}
As an example, consider the wave operator
\begin{equation}
	\mathcal{W} = \Theta P = P + T \;,
	\qquad
	P\mathcal{W} = P \;,
\end{equation}
which maps $\mathcal{P}$ onto $\mathcal{P}_T$. The invariance of
$\mathcal{P}_T$ is then the Bloch equation
\begin{equation}
	H\mathcal{W} = \mathcal{W}H\mathcal{W} \;,
	\label{eq:Bloch}
\end{equation}
whose $QP$ block is the Riccati equation and whose $PP$ block is an
identity. The effective Hamiltonian is
the upper-left block,
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:H_T_riccati}
	H_T = PH\mathcal{W} = P\Theta^{-1}H\Theta P = H_{PP}+H_{PQ}T \;.
\end{equation}
For $H=H_0+V$ with $[H_0,P]=0$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:Bloch} becomes the
generalized Bloch equation
\begin{equation}
	[\mathcal{W},H_0] = V\mathcal{W}-\mathcal{W}PV\mathcal{W} \;,
\end{equation}
the starting point of Rayleigh--Schr\"odinger perturbation theory for
$\mathcal{W}$. For a one-dimensional $\mathcal{P}$ spanned by $\Phi$,
$T$ contains the CI coefficients of $\Psi=\mathcal{W}\Phi$ in intermediate
normalization, $H_T=E$, and Eq.~\eqref{eq:Bloch} is the
Schr\"odinger equation projected onto $\mathcal{Q}$.

\subsection{Comparison to L\"owdin downfolding}
The Bloch equation should be compared with L\"owdin downfolding, that 
generates the frequency-dependent effective Hamiltonian acting in 
$\mathcal{P}$-space only,
\begin{equation}
	H_{\rm L}(\omega) = H_{PP}+H_{PQ}\left(\omega-H_{QQ}\right)^{-1}H_{QP} \;,
	\label{eq:Lowdin}
\end{equation}
Comparison with Eq.~\eqref{eq:H_T_riccati} then shows that 
\begin{equation}
		T(\omega) = \left(\omega-H_{QQ}\right)^{-1}H_{QP} \;.
\end{equation}
$H_{\rm L}(\omega)$ is
Hermitian for Hermitian $H$ and has the form of a self-energy, and
$\det\big(\omega-H_{\rm L}(\omega)\big)=0$ yields all eigenvalues of $H$
with a non-vanishing $\mathcal{P}$ component, but it is
energy-dependent and has to be solved root by root. The Bloch amplitude
is instead a single, energy-independent map for the $\dim\mathcal{P}$
selected roots,
\begin{equation}
	T = \big(T(\omega_1)x_1,\ldots,T(\omega_{\dim\mathcal{P}})x_{\dim\mathcal{P}}\big)X^{-1} \;,
\end{equation}
at the price of a nonlinear equation, a non-Hermitian $H_T$, and
one solution for each choice of roots. Expanded in $V$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:Lowdin} gives Brillouin--Wigner and
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Bloch} Rayleigh--Schr\"odinger perturbation theory. The two
are related by a Taylor expansion of $T(\omega_k)$ around a reference energy
$E_0$: with $g_0=(E_0-H_{QQ})^{-1}$ and $\omega_kx_k=H_Tx_k$,
\begin{equation}
	T = \sum_{n\geq0}g_0^{\,n+1}H_{QP}\left(E_0-H_T\right)^n \;,
\end{equation}
\ie\ the energy dependence of $T(\omega)$ becomes the dependence on
$H_T$, which is the same for all selected roots.

\section{Singles and the static self-energy}
\label{app:singles}
By Thouless' theorem, $e^{T_1}\Phi$ is a determinant with occupied
orbitals $\phi_i+\sum_at_i^a\phi_a$ (and biorthogonal bra orbitals). CCSD is
therefore CCD for $\tilde H=e^{-T_1}He^{T_1}$, together with the singles
equation
\begin{equation}
	\mel{\Phi_i^a}{e^{-T_2}\tilde He^{T_2}}{\Phi} = 0 \;.
	\label{eq:singles}
\end{equation}
For a one-body operator $f$ and $T_2=0$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:singles} is the
Riccati equation of Appendix~\ref{app:Riccati} in orbital space, with
$\mathcal{P}$ the occupied and $\mathcal{Q}$ the virtual orbitals,
\begin{equation}
	f_{ai}+\sum_bf_{ab}t_i^b-\sum_jt_j^af_{ji}-\sum_{jb}t_j^af_{jb}t_i^b = 0 \;.
	\label{eq:singles_Riccati}
\end{equation}
With $f$ the Fock operator of $e^{T_1}\Phi$, its solution is HF. With
$T_2$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:singles} is linear in $T_2$,
\begin{equation}
	\tilde f_{ai}
	+\sum_{kc}\tilde f_{kc}t_{ik}^{ac}
	+\frac12\sum_{kcd}\widetilde{\mel{ak}{}{cd}}\,t_{ik}^{cd}
	-\frac12\sum_{klc}\widetilde{\mel{kl}{}{ic}}\,t_{kl}^{ac}
	= 0 \;,
	\label{eq:singles_dressed}
\end{equation}
where $\tilde f$ and $\widetilde{\mel{pq}{}{rs}}$ are the Fock matrix and the
antisymmetrized integrals of $\tilde H$. The singles decouple occupied and
virtual orbitals for the one-body operator $\tilde f$ plus the $T_2$ terms.

For a HF reference and the first-order doubles, Eq.~\eqref{eq:mp2_double},
the $T_2$ terms are the second-order self-energy on shell. With
\begin{equation}
	\begin{aligned}
	\Sigma^>_{pq}(\omega)
	={}&
	\frac12\sum_{kcd}
	\frac{\mel{pk}{}{cd}\mel{cd}{}{qk}}{\omega+\eps_k-\eps_c-\eps_d} \;,
	\\
	\Sigma^<_{pq}(\omega)
	={}&
	\frac12\sum_{klc}
	\frac{\mel{pc}{}{kl}\mel{kl}{}{qc}}{\omega+\eps_c-\eps_k-\eps_l} \;,
	\end{aligned}
	\label{eq:Sigma2_gl}
	\end{equation}
	the $\pph$ and $\hhp$ parts of $\Sigma^{(2)}$,
\begin{equation}
	\frac12\sum_{kcd}\mel{ak}{}{cd}\,t_{ik}^{cd}
	-\frac12\sum_{klc}\mel{kl}{}{ic}\,t_{kl}^{ac}
	=
	\Sigma^>_{ai}(\eps_i)+\Sigma^<_{ai}(\eps_a) \;.
	\label{eq:singles_Sigma}
\end{equation}
The same holds for the one-body quadratic (mosaic) terms of CCD,
\begin{equation}
	\begin{aligned}
	f_{mj}+\frac12\sum_{nef}t_{jn}^{ef}\mel{mn}{}{ef}
	={}&
	f_{mj}+\Sigma^>_{mj}(\eps_j) \;,
	\\
	f_{be}-\frac12\sum_{mnf}t_{mn}^{bf}\mel{mn}{}{ef}
	={}&
	f_{be}+\Sigma^<_{be}(\eps_b) \;.
	\end{aligned}
	\label{eq:mosaic_Sigma}
\end{equation}
At this order, the static one-body operator of CCSD is thus
\begin{equation}
	\tilde\Sigma^{\rm CC}_{pq}
	=
	n_q\,\Sigma^>_{pq}(\eps_q)+\bar n_p\,\Sigma^<_{pq}(\eps_p) \;,
	\label{eq:Sigma_CC_static}
\end{equation}
to be compared with the quasiparticle self-consistent choice
\begin{equation}
	\tilde\Sigma^{\rm qs}_{pq}
	=
	\frac12\big[\Sigma_{pq}(\eps_p)+\Sigma_{pq}(\eps_q)\big] \;.
	\label{eq:Sigma_qs_static}
\end{equation}
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Sigma_CC_static} keeps only the non-resonant branches,
evaluated at M{\o}ller--Plesset denominators, and is non-Hermitian. The
resonant branches, $\Sigma^>$ in the virtual and $\Sigma^<$ in the
occupied block, remain dynamical, as in EOM-CC and ADC.
Closing the contour of $G_{\rm HF}\Sigma^{(2)}G_{\rm HF}$ in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Sigma3_static} gives
\begin{equation}
	\gamma^{(2)}_{ai}
	=
	\frac{\Sigma^>_{ai}(\eps_i)+\Sigma^<_{ai}(\eps_a)}{\eps_i-\eps_a}
	=
	t_i^{a(2)} \;,
	\label{eq:gamma2_singles}
\end{equation}
so that one Jacobi step of Eq.~\eqref{eq:singles} is one step of the
linearized Dyson equation for the occupied--virtual density.

This distinguishes two self-consistency loops around a self-energy built on
a reference $\Phi$. Updating $\Sigma_{\rm HF}[\gamma^{\rm lin}]$ with the
density of the linearized Dyson equation and diagonalizing the new mean
field rotates the orbitals with $\Sigma^{(3)}_{\infty,ai}=O(v^3)$, a
relaxation with the TDHF Hessian that does not contain the singles.
Rotating instead with the density itself,
\begin{equation}
	\phi_i\;\to\;\phi_i+\sum_a\gamma^{\rm lin}_{ai}\,\phi_a \;,
	\label{eq:Dyson_Brueckner}
\end{equation}
until $\gamma^{\rm lin}_{ai}=0$ in the current frame, reproduces
Eq.~\eqref{eq:singles} at leading order for biorthogonal orbitals, and the
Brueckner condition $t_1=0$ of Brueckner CCD for orthonormal orbitals.
Beyond leading order, the $T_2$ terms of Eq.~\eqref{eq:singles_dressed}
are built from the converged CCD amplitudes.

\subsubsection{GF3 self-energy}
We perform the integrals over $\tau$ by regarding each space-time term
as a circuit and using energy conservation at the intermediate vertices,
which leaves contour integrals over the channel frequency.
For the $\pp$-channel, one then gets
\begin{align}
\Sigma^{\pp}_{\cK,pq}(\omega)
= &
\frac{\ii}{4}
\sum_{rxyuw}
\mel{pr}{}{xy}\mel{xy}{}{uw}\mel{uw}{}{qr}
\nonumber\\
&\times
\int\frac{\dd\Omega}{2\pi}\,
L^{\pp}_{0,xy}(\Omega)\,L^{\pp}_{0,uw}(\Omega)\,
G_r(\Omega-\omega),
\label{eq:Sigma_K_pp_omega}
\end{align}
with Eq.~\eqref{eq:L0pp_omega}.
In the $\eh+\teh$ channel, Eq.~\eqref{eq:Sigma_K_eh_time}, we obtain 
\begin{align}
\Sigma^{\eh+\teh}_{\cK,pq}(\omega)
= &
-\ii
\sum_{rsxyu}
\mel{pr}{}{xy}\mel{xs}{}{ur}\mel{yu}{}{qs}
\nonumber\\
&\times
\int\frac{\dd\Omega}{2\pi}\,
L^{\eh}_{0,rx}(\Omega)\,L^{\eh}_{0,su}(\Omega)\,
G_y(\omega-\Omega),
\label{eq:Sigma_K_eh_omega}
\end{align}
with Eq.~\eqref{eq:L0eh_omega}.
In both channels, the kernel factorizes into two one-frequency
pair propagators in the channel transfer frequency $\Omega$; only the
$\Omega$ integral couples them to the spectator line
($r$ in $\pp$, $y$ in $\eh$).
What remains is to perform the contour integrals over $\Omega$. For
the $\pp$-channel, this gives 
\begin{align}
\Sigma^{\pp}_{\cK,pq}(\omega)
={}&
\frac14\sum_{abcdi}
\frac{\mel{pi}{}{ab}\mel{ab}{}{cd}\mel{cd}{}{qi}}
{(\omega-\Delta_{abi})(\omega-\Delta_{cdi})}
\label{eq:pp_in_plus}
\\
&+
\frac14\sum_{abkli}
\frac{\mel{pi}{}{ab}\,t_{kl}^{ab}\,\mel{kl}{}{qi}}
{\omega-\Delta_{abi}}
\label{eq:pp_lt_plus}
\\
&+
\frac14\sum_{cdkli}
\frac{\mel{pi}{}{kl}\,t_{kl}^{cd}\,\mel{cd}{}{qi}}
{\omega-\Delta_{cdi}}
\label{eq:pp_gt_plus}
\\
&-
\frac14\sum_{ijkla}
\frac{\mel{pa}{}{ij}\mel{ij}{}{kl}\mel{kl}{}{qa}}
{(\omega-\Delta_{ija})(\omega-\Delta_{kla})}
\label{eq:pp_in_minus}
\\
&+
\frac14\sum_{bckla}
\frac{\mel{pa}{}{bc}\,t_{kl}^{bc}\,\mel{kl}{}{qa}}
{\omega-\Delta_{kla}}
\label{eq:pp_lt_minus}
\\
&+
\frac14\sum_{ijbca}
\frac{\mel{pa}{}{ij}\,t_{ij}^{bc}\,\mel{bc}{}{qa}}
{\omega-\Delta_{ija}}.
\label{eq:pp_gt_minus}
\end{align}
For the $\eh+\teh$ channel, 
\begin{align}
\Sigma^{\eh+\teh}_{\cK,pq}(\omega)
={}&
\sum_{abcij}
\frac{\mel{pi}{}{ab}\mel{aj}{}{ci}\mel{bc}{}{qj}}
{(\omega-\Delta_{abi})(\omega-\Delta_{bcj})}
\label{eq:eh_in_plus}
\\
&-
\sum_{abcik}
\frac{\mel{pi}{}{ab}\,t_{ik}^{ac}\,\mel{bk}{}{qc}}
{\omega-\Delta_{abi}}
\label{eq:eh_lt_plus}
\\
&+
\sum_{abcjk}
\frac{\mel{pc}{}{ja}\,t_{jk}^{bc}\,\mel{ab}{}{qk}}
{\omega-\Delta_{abk}}
\label{eq:eh_gt_plus}
\\
&-
\sum_{ijkab}
\frac{\mel{pb}{}{ij}\mel{ia}{}{kb}\mel{jk}{}{qa}}
{(\omega-\Delta_{ijb})(\omega-\Delta_{jka})}
\label{eq:eh_in_minus}
\\

\sum_{ijkab}
\frac{\mel{pj}{}{ai}\,t_{jk}^{ab}\,\mel{ik}{}{qb}}

\subsubsection{From GF3 to ADC(3)}
What remains is to match the GF3 terms with the ADC(3) ones, 
following the method introduced by Schirmer.\cite{Schirmer1983}
The procedure is rather simple: we impose the resolvent 
structure on the self-energy
\begin{align}
	\label{eq:ADC3}
\Sigma_{\rm ADC(3)}(\omega)
={}&
\Sigma(\infty)
+
U^{\rm II}\big(\omega-K^{\rm II}-C^{\rm II}\big)^{-1}U^{{\rm II}\dagger}
\nonumber\\
&+
U^{\rm I}\big(\omega+K^{\rm I}+C^{\rm I}\big)^{-1}U^{{\rm I}\dagger},
\end{align}
with the static self-energy
$\Sigma(\infty)=\Sigma_{\rm HF}+\Sigma^{(3)}_{\infty}$ and
\begin{equation}
U^{\rm II}
=  U^{{\rm II},(1)}+U^{{\rm II},(2)} \;,
\qquad
U^{\rm I}
=  U^{{\rm I},(1)}+U^{{\rm I},(2)} \;.
\end{equation}
All terms are then classified according to their number of 
dynamical energy denominators. The ones that 
only contain one go inside $U$, and the ones that contain 
two go inside $C$.
Terms with the dynamical denominator $\omega-\Delta_{abi}$
($\pph$, $N+1$) belong to block II, terms with $\omega-\Delta_{ija}$
($\hhp$, $N-1$) to block I.
We use the zeroth-order matrices and first-order couplings of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:K_U1}. The unrestricted sums
of the GF3 terms are converted with
\begin{align}
\sum_{a<b}\sum_{c<d}x_{ab}\,
\hat P^-_{ab}\hat P^-_{cd}f_{ab,cd}\,y_{cd}
&=
\sum_{abcd}x_{ab}\,f_{ab,cd}\,y_{cd},
\nonumber\\
\sum_{a<b}x_{ab}\,\hat P^-_{ab}g_{ab}
&=
\sum_{ab}x_{ab}\,g_{ab},
\label{eq:pair_identities}
\end{align}
for $x$, $y$ antisymmetric in their pair indices, with $\hat P^-_{ab}$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:Pminus}.

In block II, the two-denominator terms,
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:pp_in_plus} and \eqref{eq:eh_in_plus}, combine
into
\begin{align}
\Sigma^{\rm II}_{C}(\omega)
={}&
U^{{\rm II},(1)}\big(\omega-K^{\rm II}\big)^{-1}C^{\rm II}
\big(\omega-K^{\rm II}\big)^{-1}U^{{\rm II},(1)\dagger},
\nonumber\\
C^{\rm II}_{abi,cdj}
={}&
\mel{ab}{}{cd}\delta_{ij}
-\hat P^-_{ab}\hat P^-_{cd}\mel{aj}{}{ci}\delta_{bd},
\label{eq:C_II}
\end{align}
where we used $\mel{bc}{}{qj}=-\mel{qj}{}{cb}$.
The one-denominator terms,
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:pp_lt_plus}, \eqref{eq:pp_gt_plus},
\eqref{eq:eh_lt_plus} and \eqref{eq:eh_gt_plus}, give
\begin{align}
\Sigma^{\rm II}_{U}(\omega)
={}&
U^{{\rm II},(2)}\big(\omega-K^{\rm II}\big)^{-1}U^{{\rm II},(1)\dagger}
\nonumber\\
&+
U^{{\rm II},(1)}\big(\omega-K^{\rm II}\big)^{-1}U^{{\rm II},(2)\dagger},
\nonumber\\
U^{{\rm II},(2)}_{p;abi}
={}&
\frac12\sum_{jl}t_{jl}^{ab}\mel{pi}{}{jl}
-\hat P^-_{ab}\sum_{kc}t_{ki}^{ac}\mel{pc}{}{kb},
\label{eq:U2_II}
\end{align}
where we used $t_{ik}^{ac}\mel{bk}{}{qc}=t_{ki}^{ac}\mel{qc}{}{kb}$.

In block I, the two-denominator terms,
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:pp_in_minus} and \eqref{eq:eh_in_minus}, combine
into
\begin{align}
\Sigma^{\rm I}_{C}(\omega)
={}&
-U^{{\rm I},(1)}\big(\omega+K^{\rm I}\big)^{-1}C^{\rm I}
\big(\omega+K^{\rm I}\big)^{-1}U^{{\rm I},(1)\dagger},
\nonumber\\
C^{\rm I}_{ija,klb}
={}&
\mel{ij}{}{kl}\delta_{ab}
-\hat P^-_{ij}\hat P^-_{kl}\mel{ib}{}{ka}\delta_{jl},
\label{eq:C_I}
\end{align}
where we used $\mel{jk}{}{qa}=-\mel{qa}{}{kj}$.
The sign is such that $\Sigma^{\rm I}_{C}$ is the first-order term
of $U^{{\rm I},(1)}(\omega+K^{\rm I}+C^{\rm I})^{-1}U^{{\rm I},(1)\dagger}$.
The one-denominator terms,
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:pp_lt_minus}, \eqref{eq:pp_gt_minus},
\eqref{eq:eh_lt_minus} and \eqref{eq:eh_gt_minus}, give
\begin{align}
\Sigma^{\rm I}_{U}(\omega)
={}&
U^{{\rm I},(2)}\big(\omega+K^{\rm I}\big)^{-1}U^{{\rm I},(1)\dagger}
\nonumber\\
&+
U^{{\rm I},(1)}\big(\omega+K^{\rm I}\big)^{-1}U^{{\rm I},(2)\dagger},
\nonumber\\
U^{{\rm I},(2)}_{p;ija}
={}&
\frac12\sum_{bc}t_{ij}^{bc}\mel{pa}{}{bc}
-\hat P^-_{ij}\sum_{kb}t_{ik}^{ba}\mel{pk}{}{bj}.
\label{eq:U2_I}
\end{align}
With Eqs.~\eqref{eq:C_II}--\eqref{eq:U2_I} and $\Sigma(\infty)$, all
third-order terms are reproduced by Eq.~\eqref{eq:ADC3}, which
completes ADC(3).

\section{Channel couplings}
\subsection{Coupling channels in Hermitian form}
\label{sec:coupling}

\subsubsection{Coherent emission}
The coupling $E_3$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:ADC4_3p2h} is a sum over the three
lines of the $\pph$ state,
\begin{equation}
E_3
=
\sum_{\ell=1}^{3}e_\ell \;.
\label{eq:C3_lines}
\end{equation}
$e_1$ and $e_2$ are the first term of Eq.~\eqref{eq:ADC4_3p2h} with the
particle $d$ or $e$ emitting, $e_3$ is its second term, the emission by
the hole $k$. Each $e_\ell$ is a single bare interaction on the line
$\ell$ that emits the pair $(c;j)$. With $\mathcal G_3=(\omega-K_3)^{-1}$,
\begin{equation}
E_3^\dagger\mathcal G_3E_3
=
\sum_{\ell\ell'}e_\ell^\dagger\mathcal G_3e_{\ell'}
=
\kappa^{\rm LD}+\sum_\alpha\kappa^{\times}_\alpha+\kappa^{\rm ov} \;.
\label{eq:C3_decomposition}
\end{equation}
Each contraction of the antisymmetrized $\ppphh$ states is
sorted by the lines of the $\pph$ state that its two vertices touch: one
line ($\kappa^{\rm LD}$), the two lines of the pair $\alpha$
($\kappa^\times_\alpha$), or all three lines ($\kappa^{\rm ov}$).
$\kappa^{\rm LD}$ is the $\Sigma^{(2)}$ insertion into the lines of the
$\pph$ state,
\begin{align}
\kappa^{\rm LD}_{abi,cdj}
={}&
\hat P^-_{ab}\hat P^-_{cd}\,\delta_{bd}\delta_{ij}\,
\Sigma^{>}_{ac}(\omega-\eps_b+\eps_i)
\nonumber\\
&-\delta_{ac}\delta_{bd}\,
\Sigma^{<}_{ij}(\eps_a+\eps_b-\omega) \;,
\label{eq:kappa_LD}
\end{align}
with $\Sigma^>$ and $\Sigma^<$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:Sigma2_gl}.
$\kappa^{\rm ov}$ are the orderings of
$\sum_{\alpha\neq\beta}\cK_\alpha[\Gamma^{(1)}]\cK_\beta[\Gamma^{(1)}]\mathcal G$
with a five-line subinterval between the two rungs, and
$\kappa^\times_\alpha$ are the crossed channels of the pair $\alpha$
(Sec.~\ref{sec:overlap}).
The channels are therefore not separate objects in the Hamiltonian form,
but labels of the contractions in one configuration space. None of the
three mechanisms is of the form $e^\dagger_\ell\mathcal G_3e_{\ell'}$ alone; their sum is.

\subsubsection{Configuration hierarchy}
We therefore couple the channels in common configuration spaces and
dress each space with the own-channel approximation of
Sec.~\ref{sec:depth1}. The five lines of a $\ppphh$ state form
ten pairs $\pi$, and in analogy to Eqs.~\eqref{eq:Faddeev_inverse} and
\eqref{eq:Rinv_phonons},
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
K_3+C_3
={}&
\sum_{\pi=1}^{10}\big(K_3+C_\pi\big)-9K_3 \;,
\\
\omega\mathcal N_3-\mathcal H_3
={}&
\sum_{\pi=1}^{10}Q_\pi^{-1}-9\,\mathcal G_3^{-1} \;,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:3p2h_own_channel}
\end{equation}
with $C_\pi$ the bare interaction of the pair $\pi$ and $Q_\pi$ its RPA
propagator with three spectators. As in Eq.~\eqref{eq:HN_explicit},
$\mathcal N_3=\bI-\sum_\pi T_\pi^\dagger T_\pi$, with the same channel
amplitudes: $T^{\eh}$ for the six $\eh$ pairs, $T^{\pp}$ for the three
$\pp$ pairs and $T^{\pp\dagger}$ for the $\hh$ pair. Then
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Sigma^{c,{\rm II}}(\omega)
={}&
\mathbf U_{\rm hier}\big(\omega\mathbf N_{\rm hier}-\mathbf H_{\rm hier}\big)^{-1}\mathbf U_{\rm hier}^\dagger \;,
\\
\mathbf N_{\rm hier}
={}&
\begin{pmatrix}
\mathcal N & 0
\\
0 & \mathcal N_3
\end{pmatrix} ,
\qquad
\mathbf H_{\rm hier}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
\mathcal H+\Delta C & E_3^\dagger
\\
E_3 & \mathcal H_3
\end{pmatrix} ,
\\
\mathbf U_{\rm hier}
={}&
\big(\mathcal U+\Delta U,\;U_3\big)
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:hierarchy_3p2h}
\end{equation}
is Hermitian for positive definite $\mathbf N_{\rm hier}$. Since
$\mathcal H_3-K_3=O(v)$ and $\mathcal N_3-\bI=O(v^2)$ enter the
self-energy at fifth order, and $\Delta C$, $\Delta U$ in the metric form
differ from those of Eq.~\eqref{eq:d1_ADC4} only at fourth order,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:hierarchy_3p2h} is exact through fourth order.

\subsubsection{What is resummed}
The ten pairs are of three kinds. The ladders of the pairs $\alpha$ of
the $\pph$ state continue through the $\ppphh$ intervals.
The $\eh$ RPA of the emitted pair $(c;j)$ screens it: in
$\kappa^{\rm LD}$, $\Sigma^{(2)}$ of the line becomes a TDHF-screened,
$GW$-type self-energy; in $\kappa^\times_\alpha$, the second-order
crossed vertex becomes the crossed-channel ladder, \eg\ $\Phi^{\eh}$
resummed in the pair $(12)$; in $\kappa^{\rm ov}$, the overlaps are
screened. The ladders of an emitted line with a line of the $\pph$
state are vertex corrections of the emission $e_\ell$, \ie\ crossed
channels inside the crossed channels.
Overlaps of ladders at the $\ppphh$ level, and emission from
it, require the $\pppphhh$ space, the next level of the
hierarchy with $E_4=\sum_{\ell=1}^{5}e_\ell$.
