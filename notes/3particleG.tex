\title{From the three-particle Green's function to the parquet equations,
to ADC, and beyond}

\begin{abstract}
The Faddeev-decomposition of the three-particle vertex is used to derive a 
Hermitian effective Hamiltonian of Faddeev-ADC(3) type. Besides the Parquet approximation and the use of
a Hartree--Fock Green's function, the derivation is based on 
two further approximations: couplings between different correlation channels as well as overlaps between different time-orderins
are neglected. Faddeev-ADC(3) contains FLEX (first-order resolvent), ADC(2)-X (TDA limit) ADC(3) (TDA limit + first-order backward couplings)
as limiting cases. Several self-energy schemes that are derived in Hedin's scheme 
or in the $\pp$ channel as well as PSD-completions (double counting of second-order terms aside)
are recovered as a limiting 
case of Faddeev-ADC(3), and they are reviewed. Numerical results show that 
Faddeev-ADC(3), as well as all other methods that explicitly include 
triplet backward couplings to all orders come with very large errors. Switching off 
the triplets largely overcomes these issues, but only the performance of $GW$ is recovered.
Replacing the ring- and ladder-CCD amplitudes of Faddeev-ADC(3) with CCD ones,
one recovers essentially the performance of 
CCSD. Coupling correlation channels seems to be decisive for accurate charged excitations.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
Via the three-particle Green's function $G_3$,
two routes to the one-particle self-energy are connected: the
parquet equations for the two-particle vertex and the
algebraic-diagrammatic construction (ADC).
Starting from the Martin--Schwinger hierarchy (Sec.~\ref{sec:MS}), we
express the correlation self-energy in terms of a 6-point response
function $R$, which obeys a Dyson equation with a three-particle kernel
(Sec.~\ref{sec:eliminating}). Dropping its 3-body irreducible part
leaves the Faddeev kernel,\cite{Faddeev1961,Winter1972,Ethofer1969} in
which two of the three lines scatter through a two-particle vertex
while the third line is a spectator. In the Faddeev form, this vertex
is the full parquet vertex $F$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:Faddeev}).\cite{Kugler2018}

Truncated at fixed order in $v$, the Faddeev series gives GF$n$, and
Schirmer's matching gives ADC($n$) (App.~\ref{app:ADC}). The connection
becomes useful once the ladders in the pairs are resummed beyond the
Tamm--Dancoff ladders contained in ADC (Sec.~\ref{sec:parquet_Heff}).
With the RPA ladders of the first-order Faddeev kernel in every pair,
and without overlaps between the ladders of different pairs, a
Hermitian effective Hamiltonian of the size of ADC(3) is obtained, that
describes the coupling of a single particle to $\pp$ and $\eh$
phonons. This is essentially the Faddeev random-phase approximation
(FRPA) of Barbieri and co-workers.\cite{Barbieri2001,
barbieri2003extension,barbieri2007quasiparticles,Degroote2011}
In FRPA, the couplings are those of ADC(3), $U^{(1)}+U^{(2)}$, added by
hand;\cite{Degroote2011} here they follow from the backward amplitudes
of the phonons, the ring-CCD ($\eh$) and ladder-CCD ($\pp$) amplitudes
of the channels.\cite{Scuseria2013} The hierarchy is
$\text{ADC(3)}\subset\text{FRPA}\subset\text{parquet}$ for the dynamic
self-energy. At fourth order, the crossed channels, the overlaps and the
line dressing are added in the $\ppphh$ space.

Three approximations are made by construction. The parquet
approximation, $\Lambda=\Lambda^{(1)}$, keeps $F$ exact through third
order, since $\Lambda=\Lambda^{(1)}+O(v^4)$,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:crossing_Lambda}. With a static HF reference in all lines
of the vertex, every diagram of the vertex is a product of
instantaneous interactions and $G_{\rm HF}$ lines, whose frequency
integrals turn into resolvents of configuration spaces. This makes the
effective-Hamiltonian form possible. Finally, the Faddeev approximation
affects the self-energy from fifth order on. The orders through which
the resulting self-energies are exact are collected in
Table~\ref{tab:orders}; the constructions derived in these notes are exact through fourth order at most.

\section{Faddeev Approach to the Self-Energy}

\subsection{Martin--Schwinger Equations}
\label{sec:MS}
Let us define the $n$-particle Green's function as 
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:Gn_definition}
\begin{aligned}
G_n(1\ldots n;1'\ldots n')
={}&
(-\ii)^n
\big\langle
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{C}}\,
\hat{\psi}(1)\cdots\hat{\psi}(n)
\\
&\times
\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}(n')\cdots\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}(1')
\big\rangle \;,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with $\hat{\psi}$ in the Heisenberg picture.
$G_n$ might be expressed in terms of $G_{n-1}$ and
$G_{n+1}$ through the
Martin--Schwinger hierarchy,\cite{Martin1959}
\begin{align}
&
\left[
\ii\partial_{t_1}
-
h_0(1)
\right]
G_n(1\ldots n;1'\ldots n')
\nonumber\\
={}&
\sum_{j=1}^{n}
(-1)^{j-1}
\delta(1j')\,
G_{n-1}
\bigl(
2\ldots n;
1'\ldots \widehat{j'}\ldots n'
\bigr)
\nonumber\\
&+
(-1)^{n}\,\ii\,
v(1\bar2;\bar3\bar4)\,
G_{n+1}
\bigl(
\bar3\bar4\,2\ldots n;
1'\ldots n'\bar2^{+}
\bigr) \;.
\label{eq:Martin_Schwinger_general}
\end{align}
We define 
\begin{equation}
G_0^{-1}(11')
=
\left[
\ii\frac{\partial}{\partial t_1}
-
h_0(1)
\right]
\delta(11') \;,
\label{eq:G0_inverse_def}
\end{equation}
and its inverse via
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:G0_inverse}
G_0^{-1}(1\bar{1})\,
G_0(\bar{1}1')
=
\delta(11') \;.
\end{equation}
Using both relations, $G_1$ through $G_3$ become
\begin{align}
G(11')
= & 
G_0(11')
\nonumber\\
& -\ii \,
G_0(1\bar3)\,
v(\bar3\bar4;\bar5\bar6)\,
G_2(\bar5\bar6;1'\bar4^+) \;,
\label{eq:Martin_Schwinger_G1} \\
G_2(12;1'2')
= & 
G_0(11')G(22')
-
G_0(12')G(21')
\nonumber\\
& +
\ii \,
G_0(1\bar3)\,
v(\bar3\bar4;\bar5\bar6)
\nonumber\\
&\times
G_3(2\bar5\bar6;1'2'\bar4^+) \;,
\label{eq:Martin_Schwinger_G2}\\
G_3(123;1'2'3')
= &
G_0(11')\,
G_2(23;2'3') \nonumber \\
& -
G_0(12')\,
G_2(23;1'3')
\nonumber\\
& +
G_0(13')\,
G_2(23;1'2')
\nonumber\\
& -
\ii\,
G_0(1\bar4)\,
v(\bar4\bar5;\bar6\bar7)
\nonumber\\
&\times
G_4(23\bar6\bar7;1'2'3'\bar5^+) \;,
\label{eq:Martin_Schwinger_G3}
\end{align}
Through Eq.~\eqref{eq:Martin_Schwinger_G1}, we can define the single-particle
self-energy according to 
\begin{equation}
	\Sigma(11')
	=
	-\ii
	v(1\bar2;\bar3'\bar2')
	G_2(\bar3'\bar2';\bar3\bar2^+)
	G^{-1}(\bar3 1') \;.
	\label{eq:SDE_G2}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Eliminating $G_2$}
\label{sec:eliminating}
We can now use Eq.~\eqref{eq:Martin_Schwinger_G2}
to eliminate the dependence of 
Eq.~\eqref{eq:SDE_G2} on $G_2$ in favor of $G_3$. 
$G_4$ might be eliminated as well, 
and Eq.~\eqref{eq:SDE_G2} can be expressed in terms 
of $G_3$ and $G_5$, or, more generally, in terms 
of odd-order Green's functions. Alternatively,
combining
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Martin_Schwinger_G2} with 
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Martin_Schwinger_G3}, one can express 
Eq.~\eqref{eq:SDE_G2} in terms of $G_2$ and $G_4$,
or, more generally, in terms 
of even-order Green's functions.

Here, we take the odd-order route. To do so, following 
\citet{Winter1972} and \citet{Ethofer1969}, 
we introduce the 6-point response function
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
    R(123;1'2'3')
    &=
    G_3(123;1'2'3')
    \\
    &\quad
    -
    G_2(23;3'\bar4)\,
    G^{-1}(\bar4\bar4')\,
    G_2(\bar4'1;1'2') \;,
\end{split}
\label{eq:response_function}
\end{equation}
so that the EOM for $G_2$ becomes
\begin{align}
G_2(12;1'2')
&=
G(11')G(22')
-
G(12')G(21')
\nonumber\\
&\quad+
\ii\,
G(1\bar3)\,
v(\bar3\bar4;\bar5'\bar6')\,
R(2\bar5'\bar6';1'2'\bar4^+).
\label{eq:SDE_G2_R}
\end{align}
Plugging Eq.~\eqref{eq:SDE_G2_R}
into Eq.~\eqref{eq:SDE_G2} and
using Eq.~\eqref{eq:G0_inverse} gives
\begin{equation}
\Sigma(11')
=
\Sigma_{\rm HF}(11')
+
\Sigma^{c}(11')
\label{eq:Sigma_split}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{align}
\Sigma_{\rm HF}(11')
= &
-\ii\,
\big[
v(1\bar2;1'\bar2')
-
v(1\bar2;\bar2'1')
\big]\,
G(\bar2'\bar2^+),
\label{eq:Sigma_HF}
\\[0.5em]
\Sigma^{c}(11')
= &
v(1\bar2;\bar3'\bar2')\,
G(\bar3'\bar4)\,
v(\bar4\bar5;\bar6'\bar7') \nonumber \\
& \times
R(\bar2'\bar6'\bar7';\bar3\bar2\bar5^+) 
G^{-1}(\bar3 1').
\label{eq:Sigma_3}
\end{align}
The response function $R$ obeys a Dyson equation with the
$\pph$-irreducible three-particle kernel $\Xi$, whose free propagator is
the $\pph$ propagator of three independent (dressed) lines,
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:D_pph}
\mathcal G(312;1'2'3')
=
G(33')
\left[
G(11')\,G(22')
-
G(12')\,G(21')
\right] \;.
\end{equation}
As in $G_3$, all
three lines run from the second to the first triple. From here on, the
first triple of $R$ is ordered as in Eq.~\eqref{eq:D_pph}, with the line 3 of $\mathcal G$ in the first slot. The second triple keeps the order $(1'2'3')$, so that line 3 runs from the last slot of the second triple to the first slot of the first.

\subsection{Faddeev Decomposition}
\label{sec:Faddeev}
The kernel $\Xi$ of the Dyson equation for $R$ is split into the Faddeev
(pairwise-reducible) part and the 3-body irreducible kernel
$\Xi_{\rm 3B}$, which is dropped from now on. In the Faddeev part, one pair
of lines scatters through a two-particle vertex while the third line
propagates freely.\cite{Faddeev1961,Ethofer1969,Winter1972,Barbieri2001}
Since the two lines of every pair run in the same direction, each pair
scatters in its $\pp$ channel, with the $\pp$-irreducible vertex
$\Gamma^{\pp}$ (exact at first order, see App.~\ref{app:parquet_vertices}).
As in Refs.~\citenum{Ethofer1969,Winter1972}, the Dyson equation for $R$ then reads
\begin{equation}
R=\mathcal G+\cK R \;,
\qquad
\cK
=
\cK_{12}\big[\Gamma^{\pp}\big]
+\cK_{31}\big[\Gamma^{\pp}\big]
+\cK_{32}\big[\Gamma^{\pp}\big] \;,
\label{eq:K_pairs}
\end{equation}
where the pair operators act, for any 4-point $Y$ and any $\chi$
antisymmetric in its first pair, as
\begin{align}
\big[\cK_{12}[Y]\chi\big](312;1'2'3')
={}&
-\frac14\big[G(1\bar1)G(2\bar2)-G(1\bar2)G(2\bar1)\big]
\nonumber\\
&\times
Y(\bar1\bar2;\bar1'\bar2')\,\chi(3\bar1'\bar2';1'2'3'),
\nonumber\\
\big[\cK_{31}[Y]\chi\big](312;1'2'3')
={}&
G(3\bar3)\,G(1\bar1)\,Y(\bar3\bar1;\bar1'\bar3')
\nonumber\\
&\times
\chi(\bar3'\bar1'2;1'2'3'),
\nonumber\\
\big[\cK_{32}[Y]\chi\big](312;1'2'3')
={}&
G(3\bar3)\,G(2\bar2)\,Y(\bar3\bar2;\bar2'\bar3')
\nonumber\\
&\times
\chi(\bar3'1\bar2';1'2'3').
\label{eq:pair_ops}
\end{align}

Fig.~\ref{fig:Faddeev_pairs}.

Cutting the two lines of pair $\alpha$ separates the outgoing from the
incoming legs of $Y$ in $\cK_\alpha[Y]$, \ie\ all three pairs scatter
in the $\pp$ channel of $R$.
In $\Sigma^{c}$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:Sigma_3}, line 3 becomes the hole for
$t_{1'}<t_1$ (block II) or the particle for $t_1<t_{1'}$ (block I). The
pair $(12)$ then gives the ladder-type self-energy diagrams of the $\pp$
channel, and the pairs $(31)$ and $(32)$ give the ring-type diagrams of
the $\eh$ and $\teh$ channels. From here on, we label the pairs by these
channels, $\alpha\in\{\pp,\eh,\teh\}$ for $(12)$, $(31)$ and $(32)$.

Eq.~\eqref{eq:K_pairs} is exact at first order, where all
vertices are $\Gamma^{(1)}$. Beyond first order, iterating $\cK_{\eh}[\Gamma^{\pp}]$ or $\cK_{\teh}[\Gamma^{\pp}]$ alone
would count the rungs of the ladders in these pairs twice,
since $\mathcal G$ is not antisymmetrized in the lines 3 and 1 (3 and 2). The $\pp$ channel of $R$ in these pairs is the $\eh$ channel of $F$, whose ladder $\Phi^{\eh}$ is already contained in $\Gamma^{\pp}$.\cite{Degroote2011} We therefore
replace $\Gamma^{\pp}$ in $\cK_\alpha$ by a pair vertex $\Gamma_\alpha$,
fixed such that the pair $\alpha$ alone reproduces its full two-body
scattering $F$. For the $\pp$ pair, $\Gamma_{\pp}=\Gamma^{\pp}$, and at
first order $\Gamma_\alpha=\Gamma^{(1)}$ for all pairs. Their explicit form is not needed,
since only $F$ appears in the Faddeev form derived next.

\subsection{\label{sec:Faddeev_form}Faddeev form of Eq.~\eqref{eq:Sigma_3}: the ADC--parquet link}
\subsubsection{The Faddeev Series}
Eq.~\eqref{eq:K_pairs} contains the pair-irreducible vertex
$\Gamma^{\pp}$ in all three pairs.
The aim of this subsection is to rewrite $R$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:Sigma_3} such
that only the full vertex $F$ appears in all three pairs.
This is the Faddeev form of the three-body problem.\cite{Faddeev1961}

Based on the Faddeev construction, we will then show that 
GF$n$ is the truncation of the Faddeev series,
at total order $n-2$ in $v$. 
The ADC($n$) matrices then
follow from Schirmer's matching,\cite{Schirmer1983} in which the
resolvent form of ADC($n$) is imposed and matched to the self-energy
order by order. ADC(3) is worked out as an
illustrative case in App.~\ref{app:ADC}.

With the pair vertices $\Gamma_\alpha$ in place of $\Gamma^{\pp}$,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:K_pairs} reads
\begin{equation}
R=\mathcal G+\sum_{\alpha}\cK_\alpha\big[\Gamma_\alpha\big] R \;,
\label{eq:Dyson_pairs}
\end{equation}
where $\cK_\alpha\big[\Gamma_\alpha\big]$ acts only on the pair $\alpha$, with the
third line as a free spectator [Fig.~\ref{fig:Faddeev_pairs}(b), with
the thick bars standing for $\Gamma_\alpha$].
We collect the terms of $R$ in which the last interaction, \ie\ the
one closest to the outgoing triple, acts on the pair $\alpha$ into
$R_\alpha\equiv\cK_\alpha\big[\Gamma_\alpha\big] R$. Solving
$R_\alpha=\cK_\alpha\big[\Gamma_\alpha\big]\big(\mathcal G+R_\alpha+\sum_{\beta\neq\alpha}R_\beta\big)$
for $R_\alpha$ gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Faddeev_rearr}
\begin{aligned}
R
= &
\mathcal G+R_{\pp}+R_{\eh}+R_{\teh} \;,
\\
R_\alpha
= &
\big(\bI-\cK_\alpha\big[\Gamma_\alpha\big]\big)^{-1}\cK_\alpha\big[\Gamma_\alpha\big]
\Big(\mathcal G+\sum_{\beta\neq\alpha}R_\beta\Big) \;,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $(\bI-\cK_\alpha\big[\Gamma_\alpha\big])^{-1}\cK_\alpha\big[\Gamma_\alpha\big]$ sums all
consecutive interactions in the pair $\alpha$.

Acting on $\mathcal G$, it gives all
diagrams of $R$ in which only the pair $\alpha$ interacts, while the
third line remains a free spectator. By the definition of $\Gamma_\alpha$,
this is the full two-body scattering of the pair,
$\cK_\alpha[F]=\cK_\alpha\big[\Gamma_\alpha\big]+\cK_\alpha\big[\Gamma_\alpha\big]\cK_\alpha[F]$,
so that
\begin{equation}
\big(\bI-\cK_\alpha\big[\Gamma_\alpha\big]\big)^{-1}\cK_\alpha\big[\Gamma_\alpha\big]
=
\cK_\alpha[F] \;.
\label{eq:V_to_F}
\end{equation}
Inserting
Eq.~\eqref{eq:V_to_F} into
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Faddeev_rearr} gives the Faddeev
equations\cite{Barbieri2001,Degroote2011}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Faddeev_F}
R_\alpha
=
\cK_\alpha[F]\Big(\mathcal G+\sum_{\beta\neq\alpha}R_\beta\Big) \;.
\end{equation}
Iterating Eq.~\eqref{eq:Faddeev_F}, \ie\ inserting the $R_\beta$ on
the right-hand side again and again, gives the Faddeev series
\cite{watson1953multiple,Faddeev1961,Barbieri2001}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Faddeev_series}
\begin{aligned}
R
= &
\mathcal G+\sum_{\alpha}\cK_\alpha[F]\mathcal G
+\sum_{\alpha\neq\beta}\cK_\alpha[F]\cK_\beta[F]\mathcal G
\\
& +\sum_{\substack{\alpha\neq\beta\\\beta\neq\gamma}}
\cK_\alpha[F]\cK_\beta[F]\cK_\gamma[F]\mathcal G+\cdots \;,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
in which consecutive pairs are always different (Fig.~\ref{fig:Faddeev_runs}).
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Faddeev_series} is the starting point for two
approximations. Truncated at fixed order in $v$, it gives GF$n$ and, with Schirmer's matching, ADC($n$). Keeping
only the own-channel ladders without overlaps, but to all orders, gives
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Faddeev_series_oc}, from which an effective Hamiltonian is 
derived in Sec.~\ref{sec:parquet_Heff}.

\subsubsection{Truncation at fixed order: ADC}
To relate Eq.~\eqref{eq:Faddeev_series} to ADC, we expand $F$ in powers
of $v$. By Eqs.~\eqref{eq:parquet_channels} and
\eqref{eq:crossing_Lambda}, $\Lambda$ has no second-order term,
so that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:F_orders}
\begin{aligned}
F^{(1)}
= &
\Gamma^{(1)} \;,
\\
F^{(2)}
= &
\Phi^{\pp,(2)}+\Phi^{\eh,(2)}+\Phi^{\teh,(2)} \;.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The self-energy, Eq.~\eqref{eq:Sigma_3}, contains two explicit
interactions $v$. A term of order $m$ in $v$ of $R$ therefore contributes to
$\Sigma$ at order $m+2$. ADC($n$) is complete through order $n$, so
that $R$ is needed through order $n-2$ in $v$. Collecting the terms of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Faddeev_series} up to this order,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ADC_Faddeev}
\begin{aligned}
R_{\rm ADC(2)}
= &
\mathcal G \;,
\\
R_{\rm ADC(3)}
= &
\mathcal G+\sum_{\alpha}\cK_\alpha\big[F^{(1)}\big]\mathcal G_{\rm HF} \;,
\\
R_{\rm ADC(4)}
= &
\mathcal G+\sum_{\alpha}\cK_\alpha\big[F^{(1)}+F^{(2)}\big]\mathcal G_{\rm HF}
\\
& +\sum_{\alpha\neq\beta}
\cK_\alpha\big[F^{(1)}\big]\cK_\beta\big[F^{(1)}\big]\mathcal G_{\rm HF} \;.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here, $\mathcal G$ in the first term, $G(\bar3'\bar4)$ and the static part
$\Sigma_{\rm HF}$ are evaluated with $G$ expanded to the corresponding
order, while in all terms with at least one $F$, all lines can be taken at the HF level; $\mathcal G_{\rm HF}$ denotes Eq.~\eqref{eq:D_pph} with $G_{\rm HF}$ lines.

\subsubsection{Own-Channel and No-Overlap Approximations}

We now introduce the own-channel approximation
to Eq.~\eqref{eq:Faddeev_series}.
For this, we approximate the BSEs Eq.~\eqref{eq:parquet_BSE}
in the own-channel approximation, for the special case of 
the first-order Faddeev kernel, so that 
$\Gamma^{\lambda}\to\Gamma^{(1)}$.
The two BSEs then define two separate vertices
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
F^{\pp}_{\rm oc}
= &
\Gamma^{(1)}-\frac12\,\Gamma^{(1)}\circ_{\pp}F^{\pp}_{\rm oc} \;,
\\
F^{\eh}_{\rm oc}
= &
\Gamma^{(1)}+\Gamma^{(1)}\circ_{\eh}F^{\eh}_{\rm oc} \;.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:BSE_oc}
\end{equation}
This is also done in the FLEX approximation.\cite{Bickers1989,Bickers2004}

In the own-channel approximation to Eq.~\eqref{eq:Faddeev_series},
each pair scatters only through the
ladder of its own channel with the bare kernel. 
The own-channel approximation amounts to 
neglecting the channel crossings of
parquet (and of coupled cluster) inside a pair.\cite{Barbieri2001}
\citet{Barbieri2001} combined this approximation additionally with
a no-overlap approximation that we denote here by 
$[\cdots]_{\pph}$. It neglects the time orderings in which the ladders of
different pairs overlap in time, \ie\ consecutive pairs are separated by
a $\pph$ cut, a subinterval spanned only by the three lines of a $\pph$
state (Sec.~\ref{sec:depth1}). 
Due to this restriction, at first-order Faddeev the effective
Hamiltonian only contains $\pph$ ($\hhp$) configurations.
At fourth order, the neglected terms are
added perturbatively together with the $\ppphh$
($\hhhpp$) space.
Each pair scatters with the own-channel vertex of its channel,
\begin{equation}
F_{\pp}=F^{\pp}_{\rm oc} \;,
\qquad
F_{\eh}=F_{\teh}=F^{\eh}_{\rm oc} \;.
\label{eq:F_alpha}
\end{equation}
With these approximations, Eq.~\eqref{eq:Faddeev_series} becomes 
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Faddeev_series_oc}
\begin{aligned}
R_{\rm oc}
= &
\mathcal G+\sum_{\alpha}\cK_\alpha[F_\alpha]\mathcal G
+\sum_{\alpha\neq\beta}\Big[\cK_\alpha[F_\alpha]\cK_\beta[F_\beta]\mathcal G\Big]_{\pph}
\\
& +\sum_{\substack{\alpha\neq\beta\\\beta\neq\gamma}}
\Big[\cK_\alpha[F_\alpha]\cK_\beta[F_\beta]\cK_\gamma[F_\gamma]\mathcal G\Big]_{\pph}
+\cdots \;.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Expanded in $v$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:Faddeev_series_oc} reproduces
Eq.~\eqref{eq:ADC_Faddeev} through $R_{\rm ADC(3)}$. At the next order,
it misses the crossed-channel parts of $F^{(2)}$ in each pair,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:F_orders}, and the overlapping orderings of
$\cK_\alpha[F^{(1)}]\cK_\beta[F^{(1)}]\mathcal G$.

\section{\label{sec:parquet_Heff}Parquet in effective-Hamiltonian form}
In this section, we insert the own-channel and no-overlap approximation,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Faddeev_series_oc}, into Eq.~\eqref{eq:Sigma_3}, 
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Sigma^{c}(11')
={}&
v(1\bar2;\bar3'\bar2')\,
G(\bar3'\bar4)\,
v(\bar4\bar5;\bar6'\bar7')
\\
&\times
R_{\rm oc}(\bar2'\bar6'\bar7';\bar3\bar2\bar5^+)\,
G^{-1}(\bar3 1') \;.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:Sigma_oc}
\end{equation}
We then show that, with $G_{\rm HF}$ lines, the self-energy becomes
\begin{equation}
	\begin{aligned}
	\Sigma(\omega) = &
\Sigma(\infty)
+\mathcal U^{\rm II}\big(\omega\mathcal N^{\rm II}-\mathcal H^{\rm II}\big)^{-1}\mathcal U^{{\rm II}\dagger} \\
&
+\mathcal U^{\rm I}\big(\omega\mathcal N^{\rm I}+\mathcal H^{\rm I}\big)^{-1}\mathcal U^{{\rm I}\dagger} \;,
	\end{aligned}
\label{eq:Sigma_goal}
\end{equation}
with Hermitian $\mathcal H$ and $\mathcal N$, and the static self-energy
$\Sigma(\infty)=\Sigma_{\rm HF}[G]$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:Sigma_HF}.
At the level of the first-order Faddeev kernel, this is 
essentially the Faddeev random-phase approximation
(FRPA) of Barbieri and co-workers.
\cite{Barbieri2001,
barbieri2003extension,
barbieri2007quasiparticles,
Degroote2011}
The difference is that the effective Hamiltonian derived here
remains Hermitian as long as its metric is positive definite,
whereas the Faddeev eigenvalue problem of FRPA is non-Hermitian
and contains spurious solutions.\cite{Degroote2011}

\subsection{Resummation of the no-overlap own-channel Faddeev series}
\label{sec:depth1}
Our first goal is to evaluate the terms 
of Eq.~\eqref{eq:Faddeev_series_oc} up to first order in 
$\cK_\alpha$. 
It is convenient to rewrite these contributions as
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:one_pair_split}
\mathcal G+\sum_\alpha\cK_\alpha[F_\alpha]\,\mathcal G
=
\sum_\alpha\Big(\mathcal G+\cK_\alpha[F_\alpha]\,\mathcal G\Big)
-2\,\mathcal G \;,
\end{equation}
which can then be evaluated channel by channel. The terms 
inside the sum over $\alpha$ are interacting single-channel
three-particle propagators, for which we introduce the shorthand 
$Q_{\alpha}$, 
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:Q_alpha}
	Q_\alpha = \mathcal G+\cK_\alpha[F_\alpha]\,\mathcal G \;,
\end{equation} 
so that 
\begin{equation}
	\mathcal G+\sum_\alpha\cK_\alpha[F_\alpha]\,\mathcal G = 
	\sum_{\alpha} Q_{\alpha} - 2 \mathcal G \;.
	\label{eq:R_FLEX_def}
\end{equation}
Truncating the Faddeev series, Eq.~\eqref{eq:Faddeev_series_oc}, after
these terms is the one-shot FLEX approximation\cite{Bickers1989,Bickers2004}
(Sec.~\ref{sec:flex}).
We also require that these propagators obey a three-particle 
Dyson-type equation, 
\begin{equation}
	Q_\alpha = \mathcal{G} +\mathcal{G} \mathcal C_{\alpha} Q_\alpha \;,
\end{equation}
or
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:dyson_q}
Q_\alpha^{-1}
=
\mathcal G^{-1}-\mathcal C_\alpha(\omega) \;.
\nonumber
\end{equation}
This serves as a definition for $\mathcal C_\alpha$, as soon 
as we have specified the explicit form of $Q_\alpha$. 

Once we have these propagators at hand, we must evaluate the remaining 
terms in Eq.~\eqref{eq:Sigma_oc}, i.e. the terms of the form 
\begin{equation}
	\cK_{\alpha}[F_\alpha]
	\cK_{\beta}[F_\beta] \mathcal G \;, \quad \alpha \neq \beta \;, 
\end{equation}
and so on. We can rewrite these terms using the identity and
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Q_alpha}
\begin{equation}
	\begin{aligned}
	\cK_{\alpha}[F_\alpha]
	\cK_{\beta}[F_\beta] \mathcal G = &  
	\cK_{\alpha}[F_\alpha]\mathcal G \mathcal G^{-1}
	\cK_{\beta}[F_\beta] \mathcal G \\
	= & 
	\left(Q_\alpha - \mathcal G\right)	
	\mathcal G^{-1}
	\left(Q_\beta - \mathcal G\right) \;,
	\end{aligned}	
\end{equation} 
and therefore, Eq.~\eqref{eq:Faddeev_series_oc} becomes 
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
R_{\rm oc}(\omega)
={}&
\mathcal G+\sum_{\alpha}\big(Q_\alpha-\mathcal G\big)
+\sum_{\alpha\neq\beta}\big(Q_\alpha-\mathcal G\big)\mathcal G^{-1}\big(Q_\beta-\mathcal G\big)
\\
&+\sum_{\substack{\alpha\neq\beta\\\beta\neq\gamma}}
\big(Q_\alpha-\mathcal G\big)\mathcal G^{-1}\big(Q_\beta-\mathcal G\big)
\mathcal G^{-1}\big(Q_\gamma-\mathcal G\big)+\cdots \;.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:R_oc_omega}
\end{equation}
Notice that this step strictly requires the no-overlap 
approximation, otherwise we could not just insert 
$\mathcal G \mathcal G^{-1}$ in each term.

To resum the series, we collect all terms whose leftmost interacting
pair is $\alpha$ into a Faddeev component $R_\alpha$. Thus,
\begin{equation}
	R_{\rm oc}
	=
	\mathcal G+\sum_\alpha R_\alpha \;.
	\label{eq:R_oc_Faddeev_components}
\end{equation}
The constraint that two consecutive factors $Q_\alpha-\mathcal G$ must belong to
different pairs implies
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
	R_\alpha
	={}&
	\big(Q_\alpha-\mathcal G\big)\mathcal G^{-1}
	\left(
		\mathcal G+\sum_{\beta\neq\alpha}R_\beta
	\right)
	\\
	={}&
	\big(Q_\alpha-\mathcal G\big)\mathcal G^{-1}
	\big(R_{\rm oc}-R_\alpha\big) \;.
	\label{eq:Faddeev_components}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
By the Dyson equation Eq.~\eqref{eq:dyson_q}
and after some algebra, we obtain
\begin{equation}
	\big(\mathbf 1+Q_\alpha\mathcal C_\alpha\big)R_\alpha
	=
	Q_\alpha\mathcal C_\alpha R_{\rm oc} \;.
\end{equation}
Since
\begin{equation}
	\mathbf 1+Q_\alpha\mathcal C_\alpha
	=
	Q_\alpha
	\big(Q_\alpha^{-1}+\mathcal C_\alpha\big)
	=
	Q_\alpha\mathcal G^{-1} \;,
\end{equation}
it follows that
\begin{equation}
	R_\alpha
	=
	\mathcal G\mathcal C_\alpha R_{\rm oc} \;.
	\label{eq:R_alpha_Dyson}
\end{equation}
Summing Eq.~\eqref{eq:R_alpha_Dyson} over the three pair channels and
using Eq.~\eqref{eq:R_oc_Faddeev_components} gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
	R_{\rm oc}
	={}&
	\mathcal G
	+
	\mathcal G
	\left(
		\mathcal C_{\pp}
		+\mathcal C_{\eh}
		+\mathcal C_{\teh}
	\right)
	R_{\rm oc} \;.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Hence,
\begin{equation}
	R_{\rm oc}(\omega)
	=
	\big(
		\mathcal G^{-1}
		-\mathcal C_{\pp}
		-\mathcal C_{\eh}
		-\mathcal C_{\teh}
	\big)^{-1} \;,
\end{equation}
or, equivalently,
\begin{equation}
	\mathcal C_\alpha
	=
	\mathcal G^{-1}-Q_\alpha^{-1} \;,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
	R_{\rm oc}^{-1} =
	Q_{\pp}^{-1}
	+Q_{\eh}^{-1}
	+Q_{\teh}^{-1}
	-2\mathcal G^{-1} \;.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:Faddeev_inverse}
\end{equation}
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Faddeev_inverse} holds in the
space $\mathcal P$ of the $\pph$ and $\hhp$ configurations, where
$\mathcal G$ lives. To insert it into Eq.~\eqref{eq:Sigma_oc}, we still
need to know how the two bare interactions of Eq.~\eqref{eq:Sigma_oc},
at $t_1$ and $t_{1'}$ (the time arguments of the self-energy),
connect the orbitals $p$ and $q$ of
$\Sigma^c_{pq}$ to the three-line configurations of $R_{\rm oc}$. 

In the orbital basis, each line of Eq.~\eqref{eq:Sigma_oc} is a particle
or a hole, depending on its time direction. For $R=\mathcal G$, all
lines run between $t_{1'}$ and $t_1$, and only the configurations in
$\mathcal P$ contribute. In this case, 
the free propagation between two equal-time three-line states is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal G(\omega)=\big(\omega-K\big)^{-1} \;,
\label{eq:G_free}
\end{equation}
with $\omega+\ii0^+$ in the $\pph$ and $\omega-\ii0^+$ in the $\hhp$
block, and
\begin{equation}
K=K^{\rm II}\oplus\big(-K^{\rm I}\big) \;,
\nonumber
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
K^{\rm II}_{abi,cdj}
&=
\Delta_{abi}\,\delta_{ac}\delta_{bd}\delta_{ij} \;,
\\
K^{\rm I}_{ija,klb}
&=
-\Delta_{ija}\,\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl}\delta_{ab} \;,
\end{aligned}
\nonumber
\end{equation}
with the shorthand 
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{xyz}
=
\eps_x+\eps_y-\eps_z \;.
\label{eq:denom_triple}
\end{equation}
The first-order couplings of the one-particle states to the three-line
states are
\begin{equation}
U^{(1)}=\big(U^{{\rm II},(1)},\,U^{{\rm I},(1)}\big) \;,
\label{eq:K_combined}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
U^{{\rm II},(1)}_{p;abi}
&=
\mel{pi}{}{ab} \;,
\\
U^{{\rm I},(1)}_{p;ija}
&=
\mel{pa}{}{ij} \;.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:K_U1}
\end{equation}
In these expressions, $a<b$ and $i<j$.


These are not all configurations that the two bare interactions of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Sigma_oc} can reach. If the last interaction of a pair
$\alpha$ happens after $t_1$, or its first one before $t_{1'}$, the two
lines of the pair arrive at the vertex in reversed time direction. For
the $\pp$ pair, for instance, the vertex at $t_1$ then meets the three
holes $(kl;i)$ instead of $(ab;i)$.
Fig.~\ref{fig:reversed} shows the lowest-order example. In (a), the
$\pp$ rung lies between the two vertices, and all intermediate states are
$\pph$ configurations. In (b), the rung lies after $t_1$. At $t_1$, the
bare interaction $\mel{pi}{}{kl}$ removes the electrons $k$ and $l$,
\ie\ it creates the holes $k$ and $l$, which end at the later rung
together with the particles $a$ and $b$. The pair attached at $t_1$ is
thus the hole pair $(kl)$, and the rung enters with the static
denominator $\eps_k+\eps_l-\eps_a-\eps_b$. This is the coupling
$U^{(2)}$ of ADC(3).

The diagram in Fig.~\ref{fig:reversed}(b) and its mirror image, with the
rung before $t_{1'}$, are the GF3 terms of
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:pp_lt_plus} and \eqref{eq:pp_gt_plus}; the $\eh$
analogues are Eqs.~\eqref{eq:eh_lt_plus} and \eqref{eq:eh_gt_plus}. They
arise from the time orderings of the three-particle propagator in which
the pair lines run outside $[t_{1'},t_1]$. When the pair interactions
are resummed, reversed configurations appear in two places:
\begin{itemize}
\item At the two outer vertices, when the ladder or ring of a pair
continues beyond $t_1$ or before $t_{1'}$. These configurations are
folded into the couplings, $\mathcal U=U^{(1)}+\sum_\alpha\bar U^{(1)}_\alpha T_\alpha$,
and enter $\Sigma$ from third order on.
\item Inside $[t_{1'},t_1]$, within one pair, when the pair turns
backward in time and forward again. These orderings give the terms
$B_\alpha T_\alpha$ in $\mathcal H$ and the metric
$\mathcal N=\bI-\sum_\alpha T_\alpha^\dagger T_\alpha$, and enter
$\Sigma$ from fourth order on.
\end{itemize}
Between two different pairs, reversed configurations do not occur,
since the no-overlap approximation separates consecutive pairs by a
$\pph$ cut, the factor $\mathcal G^{-1}$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:R_oc_omega}.

We call $\mathcal P$ the $\pph$ and $\hhp$ configurations of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:G_free}, and $\bar{\mathcal P}_\alpha$ the reversed
configurations of the pair $\alpha$. Then:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\mathcal G$ lives on $\mathcal P$ only.
\item $Q_\alpha$ lives on $\mathcal P\oplus\bar{\mathcal P}_\alpha$. We
keep the symbol $Q_\alpha$ for its block on $\mathcal P$ and write
$[Q_\alpha]_{\bar{\mathcal P}_\alpha\mathcal P}$ for its block from
$\mathcal P$ to $\bar{\mathcal P}_\alpha$.
\item The bare interaction couples the orbital $p$ to $\mathcal P$
through $U^{(1)}$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:K_combined}, and to
$\bar{\mathcal P}_\alpha$ through antisymmetrized integrals
$\bar U^{(1)}_\alpha$.
\end{itemize}
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Sigma_oc} is therefore a sum over the blocks of $R$,
\begin{equation}
\Sigma^{c}(\omega)
=
\sum_{X,Y}
V_X\,\big[R(\omega)\big]_{XY}\,V_Y^\dagger \;,
\label{eq:Sigma_full_space}
\end{equation}
with $X,Y\in\{\mathcal P,\bar{\mathcal P}_{\pp},\bar{\mathcal P}_{\eh},\bar{\mathcal P}_{\teh}\}$,
$V_{\mathcal P}=U^{(1)}$ and $V_{\bar{\mathcal P}_\alpha}=\bar U^{(1)}_\alpha$.
For $R=\mathcal G$, only $X=Y=\mathcal P$ contributes, and
$\Sigma^c=U^{(1)}\mathcal G\,U^{(1)\dagger}=\Sigma^{(2)}$.

The block on $\mathcal P$ is $R_{\rm oc}$,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Faddeev_inverse}. The other blocks follow in three steps.

First, in Eq.~\eqref{eq:R_oc_omega}, consecutive factors are joined by
$\mathcal G^{-1}$, which lives on $\mathcal P$. Only the first factor of
a term can reach $\bar{\mathcal P}_\alpha$, and only if it is
$Q_\alpha-\mathcal G$. These terms make up $R_\alpha$,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Faddeev_components}, so that
\begin{equation}
\big[R\big]_{\bar{\mathcal P}_\alpha\mathcal P}
=
\big[Q_\alpha\big]_{\bar{\mathcal P}_\alpha\mathcal P}\,
\mathcal G^{-1}\big(R_{\rm oc}-R_\alpha\big) \;.
\label{eq:R_Pbar_row}
\end{equation}

Second, we eliminate $R_\alpha$. Adding $R_{\rm oc}-R_\alpha$ to both
sides of Eq.~\eqref{eq:Faddeev_components} gives
\begin{equation}
R_{\rm oc}
=
Q_\alpha\,\mathcal G^{-1}\big(R_{\rm oc}-R_\alpha\big) \;,
\nonumber
\end{equation}
\ie\
\begin{equation}
\mathcal G^{-1}\big(R_{\rm oc}-R_\alpha\big)
=
Q_\alpha^{-1}R_{\rm oc} \;.
\nonumber
\end{equation}
Inserted into Eq.~\eqref{eq:R_Pbar_row},
\begin{equation}
\big[R\big]_{\bar{\mathcal P}_\alpha\mathcal P}
=
\big[Q_\alpha\big]_{\bar{\mathcal P}_\alpha\mathcal P}\,
Q_\alpha^{-1}\,R_{\rm oc} \;.
\nonumber
\end{equation}

Third, the same argument for the last factor of a term gives the
columns, and both together the corners,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\big[R\big]_{\mathcal P\bar{\mathcal P}_\beta}
&=
R_{\rm oc}\,Q_\beta^{-1}\big[Q_\beta\big]_{\mathcal P\bar{\mathcal P}_\beta} \;,
\\
\big[R\big]_{\bar{\mathcal P}_\alpha\bar{\mathcal P}_\beta}
&=
\big[Q_\alpha\big]_{\bar{\mathcal P}_\alpha\mathcal P}\,Q_\alpha^{-1}\,
R_{\rm oc}\,
Q_\beta^{-1}\big[Q_\beta\big]_{\mathcal P\bar{\mathcal P}_\beta} \;.
\end{aligned}
\nonumber
\end{equation}

All blocks of $R$ are thus $R_{\rm oc}$, multiplied from the left by
$[Q_\alpha]_{\bar{\mathcal P}_\alpha\mathcal P}Q_\alpha^{-1}$ for a row
in $\bar{\mathcal P}_\alpha$ and from the right by its adjoint for a
column in $\bar{\mathcal P}_\beta$. Inserted into
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Sigma_full_space}, the sum over the blocks factorizes,
\begin{equation}
\Sigma^{c}(\omega)
=
\mathcal U\,R_{\rm oc}(\omega)\,\mathcal U^\dagger \;,
\nonumber
\end{equation}
with the couplings
\begin{equation}
\mathcal U
=
U^{(1)}+\sum_\alpha\bar U^{(1)}_\alpha
\big[Q_\alpha\big]_{\bar{\mathcal P}_\alpha\mathcal P}\,Q_\alpha^{-1} \;.
\label{eq:U_general}
\end{equation}
The reversed configurations are thus folded into the couplings, and
$R_{\rm oc}$ is needed only on $\mathcal P$. It remains to specify the
three-particle propagators in the $\pp$ and $\eh$ channels, which we do
in the next section.


\subsection{Pair and three-particle propagators}
\label{sec:propagators}
The Faddeev series correlates two particles at a time, while the third one
is a spectator. The three-particle propagators $Q_\alpha$ of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Faddeev_inverse} therefore follow from two-body pair problems
and the free propagation of the spectator.
The two-body problems are well known
for the herein used first-order Faddeev kernel.\cite{orlando2023three}
The $\eh$ propagator is obtained by solving the TDHF equations Eq.~\eqref{eq:TDHF}, and the 
$\teh$ propagator follows by crossing. Analogously, the $\pp$ propagator follows from the 
solution of the ppRPA, Eq.~\eqref{eq:ppRPA}.
Coupled to two external orbitals, they give the two-body pair vertices $M^{\eh}_{pq,n}$ and
$M^{\pp}_{pq,n}$ of the reducible vertices, Eqs.~\eqref{eq:Phi_eh_phonons}
and \eqref{eq:Phi_pp_phonons}.

With one interacting pair, the three-particle propagator is the pair
propagator times the free propagation of the spectator. For the $\pp$
pair with spectator $r$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
Q_{\pp;xyr,uwr}(\omega)
={}&
n_r\sum_{n\in N+2}
\frac{Z^n_{xy}Z^{n}_{uw}}{\omega+\eps_r-\Omega^{+}_n+\ii0^+}
\\
&+
\bar n_r\sum_{m\in N-2}
\frac{Z^m_{xy}Z^{m}_{uw}}{\omega+\eps_r-\Omega^{-}_m-\ii0^+} \;,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:Q12_full}
\end{equation}
and for the $\eh$ pair with spectator $s$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
Q_{\eh;xrs,yus}(\omega)
={}&
\bar n_s\sum_{\Omega_n>0}
\frac{Z^n_{xr}Z^{n}_{yu}}{\omega-\eps_s-\Omega_n+\ii0^+}
\\
&+
n_s\sum_{\Omega_n>0}
\frac{\tilde Z^n_{xr}\tilde Z^{n}_{yu}}{\omega-\eps_s+\Omega_n-\ii0^+} \;.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:Q31_full}
\end{equation}
Here, $Z^n_{xy}$ are the components of the two-body eigenvectors of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:pair_eigenproblem} on the pair indices $(xy)$, forward ($X$)
or backward ($Y$). $Q_{\teh}$ follows from $Q_{\eh}$ with the lines 1 and 2
exchanged. For a particle spectator only the forward $\eh$ excitations
$Z_n$ survive, for a hole spectator only the backward ones, $\tilde Z_n$.

Both propagators are block diagonal in $\pph\oplus\hhp$. The occupation
factors select the first terms of Eqs.~\eqref{eq:Q12_full} and
\eqref{eq:Q31_full} in block II, where the spectator of the $\pp$ pair is
the hole $i$ and the spectators of the $\eh$ and $\teh$ pairs are the
particles $b$ and $a$. The second term is selected in block I, where the
spectator of the $\pp$ pair is the particle $a$ and the spectators of the
$\eh$ and $\teh$ pairs are the holes $j$ and $i$. Between the
configurations of $\pph\oplus\hhp$, Eqs.~\eqref{eq:Q12_full} and
\eqref{eq:Q31_full} read, in matrix notation,
\begin{equation}
Q_{\pp}(\omega)
=
Z^{\rm II}_{\pp}\big(\omega+\eps_i-\boldsymbol\Omega^{+}\big)^{-1}Z^{{\rm II}\dagger}_{\pp}
\;\oplus\;
Z^{\rm I}_{\pp}\big(\omega+\eps_a-\boldsymbol\Omega^{-}\big)^{-1}Z^{{\rm I}\dagger}_{\pp} \;,
\label{eq:Q12}
\end{equation}
with the $N+2$ solutions in block II and the $N-2$ solutions in block I, and
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
Q_{\eh}(\omega)
={}&
Z_{\eh}\big(\omega-\eps_b-\boldsymbol\Omega\big)^{-1}Z_{\eh}^{\dagger}
\;\oplus\;
Z_{\eh}\big(\omega-\eps_j+\boldsymbol\Omega\big)^{-1}Z_{\eh}^{\dagger} \;,
\\
Q_{\teh}(\omega)
={}&
Z_{\teh}\big(\omega-\eps_a-\boldsymbol\Omega\big)^{-1}Z_{\teh}^{\dagger}
\;\oplus\;
Z_{\teh}\big(\omega-\eps_i+\boldsymbol\Omega\big)^{-1}Z_{\teh}^{\dagger} \;.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:Q31}
\end{equation}
Here, $Z_\alpha$ contains the components of the pair eigenvectors on the
configurations of the block, $(ab;i)$, $(ia;b)$ in block II and $(ij;a)$,
$(ia;j)$ in block I. The components on the remaining configurations of
the pair, $\bar Z_\alpha$, enter only at the external vertices,
Sec.~\ref{sec:ext_vertices}.

\subsubsection{Amplitude Form of Propagators}
It is useful to replace the propagators above by a form
that uses CC-amplitudes. This is possible as
the RPA eigenvalue problem in each channel
can equivalently be expressed as
the Riccati equation (see Appendix~\ref{app:Riccati}, and
Refs.~\citenum{tolle2026connection,Scuseria2013,Shepherd2014})
\begin{equation}
B^\dagger+DT+TA
+TBT
=0 \;.
\label{eq:Riccati}
\end{equation}
The amplitudes map the forward components on the backward components,
\begin{equation}
Y
=
T X \;.
\label{eq:T_YX}
\end{equation}

Eq.~\eqref{eq:Riccati} is
the ring-CCD ($\eh$) and ladder-CCD ($\pp$) amplitude equation of the
channel.\cite{Scuseria2013} The relations between $GW$, the RPA and
(equation-of-motion) coupled cluster theory are analyzed in
Refs.~\citenum{Lange2018,QuinteroMonsebaiz2022,Tolle2023,tolle2026connection}.
Each channel introduces its own set of
amplitudes, but they remain decoupled.
Full CCD couples them through the
crossed-ring and mosaic terms of the amplitude
equation.\cite{Shepherd2014}
These couplings play the role of the crossed channels
that the own-channel approximation neglects.

One can then show the relations
\begin{equation}
X\boldsymbol\Omega X^{-1}
=
A+B T \;.
\label{eq:ZOmegaZ}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
XX^\dagger
=
\big(\bI-T^\dagger T\big)^{-1} \;.
\label{eq:X_norm}
\end{equation}

These relations are readily upgraded to the three-particle space 
in each channel,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\bar Z_\alpha
&=
T_\alpha Z_\alpha \;,
\\
Z_\alpha\boldsymbol\Omega_\alpha Z_\alpha^{-1}
&=
A_\alpha+B_\alpha T_\alpha \;,
\\
Z_\alpha Z_\alpha^\dagger
&=
\big(\bI-T_\alpha^\dagger T_\alpha\big)^{-1} \;,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:three_line_T}
\end{equation}
with $\boldsymbol\Omega_{\pp}=\boldsymbol\Omega^+$ and
$\boldsymbol\Omega_{\eh}=\boldsymbol\Omega_{\teh}=\boldsymbol\Omega$.


For block II (block I follows equivalently),
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:Q12} and \eqref{eq:Q31} are
$Q_\alpha=Z_\alpha(\omega-s_\alpha-\boldsymbol\Omega_\alpha)^{-1}Z_\alpha^\dagger$,
with the spectator energies
\begin{equation}
s_{\pp}=-\eps_i \;,
\qquad
s_{\eh}=\eps_b \;,
\qquad
s_{\teh}=\eps_a \;.
\label{eq:s_alpha}
\end{equation}
With Eq.~\eqref{eq:three_line_T}, it can be written as
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
Q_\alpha(\omega)
={}&
Z_\alpha\big(\omega-s_\alpha-\boldsymbol\Omega_\alpha\big)^{-1}Z_\alpha^{-1}
\,Z_\alpha Z_\alpha^\dagger
\\
={}&
\big[Z_\alpha\big(\omega-s_\alpha-\boldsymbol\Omega_\alpha\big)Z_\alpha^{-1}\big]^{-1}
Z_\alpha Z_\alpha^\dagger
\\
={}&
\big(\omega-s_\alpha-Z_\alpha\boldsymbol\Omega_\alpha Z_\alpha^{-1}\big)^{-1}
Z_\alpha Z_\alpha^\dagger
\\
={}&
\big(\omega-s_\alpha-A_\alpha-B_\alpha T_\alpha\big)^{-1}
\big(\bI-T_\alpha^\dagger T_\alpha\big)^{-1} \;.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:Q_blockII_T}
\end{equation}
Next, the spectator energy and the orbital energies in
$A_\alpha$ add
up to $K$, and the interaction part of $A_\alpha$ is the pair
interaction $C^{\rm II}_\alpha$. For the $\pp$ pair in the configuration
$(ab;i)$ and the $\eh$ pair in $(ia;b)$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
s_{\pp}+A^{\pp}_{ab,cd}
&=
\big(\eps_a+\eps_b-\eps_i\big)\delta_{ac}\delta_{bd}+\mel{ab}{}{cd} \;,
\\
s_{\eh}+A^{\eh}_{ia,jc}
&=
\big(\eps_a+\eps_b-\eps_i\big)\delta_{ac}\delta_{ij}+\mel{aj}{}{ic} \;,
\end{aligned}
\nonumber
\end{equation}
\ie\ $s_\alpha+A_\alpha=K+C^{\rm II}_\alpha$, and
\begin{equation}
Q_\alpha(\omega)
=
\big(\omega-K-C^{\rm II}_\alpha-B_\alpha T_\alpha\big)^{-1}
\big(\bI-T_\alpha^\dagger T_\alpha\big)^{-1} \;.
\nonumber
\end{equation}
After a similar treatment for block I, and with
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
C_\alpha
={}&
C^{\rm II}_\alpha\oplus\big(-C^{\rm I}_\alpha\big) \;,
\\
B_\alpha T_\alpha
={}&
B_\alpha^{\rm II}T_\alpha^{\rm II}\oplus
\big(-B_\alpha^{\rm I}T_\alpha^{\rm I}\big) \;,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:CB_blocks}
\end{equation}
both blocks combine into
\begin{equation}
Q_\alpha(\omega)
=
\big(\omega-K-C_\alpha-B_\alpha T_\alpha\big)^{-1}
\big(\bI-T_\alpha^\dagger T_\alpha\big)^{-1} \;.
\label{eq:Q_alpha_inverse}
\end{equation}
In the Tamm--Dancoff limit, $B_\alpha=0$ and $T_\alpha=0$, and
\begin{equation}
Q_\alpha(\omega)
=
\big(\omega-K-C_\alpha\big)^{-1} \;,
\label{eq:Q_TDA}
\end{equation}
where $K+C_\alpha$ is the Tamm--Dancoff Hamiltonian of the pair,
shifted by the spectator energy.

\subsection{External vertices and effective Hamiltonian}
\label{sec:ext_vertices}
We are now in a position to state the central result of 
these derivations.
Inserting the amplitude form of $Q_\alpha$ defined by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Q_alpha_inverse} into Eq.~\eqref{eq:Faddeev_inverse},
we obtain
\begin{equation}
	\begin{aligned}
R_{\rm oc}^{-1}(\omega)
= &
\omega\, \mathcal N -\mathcal H \;, \\
\mathcal H 
= &
K+C
+\sum_\alpha B_\alpha T_\alpha
 \\
&-\sum_\alpha T_\alpha^\dagger T_\alpha
\big(K+C_\alpha+B_\alpha T_\alpha\big) \;,
\\
\mathcal N
= &
\bI-\sum_\alpha T_\alpha^\dagger T_\alpha \;,
\label{eq:depth1_amplitudes}
\end{aligned} 
\end{equation}
in both blocks. $\mathcal H$ and the metric $\mathcal N$ are Hermitian
and block diagonal,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal N=\mathcal N^{\rm II}\oplus\mathcal N^{\rm I} \;,
\qquad
\mathcal H=\mathcal H^{\rm II}\oplus\big(-\mathcal H^{\rm I}\big) \;.
\nonumber
\end{equation}

For the couplings, Eq.~\eqref{eq:U_general}, we need the blocks of the
three-particle propagators between $\bar{\mathcal P}_\alpha$ and
$\mathcal P$. With all rows of the pair eigenvectors,
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:Q12_full} and \eqref{eq:Q31_full} have the block
$\bar Z_\alpha(\omega-s_\alpha-\boldsymbol\Omega_\alpha)^{-1}Z_\alpha^\dagger$
between $\bar{\mathcal P}_\alpha$ and $\mathcal P$. With
$\bar Z_\alpha=T_\alpha Z_\alpha$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:three_line_T},
\begin{equation}
\big[Q_\alpha\big]_{\bar{\mathcal P}_\alpha\mathcal P}
=
T_\alpha Z_\alpha\big(\omega-s_\alpha-\boldsymbol\Omega_\alpha\big)^{-1}Z_\alpha^\dagger
=
T_\alpha Q_\alpha \;,
\nonumber
\end{equation}
so that Eq.~\eqref{eq:U_general} becomes
$\mathcal U=U^{(1)}+\sum_\alpha\bar U^{(1)}_\alpha T_\alpha$. With all indices 
given explicitly, the couplings become
\begin{align}
\mathcal U
={}&
\big(\mathcal U^{\rm II},\,\mathcal U^{\rm I}\big)
=
U^{(1)}+\sum_\alpha \bar U^{(1)}_{\alpha}\,T_\alpha \;,
\nonumber\\
\mathcal U^{\rm II}_{p;abi}
={}&
\mel{pi}{}{ab}
+ \hat P^-_{ab}\sum_{cj}\mel{pc}{}{jb}\,T^{\eh}_{jc,ia}
+\sum_{k<l}\mel{pi}{}{kl}\,T^{\pp}_{kl,ab} \;,
\nonumber\\
\mathcal U^{\rm I}_{p;ija}
={}&
\mel{pa}{}{ij}
-\hat P^-_{ij}\sum_{kb}\mel{pk}{}{bj}\,T^{\eh}_{ib,ka}
+\sum_{b<c}\mel{pa}{}{bc}\,T^{\pp}_{ij,bc} \;.
\label{eq:Ueff_depth1}
\end{align}
Here, $\bar U^{(1)}_{\alpha}$ couples to the backward configurations of the
pair $\alpha$ (all but the first terms). 
In contrast to ADC(3), the couplings
contain the channel-restricted CCD amplitudes instead of the
first-order doubles ones. With the substitutions
\begin{equation}
	T^{\eh}_{jc,ia} \rightarrow t^{ac}_{ij}
\end{equation}
and 
\begin{equation}
	T^{\pp}_{kl,ab} \rightarrow t^{ab}_{kl} \;,
\end{equation}
these are the ADC(3) couplings, that describe the first-order coupling 
of the backward amplitudes to single-particle states.

Projected onto $a<b$ and
$i<j$, and with
\begin{equation}
\omega\mathcal N-\mathcal H
=
\mathcal N^{1/2}\big(\omega-H_{\rm eff}\big)\mathcal N^{1/2}
\nonumber
\end{equation}
for positive definite $\mathcal N$, we obtain the self-energy
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Sigma^{c}(\omega)
={}&
\mathcal U\,R_{\rm oc}(\omega)\,\mathcal U^{\dagger}
=
\mathcal U\big(\omega\,\mathcal N-\mathcal H\big)^{-1}
\mathcal U^{\dagger}
\\
={}&
\left(\mathcal U\,\mathcal N^{-1/2}\right)
\big(\omega-H_{\rm eff}\big)^{-1}
\left(\mathcal U\,\mathcal N^{-1/2}\right)^\dagger \;,
\\
H_{\rm eff}
={}&
\mathcal N^{-1/2}\mathcal H\,\mathcal N^{-1/2} \;,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:Heff_depth1}
\end{equation}
which, written out block by block, is Eq.~\eqref{eq:Sigma_goal}.
The quasiparticle energies are the eigenvalues of the corresponding
Hermitian Dyson supermatrix
\begin{equation}
\begin{pmatrix}
h_0+\Sigma(\infty) & \mathcal U\,\mathcal N^{-1/2}
\\
\mathcal N^{-1/2}\,\mathcal U^\dagger & H_{\rm eff}
\end{pmatrix} ,
\nonumber
\end{equation}
with $h_0$ the one-body Hamiltonian of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Martin_Schwinger_general}.
In block II, the matrix elements of Eq.~\eqref{eq:depth1_amplitudes} are
\begin{align}
\mathcal H_{abi,cdj}
={}&
K^{\rm II}_{abi,cdj}+C^{\rm II}_{abi,cdj}
+\delta_{ij}\,h^{\pp}_{ab,cd}
\nonumber\\
&+\hat P^-_{ab}\hat P^-_{cd}\,\delta_{bd}\,h^{\eh}_{ia,jc} \;,
\nonumber\\
\mathcal N_{abi,cdj}
={}&
\delta_{ac}\delta_{bd}\delta_{ij}
-\delta_{ij}\sum_{k<l}T^{\pp}_{kl,ab}T^{\pp}_{kl,cd}
\nonumber\\
&-\hat P^-_{ab}\hat P^-_{cd}\,\delta_{bd}\sum_{ke}T^{\eh}_{ke,ia}T^{\eh}_{ke,jc} \;,
\label{eq:HN_explicit}
\end{align}
with
\begin{align}
h^{\pp}_{ab,cd}
={}&
\sum_{k<l}\Big[\mel{ab}{}{kl}\,T^{\pp}_{kl,cd}+T^{\pp}_{kl,ab}\,\mel{kl}{}{cd}\Big]
\nonumber\\
&+\sum_{k<l}\sum_{m<n}T^{\pp}_{kl,ab}
\big(D^{\pp}_{kl,mn}+\eps_i\,\delta_{km}\delta_{ln}\big)T^{\pp}_{mn,cd} \;,
\nonumber\\
h^{\eh}_{ia,jc}
={}&
\sum_{ke}\Big[\mel{ae}{}{ik}\,T^{\eh}_{ke,jc}+T^{\eh}_{ke,ia}\,\mel{ce}{}{jk}\Big]
\nonumber\\
&+\sum_{ke}\sum_{lf}T^{\eh}_{ke,ia}
\big(A^{\eh}_{ke,lf}-\eps_b\,\delta_{kl}\delta_{ef}\big)T^{\eh}_{lf,jc} \;.
\label{eq:h_pair}
\end{align}

\subsection{Phonon form}
\label{sec:phonon_form}
It is instructive to write the effective 
Hamiltonian in terms of the pair excitations (phonons). 
We do this for block II; block I follows in the same way with the block-I parts of Eqs.~\eqref{eq:Q12} and \eqref{eq:Q31}. By Eqs.~\eqref{eq:Faddeev_inverse} and \eqref{eq:depth1_amplitudes},
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\omega\mathcal N-\mathcal H
={}&
\sum_\alpha Q_\alpha^{-1}-2\mathcal G^{-1} \;,
\\
Q_\alpha^{-1}
={}&
Z_\alpha^{-\dagger}\big(\omega-s_\alpha-\boldsymbol\Omega_\alpha\big)Z_\alpha^{-1} \;.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:Rinv_phonons}
\end{equation}
Here, $\boldsymbol\Omega_\alpha$ is the diagonal matrix of the pair
energies, $\boldsymbol\Omega^+$ for the $\pp$ pair and $\boldsymbol\Omega$
for the $\eh$ and $\teh$ pairs, and $s_\alpha$ is the spectator energy, Eq.~\eqref{eq:s_alpha}.
Its $\omega$-linear part gives $\mathcal N$, its constant part $\mathcal H$.
With Eqs.~\eqref{eq:Q31}~and~\eqref{eq:Q12},
as well as the normalization in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Q_alpha_inverse}, the quantities 
appearing in the self-energy defined by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Heff_depth1}
are
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:HNU_phonons}
	\begin{aligned}
\mathcal N
= &
\bI+\sum_\alpha\Big
[\big(Z_\alpha Z_\alpha^\dagger\big)^{-1}
-\bI\Big] \;, \\
\mathcal H
= &
K^{\rm II}+C^{\rm II}
+\sum_\alpha\Big[Z_\alpha^{-\dagger}\big(\boldsymbol\Omega_\alpha+s_\alpha\big)Z_\alpha^{-1}
-K^{\rm II}-C_\alpha\Big] \;, \\
\mathcal U
= &
U^{(1)}+\sum_\alpha\Big[M_\alpha Z_\alpha^{-1}-U^{(1)}\Big] \;,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with the three-body couplings of the pairs
$M_\alpha=U^{(1)}Z_\alpha+\bar U^{(1)}_{\alpha}\bar Z_\alpha$. Their entries are the
two-body vertices of Eqs.~\eqref{eq:Phi_eh_phonons} and
\eqref{eq:Phi_pp_phonons}, with the spectator as second orbital index,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
M_{\pp,p;ni}
&=
M^{\pp}_{pi,n} \;,
\\
M_{\eh,p;nb}
&=
-M^{\eh}_{bp,n} \;.
\end{aligned}
\nonumber
\end{equation}

\section{Relations to various schemes to construct electronic self-energies}

\subsection{Tamm--Dancoff limit and ADC(3)}
\label{sec:adc3_tda}
In the Tamm--Dancoff limit, $B_\alpha=0$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:Riccati} gives
$T_\alpha=0$, and the three-particle propagators reduce to
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Q_TDA}. Eq.~\eqref{eq:Faddeev_inverse} then gives
\begin{equation}
R_{\rm oc}(\omega)
=
\big(\omega-K-C\big)^{-1}
=
\big(\omega-K^{\rm II}-C^{\rm II}\big)^{-1}\oplus
\big(\omega+K^{\rm I}+C^{\rm I}\big)^{-1} \;,
\label{eq:R_TDA}
\end{equation}
with $C=C_{\pp}+C_{\eh}+C_{\teh}$, \ie\ $\mathcal N=\bI$ and
$\mathcal H=K+C$. Since $T_\alpha=0$, the couplings reduce to
$\mathcal U=U^{(1)}$, and the self-energy is that of
ADC(2)-X,\cite{von1984computational} also known as the 
2ph-TDA,\cite{Cederbaum1977,
cederbaum1977theoretical,
schirmer1978two} or multi-channel Dyson equation (MCDE).
\cite{Riva2023,Riva2024,Riva2025}

ADC(3) is not the strict Tamm--Dancoff limit. Instead, 
the amplitudes are restricted to second order,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal U
=
U^{(1)}+U^{(2)}+O(v^3) \;.
\end{aligned}
\nonumber
\end{equation}
The TDA-limit [ADC(2)-X] and ADC(3) differ, in principle,
only in the couplings. However, since ADC(3) is usually constructed to 
be third order complete, $\Sigma(\infty)$ is chosen to be the 
third-order static self-energy, while ADC(2)-X keeps it at the 
Hartree--Fock level. For molecules, ADC(2)-X is a very crude approximation anyway, with 
large errors for charged excitations, while offering no tangible numerical 
advantage over ADC(3). Changing the static part has a non-negligible effect, 
but its accuracy remains poor either way.\cite{Keizer2026}

Recent work has used CC-amplitudes in ADC(3).
The CCD-ADC(3) from Ref.~\citenum{forster2026third} 
uses exactly Eq.~\eqref{eq:Ueff_depth1}, but solves the 
full CCD equations to couple all channel amplitudes. However,
the effective Hamiltonian is kept at the TDA level. Their 
EN-ADC(3) is the linearized, diagonal version of the ladder-CCD couplings.

Superficially, this scheme seems related to recent work by \citet{Coveney2025}, 
since also Coveney\cite{Coveney2025} uses the full CCD amplitudes. However, 
his construction differs in key points.
His Hamiltonian is constructed in the CC similarity transform structure.
It does not contain any metric and is non-Hermitian due to the similarity transformation.
It also contains a line dressing that ADC(4) contains
only at 4th order.

\subsection{Connections to (vertex-corrected) \texorpdfstring{$GW$}{GW}}
The $GW$ approximation and other schemes derived in the framework of
Hedin's equations\cite{Hedin1965} are formulated in the $\eh$ channel only.
It is therefore instructive to retain only the $\eh$ pair in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:HNU_phonons}. The quantities entering the 
effective Hamiltonian then simplify to  
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:GW_phonon_HNU}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal N^{\rm II}_{iab,jcd}
&=
\big[(Z_{\eh}Z_{\eh}^\dagger)^{-1}\big]_{ia,jc}\,\delta_{bd} \;,
\\
\mathcal H^{\rm II}_{iab,jcd}
&=
\big[Z_{\eh}^{-\dagger}\big(\boldsymbol\Omega_{\eh}+\eps_b\big)Z_{\eh}^{-1}\big]_{ia,jc}\,\delta_{bd} \;,
\\
\mathcal U^{\rm II}_{p;iab}
&=
\sum_n M^{\rm II}_{\eh,p;nb}\,\big[Z_{\eh}^{-1}\big]_{n,ia} \;,
\\
\mathcal N^{\rm I}_{iaj,kbl}
&=
\big[(Z_{\eh}Z_{\eh}^\dagger)^{-1}\big]_{ia,kb}\,\delta_{jl} \;,
\\
\mathcal H^{\rm I}_{iaj,kbl}
&=
\big[Z_{\eh}^{-\dagger}\big(\boldsymbol\Omega_{\eh}-\eps_j\big)Z_{\eh}^{-1}\big]_{ia,kb}\,\delta_{jl} \;,
\\
\mathcal U^{\rm I}_{p;iaj}
&=
\sum_n M^{\rm I}_{\eh,p;nj}\,\big[Z_{\eh}^{-1}\big]_{n,ia} \;,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where we have specified the indices explicitly for later reference. In
block II, the pair $(ia)$ has the spectator particle $b$, in block I
the spectator hole $j$, and the pair and its spectator are independent.
With Eq.~\eqref{eq:Sigma_goal},
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\omega\,\mathcal N^{\rm II}-\mathcal H^{\rm II}
&=
Z_{\eh}^{-\dagger}\big(\omega-\eps_b-\boldsymbol\Omega_{\eh}\big)Z_{\eh}^{-1} \;,
\\
\omega\,\mathcal N^{\rm I}+\mathcal H^{\rm I}
&=
Z_{\eh}^{-\dagger}\big(\omega-\eps_j+\boldsymbol\Omega_{\eh}\big)Z_{\eh}^{-1} \;,
\end{aligned}
\nonumber
\end{equation}
and the self-energy becomes
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:GW_phonon_selfenergy}
\Sigma_{\eh,pq}(\omega)
=
\sum_{nb}
\frac{M^{\rm II}_{\eh,p;nb}\,M^{{\rm II}*}_{\eh,q;nb}}{\omega-\eps_b-\Omega_n}
+\sum_{nj}
\frac{M^{\rm I}_{\eh,p;nj}\,M^{{\rm I}*}_{\eh,q;nj}}{\omega-\eps_j+\Omega_n} \;.
\end{equation}
The $\eh$-first-order reducible vertices have been
defined in Eq.~\eqref{eq:Phi_eh_phonons}, with $M_{\eh,p;nb}=-M^{\eh}_{bp,n}$.
The self-energy Eq.~\eqref{eq:GW_phonon_selfenergy}
is completely equivalent to the PSD completion of
the $\Sigma^{\text{TDHF}}$ self-energy\cite{Forster2024} from
Ref.~\citenum{Pavlyukh2026}, therein denoted as $\Sigma^\text{PSDII}$. The 
only difference is that they construct
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Phi_eh_phonons} with a statically
screened TDHF kernel. This also illustrates the
interpretation of the PSD self-energy of
Ref.~\citenum{Pavlyukh2026} as a ``single-channel parquet''-type
approximation.

\subsubsection{Double counting}
It has been remarked in Ref.~\citenum{Pavlyukh2026} that $\Sigma^\text{PSDII}$ 
double counts the $\Sigma^{(2)}$ contribution.
This raises the natural question of how this can be the case, 
since the Faddeev-ADC(3) construction performed here is double-counting-free 
by construction. 

To see how the double counting of $\Sigma^{(2)}$ arises, we take
$v\to0$. Then $Z_{\eh}\to\bI$, $\bar Z_{\eh}\to0$, $\Omega_n\to\eps_a-\eps_i$,
$\mathcal N\to\bI$, $\mathcal H^{\rm II}\to\Delta_{abi}$,
$\mathcal H^{\rm I}\to-\Delta_{ija}$, and the couplings become those of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:K_U1}, $\mathcal U^{\rm II}_{p;iab}\to\mel{pi}{}{ab}$ and
$\mathcal U^{\rm I}_{p;iaj}\to\mel{pa}{}{ij}$.
Eq.~\eqref{eq:GW_phonon_selfenergy} gives
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:Sigma31_GF2}
\begin{aligned}
\Sigma_{\eh,pq}
\to{}&
\sum_{abi}
\frac{\mel{pi}{}{ab}\mel{ab}{}{qi}}{\omega+\eps_i-\eps_a-\eps_b}
+\sum_{ija}
\frac{\mel{pa}{}{ij}\mel{ij}{}{qa}}{\omega+\eps_a-\eps_i-\eps_j}
\\ = &
2\,\Sigma^{(2)}_{pq} \;.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Every $\pph$ and $\hhp$ configuration
appears twice: first, as the pair $(ia)$ with spectator $b$
and then as the pair $(ib)$ with spectator $a$.
Of course, it would be possible to subtract this contribution
\begin{equation}
\Sigma^{c}
=
\Sigma_{\eh}-\Sigma^{(2)} \;,
\label{eq:eh_dc}
\end{equation}
as is done in the FLEX approximation\cite{Bickers1989,Bickers1989b,Bickers2004,MarieLoos2025}
and in the several flavors of combinations of the 
$GW + T$-matrix approximations.\cite{Springer1998,Zhukov2004}
This would however defeat the 
purpose of the PSD-construction, as the resulting self-energy 
is then not a sum of squares anymore and hence not PSD. 


\subsubsection{$GW$}

Constructing Eq.~\eqref{eq:Phi_eh_phonons} with the standard (direct
electron--hole) RPA, and keeping only the direct vertex, gives the $GW$
approximation, 
\begin{equation}
\Sigma_{GW}(\omega)
=
M^{\rm D}_{\eh}
\big[
\omega-s_{\eh}-\boldsymbol\Omega_{\eh}
\big]^{-1}
M^{\rm D^{\dagger}} \;.
\label{eq:GW}
\end{equation}
Written as an eigenvalue problem in the $\pph$ and $\hhp$ spaces, this is
the upfolded form of $GW$.\cite{Bintrim2021,Monino2023}
The functional form of the $GW$ self-energy is retained independent of the 
linear-response problem that is solved to construct the $M^{\rm D}_{\eh}$ and to 
obtain $\boldsymbol\Omega_{\eh}$. Usually, the direct RPA is used, which is 
the TDHF form of the RPA without the direct electron--hole interaction. 
In Hedin's framework, the $GW$ approximation naturally goes together with the RPA, 
since both arise from the lowest-order vertex, $\Gamma=1$, when the non-interacting propagator is 
calculated at the Hartree level. In practice, the non-interacting Green's function is 
almost always chosen to be a (generalized) Kohn--Sham one.\cite{Hybertsen1986} 
This, together with the availability of TDDFT codes, has motivated the use of 
TDDFT to construct Eq.~\eqref{eq:GW} in $GW$ calculations for solids,
\cite{Hybertsen1987,Northrup1987,Northrup1989,DelSole1994,Morris2007,Chen2015,Sharma2011,Schmidt2017,Ma2019,Olsen2019}
but also in molecules,\cite{Tiago2006,vanSetten2013,Hung2016,Hung2017,Ma2019}
and it is to be noted that local vertices from the same kernels have also been placed in the
self-energy.\cite{Northrup1987,Northrup1989,Mahan1989,DelSole1994,Shirley1996,Hindgren1997,Takada2001,Bruneval2005,Tiago2006,Morris2007,Romaniello2009,Hung2016,Hung2017,Schmidt2017,Hellgren2018,Ma2019,Olsen2019,Tal2021,Kutepov2022,Lorin2023,Tal2024}
More recently, the solid state community has used statically screened TDHF, i.e. the usual 
BSE. This approach is quite successful as it
lowers the overestimated band gaps of quasiparticle
self-consistent $GW$ in solids.\cite{Shishkin2007,Cunningham2018,Sakakibara2020,Cunningham2023}
For molecules, this approach significantly worsens ionization potentials\cite{Forster2025}
compared to standard qs$GW$.
For molecules, TDHF screening has been tested,\cite{Bruneval2021,Patterson2024}
and even polarizabilities from EOM-CCSD.\cite{Lewis2019}
Combined with HF Green's functions, they somewhat improve over the RPA. However, 
they are unbalanced and miss the corresponding vertex in the self-energy.\cite{Forster2024}

\subsubsection{$\Sigma^{\text{TDHF}}$}
This naturally motivates the choice of the $\Sigma^{\text{TDHF}}$ self-energy
that has been introduced by 
\citet{Maggio2017} and investigated later by several authors.
\cite{Vlcek2019,MejutoZaera2021,Vacondio2024,Bruneval2024,Patterson2024, Forster2025}
A first-order vertex constructed to satisfy the Ward identity had been
used before in self-consistent calculations for atoms and small
molecules.\cite{Kuwahara2014,Kuwahara2016}
It operates in Hedin's framework. It solves the BSE for the $\eh$ channel
with the complete first-order vertex (TDHF), and uses the same vertex in the 
self-energy. This self-energy is the natural choice if based on a HF non-interacting reference, 
since the TDHF vertex is the functional derivative of the HF self-energy with respect to $G$.

Hedin's formalism operates with a self-energy with two vertices, 
but one of them stays bare at first order. The resulting self-energies cannot be written 
in a Hermitian (PSD) resolvent form, which has been the motivation to introduce the PSD construction
from above\cite{Pavlyukh2026} in the first place. Of course, the idea of the 
PSD completion of the self-energy is much older.\cite{stefanucci2014diagrammatic} 
Either way, the $\Sigma^{\text{TDHF}}$ self-energy can be obtained by 
replacing one of
the two vertices of Eq.~\eqref{eq:GW_phonon_selfenergy} by its direct
part $M^{\rm D}_{\eh}$,
\begin{equation}
\Sigma_{GW\Gamma}(\omega)
=
M^{\rm D}_{\eh}
\big[
\omega-s_{\eh}-\boldsymbol\Omega_{\eh}
\big]^{-1}
M_{\eh}^{\dagger} \;.
\label{eq:GWGamma}
\end{equation}
This clearly looks like a hybrid between the $GW$ approximation
Eq.~\eqref{eq:GW} and the PSDII self-energy Eq.~\eqref{eq:GW_phonon_selfenergy}. 
From the perspective of the present review,
such a hybrid approximation seems to make very little sense.
We however emphasize that, unlike the PSDII self-energy, it is derived 
rigorously in Hedin's framework. It of course comes with the downside 
that it stops being PSD. It also eliminates the contribution from the 
$\eh$-triplet channel. The $\eh$-triplet channel turns out to be absolutely 
devastating for numerical accuracy. In fact, in Ref.~\citenum{Pavlyukh2026}, the 
triplet channel has been eliminated completely, and the resulting self-energy, 
coined PSD1, was found to give excellent agreement with experiment.
This is of course not a very satisfactory ad hoc approximation, albeit a physically well-motivated one.


A significant upside of the $\Sigma^{\text{TDHF}}$ self-energy is that it is 
double-counting-free, by virtue of the fact that Hedin's equations only create 
a single diagram once. However, at third order in perturbation theory, it only 
contains half of the $\eh$ contribution. 
In the correlation channel language adopted here, it contains 
the contribution from $C_{\eh}$
(through $U^{\rm D}\mathcal G\,C_{\eh}\,\mathcal G\,U^{(1)\dagger}$), but not from $C_{\teh}$
(which is also the reason why it is double-counting-free at second order).

In Hedin's scheme, these missing diagrams would be added to the self-energy 
through the second-order vertex. In addition to the missing half of the 
$\eh$ channel, the second-order vertex also adds the missing $\pp$ channel
to the self-energy; the $T$ matrix is fully contained in Hedin's
equations.\cite{MejutoZaera2022} It is a feature of Hedin's equations
that they never add diagrammatically complete sets of diagrams at an
iteration order. This can be understood from the structure of the equations
and has for instance been pointed out by \citet{Schindlmayr1998}.
Being diagrammatically complete at a certain order, a quality closely linked 
but not equivalent to being crossing symmetric, is a feature we consider an important property 
of any theory of the electronic self-energy.

\subsubsection{$G3W2$-type vertex corrections}
Within Hedin's equations, some of the simplest 
possible vertex corrections arise from a first-order (3-point) vertex 
of the form\cite{Romaniello2012,Bruneval2024} 
\begin{equation}
	\Gamma(123) = \delta(12)\delta(13) + \ii W(21)G(13)G(32) \;,
\end{equation} 
that, inserted into Hedin's expression for $\Sigma$, results in the 
family of the so-called $G3W2$-type self-energies, 
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:g3w2}
	\Sigma_{xc}(11') = \Sigma^{GW} - G(14)W(1'4)G(42)G(21')W(21)
\end{equation}
that have been investigated first for solids
\cite{Bobbert1994,Ummels1998,Kutepov2016,Kutepov2017a,Pavlyukh2020} and later for molecules.\cite{Bruneval2024,Yoneyama2024}
By splitting the screened Coulomb interaction into the bare interaction
and its polarizable part $W = v + W^\text{c}$, the $G3W2$ term splits into 
4 contributions, schematically
\begin{equation}
	GWGWG = GvGvG + GW^\text{c}GvG + GvGW^\text{c}G + GW^\text{c}GW^\text{c}G \;.
\end{equation}
The sum of the first two terms is known as the SOSEX-approximation.\cite{Ren2015,Vacondio2022}
\citet{Bruneval2024} have coined the sum of the first three terms the 
2SOSEX-approximation, pointing out that it constitutes a more systematic 
approximation to the full $G3W2$. We note that also a statically screened 
$G3W2$ approximation has been investigated both for
solids\cite{Gruneis2014} and molecules,\cite{Forster2022} and more recently all these
approximations to $G3W2$ have been used in a fully self-consistent scheme by
\citet{Pokhilko2025} and assessed for molecules\cite{Wang2026}
(notice that the authors of this method denote the
static approximation to $G3W2$ as $G3W2$).
Resumming the statically screened $G3W2$ vertex to all orders as an
$\eh$ ladder gives the screened $\eh$ $T$ matrix, recast as a
vertex.\cite{Romaniello2012} With $W$ in the static limit, as used in
practice by \citet{Romaniello2012}, this is the vertex of
$\Sigma^{\rm BSE}$ of Ref.~\citenum{Forster2024}, but in the self-energy
only, while the $\eh$ propagator is kept at the level of the direct RPA.

Without any vertex corrections to the inner vertex that is used to generate $W$, 
the performance of all of these approximations is exceptionally poor. 
The performance of the statically screened $G3W2$ self-energy is satisfactory, 
but only because the magnitude of its effect in QP energies is very small.
\cite{Forster2022} However, when paired with vertex corrections for $W$,
$G3W2$-type approximations perform well.\cite{Gruneis2014, Forster2024}

\subsubsection{PSD-version of $G3W2$-type self-energies}

\paragraph{PSD-2SOSEX.}
Like all vertex-corrected self-energies derived in Hedin's formalism,
also the ones of $G3W2$-type cannot be written in resolvent form. Instead, their pole 
structures are rather complicated. Recently, the PSD-completion procedure of 
\citet{stefanucci2014diagrammatic} has been applied to derive a PSD-version of the 
$GW+\text{2SOSEX}$ self-energy,\cite{Bruneval2025} and it is interesting to investigate 
how it fits into the effective Hamiltonian framework of Faddeev-ADC(3). 
Like $GW$, the $GW+\text{2SOSEX}$ self-energy is of the form of 
Eq.~\eqref{eq:GW_phonon_selfenergy} and can be written in 
the form of Eq.~\eqref{eq:GW},
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:psd-2SOSEX}
    \Sigma_{pq}^{{\rm PSD},p}(\omega)
    =
    \sum_{c,bi}
    \frac{
        \bigl(
            M^{\rm D}_{pc,bi}
            +
            \widetilde M^{\rm D}_{pc,bi}
        \bigr)
        \bigl(
            M^{\rm D}_{qc,bi}
            +
            \widetilde M^{\rm D}_{qc,bi}
        \bigr)^*
    }
    {
        \omega-\eps_c-\Delta_{bi}
    }.
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
    \widetilde{M}^{\rm D}_{pc,n}
    =
    \sum_{aj}
    M^{\rm D}_{aj,n}
    \left[
        \frac{\braket{pa}{jc}}
        {\eps_a-\eps_j-\Omega_n}
        +
        \frac{\braket{pj}{ac}}
        {\eps_a-\eps_j+\Omega_n}
    \right].
    \label{eq:2sosex-tilde-couplings}
\end{equation}
Clearly, in their first-order limit, $\Omega_n \rightarrow \eps_b -\eps_i$ 
and $M^{\rm D}_{aj,n} \rightarrow \braket{ab}{ij}$, 
Eq.~\eqref{eq:2sosex-tilde-couplings} becomes  
\begin{equation}
    \widetilde{M}^{\rm D,(1)}_{pc,bi}
    =
    \sum_{aj}
    \langle ab|ji\rangle
    \left[
        \frac{\langle pa|jc\rangle}
        {\eps_a+\eps_i-\eps_b-\eps_j}
        +
        \frac{\langle pj|ac\rangle}
        {\eps_a+\eps_b-\eps_i-\eps_j}
    \right].
    \label{eq:2sosex-weak-coupling}
\end{equation}
The second term in this expression is a non-antisymmetrized 
second-order doubles amplitude, that is contained in 
ADC(3), and its renormalized version
Eq.~\eqref{eq:2sosex-tilde-couplings} is completely 
contained in Faddeev-ADC(3) as part of the ring-CCD 
amplitudes. The first term contains instead
the transition-energy difference
\begin{equation}
    \Delta_{aj}-\Delta_{bi}
    =
    (\epsilon_a-\epsilon_j)-(\epsilon_b-\epsilon_i).
    \label{eq:forward-ph-denominator}
\end{equation}
This is the
denominator for first-order mixing of the two forward particle--hole
transitions $(bi)$ and $(aj)$, and it is part neither of the $\pp$ nor 
the $\eh$-type couplings in Eq.~\eqref{eq:Ueff_depth1}. 

To see how 
it fits into Faddeev-ADC(3), define the direct electron--hole scattering
matrix between the $2p1h$ configurations $(c;bi)$ and $(c;aj)$ by
\begin{equation}
    C^{{\rm eh},{\rm D},(1)}_{c,bi;\,c,aj}
    \equiv
    -\braket{ab}{ji}.
    \label{eq:direct-eh-interaction}
\end{equation}
where $c$ is a fixed spectator. Their unperturbed energies are
$E_{c,bi}=\epsilon_c+\Delta_{bi}$ and $E_{c,aj}=\epsilon_c+\Delta_{aj}$.
They enter the Faddeev-ADC(3) self-energy, Eq.~\eqref{eq:Heff_depth1}, via 
the expansion of the resolvent $(\omega\mathcal{N} - \mathcal{H})^{-1}$ via 
Eq.~\eqref{eq:depth1_amplitudes} through the contribution
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    \Sigma^{(3)}_{\rm fwd,pq}(\omega)
    ={}&
    \left[
        U^{(1)}g(\omega)C^{{\rm eh},(1)}
        g(\omega)U^{(1)\dagger}
    \right]_{pq}
    \\
    ={}&
    \sum_{c,bi,aj}
    \frac{
        m_{p;c,bi}
        C^{{\rm eh},(1)}_{c,bi;\,c,aj}
        m_{q;c,aj}^{*}
    }
    {(\omega-E_{c,bi})(\omega-E_{c,aj})}
    \\
    ={}&
    -\sum_{c,bi,aj}
    \frac{
        \braket{pb}{ci}\,
        \braket{ab}{ji}\,
        \braket{qa}{cj}
    }
    {(\omega-E_{c,bi})(\omega-E_{c,aj})}.
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:adc3-forward-eh-insertion}
\end{equation}
The denominator of this term can be expanded through a partial 
fraction decomposition as 
\begin{equation}
    \frac{1}
    {(\omega-E_{c,bi})(\omega-E_{c,aj})}  
    =
    -\frac{1}{\Delta_{aj}-\Delta_{bi}}
    \left[
        \frac{1}{\omega-E_{c,bi}}
        -
        \frac{1}{\omega-E_{c,aj}}
    \right] \;,
    \label{eq:forward-eh-partial-fraction}
\end{equation}
which shows that it contains
the denominator of the first term in Eq.~\eqref{eq:2sosex-weak-coupling}.
The numerators are the same as the ones of the 
third-order terms of Eq.~\eqref{eq:psd-2SOSEX},
\begin{equation}
    \Sigma_{pq}^{(3),{\rm PSD},p}(\omega)
    =
    \sum_{c,bi}
    \frac{
        \widetilde M_{pc,bi}^{\rm D,(1)}
        M_{qc,bi}^{\rm D,(0)*}
        +
        M_{pc,bi}^{\rm D,(0)}
        \widetilde M_{qc,bi}^{\rm D,(1)*}
    }
    {
        \omega-\eps_c-\Delta_{bi}
    }.
    \label{eq:PSD-third-order-cross-term}
\end{equation}
Of course, by expanding the 
resolvent, one can match the terms in the PSD-2SOSEX and 
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Heff_depth1} order by order. 

Like its PSDII sibling, PSD-2SOSEX double counts part of $\Sigma^{(2)}$.
The origin is the first term of Eq.~\eqref{eq:2sosex-tilde-couplings}.
For the phonon $n$ that reduces to the transition $(aj)$ for $v\to0$,
its denominator is the RPA shift of that transition,
\begin{equation}
\eps_a-\eps_j-\Omega_n
=
O(v) \;,
\nonumber
\end{equation}
and the term is of first order, not of second. In
Eq.~\eqref{eq:2sosex-weak-coupling} it is the diagonal term
$(aj)=(bi)$, where $\Delta_{aj}-\Delta_{bi}=0$. With the singlet
phonons and spin-adapted vertices of
Refs.~\citenum{Bruneval2025,Marie2026},
$M^{\rm D}_{aj,n}/(\eps_a-\eps_j-\Omega_n)\to-1/\sqrt2$, and the vertex of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:psd-2SOSEX} becomes, to first order,
\begin{equation}
M^{\rm D}_{pc,aj}+\widetilde M^{\rm D}_{pc,aj}
\to
\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\Big(2\braket{pa}{cj}-\braket{pa}{jc}\Big) \;,
\nonumber
\end{equation}
the singlet antisymmetrized vertex of PSDI. Summed over $a$, $c$ and
$j$,
\begin{equation}
\Sigma^{{\rm PSD}}
=
\tfrac54\,\Sigma^{(2)}_{\rm D}+2\,\Sigma^{(2)}_{\rm X}+O(v^3) \;,
\nonumber
\end{equation}
the same second order as PSDI. The cross term $2\,\Sigma^{(2)}_{\rm X}$
is the second-order exchange that $GW$+2SOSEX-aug already counts
twice;\cite{Bruneval2025} completing the square adds
$\frac14\Sigma^{(2)}_{\rm D}$. 

In summary, double counting aside,
the PSD-2SOSEX self-energy is a (severely) channel-restricted subset of
the Faddeev-ADC(3) self-energy, but not through a one-to-one 
correspondence of individual pieces. As it does not assume the TDA, 
it contains terms that are not contained in ADC(3).

\paragraph{PSD-$G3W2$.}
\citet{Marie2026} applied the ADC construction to the expansion of the
$G3W2$ self-energy in $W$, with the goal to complete it into a PSD expression.
The configurations are those of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:GW_phonon_HNU}, with direct-RPA phonons,
$K=\eps_a+\Omega_\nu$ ($\eps_i-\Omega_\nu$). Their PSD-$G3W2$ effective Hamiltonian 
goes beyond the Faddeev-ADC(3) configuration space and adds 
3p2h configurations that would show up in ADC(4) and ADC(5). It also 
correlates the 5-particle block, terms that would otherwise only show up in 
ADC(5). Of course, since they solve the RPA problem, their scheme goes beyond 
ADC, but it is reasoanble to assume that their self-energy is obtained in 
Faddeev-ADC(5).
In the bare interaction PSD-$G3W2$ contains PSD-2SOSEX.
A doubly resonant term of $U^{(3)}$ adds a
further first-order exchange vertex, so that ADC-$G3W2$ gives
$\frac{17}{16}\Sigma^{(2)}_{\rm D}+\Sigma^{(2)}_{\rm X}$ in total.

All constructions
that make self-energies derived in Hedins method PSD seem to share this shortcoming, 
and it is unclear how it can be overcome. It is also reasonable to assume that the PSD
completions of higher-order self-energie derived in Hedins equations would 
also double count contriburions at higher orders. As already pointed out, 
the double cunted contributions can of course be subtracted, but this would make the 
self-energies non-PSD. For this reason, the current PSD-completions do not seem 
to consitute a path toward systemarically improvable self-energy approximations 

\subsection{The $\pp$ channel}
Very similar to working in the $\eh$ channel only, it is 
of course also possible to restrict Eq.~\eqref{eq:HNU_phonons} to the 
$\pp$ channel only. The canonical self-energy approximation that operates in this 
channel is the $T$-matrix approximation.\cite{orlando2023three, Marie2024a}
Unlike in the $\eh$ channel, there is no double counting ambiguity in the 
$\pp$ channel, simply due to the fact that it is not related by a simple symmetry to another 
channel in the same way as the $\eh$ channel. In the same way as the PSDII self-energy, 
the resulting self-energy is complete to third order in the $\pp$ channel, and of course, by construction, 
it is also PSD.

$T$-matrix self-energies have been used and extended in several
communities. In molecules, the $G_0T_0$ approximation is built on the
ppRPA,\cite{Zhang2017} on a renormalized-singles
reference,\cite{Li2021} compared to $GW$ and the $\eh$ $T$
matrix,\cite{orlando2023three} used in the Bethe--Salpeter
equation,\cite{Loos2022} and obtained as the first order of the $\pp$
Hedin equations.\cite{Marie2024a} Self-consistent $T$ matrices are used
in Kadanoff--Baym dynamics,\cite{Kohler1999,PuigvonFriesen2009,PuigvonFriesen2010,Schluenzen2020,Joost2020}
and, with anomalous propagators, in nuclear
matter.\cite{Bozek2002,Bozek2003} Screening enters the $\pp$ kernel in
the screened ladder approximation of plasma
physics,\cite{Kraeft1986,Kremp2005,Bonitz2016} through the
Gorkov--Melik-Barkhudarov correction,\cite{Pisani2018} and through the
vertex corrections of the $\pp$ Hedin equations.\cite{Marie2024a}

\subsection{FLEX and other combinations of $\eh$ and $\pp$ channels}
\label{sec:flex}
Truncating the Faddeev series, Eq.~\eqref{eq:R_oc_omega}, after the
terms with a single interacting pair, Eq.~\eqref{eq:R_FLEX_def}, gives the one-shot FLEX
approximation,\cite{Bickers1989,Bickers2004}
\begin{equation}
R_{\rm FLEX}
=
\mathcal G+\sum_\alpha\big(Q_\alpha-\mathcal G\big)
=
Q_{\pp}+Q_{\eh}+Q_{\teh}-2\,\mathcal G \;.
\nonumber
\end{equation}
Each pair couples to the one-particle states through its own couplings,
$\mathcal U_\alpha=M_\alpha Z_\alpha^{-1}$, and the free propagation through
$U^{(1)}$,
\begin{equation}
\Sigma_{\rm FLEX}
=
\sum_\alpha\mathcal U_\alpha\,Q_\alpha\,\mathcal U_\alpha^\dagger
-2\,U^{(1)}\mathcal G\,U^{(1)\dagger} \;.
\label{eq:FLEX}
\end{equation}
Each pair term contains $\Sigma^{(2)}$ once, and $-2\,\Sigma^{(2)}$
removes the excess. Faddeev-ADC(3) and FLEX 
agree through first order in $\mathcal C$; both are exact
through third order. FLEX however misses the sequences of different pairs in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:R_oc_omega}, which enter at fourth order. Since the
subtraction is outside the resolvent, $\Sigma_{\rm FLEX}$ has negative
residues and no Hermitian effective Hamiltonian. In the Tamm--Dancoff
limit,
\begin{equation}
R_{\rm FLEX}
=
\sum_\alpha\big(\omega-K-C_\alpha\big)^{-1}-2\big(\omega-K\big)^{-1} \;.
\nonumber
\end{equation}

FLEX is usually motivated as a drastic approximation to the parquet 
approximation,\cite{Bickers2004,MarieLoos2025} but closely related 
approximations can also be motivated as a rather heuristic combination 
of correlation channels. For instance, to place $\eh$ and $\pp$ on the 
same footing, $GW$ and
the $T$ matrix have been summed with a subtraction of the common second-order
terms in $GW+T$.\cite{Springer1998,Zhukov2004}
Starting from Hedin's equations, also \citet{Cunningham2024} has 
rederived FLEX, but he suggested screening all pair channels statically in the TDA. 
In the $\eh$ channel he suggested to use the kernel $v-W$, and in the $\pp$
channel he screened all interactions ($W-W$, schematically). The 
same has been done for instance also by \citet{romaniello2026direct}.
Instead of being summed, the channels are coupled within one
two-particle correlation function in the dynamically screened ladder
approximation\cite{Joost2022,Bonitz2024} and in the parquet
approximation.\cite{Bickers1991,Yang2009,MarieLoos2025}

The static screening approximation is of course motivated by the 
usual $GW$-BSE approximation for neutral excitations, and many authors
have solved the $\eh$ channel RPA with a statically screened HF problem.
\cite{Cunningham2018,Cunningham2023,Bruneval2024,Forster2025,Patterson2024,Pavlyukh2026} 
This is well motivated for solids, where TDHF is problematic and will even diverge for metals.
For molecules, this replacement is motivated by the superior performance of
$GW$-BSE over TDHF. However, due to the fact that static screening effects are usually rather weak in molecules,
the effects on ionization potentials are minor.\cite{Forster2024,Keizer2026}

Most of these approximations have never been evaluated for the same set of 
molecules in a reasonable basis set. Here, we therefore 
evaluate many of them for the set of 58 IPs of 23 small molecules for which
\citet{Marie2024} have recently calculated reference values of near FCI quality 
in the aug-cc-pVQZ basis. We do not consider statically screened interactions to calculate 
the ph/pp RPA phonons. For once, these are heuristic adjustments, and, if not applied carefully,
they can lead to double counting issues. Moreover, they 
can only be expected to have a minor influence on the final results. A method that 
does not perform well with bare interactions will not perform well with statically 
screened interaction either. 

Table~\ref{tab:supinf_stats} summarizes the errors, and all raw numerical data 
is shown in Table~\ref{tab:supinf_ips}. 
All calculations 
use RHF references, are performed in an all-electron framework,
make use of density fitting with the
aug-cc-pVQZ-RI basis, and, except $GW$, add the MP2-relaxed static
self-energy. Faddeev-ADC(3) and the ADC(3) variants are solved as Dyson
supermatrices; FLEX, PSD and GF2 in the diagonal quasiparticle
approximation. T1 and T0 denote
the triplet $\eh$ channel at first order and switched off, respectively.
C$_2$ and BN are excluded, since their RHF references are unstable in the TDHF
singlet (and triplet) channel, and F$_2$, since its TDHF triplet is unstable. The
statistics run over the 48 states with a value for every method. FLEX
finds no quasiparticle root for NH$_3$ (3) (RPA) and Ne (2) (T0).

From a formal perspective, Faddeev-ADC(3) has many desirable properties. 
It is exact to third order, double-counting-free, PSD, Hermitian, and includes 
renormalized amplitudes via the solution of the ring- and ladder-CCD equations.
However, its numerical performance is abysmal, coming with a MAD of 2.05 eV. 
FLEX performs nearly equally badly, and the only method that performs even worse 
than Faddeev-ADC(3) is the PSDII approximation of Ref.~\citenum{Pavlyukh2026}. 
What is the reason for this bad performance of these methods? All of them have in common
that they include TDHF triplet amplitudes. The negative effect on performance has
already been observed by \citet{Pavlyukh2026}, who then introduced the PSD1 approximation, 
that omits triplet excitations altogether. This improves the MAD from more than 2.0 eV to about 0.5 eV, 
which is not great, but somewhat reasonable, in the same ballpark as $G_0W_0$. 
By making it double-counting-free, its MAD nearly halves, to 0.29 eV, on par with ADC(3).

This motivates evaluating Faddeev-ADC(3) and FLEX without triplet excitations. 
When the triplet channel is omitted altogether (T0), both methods significantly improve, 
and the MADs become 0.51 and 0.49 eV. As a less drastic approximation, one can replace the 
triplet $\eh$ amplitudes by their first-order doubles counterparts that are also used in 
ADC(3). For Faddeev-ADC(3), this reduces the error further to 0.33 eV.
For FLEX it does not help; the error increases again to more than 1.0 eV.
Another way to avoid triplet instabilities is to use direct RPA instead of TDHF in the $\eh$ channel. 
This can be done both for Faddeev-ADC(3) and FLEX, but we have only numerically evaluated it for Faddeev-ADC(3).
For the subset of 48 states for which Faddeev-ADC(3) is defined this gives a MAD of 0.67 eV, 
which is somewhat more reasonable than the 2.05 eV of Faddeev-ADC(3) with the RPA channels, but still far from good. 

The results clearly show that channel coupling is important. The
same has been found for correlation energies in coupled-cluster theory,
where each single channel of CCD, rings, ladders or crossed rings, individually,
diverges for the electron gas. Only the terms that couple the channels (the mosaic terms)
remove the divergence.\cite{Shepherd2014,Scuseria2013} The reason is that the 
triplet $\eh$ channel resummed with the bare interaction is close to unstable, 
and sometimes even completely unstable, as for instance seen for F$_2$.
As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:triplet_proximity},
the error of Faddeev-ADC(3) grows as the lowest TDHF triplet approaches zero.

\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/triplet_proximity.pdf}

The observation that keeping it at first order (T1) or switching it off (T0) improves the
results parallels the instabilities of RPA with exact exchange,
which are removed by keeping exchange exact only at first
order\cite{Colonna2014,Hesselmann2011} and the improved stability of
singlet-paired coupled cluster.\cite{Bulik2015,Gomez2016}

This motivates the replacement of the uncoupled doubles amplitudes in
the Faddeev-ADC(3) self-energy by CCD ones, which contain the couplings
between the channels. The resulting method, to which we refer
as CCD-Faddeev-ADC(3), is the same substitution that turns
ADC(3) into CCD-ADC(3).\cite{forster2026third} Since no RPA problem has to be
solved, it is also defined in case of a triplet instability.
The triplet problem disappears.
The MAD over all 56 states with an sCI reference value is 0.23 eV, against 0.33 eV for ADC(3),
0.29 eV for CCD-ADC(3) and 0.18 eV for CCSD. The error distributions are shown in
Fig.~\ref{fig:supinf_ccd_violins}.
For the subset of 48 states
for which Faddeev-ADC(3) is actually defined, 
CCD-Faddeev-ADC(3) is a substantial improvement over CCD-ADC(3), with a MAD of 0.19 eV.
This is very close to CCSD that comes with a MAD of 0.16 eV. 

Maybe a bit surprisingly, the CCD amplitudes do not rescue FLEX. With a
MAD of 1.24 eV, it is almost as bad as FLEX with RPA
channels (MAD of 1.42 eV).
The CCD channels lower the IPs, which FLEX already underestimates. The
deficiency of FLEX is not the quality of its pair channels but the way it
combines them.


\section{\label{sec:conclusions}Conclusions}
Through the response function $R$, Eqs.~\eqref{eq:Sigma_3} and
\eqref{eq:K_pairs}, the self-energy is expressed in
terms of the three-particle kernel. In the
Faddeev form, Eq.~\eqref{eq:Faddeev_F}, the only two-body input is the
parquet vertex $F$. ADC($n$) is the
Schirmer matching of the Faddeev series at total order $n-2$ in $v$.
The orders through which the various
approximations are exact are collected in Table~\ref{tab:orders}.

Mapping the Faddeev form of parquet onto an effective Hamiltonian of
the $\pph$ ($\hhp$) space involves a few approximations. First, the
parquet approximation, $\Lambda=\Lambda^{(1)}$, is combined with a
static reference, $G=G_{\rm HF}$ in all lines of the vertex. Every building block of
the vertex is then an instantaneous interaction $\bar v$ or a
$G_{\rm HF}$ line, and the frequency integrals of the pair propagators
turn into resolvents of configuration spaces (Sec.~\ref{sec:propagators}).
Next, an own-channel approximation is invoked that consists in
letting each pair scatter only through the ladder of its own
channel with the bare kernel. Moreover, 
the ladders of different pairs do not
overlap in time (Sec.~\ref{sec:depth1}). 

In the own-channel and no-overlap approximation, Hermitian effective Hamiltonians
(for a positive definite metric) are obtained that go beyond ADC by going beyond the TDA. 
At the ADC(3) level, this amounts to an effective Hamiltonian that
replaces the first-order double amplitudes in the couplings with the 
ring-CCD (for the $\eh$ channel) and the ladder-CCD amplitudes
(for the $\pp$ channel), and adds the metric $\mathcal N$ and the terms $B_\alpha T_\alpha$ to the matrix. Channel crossings
that are not already part of the Faddeev kernel itself,
as in the full CCD amplitude equations, are not considered. 
At first order, the Faddeev kernel does not contain any 
channel crossings.

Starting from the second-order Faddeev kernel, crossed channels 
already enter before any resummation, by Eq.~\eqref{eq:Phi2_explicit}
and Eq.~\eqref{eq:ADC_Faddeev}. These crossed ladders, together with the overlapping orderings of two first-order kernels and the line dressing, lead to
the presence of $\ppphh$ configurations. Obtaining 
an effective Hamiltonian requires including this configuration space,
in the same way as in the ADC(4) approximation.

An advantage of our Faddeev construction is that 
it preserves the resolvent structure of the 
self-energy through a Hermitian effective Hamiltonian 
formalism, in the same way as in ADC. Unlike ADC, it also 
introduces a significantly larger amount of 
ground-state correlation through an 
infinite-order amplitude renormalization. 
The derivations shown here allow us to make precise
connections to recent PSD completions of 
vertex-corrected self-energies.\cite{Pavlyukh2026,Bruneval2025,Marie2026}
The formal properties of the self-energies discussed here are
summarized in Table~\ref{tab:properties}.

However, the numerical
results of Faddeev-ADC(3) are very poor, which can be linked to 
the triplet channel. The same goes for the FLEX approximation
and the PSDII approximation of \citet{Pavlyukh2026}. Switching 
off the triplet channel entirely or leaving the triplet amplitudes 
at first order, like in ADC(3), significantly improves performance.
Nevertheless, these schemes are clearly not competitive with 
methods based on the CC scheme, for instance diagonalization of the 
CCSD Hamiltonian. Clearly, coupling of the different correlation 
channels is mandatory whenever single-particle states are coupled to 
triplets beyond the TDA. To support this point, the replacement of the 
uncoupled amplitudes in Faddeev-ADC(3) with CCD ones results in improved 
results, but ADC(3) is only outperformed slightly. The performance is 
almost identical to CCD-ADC(3), and does not reach the performance of CCSD.

\section{Parquet vertices}
\label{app:parquet_vertices}
The Faddeev kernel is expanded order by order in 
$\bar{v}$,
\begin{equation}
\cK
=
\cK^{(1)}+\cK^{(2)}+\cK^{(3)}+O(v^4).
\label{eq:K_expansion}
\end{equation}
At first order, all channels contain only the bare antisymmetrized
interaction,
\begin{align}
\Gamma^{\pp,(1)}
&=
\Gamma^{\eh,(1)}
=
\Gamma^{\teh,(1)}
=
\Lambda^{(1)}
\equiv
\Gamma^{(1)},
\nonumber\\
\Gamma^{(1)}(12;1'2')
&=
-\ii\,\bar v(12;1'2'),
\nonumber\\
\bar v(12;1'2')
&\equiv
v(12;1'2')-v(12;2'1') \;.
\label{eq:Gamma1_bare}
\end{align}
To all orders,
\begin{align}
\Gamma^{\eh}
&=
\Lambda+\Phi^{\teh}+\Phi^{\pp},
\nonumber\\
\Gamma^{\teh}
&=
\Lambda+\Phi^{\eh}+\Phi^{\pp},
\nonumber\\
\Gamma^{\pp}
&=
\Lambda+\Phi^{\eh}+\Phi^{\teh},
\nonumber\\
F
&=
\Gamma^{\lambda}+\Phi^{\lambda}
=
\Lambda+\Phi^{\pp}+\Phi^{\eh}+\Phi^{\teh} \;.
\label{eq:parquet_channels}
\end{align}
for each channel $\lambda\in\{\pp,\eh,\teh\}$.
Note that\cite{MarieLoos2025}
\begin{equation}
\Lambda = \Lambda^{(1)}+O(v^4) \;.
\label{eq:crossing_Lambda}
\end{equation}
Defining 
\begin{align}
(A\circ_{\pp} B)(12;1'2')
&\equiv
A(12;\bar1\bar2)\,G(\bar1\bar1')\,G(\bar2\bar2')
\nonumber\\
&\hphantom{\equiv}\times
B(\bar1'\bar2';1'2'),
\label{eq:circ_def}
\\
(A\circ_{\eh}B)(12;1'2')
&\equiv
A(1\bar4;1'\bar3)\,G(\bar3\bar3')\,G(\bar4'\bar4)
\nonumber\\
&\hphantom{\equiv}\times
B(\bar3'2;\bar4'2'),
\label{eq:circ_eh_def}
\end{align}
the $\pp$ and $\eh$ BSEs are\cite{Kugler2018} 
\begin{equation}
	\begin{aligned}
F
={}&
\Gamma^{\pp}-\frac12\,\Gamma^{\pp}\circ_{\pp} F \;, \\
F
={}&
\Gamma^{\eh}+\Gamma^{\eh}\circ_{\eh}F \;.
\label{eq:parquet_BSE}
	\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
At second order, the reducible vertices are
\begin{equation}
	\begin{aligned}
\Phi^{\eh,(2)}
={}&
\Gamma^{(1)}\circ_{\eh}\Gamma^{(1)}, \\
\Phi^{\pp,(2)}
={}&
-\frac12\,\Gamma^{(1)}\circ_{\pp}\Gamma^{(1)},
\label{eq:Phi2_explicit}
	\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
and at third order 
\begin{equation}
	\begin{aligned}
\Phi^{\eh,(3)}
={}&
\Gamma^{\eh,(2)}\circ_{\eh}\Gamma^{(1)}
+\Gamma^{(1)}\circ_{\eh}\Gamma^{\eh,(2)}
\\
&+\Gamma^{(1)}\circ_{\eh}\Gamma^{(1)}\circ_{\eh}\Gamma^{(1)},
\\
\Phi^{\pp,(3)}
={}&
-\frac12\big(\Gamma^{\pp,(2)}\circ_{\pp}\Gamma^{(1)}
+\Gamma^{(1)}\circ_{\pp}\Gamma^{\pp,(2)}\big)
\\
&+\frac14\,\Gamma^{(1)}\circ_{\pp}\Gamma^{(1)}\circ_{\pp}\Gamma^{(1)}.
\label{eq:Phi3_st}
\end{aligned} 
\end{equation}
Together with Eqs.~\eqref{eq:parquet_channels} and
\eqref{eq:crossing_Lambda} and fermionic crossing,
\begin{equation}
\Phi^{\teh}(12;1'2')
=
-\Phi^{\eh}(21;1'2') \;,
\label{eq:crossing}
\end{equation}
this gives $\Gamma^{\lambda,(2)}$ and $\Gamma^{\lambda,(3)}$.

\section{Pair and three-particle propagators}
\label{app:propagators}
Here we derive the propagators stated in Sec.~\ref{sec:propagators}.

\subsection{Notation}
We use real canonical spin-orbitals and map numbers to orbitals
with $1,2,1',2'\to p,q,r,s$.
Since $\bar v$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:Gamma1_bare}, is instantaneous,
\begin{equation}
\bar v(12;1'2')
\to
\mel{pq}{}{rs}\,
\delta(t_1-t_2)\,\delta(t_1-t_{1'})\,\delta(t_2-t_{2'}),
\label{eq:vbar_orbital}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
\mel{pq}{}{rs} =
\braket{pq}{rs}-\braket{pq}{sr} \;.
\label{eq:mel_def}
\end{equation}
The antisymmetrizer of a pair of indices is
\begin{equation}
\hat P^-_{ab}=1-\hat P_{ab} \;,
\label{eq:Pminus}
\end{equation}
where $\hat P_{ab}$ exchanges $a$ and $b$.
In canonical spin-orbitals $p$ with energies $\eps_p$, occupations
$n_p$ ($n_i=1$, $n_a=0$, $\bar n_p=1-n_p$), and
$\sigma_p=\bar n_p-n_p$, the HF Green's function is
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:GHF_omega}
G_{{\rm HF},p}(\omega)
=
\frac{\bar n_p}{\omega-\eps_p+\ii0^+}
+
\frac{n_p}{\omega-\eps_p-\ii0^+}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Pair problems and reducible vertices}
In the own-channel approximation, each pair scatters with the bare kernel.
In the $\eh$ channel this is the TDHF problem, in the $\pp$ channel the ppRPA
problem,
\begin{equation}
\eta^{\lambda}\mathbf H^{\lambda}Z_n
=
\Omega_n Z_n \;,
\qquad
\eta^{\lambda}={\rm diag}(\bI,-\bI) \;,
\label{eq:pair_eigenproblem}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
\mathbf H^{\eh}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
A^{\eh} & B^{\eh}\\ B^{\eh} & A^{\eh}
\end{pmatrix} \;,
\label{eq:TDHF}
\end{equation}
\begin{align}
A^{\eh}_{ia,jb}
= & 
(\eps_a-\eps_i)\,\delta_{ab}\delta_{ij}+\mel{aj}{}{ib},
\nonumber\\
B^{\eh}_{ia,jb}
= &
\mel{ab}{}{ij} \;,
\nonumber
\end{align}
and
\begin{equation}
\mathbf H^{\pp}
= 
\begin{pmatrix}
A^{\pp} & B^{\pp}\\ B^{\pp\dagger} & D^{\pp}
\end{pmatrix} \;,
\nonumber
\end{equation}
\begin{align}
A^{\pp}_{cd,ef}
= &
(\eps_c+\eps_d)\,\delta_{ce}\delta_{df}+\mel{cd}{}{ef},
\nonumber\\
B^{\pp}_{cd,kl}
= &
\mel{cd}{}{kl},
\nonumber\\
D^{\pp}_{kl,mn}
= &
-(\eps_k+\eps_l)\,\delta_{km}\delta_{ln}+\mel{kl}{}{mn} \;,
\label{eq:ppRPA}
\end{align}
with $c<d$, $k<l$. The $\eh$ eigenvalues come in pairs $\pm\Omega_n$,
with eigenvectors $Z_n=(X_n,Y_n)$ and $\tilde Z_n=(Y_n,X_n)$. The $\pp$
spectrum is not symmetric: the positive-norm eigenvectors are the $N+2$
states ($\Omega^+_n$), the negative-norm ones the $N-2$ states
($\Omega^-_m$).

The pair propagators give the reducible vertices of the own-channel
approximation, the resummed counterparts of $\Phi^{\lambda,(2)}$ in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Phi2_explicit}, as sums over pair excitations with the
two-body pair vertices $M^{\eh}$ and $M^{\pp}$,
\begin{align}
\ii\Phi^{\eh}_{pqrs}(\Omega)
={}&
\sum_{\Omega_n>0}
\bigg[
\frac{M^{\eh}_{pr,n}\,M^{\eh}_{qs,n}}{\Omega-\Omega_n+\ii0^+}
-
\frac{M^{\eh}_{rp,n}\,M^{\eh}_{sq,n}}{\Omega+\Omega_n-\ii0^+}
\bigg],
\nonumber\\
M^{\eh}_{pr,n}
={}&
\sum_{ia}\Big[\mel{pa}{}{ri}\,X^n_{ia}+\mel{pi}{}{ra}\,Y^n_{ia}\Big] \;,
\label{eq:Phi_eh_phonons}
\end{align}
and
\begin{align}
\ii\Phi^{\pp}_{pqrs}(\Omega)
= & 
\sum_{n\in N+2}
\frac{M^{\pp}_{pq,n}\,M^{\pp}_{rs,n}}{\Omega-\Omega^{+}_n+\ii0^+}
-
\sum_{m\in N-2}
\frac{M^{\pp}_{pq,m}\,M^{\pp}_{rs,m}}{\Omega-\Omega^{-}_m-\ii0^+},
\nonumber\\
M^{\pp}_{pq,n}
= & 
\sum_{c<d}\mel{pq}{}{cd}\,X^n_{cd}
+\sum_{k<l}\mel{pq}{}{kl}\,Y^n_{kl} \;.
\label{eq:Phi_pp_phonons}
\end{align}
For real orbitals the backward $\eh$ vertex is the forward one with
exchanged orbital indices, $M^{\eh}_{rp,n}$. The same first-order vertex
appears in the vertex-corrected $GW$ self-energies of
Refs.~\citenum{Maggio2017,Vacondio2024,Forster2024}. In the $\eh$
variables, $\Phi^{\eh}$ depends only on the bosonic transfer frequency
$\Omega$; the crossed channel, $\teh$, follows by crossing,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:crossing}.


\subsection{Pair propagators}
The non-interacting particle--particle propagator is 
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:L_pp}
	L^{\pp}_0(12;1'2')=G(11')G(22')-G(12')G(21') \;,
\end{equation}
and performing the frequency integrations,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:L0pp_omega}
\begin{aligned}
L^{\pp}_{0,xy}(\Omega)
= &
\int\frac{\dd\nu}{2\pi}\,
G_x(\nu)\,G_y(\Omega-\nu)
\\
= &
\frac{-\ii\sigma_x\,\delta_{\sigma_x\sigma_y}}
{\Omega-(\eps_x+\eps_y)+\ii\sigma_x0^+} \;,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with its indices restricted to 
$(xy)\in\big\{(cd),\,c<d\big\}\cup\big\{(kl),\,k<l\big\}$.
The particle--hole propagator is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:L_eh}
L^{\eh}_0(31;1'3')=G(33')G(11') \;.
\end{equation}
After performing the frequency integrations, we obtain
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:L0eh_omega}
\begin{aligned}
L^{\eh}_{0,xr}(\Omega)
= & 
\int\frac{\dd\nu}{2\pi}\,
G_x(\nu)\,G_r(\nu+\Omega)
\\
= &
\frac{\ii\sigma_r\,\delta_{\sigma_x,-\sigma_r}}
{\Omega-(\eps_r-\eps_x)+\ii\sigma_r0^+} \;,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with its indices restricted to 
$(xr)\in\big\{(ia)\big\}\cup\big\{(ai)\big\}$.

The interacting propagators follow from the own-channel BSEs,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:BSE_oc}. Restricting the $\pp$ pairs to $c<d$, $k<l$
absorbs the $\tfrac12$ of the $\pp$ BSE, and

Eqs.~\eqref{eq:TDHF} and \eqref{eq:ppRPA},
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}

&=

\\

\end{aligned}
\nonumber
\end{equation}
with the spectral representations
\begin{equation}

\bigg]
\label{eq:L_eh_phonons}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:L_pp_phonons}
L^{\pp}(\Omega)

-\ii\sum_{n\in N+2}
\frac{Z_nZ_n^\dagger}{\Omega-\Omega^{+}_n+\ii0^+}

\end{equation}
Inserting them into the own-channel reducible vertices gives

\section{Riccati equations and decoupling conditions}
\label{app:Riccati}
Consider a space $\mathcal{P}\oplus\mathcal{Q}$, where $\mathcal{P}$ is
some active space. Let $x\in\mathcal{P}\oplus\mathcal{Q}$ be an
eigenvector of $H$ and write 
\begin{equation}
	x = 
	\begin{pmatrix}
		X \\
		Y 
	\end{pmatrix} = 
	\begin{pmatrix}
		X \\
		TX 
	\end{pmatrix}	
	\end{equation} 
which implies $Y = TX$. We then define the graph of $T$ as
\begin{equation}
	\mathcal{P}_T = \text{graph}(T) = 
	\left\{
	\begin{pmatrix}
		X \\
		TX 
	\end{pmatrix}	\;, X \in \mathcal{P}
	\right\}
\end{equation}
and require it to be invariant under the action of the partitioned Hamiltonian 
\begin{equation}
	\begin{pmatrix}
		H_{PP} & H_{PQ} \\ H_{QP} & H_{QQ}
	\end{pmatrix}
	\begin{pmatrix}
		X \\
		Y 
	\end{pmatrix}		= 
	\begin{pmatrix}
		H_{PP} & H_{PQ} \\ H_{QP} & H_{QQ}
	\end{pmatrix}
	\begin{pmatrix}
		X \\
		TX 
	\end{pmatrix} =
	\begin{pmatrix}
		\left(H_{PP} + H_{PQ}T\right)X \\
		\left(H_{QP} + H_{QQ}T \right)X
	\end{pmatrix}	\;.	
\end{equation}
Invariance under the action of $H$ then gives the condition 
\begin{equation}
	\left(H_{QP} + H_{QQ}T\right)X = T\left(H_{PP} + H_{PQ}T \right)X \;,
\end{equation}
and as this must be valid for all $X \in \mathcal{P}$, this yields the Riccati 
equation 
\begin{equation}
	H_{QP} + H_{QQ}T = TH_{PP} + T H_{PQ}T \;.
\end{equation}
The rewriting of the $\pp$ and $\eh$-RPA equations, Eqs.~\eqref{eq:ppRPA} and \eqref{eq:TDHF}, as the Riccati equations, Eq.~\eqref{eq:Riccati}, is 
easily seen to be a special case of this general transformation. 
Equivalently, with
\begin{equation}
	\Theta =
		\begin{pmatrix}
		\bI & 0 \\ T & \bI
	\end{pmatrix} \;,
	\qquad
	\Theta^{-1} =
	\begin{pmatrix}
		\bI & 0 \\ -T & \bI
	\end{pmatrix} \;,
\end{equation}
whose first block column spans $\mathcal{P}_T$,
\begin{equation}
	\Theta^{-1}H\Theta =
	\begin{pmatrix}
		H_{PP} + H_{PQ}T & H_{PQ} \\
		H_{QP} + H_{QQ}T - TH_{PP} - TH_{PQ}T & H_{QQ} - TH_{PQ}
	\end{pmatrix} \;.
	\label{eq:SHS_Riccati}
\end{equation}
The lower-left block is the Riccati equation, and the invariance of
$\mathcal{P}_T$ is the decoupling condition
\begin{equation}
	Q\,\Theta^{-1}H\Theta\,P = 0 \;,
	\label{eq:PQ_decoupling}
\end{equation}
with $P$ and $Q$ the projectors onto $\mathcal{P}$ and $\mathcal{Q}$.
$\Theta^{-1}H\Theta$ is then block upper triangular, and $H_{PP}+H_{PQ}T$ carries
the eigenvalues of $H$ on $\mathcal{P}_T$.
For $H=\eta^\lambda\mathbf H^\lambda$ of Eqs.~\eqref{eq:TDHF} and \eqref{eq:ppRPA}, \ie\
$H_{PP}=A_\alpha$, $H_{PQ}=B_\alpha$, $H_{QP}=-B_\alpha^\dagger$ and
$H_{QQ}=-D_\alpha$, the lower-left block is minus the left-hand side of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Riccati}.

As an example, consider the wave operator
\begin{equation}
	\mathcal{W} = \Theta P = P + T \;,
	\qquad
	P\mathcal{W} = P \;,
\end{equation}
which maps $\mathcal{P}$ onto $\mathcal{P}_T$. The invariance of
$\mathcal{P}_T$ is then the Bloch equation\cite{Bloch1958,Brandow1967,ShavittBartlett2009}
\begin{equation}
	H\mathcal{W} = \mathcal{W}H\mathcal{W} \;,
	\label{eq:Bloch}
\end{equation}
whose $QP$ block is the Riccati equation and whose $PP$ block is an
identity. The effective Hamiltonian is
the upper-left block,
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:H_T_riccati}
	H_T = PH\mathcal{W} = P\Theta^{-1}H\Theta P = H_{PP}+H_{PQ}T \;.
\end{equation}
For $H=H_0+V$ with $[H_0,P]=0$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:Bloch} becomes the
generalized Bloch equation
\begin{equation}
	[\mathcal{W},H_0] = V\mathcal{W}-\mathcal{W}PV\mathcal{W} \;,
\end{equation}
the starting point of Rayleigh--Schr\"odinger perturbation theory for
$\mathcal{W}$. For a one-dimensional $\mathcal{P}$ spanned by $\Phi$,
$T$ contains the CI coefficients of $\Psi=\mathcal{W}\Phi$ in intermediate
normalization, $H_T=E$, and Eq.~\eqref{eq:Bloch} is the
Schr\"odinger equation projected onto $\mathcal{Q}$.

\subsection{Comparison to L\"owdin downfolding}
The Bloch equation should be compared with L\"owdin downfolding, which 
generates the frequency-dependent effective Hamiltonian acting in 
$\mathcal{P}$-space only,
\begin{equation}
	H_{\rm L}(\omega) = H_{PP}+H_{PQ}\left(\omega-H_{QQ}\right)^{-1}H_{QP} \;,
	\label{eq:Lowdin}
\end{equation}
Comparison with Eq.~\eqref{eq:H_T_riccati} then shows that 
\begin{equation}
		T(\omega) = \left(\omega-H_{QQ}\right)^{-1}H_{QP} \;.
\end{equation}
$H_{\rm L}(\omega)$ is
Hermitian for Hermitian $H$ and has the form of a self-energy, and
$\det\big(\omega-H_{\rm L}(\omega)\big)=0$ yields all eigenvalues of $H$
with a non-vanishing $\mathcal{P}$ component, but it is
energy-dependent and has to be solved root by root. The Bloch amplitude
is instead a single, energy-independent map for the $\dim\mathcal{P}$
selected roots,
\begin{equation}
	T = \big(T(\omega_1)x_1,\ldots,T(\omega_{\dim\mathcal{P}})x_{\dim\mathcal{P}}\big)X^{-1} \;,
\end{equation}
at the price of a nonlinear equation, a non-Hermitian $H_T$, and
one solution for each choice of roots. Expanded in $V$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:Lowdin} gives Brillouin--Wigner and
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Bloch} Rayleigh--Schr\"odinger perturbation theory. The two
are related by a Taylor expansion of $T(\omega_k)$ around a reference energy
$E_0$: with $g_0=(E_0-H_{QQ})^{-1}$ and $\omega_kx_k=H_Tx_k$,
\begin{equation}
	T = \sum_{n\geq0}g_0^{\,n+1}H_{QP}\left(E_0-H_T\right)^n \;,
\end{equation}
\ie\ the energy dependence of $T(\omega)$ becomes the dependence on
$H_T$, which is the same for all selected roots.

\section{The ADC hierarchy}
\label{app:ADC}
In this appendix, we work out ADC(3) explicitly, as an illustrative
case of Sec.~\ref{sec:Faddeev_form}.
ADC(1), \ie\ HF, needs no three-particle propagator at all, and
ADC(2), \ie\ GF2, only the $\pph$ propagator $\mathcal G$.

\subsection{ADC(3) from the first-order Faddeev kernel}
We here derive the ADC(3) equation starting 
from Eq.~\eqref{eq:Sigma_3} with $R_{\rm ADC(3)}$ of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:ADC_Faddeev}, \ie\ with the first-order Faddeev kernel
acting once on $\mathcal G$. We then collect all diagrams by the pair on which
$\cK^{(1)}$ acts, \ie\ in the $\pp$ and $\eh$ channels. This will 
give us the GF3 self-energy. Then we use 
Schirmer's approach of imposing the resolvent form 
of the self-energy and diagrammatic matching to 
get ADC(3).

The self-energy to third order is
\begin{align}
\Sigma-\Sigma_{\rm HF}[G_{\rm HF}]
&=
\Sigma^{(2)}+\Sigma^{(3)}+O(v^4) \;.
\end{align} 
The Green's function is expanded to second order,
which will later give the static self-energy to third order
(notice that the 
$G_{\rm HF}
\Sigma_{\rm HF}
G_{\rm HF}
\Sigma_{\rm HF}
G_{\rm HF}$ term has been absorbed in the mean field, 
$G^{-1}=G_{\rm HF}^{-1}+O(v^2)$),
\begin{equation}
G
=
G_{\rm HF}+G_{\rm HF}\,\Sigma^{(2)}G_{\rm HF}+O(v^3) \;,
\end{equation}
$\mathcal G$ is built from $G_{\rm HF}$,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal G=\mathcal G_{\rm HF}+O(v^2) \;.
\end{equation}
For ADC(3), we must consider the first-order 
Faddeev kernel, 
\begin{equation}
\cK
= \cK^{(1)}+O(v^2) \;,
\label{eq:K_first_order}
\end{equation}
that only contains the bare antisymmetrized interactions
in all channels, Eq.~\eqref{eq:Gamma1_bare}.
Recall that ADC(3) follows from the second line of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:ADC_Faddeev}, where
\begin{equation}
\sum_\alpha\cK_\alpha\big[F^{(1)}\big]=\cK^{(1)} \;.
\nonumber
\end{equation}
The third-order self-energy splits into 
static and dynamical contributions,
\begin{align}
\Sigma^{(3)}(11')
={}&
\Sigma^{(3)}_{\infty}(11')+\Sigma^{(3)}_{\cK}(11'),
\label{eq:Sigma3_split}
\\
\Sigma^{(3)}_{\infty}(11')
={}&
-\ii\big[v(1\bar2;1'\bar2')-v(1\bar2;\bar2'1')\big]
\nonumber\\
&\times
\big[G_{\rm HF}\Sigma^{(2)}G_{\rm HF}\big](\bar2'\bar2^+),
\label{eq:Sigma3_static}
\\
\Sigma^{(3)}_{\cK}(11')
={}&
v(1\bar2;\bar3'\bar2')\,G_{\rm HF}(\bar3'\bar4)\,v(\bar4\bar5;\bar6'\bar7')
\nonumber\\
&\times
\big[\cK^{(1)}\mathcal G_{\rm HF}\big](\bar2'\bar6'\bar7';\bar3\bar2\bar5^+)
\nonumber\\
&\times
G_{\rm HF}^{-1}(\bar3 1').
\label{eq:Sigma3_K}
\end{align}
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Sigma3_static} is the HF self-energy 
evaluated with the second-order density matrix,
\begin{equation}
\Sigma^{(3)}_{\infty,pq}
=
\sum_{rs}\mel{pr}{}{qs}\,\gamma^{(2)}_{sr}.
\end{equation}
Defining the first-order doubles 
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:mp2_double}
t_{ij}^{ab}
=
\frac{\mel{ab}{}{ij}}{\eps_i+\eps_j-\eps_a-\eps_b} \;,
\end{equation}
and second-order singles amplitudes
\begin{equation}
t_i^{a(2)} = 
\frac{1}{\eps_i-\eps_a}
\Big[
\frac12\sum_{kbc}t_{ik}^{bc}\mel{bc}{}{ak}
-\frac12\sum_{jkc}t_{jk}^{ac}\mel{ic}{}{jk}
\Big] \;,
\end{equation}
the blocks of $\gamma^{(2)}$ are\cite{Keizer2026} 
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\gamma^{(2)}_{ij}
={}& 
-\frac12\sum_{kab}t_{ik}^{ab}t_{jk}^{ab} \;, \\
\gamma^{(2)}_{ab}
={}& 
\frac12\sum_{ijc}t_{ij}^{ac}t_{ij}^{bc} \;, \\
\gamma^{(2)}_{ia}
={}& 
\gamma^{(2)}_{ai}
=
t_i^{a(2)} \;.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

Using Eqs.~\eqref{eq:K_pairs} and \eqref{eq:pair_ops} in Eq.~\eqref{eq:Sigma3_K} with $\chi = \mathcal G$, 
we obtain the contributions of the three channels to
$\Sigma^{(3)}_{\cK}$. Here and below, $G\equiv G_{\rm HF}$, and all interactions appear
antisymmetrized, Eq.~\eqref{eq:Gamma1_bare}.
For the $\pp$ channel, we get
\begin{align}
\Sigma^{\pp}_{\cK}(11')
={}&
\frac{\ii}{4}\,
\bar v(1\bar2;\bar3'\bar2')\,G(\bar3'\bar4)\,G(\bar2'\bar5^+)
\nonumber\\
&\times
\bar v(\bar4\bar5;\bar6'\bar7')\,G(\bar6'\bar8)\,G(\bar7'\bar9)
\nonumber\\
&\times
\bar v(\bar8\bar9;1'\bar9')\,G(\bar9'\bar2) \;.
\label{eq:Sigma_K_pp}
\end{align}
For the $\eh$, $\teh$ channels, we obtain 
\begin{align}
\Sigma^{\eh}_{\cK}(11')
={}&
-\frac{\ii}{2}\,
\bar v(1\bar2;\bar3'\bar2')\,G(\bar3'\bar4)\,\bar v(\bar4\bar5;\bar6'\bar7')
\nonumber\\
&\times
G(\bar2'\bar8)\,G(\bar6'\bar9)\,
\bar v(\bar8\bar9;1'\bar8')
\nonumber\\
&\times
G(\bar8'\bar5^+)\,G(\bar7'\bar2)
=
\Sigma^{\teh}_{\cK}(11') \;.
\label{eq:Sigma_K_eh}
\end{align}
In the time domain, the non-interacting pair propagators follow from
$G_{{\rm HF},p}(t)=-\ii\sigma_p\,\theta(\sigma_pt)\,e^{-\ii\eps_pt}$,
\begin{equation}
L^{\pp}_{0,xy}(t)
= 
G_x(t)\,G_y(t)
=
-\delta_{\sigma_x\sigma_y}\,\theta(\sigma_xt)\,
e^{-\ii(\eps_x+\eps_y)t} \;,
\label{eq:L0pp_time}
\end{equation}
and 
\begin{equation}
L^{\eh}_{0,xr}(t)
= 
G_x(-t)\,G_r(t) =
\delta_{\sigma_x,-\sigma_r}\,\theta(\sigma_rt)\,
e^{-\ii(\eps_r-\eps_x)t} \;.
\label{eq:L0eh_time}
\end{equation}
Exploiting the temporal structure of $\bar v$ and the space-time to 
orbital index mapping of Eq.~\eqref{eq:vbar_orbital}, and with 
Eq.~\eqref{eq:L0pp_time} the $\pp$ term becomes
\begin{align}
\Sigma^{\pp}_{\cK,pq}(t_1,t_{1'})
={}&
\frac{\ii}{4}
\sum_{rxyuw}
\mel{pr}{}{xy}\mel{xy}{}{uw}\mel{uw}{}{qr}
\nonumber\\
&\times
\int\dd\tau\;
L^{\pp}_{0,xy}(t_1-\tau)\,L^{\pp}_{0,uw}(\tau-t_{1'})
\nonumber\\
&\times
G_r(t_{1'}-t_1) \;,
\label{eq:Sigma_K_pp_time}
\end{align}
in the orbital basis. 
Summing up the identical $\eh$ and $\teh$ channels
and with Eq.~\eqref{eq:L0eh_time}, the 
sum of $\Sigma^{\eh}$ and $\Sigma^{\teh}$ becomes
\begin{align}
\Sigma^{\eh+\teh}_{\cK,pq}(t_1,t_{1'})
={}&
-\ii
\sum_{rsxyu}
\mel{pr}{}{xy}\mel{xs}{}{ur}\mel{yu}{}{qs}
\nonumber\\
&\times
\int\dd\tau\;
L^{\eh}_{0,rx}(t_1-\tau)\,L^{\eh}_{0,su}(\tau-t_{1'})
\nonumber\\
&\times
G_y(t_1-t_{1'}) \;.
\label{eq:Sigma_K_eh_time}
\end{align}
Not coincidentally, one can identify the second-order $\pp$ and
$\eh$ reducible vertices, Eq.~\eqref{eq:Phi2_explicit}, of the parquet
equations.

\subsubsection{GF3 self-energy}
We perform the integrals over $\tau$ by regarding each space-time term
as a circuit and using energy conservation at the intermediate vertices,
which leaves contour integrals over the channel frequency.
For the $\pp$ channel, one then gets
\begin{align}
\Sigma^{\pp}_{\cK,pq}(\omega)
= &
\frac{\ii}{4}
\sum_{rxyuw}
\mel{pr}{}{xy}\mel{xy}{}{uw}\mel{uw}{}{qr}
\nonumber\\
&\times
\int\frac{\dd\Omega}{2\pi}\,
L^{\pp}_{0,xy}(\Omega)\,L^{\pp}_{0,uw}(\Omega)\,
G_r(\Omega-\omega),
\label{eq:Sigma_K_pp_omega}
\end{align}
with Eq.~\eqref{eq:L0pp_omega}.
In the $\eh+\teh$ channel, Eq.~\eqref{eq:Sigma_K_eh_time}, we obtain 
\begin{align}
\Sigma^{\eh+\teh}_{\cK,pq}(\omega)
= &
-\ii
\sum_{rsxyu}
\mel{pr}{}{xy}\mel{xs}{}{ur}\mel{yu}{}{qs}
\nonumber\\
&\times
\int\frac{\dd\Omega}{2\pi}\,
L^{\eh}_{0,rx}(\Omega)\,L^{\eh}_{0,su}(\Omega)\,
G_y(\omega-\Omega),
\label{eq:Sigma_K_eh_omega}
\end{align}
with Eq.~\eqref{eq:L0eh_omega}.
In both channels, the kernel factorizes into two one-frequency
pair propagators in the channel transfer frequency $\Omega$; only the
$\Omega$ integral couples them to the spectator line
($r$ in $\pp$, $y$ in $\eh$).
What remains is to perform the contour integrals over $\Omega$. For
the $\pp$ channel, this gives 
\begin{align}
\Sigma^{\pp}_{\cK,pq}(\omega)
={}&
\frac14\sum_{abcdi}
\frac{\mel{pi}{}{ab}\mel{ab}{}{cd}\mel{cd}{}{qi}}
{(\omega-\Delta_{abi})(\omega-\Delta_{cdi})}
\label{eq:pp_in_plus}
\\
&+
\frac14\sum_{abkli}
\frac{\mel{pi}{}{ab}\,t_{kl}^{ab}\,\mel{kl}{}{qi}}
{\omega-\Delta_{abi}}
\label{eq:pp_lt_plus}
\\
&+
\frac14\sum_{cdkli}
\frac{\mel{pi}{}{kl}\,t_{kl}^{cd}\,\mel{cd}{}{qi}}
{\omega-\Delta_{cdi}}
\label{eq:pp_gt_plus}
\\
&-
\frac14\sum_{ijkla}
\frac{\mel{pa}{}{ij}\mel{ij}{}{kl}\mel{kl}{}{qa}}
{(\omega-\Delta_{ija})(\omega-\Delta_{kla})}
\label{eq:pp_in_minus}
\\
&+
\frac14\sum_{bckla}
\frac{\mel{pa}{}{bc}\,t_{kl}^{bc}\,\mel{kl}{}{qa}}
{\omega-\Delta_{kla}}
\label{eq:pp_lt_minus}
\\
&+
\frac14\sum_{ijbca}
\frac{\mel{pa}{}{ij}\,t_{ij}^{bc}\,\mel{bc}{}{qa}}
{\omega-\Delta_{ija}}.
\label{eq:pp_gt_minus}
\end{align}
For the $\eh+\teh$ channel, 
\begin{align}
\Sigma^{\eh+\teh}_{\cK,pq}(\omega)
={}&
\sum_{abcij}
\frac{\mel{pi}{}{ab}\mel{aj}{}{ci}\mel{bc}{}{qj}}
{(\omega-\Delta_{abi})(\omega-\Delta_{bcj})}
\label{eq:eh_in_plus}
\\
&-
\sum_{abcik}
\frac{\mel{pi}{}{ab}\,t_{ik}^{ac}\,\mel{bk}{}{qc}}
{\omega-\Delta_{abi}}
\label{eq:eh_lt_plus}
\\
&+
\sum_{abcjk}
\frac{\mel{pc}{}{ja}\,t_{jk}^{bc}\,\mel{ab}{}{qk}}
{\omega-\Delta_{abk}}
\label{eq:eh_gt_plus}
\\
&-
\sum_{ijkab}
\frac{\mel{pb}{}{ij}\mel{ia}{}{kb}\mel{jk}{}{qa}}
{(\omega-\Delta_{ijb})(\omega-\Delta_{jka})}
\label{eq:eh_in_minus}
\\
&-
\sum_{ijkab}
\frac{\mel{pj}{}{ai}\,t_{jk}^{ab}\,\mel{ik}{}{qb}}
{\omega-\Delta_{ikb}}
\label{eq:eh_lt_minus}
\\
&+
\sum_{ijkab}
\frac{\mel{pb}{}{ij}\,t_{ik}^{ab}\,\mel{ja}{}{qk}}
{\omega-\Delta_{ijb}} \;.
\label{eq:eh_gt_minus}
\end{align}
We have used the shorthand of Eq.~\eqref{eq:denom_triple}.
These terms constitute the complete dynamic GF3 self-energy, which is 
for instance given in the appendix of Ref.~\citenum{von1984computational}.

\subsubsection{From GF3 to ADC(3)}
What remains is to match the GF3 terms with the ADC(3) ones, 
following the method introduced by Schirmer.\cite{Schirmer1983}
The procedure is rather simple: we impose the resolvent 
structure on the self-energy
\begin{align}
	\label{eq:ADC3}
\Sigma_{\rm ADC(3)}(\omega)
={}&
\Sigma(\infty)
+
U^{\rm II}\big(\omega-K^{\rm II}-C^{\rm II}\big)^{-1}U^{{\rm II}\dagger}
\nonumber\\
&+
U^{\rm I}\big(\omega+K^{\rm I}+C^{\rm I}\big)^{-1}U^{{\rm I}\dagger},
\end{align}
with the static self-energy
$\Sigma(\infty)=\Sigma_{\rm HF}+\Sigma^{(3)}_{\infty}$ and
\begin{equation}
U^{\rm II}
=  U^{{\rm II},(1)}+U^{{\rm II},(2)} \;,
\qquad
U^{\rm I}
=  U^{{\rm I},(1)}+U^{{\rm I},(2)} \;.
\end{equation}
All terms are then classified according to their number of 
dynamical energy denominators. The ones that 
only contain one go inside $U$, and the ones that contain 
two go inside $C$.
Terms with the dynamical denominator $\omega-\Delta_{abi}$
($\pph$, $N+1$) belong to block II, terms with $\omega-\Delta_{ija}$
($\hhp$, $N-1$) to block I.
We use the zeroth-order matrices and first-order couplings of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:K_U1}. The unrestricted sums
of the GF3 terms are converted with
\begin{align}
\sum_{a<b}\sum_{c<d}x_{ab}\,
\hat P^-_{ab}\hat P^-_{cd}f_{ab,cd}\,y_{cd}
&=
\sum_{abcd}x_{ab}\,f_{ab,cd}\,y_{cd},
\nonumber\\
\sum_{a<b}x_{ab}\,\hat P^-_{ab}g_{ab}
&=
\sum_{ab}x_{ab}\,g_{ab},
\label{eq:pair_identities}
\end{align}
for $x$, $y$ antisymmetric in their pair indices, with $\hat P^-_{ab}$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:Pminus}.

In block II, the two-denominator terms,
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:pp_in_plus} and \eqref{eq:eh_in_plus}, combine
into
\begin{align}
\Sigma^{\rm II}_{C}(\omega)
={}&
U^{{\rm II},(1)}\big(\omega-K^{\rm II}\big)^{-1}C^{\rm II}
\big(\omega-K^{\rm II}\big)^{-1}U^{{\rm II},(1)\dagger},
\nonumber\\
C^{\rm II}_{abi,cdj}
={}&
\mel{ab}{}{cd}\delta_{ij}
-\hat P^-_{ab}\hat P^-_{cd}\mel{aj}{}{ci}\delta_{bd},
\label{eq:C_II}
\end{align}
where we used $\mel{bc}{}{qj}=-\mel{qj}{}{cb}$.
The one-denominator terms,
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:pp_lt_plus}, \eqref{eq:pp_gt_plus},
\eqref{eq:eh_lt_plus} and \eqref{eq:eh_gt_plus}, give
\begin{align}
\Sigma^{\rm II}_{U}(\omega)
={}&
U^{{\rm II},(2)}\big(\omega-K^{\rm II}\big)^{-1}U^{{\rm II},(1)\dagger}
\nonumber\\
&+
U^{{\rm II},(1)}\big(\omega-K^{\rm II}\big)^{-1}U^{{\rm II},(2)\dagger},
\nonumber\\
U^{{\rm II},(2)}_{p;abi}
={}&
\frac12\sum_{jl}t_{jl}^{ab}\mel{pi}{}{jl}
-\hat P^-_{ab}\sum_{kc}t_{ki}^{ac}\mel{pc}{}{kb},
\label{eq:U2_II}
\end{align}
where we used $t_{ik}^{ac}\mel{bk}{}{qc}=t_{ki}^{ac}\mel{qc}{}{kb}$.

In block I, the two-denominator terms,
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:pp_in_minus} and \eqref{eq:eh_in_minus}, combine
into
\begin{align}
\Sigma^{\rm I}_{C}(\omega)
={}&
-U^{{\rm I},(1)}\big(\omega+K^{\rm I}\big)^{-1}C^{\rm I}
\big(\omega+K^{\rm I}\big)^{-1}U^{{\rm I},(1)\dagger},
\nonumber\\
C^{\rm I}_{ija,klb}
={}&
\mel{ij}{}{kl}\delta_{ab}
-\hat P^-_{ij}\hat P^-_{kl}\mel{ib}{}{ka}\delta_{jl},
\label{eq:C_I}
\end{align}
where we used $\mel{jk}{}{qa}=-\mel{qa}{}{kj}$.
The sign is such that $\Sigma^{\rm I}_{C}$ is the first-order term
of $U^{{\rm I},(1)}(\omega+K^{\rm I}+C^{\rm I})^{-1}U^{{\rm I},(1)\dagger}$.
The one-denominator terms,
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:pp_lt_minus}, \eqref{eq:pp_gt_minus},
\eqref{eq:eh_lt_minus} and \eqref{eq:eh_gt_minus}, give
\begin{align}
\Sigma^{\rm I}_{U}(\omega)
={}&
U^{{\rm I},(2)}\big(\omega+K^{\rm I}\big)^{-1}U^{{\rm I},(1)\dagger}
\nonumber\\
&+
U^{{\rm I},(1)}\big(\omega+K^{\rm I}\big)^{-1}U^{{\rm I},(2)\dagger},
\nonumber\\
U^{{\rm I},(2)}_{p;ija}
={}&
\frac12\sum_{bc}t_{ij}^{bc}\mel{pa}{}{bc}
-\hat P^-_{ij}\sum_{kb}t_{ik}^{ba}\mel{pk}{}{bj}.
\label{eq:U2_I}
\end{align}
With Eqs.~\eqref{eq:C_II}--\eqref{eq:U2_I} and $\Sigma(\infty)$, all
third-order terms are reproduced by Eq.~\eqref{eq:ADC3}, which
completes ADC(3).
