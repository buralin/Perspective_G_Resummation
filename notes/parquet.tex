\title{From Parquet to Hedin's Equations}

\author{Pierre-Fran\c{c}ois \surname{Loos}}
	\email{loos@irsamc.ups-tlse.fr}
	\affiliation{\LCPQ}

\begin{abstract}
Hedin's and parquet equations provide two formally exact but apparently
rather different organizations of many-body perturbation theory.
Hedin's equations are formulated in terms of a self-energy, a screened
interaction, and a 3-point vertex, whereas parquet theory resolves the
full 4-point vertex into contributions associated with the three
two-particle scattering channels.
In these notes, we examine explicitly the connection between these two
formulations.
We show that the electron-hole Bethe--Salpeter equation of parquet theory,
once the external propagators are attached, is identical to the 4-point
form of the Hedin response equation.
The corresponding Hedin kernel is therefore the electron-hole irreducible
parquet vertex,
$
	\fdv*{\Sigma}{G}
	=
	\Gamma^\eh
	=
	\Lambda
	+
	\Phi^{\teh}
	+
	\Phi^\pp.
$
This identity provides a simple interpretation of the relation between the
two theories: parquet keeps the different scattering channels explicit,
whereas the 4-point Hedin formulation downfolds the crossed
electron-hole and particle-particle channels into a single three-frequency
kernel.
Starting from this 4-point formulation, the usual Hedin equations are
then recovered by separating the direct Coulomb contribution and exploiting
the density-density structure of the Coulomb interaction.
This converts the 4-point screened interaction into the usual two-point
screened interaction $W$ and the 4-point response vertex into the
standard 3-point Hedin vertex.
The analogous construction in the particle-particle channel is also briefly
discussed, providing a direct connection with particle-particle Hedin's
equations and $T$-matrix theory.

\section{Introduction}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

Many-body perturbation theory admits several formally exact organizations
of the same underlying many-electron problem.
Among the most prominent are Hedin's equations and the parquet equations.\cite{Hedin1965,DeDominicis_1964b,Bickers_2004}
Although both formulations ultimately describe the same one- and
two-particle correlation functions, their structures appear rather
different.

The standard Hedin equations are built around the one-particle Green's
function $G$, the self-energy $\Sigma$, the screened Coulomb interaction
$W$, the irreducible polarizability $P$, and a 3-point vertex.\cite{Hedin1965,MarieAmmarLoos2024}
The central 4-point quantity entering the vertex equation is the
functional derivative
\begin{equation}
	\Xi
	=
	\frac{\delta \Sigma}{\delta G},
	\label{eq:Hedin_kernel_intro}
\end{equation}
which is, in general, a three-frequency object.
Despite the compact appearance of Hedin's equations, the exact kernel
$\Xi$ therefore contains a highly nontrivial amount of two-particle
physics.

Parquet theory organizes the same physics differently.\cite{DeDominicis_1964b,Eckhardt2023,MarieLoos2025}
The full 4-point vertex $F$ is decomposed according to its
two-particle reducibility in the three possible scattering channels:
the electron-hole ($\eh$), transverse electron-hole
($\teh$), and particle-particle ($\pp$) channels.
Introducing the fully two-particle-irreducible vertex $\Lambda$ and the
three channel-reducible vertices
$\Phi^\eh$, $\Phi^{\teh}$, and $\Phi^\pp$, one writes
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	F
	=
	\Lambda
	+
	\Phi^\eh
	+
	\Phi^{\teh}
	+
	\Phi^\pp
	}.
	\label{eq:parquet_decomposition_intro}
\end{equation}
Correspondingly, the vertex irreducible in the $\eh$ channel is
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\Gamma^\eh
	=
	\Lambda
	+
	\Phi^{\teh}
	+
	\Phi^\pp
	}.
	\label{eq:Gamma_eh_intro}
\end{equation}
Analogous expressions hold for the vertices irreducible in the
$\teh$ and $\pp$ channels.

Equations~\eqref{eq:Hedin_kernel_intro}
and~\eqref{eq:Gamma_eh_intro} already suggest a close connection between
the two formalisms.
Indeed, as we shall show explicitly, the 4-point Hedin kernel and the
$\eh$-irreducible parquet vertex are the same exact object,
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\Xi
	=
	\frac{\delta\Sigma}{\delta G}
	=
	\Gamma^{\eh}
	}.
	\label{eq:central_identity_intro}
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\frac{\delta\Sigma}{\delta G}
	=
	\Lambda
	+
	\Phi^{\teh}
	+
	\Phi^\pp
	}.
	\label{eq:central_parquet_hedin_intro}
\end{equation}
The Hedin kernel is therefore $\eh$ irreducible, but it does not contain
only electron-hole physics.
Rather, the fully irreducible vertex and the contributions reducible in
the other two channels are all contained in the same kernel.

This observation suggests a useful way of interpreting the relation
between parquet and Hedin theories.
Parquet may be viewed as a \emph{channel-resolved} representation in which
the different two-particle scattering processes are retained as explicit
variables.
Conversely, the 4-point Hedin formulation may be viewed as a
\emph{channel-downfolded} representation in which the crossed
electron-hole and particle-particle contributions are incorporated into
the single kernel $\Xi$.
The terminology ``upfolding'' and ``downfolding'' used throughout these
notes is meant in this representational sense and does not imply an
approximation.

Because I suck at diagrams, the purpose of these notes is to make this connection explicit without
relying primarily on diagrammatic arguments.
The central route will be
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\text{parquet}
	\qq{$\longrightarrow$}
	\text{4-point Hedin}
	\qq{$\longrightarrow$}
	\text{3-point Hedin}
	}.
	\label{eq:route_intro}
\end{equation}
The first transformation follows directly from the $\eh$ Bethe--Salpeter
equation of parquet theory.
Attaching the external one-particle propagators converts this equation
into the 4-point Hedin response equation and identifies its kernel with
$\Gamma^\eh$.
\titou{The second transformation relies on the special density-density structure
of the Coulomb interaction.}
After separating the direct Coulomb contribution from the full 4-point
kernel, the corresponding repeated electron-hole scattering can be
resummed into the screened interaction $W$.
\titou{At the same time, contraction of one fermionic pair with the bare density
vertex converts the 4-point response vertex into the familiar
3-point Hedin vertex.}

This derivation also clarifies why the standard Hedin formulation appears
considerably more compact than parquet theory.
The three parquet channels are not absent from Hedin theory; rather, two
of them have been absorbed into the three-frequency functional kernel.
Parquet reverses this compression by assigning the different classes of
two-particle-reducible diagrams to separate channel equations.
In this sense, the two formulations differ primarily in how the
two-particle information is distributed among their fundamental
variables.

For completeness, we shall also consider the analogous construction in
the particle-particle channel.
Selecting the $\pp$ parquet equation leads naturally to a 4-point
particle-particle Hedin formulation in which the other two parquet
channels are contained in the $\pp$-irreducible kernel.
The corresponding repeated pair scattering is described by a
4-point $T$ matrix. 
This parallel construction illustrates that ordinary Hedin theory is one
particular channel-centered representation of the more symmetric parquet
structure.

Throughout most of the derivation, infinitesimal equal-time shifts are
suppressed to lighten the notation.
They are restored whenever an equal-coordinate projection is performed
or the instantaneous Coulomb interaction is involved.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{One- and two-particle quantities}
\label{sec:quantities}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

Throughout these notes, numerical labels denote collective
space-spin-time variables,
\begin{equation}
	1 \equiv (\bx_1,t_1)
	= (\br_1,\sigma_1,t_1),
\end{equation}
and integration over repeated labels is implied.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{One-particle Green's function}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The time-ordered one-particle Green's function is defined as
\begin{equation}
	G(11')
	=
	-\ii
	\mel{
		\Psi_0^N
		}{
		\hat{T}
		[
			\hat{\psi}(1)
			\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}(1')
		]
		}{
		\Psi_0^N
	},
	\label{eq:G_def}
\end{equation}
where $\ket*{\Psi_0^N}$ is the exact $N$-electron ground state and
$\hat{T}$ is the time-ordering operator.

The interacting Green's function satisfies the Dyson equation
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	G(11')
	=
	G_0(11')
	+
	G_0(12)
	\Sigma(22')
	G(2'1')
	},
	\label{eq:Dyson}
\end{equation}
where $G_0$ is the non-interacting Green's function and $\Sigma$ is
the one-particle irreducible self-energy.

The Dyson equation provides the first example of a recurring idea in
many-body theory: reducible contributions are generated by iterating
an irreducible kernel.
The same principle will appear at the two-particle level through the
Bethe--Salpeter and parquet equations.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Two-particle Green's function and full vertex}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The time-ordered two-particle Green's function is
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	G_2(12;1'2')
	=
	(-\ii)^2
	\mel{
		\Psi_0^N
		}{
		\hat{T}
		[
			\hat{\psi}(1)
			\hat{\psi}(2)
			\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}(2')
			\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}(1')
		]
		}{
		\Psi_0^N
	}.
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:G2_def}
\end{equation}

The full two-particle vertex $F$ is defined by amputating the
connected part of $G_2$.
With the conventions adopted here,
\begin{align}
	G_2(12;34)
	={}&
	G(13)G(24)
	-
	G(14)G(23)
	\nonumber
	\\
	&-
	G(11')
	G(3'3)
	F(1'2';3'4')
	G(4'4)
	G(22').
	\label{eq:G2_F}
\end{align}
The first two terms correspond to the two possible independent
propagations of a pair of fermions, while $F$ contains all connected
two-particle scattering processes.

Fermionic antisymmetry implies the crossing relations
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\begin{aligned}
	F(12;34)
	&=
	-F(21;34)
	\\
	&=
	-F(12;43)
	\\
	&=
	+F(21;43).
	\end{aligned}
	}
	\label{eq:F_crossing}
\end{equation}
These relations will play an important role when connecting the two
electron-hole channels.

For a time-independent Hamiltonian, time-translation invariance removes
one overall time variable from a 4-point quantity.
Consequently, the full vertex $F$ depends on three independent time
differences or, equivalently, three independent frequencies.
Different choices of these frequency variables are naturally adapted to
the different parquet channels.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Electron-hole correlation function}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

For the connection with Hedin's equations, it is convenient to introduce
the electron-hole correlation function
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	L(12;1'2')
	=
	G(11')G(22')
	-
	G_2(12;1'2')
	}.
	\label{eq:L_def}
\end{equation}
Its independent-propagation contribution is
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	L_0(12;1'2')
	=
	G(12')G(21')
	}.
	\label{eq:L0_def}
\end{equation}
Indeed, in the absence of a connected two-particle vertex,
\begin{equation}
	G_2^{(0)}(12;1'2')
	=
	G(11')G(22')
	-
	G(12')G(21'),
\end{equation}
so that $L=L_0$.

Substituting Eq.~\eqref{eq:G2_F} into Eq.~\eqref{eq:L_def} gives
\begin{align}
	L(12;1'2')
	={}&
	L_0(12;1'2')
	\nonumber
	\\
	&+
	G(13)
	G(3'1')
	F(34;3'4')
	G(4'2')
	G(24).
	\label{eq:L_F_explicit}
\end{align}
Using Eq.~\eqref{eq:L0_def}, this can be written more compactly as
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\begin{aligned}
	L(12;1'2')
	={}&
	L_0(12;1'2')
	\\
	&+
	L_0(13';1'3)
	F(34;3'4')
	L_0(4'2;42').
	\end{aligned}
	}
	\label{eq:L_F}
\end{equation}

Equation~\eqref{eq:L_F} establishes the direct relation between the
full 4-point vertex and the electron-hole response.
In symbolic notation,
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	L
	=
	L_0
	+
	L_0 F L_0
	}.
	\label{eq:L_F_symbolic}
\end{equation}
This identity will be central below: inserting the electron-hole
Bethe--Salpeter equation for $F$ into Eq.~\eqref{eq:L_F_symbolic}
immediately produces the 4-point form of Hedin's response equation.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{Two-particle reducibility and parquet equations}
\label{sec:parquet}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

In contrast to the one-particle case, where reducibility is associated
with cutting a single propagator line, a 4-point diagram may become
disconnected by cutting two propagator lines.
For a number-conserving fermionic system, there are three topologically
distinct ways of performing such a cut.
They define the electron-hole ($\eh$), transverse electron-hole
($\teh$), and particle-particle ($\pp$) channels.

A connected 4-point diagram is said to be \emph{reducible} in a given
channel if it can be separated into two disconnected pieces by cutting
the corresponding pair of one-particle propagators.
Otherwise, it is irreducible in that channel.
A diagram that is irreducible in all three channels is said to be
\emph{fully two-particle irreducible}.

For a number-conserving theory, a two-particle-reducible diagram belongs
to one and only one of the three channels.
The full vertex therefore admits the exact decomposition
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\begin{aligned}
	F(12;34)
	={}&
	\Lambda(12;34)
	+
	\Phi^\eh(12;34)
	\\
	&+
	\Phi^{\teh}(12;34)
	+
	\Phi^\pp(12;34),
	\end{aligned}
	}
	\label{eq:parquet_decomposition}
\end{equation}
where $\Lambda$ is the fully two-particle-irreducible vertex and
$\Phi^\eh$, $\Phi^{\teh}$, and $\Phi^\pp$ collect all
diagrams reducible in the corresponding channel.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Channel-irreducible vertices}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

It is useful to introduce vertices that are irreducible in one specific
channel but may be reducible in the other two.
For the $\eh$ channel,
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\Gamma^\eh(12;34)
	=
	\Lambda(12;34)
	+
	\Phi^{\teh}(12;34)
	+
	\Phi^\pp(12;34)
	}.
	\label{eq:Gamma_eh}
\end{equation}
Similarly,
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\Gamma^{\teh}(12;34)
	=
	\Lambda(12;34)
	+
	\Phi^\eh(12;34)
	+
	\Phi^\pp(12;34)
	},
	\label{eq:Gamma_ehbar}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\Gamma^\pp(12;34)
	=
	\Lambda(12;34)
	+
	\Phi^\eh(12;34)
	+
	\Phi^{\teh}(12;34)
	}.
	\label{eq:Gamma_pp}
\end{equation}

The parquet decomposition,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:parquet_decomposition}, can therefore be written in three
equivalent forms,
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\begin{aligned}
	F
	&=
	\Gamma^\eh
	+
	\Phi^\eh
	\\
	&=
	\Gamma^{\teh}
	+
	\Phi^{\teh}
	\\
	&=
	\Gamma^\pp
	+
	\Phi^\pp.
	\end{aligned}
	}
	\label{eq:F_Gamma_Phi}
\end{equation}

Equation~\eqref{eq:F_Gamma_Phi} is particularly important for what
follows.
Choosing one of the three channels does not amount to neglecting the
other two.
For example, $\Gamma^\eh$ already contains the complete
$\teh$ and $\pp$ contributions through
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Gamma_eh}.
The distinction between the three formulations is therefore a distinction
in how the two-particle information is organized rather than in the
information retained.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Bethe--Salpeter equations}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The reducible contribution in each channel can be generated by iterating
the corresponding channel-irreducible vertex.
This leads to three Bethe--Salpeter equations for the same full vertex
$F$.

In the $\eh$ channel,
\begin{align}
	F(12;34)
	={}&
	\Gamma^\eh(12;34)
	\nonumber
	\\
	&+
	\Gamma^\eh(13';31')
	G(1'4')
	G(2'3')
	F(4'2;2'4).
	\label{eq:BSE_eh}
\end{align}
The second term is, by construction, reducible in the $\eh$ channel.
Hence,
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\begin{aligned}
	\Phi^\eh(12;34)
	={}&
	\Gamma^\eh(13';31')
	G(1'4')
	G(2'3')
	F(4'2;2'4).
	\end{aligned}
	}
	\label{eq:Phi_eh}
\end{equation}

In the transverse electron-hole channel,
\begin{align}
	F(12;34)
	={}&
	\Gamma^{\teh}(12;34)
	\nonumber
	\\
	&-
	\Gamma^{\teh}(3'2;32')
	G(1'3')
	G(2'4')
	F(14';1'4),
	\label{eq:BSE_ehbar}
\end{align}
so that
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\begin{aligned}
	\Phi^{\teh}(12;34)
	={}&
	-
	\Gamma^{\teh}(3'2;32')
	G(1'3')
	G(2'4')
	F(14';1'4).
	\end{aligned}
	}
	\label{eq:Phi_ehbar}
\end{equation}
The minus sign follows from fermionic crossing.

Finally, the particle-particle BSE reads
\begin{align}
	F(12;34)
	={}&
	\Gamma^\pp(12;34)
	\nonumber
	\\
	&-
	\frac{1}{2}
	\Gamma^\pp(12;1'2')
	G(1'3')
	G(2'4')
	F(3'4';34),
	\label{eq:BSE_pp}
\end{align}
and therefore
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\begin{aligned}
	\Phi^\pp(12;34)
	={}&
	-
	\frac{1}{2}
	\Gamma^\pp(12;1'2')
	G(1'3')
	G(2'4')
	F(3'4';34).
	\end{aligned}
	}
	\label{eq:Phi_pp}
\end{equation}
The factor $1/2$ originates from the antisymmetry of the two-fermion
pair space and avoids double counting equivalent particle-particle
configurations.

Equations~\eqref{eq:BSE_eh}--\eqref{eq:BSE_pp}, together with
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:Gamma_eh}--\eqref{eq:Gamma_pp}, form the two-particle
core of the parquet equations.
Each BSE resums diagrams reducible in one particular channel, while its
irreducible kernel contains the fully irreducible vertex and the
contributions reducible in the other two channels.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Crossing relations}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The three channel equations are not independent.
Their mutual consistency is enforced by fermionic crossing symmetry.
While the $\pp$-irreducible vertex possesses the same antisymmetry as the
full vertex, the two electron-hole irreducible vertices are related to
one another through
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\Gamma^\eh(21;34)
	=
	-
	\Gamma^{\teh}(12;34)
	},
	\label{eq:Gamma_crossing_1}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\Gamma^\eh(12;43)
	=
	-
	\Gamma^{\teh}(12;34)
	}.
	\label{eq:Gamma_crossing_2}
\end{equation}

Consequently, the three BSEs should not be viewed as three independent
descriptions of different physical systems.
They are three channel-resolved representations of the same full
4-point vertex.
Their simultaneous solution makes the different two-particle scattering
processes explicit while avoiding double counting between the channels.

This observation will be central in the following section.
In particular, selecting the $\eh$ Bethe--Salpeter equation while retaining
the exact kernel $\Gamma^\eh$ does not discard either the
$\teh$ or the $\pp$ channel.
Rather, according to Eq.~\eqref{eq:Gamma_eh}, these two channels are
already contained in the $\eh$-irreducible kernel.
This provides the starting point for the connection between parquet theory
and the 4-point Hedin equations.\cite{StarkeKresse2012,OrlandoRomanielloLoos2023}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{Closing the parquet equations}
\label{sec:closing_parquet}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The parquet equations determine the full two-particle vertex once the
fully irreducible vertex $\Lambda$ and the one-particle Green's function
are known.
To obtain a closed many-body theory, the two-particle vertex must in turn
be connected to the one-particle self-energy.

This connection follows from the equation of motion for the one-particle
Green's function.\cite{MarieLoos2025}
It yields the exact Dyson--Schwinger equation
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\Sigma(11')
	=
	-\ii
	v(12;3'2')
	G_2(3'2';32)
	G^{-1}(31')
	},
	\label{eq:SDE_G2}
\end{equation}
where $v$ is the bare Coulomb interaction.

Using the decomposition of the two-particle Green's function in terms of
the full vertex $F$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:G2_F}, one obtains
\begin{align}
	\Sigma(11')
	={}&
	\Sigma_{\Hx}(11')
	\nonumber
	\\
	&-
	\ii
	v(12;3'2')
	G(3'3)
	G(4'2)
	G(2'4)
	F(34;4'1').
	\label{eq:SDE_F}
\end{align}
Here $\Sigma_{\Hx}$ denotes the Hartree--exchange self-energy.
The second term is the exact correlation self-energy,
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\Sigma_\text{c}(11')
	=
	-\ii
	v(12;3'2')
	G(3'3)
	G(4'2)
	G(2'4)
	F(34;4'1')
	}.
	\label{eq:Sigma_c_F}
\end{equation}

Equation~\eqref{eq:Sigma_c_F} is the one-particle counterpart of the
parquet equations.
It shows that the full two-particle vertex feeds back into the
one-particle propagator through the self-energy.
Together with the Dyson equation,
\begin{equation}
	G
	=
	G_0
	+
	G_0 \Sigma G,
	\label{eq:Dyson_compact}
\end{equation}
the parquet decomposition and the three Bethe--Salpeter equations therefore
form a closed self-consistent hierarchy, provided that the fully
irreducible vertex $\Lambda$ is known.

For reference, the exact parquet system may be summarized schematically as
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\begin{aligned}
	G
	&=
	G_0 + G_0 \Sigma G,
	\\[1mm]
	\Sigma
	&=
	\Sigma_{\Hx}
	+
	\Sigma_\text{c}[G,F],
	\\[1mm]
	F
	&=
	\Lambda
	+
	\Phi^\eh
	+
	\Phi^{\teh}
	+
	\Phi^\pp,
	\\[1mm]
	F
	&=
	\Gamma^{r}
	+
	\Phi^{r},
	\qquad
	r\in\{\eh,\teh,\pp\},
	\\[1mm]
	\Gamma^{r}
	&=
	\Lambda
	+
	\sum_{r'\neq r}\Phi^{r'}.
	\end{aligned}
	}
	\label{eq:parquet_system_summary}
\end{equation}

The fully irreducible vertex $\Lambda$ is the only quantity in this set
that is not determined by the parquet equations themselves.
At the exact level it contains all diagrams that are irreducible in each
of the three two-particle channels.

A frequently employed closure is the \emph{parquet approximation}, in
which $\Lambda$ is replaced by the antisymmetrized bare interaction,
\begin{equation}
	\Lambda(12;1'2')
	\approx
	-\ii \bar v(12;1'2')
	=
	-\ii
	\left[
		v(12;1'2')
		-
		v(12;2'1')
	\right].
	\label{eq:parquet_approximation}
\end{equation}
This approximation will not be required in the following derivation:
the connection with Hedin's equations is established at the exact level.


%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{From parquet to the 4-point Hedin equation}
\label{sec:parquet_to_hedin4}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

We now establish the central connection between parquet theory and the
4-point formulation of Hedin's equations.

The derivation relies on two identities introduced in the preceding
sections.
The first relates the electron-hole correlation function to the full
4-point vertex,
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	L
	=
	L_0
	+
	L_0 F L_0
	},
	\label{eq:L_F_again}
\end{equation}
whereas the second is the electron-hole Bethe--Salpeter equation,
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	F
	=
	\Gamma^\eh
	+
	\Gamma^\eh L_0 F
	}.
	\label{eq:BSE_eh_symbolic}
\end{equation}
No approximation is involved in either relation.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Attaching the external propagators}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

Substituting Eq.~\eqref{eq:BSE_eh_symbolic} into
Eq.~\eqref{eq:L_F_again} gives
\begin{align}
	L
	&=
	L_0
	+
	L_0
	\left(
		\Gamma^\eh
		+
		\Gamma^\eh L_0F
	\right)
	L_0
	\nonumber
	\\
	&=
	L_0
	+
	L_0\Gamma^\eh L_0
	+
	L_0\Gamma^\eh L_0FL_0
	\nonumber
	\\
	&=
	L_0
	+
	L_0\Gamma^\eh
	\left(
		L_0+L_0FL_0
	\right).
\end{align}
Using Eq.~\eqref{eq:L_F_again} once more, one immediately obtains
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	L
	=
	L_0
	+
	L_0\Gamma^\eh L
	}.
	\label{eq:L_BSE_Gammaeh}
\end{equation}

In explicit space-spin-time notation,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:L_BSE_Gammaeh} reads
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\begin{aligned}
	L(12;1'2')
	={}&
	L_0(12;1'2')
	\\
	&+
	L_0(13';1'3)
	\Gamma^\eh(34;3'4')
	L(4'2;42').
	\end{aligned}
	}
	\label{eq:L_BSE_Gammaeh_explicit}
\end{equation}

Equation~\eqref{eq:L_BSE_Gammaeh} is precisely the 4-point
Bethe--Salpeter form of the Hedin response equation.
Thus, attaching the external one-particle propagators to the $\eh$ parquet
Bethe--Salpeter equation converts it directly into the 4-point Hedin
equation.

Conversely, amputating the external propagators in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:L_BSE_Gammaeh} reproduces
Eq.~\eqref{eq:BSE_eh_symbolic}.
The two equations therefore contain exactly the same two-particle
information.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Identification of the Hedin kernel}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

In the 4-point formulation of Hedin's equations, the response function
satisfies\cite{StarkeKresse2012,OrlandoRomanielloLoos2023}
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	L
	=
	L_0
	+
	L_0 \Xi L
	},
	\label{eq:Hedin4_response}
\end{equation}
where the 4-point irreducible kernel is defined by the functional
derivative
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\Xi(12;1'2')
	=
	\frac{\delta\Sigma(11')}
	     {\delta G(2'2)}
	}.
	\label{eq:Xi_def}
\end{equation}

Comparison of Eqs.~\eqref{eq:L_BSE_Gammaeh} and
\eqref{eq:Hedin4_response} therefore yields the exact identity
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\Xi
	=
	\Gamma^\eh
	}.
	\label{eq:Xi_Gammaeh}
\end{equation}
Equivalently,
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\frac{\delta\Sigma(11')}
	     {\delta G(2'2)}
	=
	\Gamma^\eh(12;1'2')
	}.
	\label{eq:functional_Gammaeh}
\end{equation}

It is important that Eq.~\eqref{eq:functional_Gammaeh} involves the
\emph{full} self-energy.
The separation of the Hartree contribution from the exchange-correlation
kernel will be performed in Sec.~\ref{sec:fourpoint_hedin}.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Parquet content of the Hedin kernel}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The parquet decomposition now gives an immediate interpretation of the
4-point Hedin kernel.
From Eq.~\eqref{eq:Gamma_eh},
\begin{equation}
	\Gamma^\eh
	=
	\Lambda
	+
	\Phi^{\teh}
	+
	\Phi^\pp.
\end{equation}
Combining this relation with Eq.~\eqref{eq:Xi_Gammaeh} gives
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\Xi
	=
	\frac{\delta\Sigma}{\delta G}
	=
	\Lambda
	+
	\Phi^{\teh}
	+
	\Phi^\pp
	}.
	\label{eq:central_identity}
\end{equation}

Equation~\eqref{eq:central_identity} is the central relation connecting
the Hedin and parquet organizations of the two-particle problem.

The Hedin kernel is irreducible in the $\eh$ channel, but this does not
mean that it contains only electron-hole physics.
On the contrary, its exact content consists of the fully
two-particle-irreducible vertex together with all contributions reducible
in the transverse electron-hole and particle-particle channels.

In parquet theory these three contributions,
\begin{equation}
	\Lambda,
	\qquad
	\Phi^{\teh},
	\qquad
	\Phi^\pp,
\end{equation}
are retained as separate objects and determined through coupled channel
equations.
In the 4-point Hedin representation they are combined into the single
three-frequency kernel $\Xi$.

This provides a precise meaning to the notion of \emph{channel
downfolding} used in these notes:
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\left\{
		\Lambda,
		\Phi^{\teh},
		\Phi^\pp
	\right\}
	\qq{$\longrightarrow$}
	\Xi
	=
	\Gamma^\eh
	}.
	\label{eq:channel_downfolding}
\end{equation}
No physical contribution has been discarded in this transformation.
Only the choice of explicit variables has changed.

Conversely, parquet theory may be regarded as a channel-resolved
representation of the same kernel,
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\Xi
	\qq{$\longrightarrow$}
	\Lambda
	+
	\Phi^{\teh}
	+
	\Phi^\pp
	}.
	\label{eq:channel_upfolding}
\end{equation}

The distinction between the two formulations is therefore particularly
transparent at the exact level:
Hedin theory retains one channel-irreducible kernel as a fundamental
quantity, whereas parquet theory resolves the internal two-particle
structure of this kernel according to reducibility in the remaining
scattering channels.

\subsection{First-order consistency}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

It is useful to verify Eq.~\eqref{eq:Xi_xc_parquet} at first order.

\begin{equation}
	v_\text{x}(12;1'2')
	=
	V(12;2'1'),
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\Lambda^{(1)}

	-\ii

	\label{eq:Lambda_first_order}

\subsection{Response irreducible with respect to the direct interaction}

Using Eq.~\eqref{eq:Xi_split}, the full 4-point response equation reads
\begin{equation}
	L
	=
	L_0
	+
	L_0
	\left(
		-\ii V
		+
		\Xi_{\xc}
	\right)
	L.
	\label{eq:L_split}
\end{equation}

We introduce the response function $\widetilde L$ that is irreducible
with respect to insertions of the direct Coulomb interaction,
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\widetilde L
	=
	L_0
	+
	L_0
	\Xi_{\xc}
	\widetilde L.
	}
	\label{eq:Ltilde_def}
\end{equation}
In explicit notation,
\begin{equation}
	\begin{aligned}
	\widetilde L(12;1'2')

	L_0(12;1'2')

\end{equation}

\begin{equation}

	L
	=
	\widetilde L
	-
	\ii
	\widetilde L
	V
	L.
	}
	\label{eq:L_Ltilde}
\end{equation}

Thus $\widetilde L$ contains all exchange-correlation vertex corrections,
including the downfolded $\teh$ and $\pp$ channels, while repeated

$\widetilde L$ and may be resummed separately.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{4-point screened interaction}
\label{sec:fourpoint_screening}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

We now collect the repeated insertions of the direct Coulomb interaction
into a screened 4-point interaction.\cite{StarkeKresse2012,MarieAmmarLoos2024}

With the conventions introduced above, the full Hedin kernel is
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\Xi
	=
	-\ii V
	+
	\Xi_{\xc}
	},
	\label{eq:Xi_split_correct}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
	V(12;1'2')
	=
	\delta(11')
	\delta(22')
	v(12)
	\label{eq:V_fourpoint}
\end{equation}
is the 4-point representation of the bare Coulomb interaction.

The response function irreducible with respect to insertions of $V$
is defined by
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\widetilde L
	=
	L_0
	+
	L_0
	\Xi_{\xc}
	\widetilde L
	}.
	\label{eq:Ltilde_again}
\end{equation}
As shown in the previous section, the full response can then be written as
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	L
	=
	\widetilde L
	-
	\ii
	\widetilde L
	V
	L
	}.
	\label{eq:L_Ltilde_correct}
\end{equation}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Definition of the screened interaction}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The dynamically screened 4-point interaction is defined by
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	W
	=
	V
	-
	\ii
	V L V
	}.
	\label{eq:W4_def}
\end{equation}
Here and in the following, products between 4-point quantities imply
integration over the intermediate fermionic coordinates, with the
external-leg ordering determined by the electron-hole channel.

Equation~\eqref{eq:W4_def} is the 4-point analogue of the familiar
relation
\begin{equation}
	W
	=
	v
	+
	v\chi v
\end{equation}
between the screened Coulomb interaction and the reducible density
response.
The apparent difference in sign follows from the convention relating
the electron-hole correlation function $L$ to the density response.

Substituting Eq.~\eqref{eq:L_Ltilde_correct} into
Eq.~\eqref{eq:W4_def}, we obtain
\begin{align}
	W
	&=
	V
	-
	\ii
	V
	\left(
		\widetilde L
		-
		\ii
		\widetilde L V L
	\right)
	V
	\nonumber
	\\
	&=
	V
	-
	\ii
	V\widetilde L V
	-
	V\widetilde L V L V.
	\label{eq:W4_intermediate}
\end{align}
Using again the definition
\begin{equation}
	W
	=
	V
	-
	\ii V L V,
\end{equation}
the last two terms can be collected to give
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	W
	=
	V
	-
	\ii
	V
	\widetilde L
	W
	}.
	\label{eq:W4_Dyson}
\end{equation}

Equation~\eqref{eq:W4_Dyson} is the 4-point screening equation.
It has precisely the same Dyson structure as the usual Hedin equation
for the screened interaction, but all quantities still carry four
fermionic coordinates.

The physical meaning of Eq.~\eqref{eq:W4_Dyson} is particularly simple.
The response $\widetilde L$ contains all exchange-correlation vertex
effects through $\Xi_{\xc}$, while repeated insertions of the direct
Coulomb interaction are resummed into $W$.

\subsection{4-point irreducible response vertex}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

It is useful to factor the response $\widetilde L$ into independent
propagation and a 4-point vertex.
We therefore introduce the 4-point irreducible response vertex
$\widetilde\Gamma$ through
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\widetilde\Gamma
	=
	1
	+
	\Xi_{\xc}
	L_0
	\widetilde\Gamma
	}.
	\label{eq:Gamma4_def}
\end{equation}
Here the identity operator in electron-hole pair space is understood as
\begin{equation}
	1(12;1'2')
	=
	\delta(12')
	\delta(21').
	\label{eq:eh_identity}
\end{equation}

Multiplication by $L_0$ immediately gives
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\widetilde L
	=
	L_0
	\widetilde\Gamma
	}.
	\label{eq:Ltilde_Gamma4}
\end{equation}
Indeed,
\begin{align}
	L_0\widetilde\Gamma
	&=
	L_0
	+
	L_0
	\Xi_{\xc}
	L_0
	\widetilde\Gamma
	\nonumber
	\\
	&=
	L_0
	+
	L_0
	\Xi_{\xc}
	\widetilde L,
\end{align}
which coincides with Eq.~\eqref{eq:Ltilde_again}.

The 4-point screening equation can therefore also be written as
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	W
	=
	V
	-
	\ii
	V
	L_0
	\widetilde\Gamma
	W
	}.
	\label{eq:W4_Gamma4}
\end{equation}

At this stage the relation with the conventional Hedin equations is
already apparent.
The quantity
\begin{equation}
	-\ii
	L_0
	\widetilde\Gamma
\end{equation}
is the 4-point precursor of the irreducible polarization, while
$\widetilde\Gamma$ is the 4-point precursor of the usual 3-point
Hedin vertex.

Importantly, the complete parquet content of the vertex corrections
remains contained in
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\Xi_{\xc}
	=
	\Lambda
	+
	\Phi^{\teh}
	+
	\Phi^\pp
	+
	\ii V
	}.
	\label{eq:Xi_xc_parquet_again}
\end{equation}
Thus the introduction of the screened interaction $W$ has not removed
the transverse electron-hole or particle-particle channels.
It has only resummed the direct Coulomb part of the distinguished
electron-hole channel into a separate effective interaction.

\section{4-point self-energy}
\label{sec:fourpoint_selfenergy}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

It remains to express the one-particle self-energy in terms of the
quantities introduced in the previous section.\cite{StarkeKresse2012,MarieAmmarLoos2024}
Starting from the exact equation of motion, the exchange-correlation
self-energy can be written in the 4-point form
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\Sigma_{\xc}(11')
	=
	\ii
	G(33')
	W(12';32)
	\widetilde\Gamma(3'2;1'2')
	}.
	\label{eq:Sigma_xc_fourpoint}
\end{equation}
As throughout these notes, integration over repeated space-spin-time
variables is implied.

Equation~\eqref{eq:Sigma_xc_fourpoint} is the 4-point counterpart of
the familiar Hedin expression
\begin{equation}
	\Sigma_{\xc}
	=
	\ii G W \Gamma.
\end{equation}
At this stage, however, both $W$ and $\widetilde\Gamma$ remain genuine
4-point quantities.

The vertex $\widetilde\Gamma$ is the irreducible response vertex introduced
in the previous section,
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\widetilde\Gamma(12;1'2')
	=
	\delta(12')
	\delta(1'2)
	+
	\Xi_{\xc}(13';1'3)
	G(34)
	G(4'3')
	\widetilde\Gamma(42;4'2')
	}.
	\label{eq:Gamma4_explicit}
\end{equation}
Correspondingly, the irreducible electron-hole response is
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\widetilde L(12;1'2')
	=
	G(13)
	G(3'1')
	\widetilde\Gamma(32;3'2')
	}.
	\label{eq:Ltilde_Gamma_explicit}
\end{equation}

Together with
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	W
	=
	V
	-
	\ii
	V\widetilde L W
	},
	\label{eq:W4_again}
\end{equation}
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:Sigma_xc_fourpoint}--\eqref{eq:W4_again} constitute the
4-point form of the central Hedin relations.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Connection with the Schwinger--Dyson equation}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

Equation~\eqref{eq:Sigma_xc_fourpoint} does not introduce a new
approximation or an additional physical ingredient.
It is an exact reorganization of the Schwinger--Dyson equation introduced
in Sec.~\ref{sec:closing_parquet}.

Indeed, the exchange-correlation self-energy consists of the exchange
contribution together with the correlation term generated by the full
4-point vertex,
\begin{equation}
	\Sigma_{\xc}
	=
	\Sigma_\text{x}
	+
	\Sigma_\text{c}[F].
	\label{eq:Sigma_xc_SDE}
\end{equation}
The exchange term corresponds to the bare part of the screened
interaction and of the response vertex, whereas the remaining terms are
generated by repeated insertions of the electron-hole response.

The identities
\begin{equation}
	L
	=
	\widetilde L
	-
	\ii
	\widetilde L V L
	\label{eq:L_Ltilde_selfenergy}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
	W
	=
	V
	-
	\ii
	V L V
	\label{eq:W_L_selfenergy}
\end{equation}
allow these repeated direct Coulomb insertions to be collected into $W$,
while
\begin{equation}
	\widetilde L
	=
	L_0\widetilde\Gamma
\end{equation}
collects the remaining vertex corrections into
$\widetilde\Gamma$.
The result of this exact rearrangement is
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Sigma_xc_fourpoint}.

Thus the two exact representations
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\Sigma_{\xc}
	=
	\Sigma_\text{c}
	+
	\Sigma_\text{c}[F]
	=
	\ii G W\widetilde\Gamma
	}
	\label{eq:SDE_Hedin_equivalence}
\end{equation}
contain identical information.
The first expression is naturally associated with the full 4-point
scattering vertex $F$, whereas the second separates direct electron-hole
screening from the remaining vertex corrections.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{The complete 4-point Hedin system}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

We may now collect the equations obtained so far.
Using a Hartree Green's function $G_H$ as the reference propagator,
the exact 4-point Hedin equations can be written schematically as
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\begin{aligned}
	G
	&=
	G_H
	+
	G_H\Sigma_{\xc}G,
	\\[1mm]
	\Sigma_{\xc}
	&=
	\ii G W\widetilde\Gamma,
	\\[1mm]
	\widetilde L
	&=
	L_0\widetilde\Gamma,
	\\[1mm]
	W
	&=
	V
	-
	\ii V\widetilde L W,
	\\[1mm]
	\widetilde\Gamma
	&=
	1
	+
	\Xi_{\xc}
	L_0
	\widetilde\Gamma.
	\end{aligned}
	}
	\label{eq:fourpoint_Hedin_system}
\end{equation}
The exchange-correlation kernel closing this system is
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\Xi_{\xc}
	=
	\frac{\delta\Sigma_{\xc}}{\delta G}
	=
	\Lambda
	+
	\Phi^{\teh}
	+
	\Phi^\pp
	+
	\ii V
	}.
	\label{eq:Xi_xc_parquet_complete}
\end{equation}

Equation~\eqref{eq:fourpoint_Hedin_system} is already Hedin's theory.
The usual distinction between two-point, 3-point, and 4-point
objects has simply not yet been exploited.
All quantities are expressed in a representation that remains covariant
under unitary transformations of the one-particle basis.

From the parquet perspective, the structure of the 4-point theory is
particularly transparent.
The transverse electron-hole and particle-particle channels are contained
in $\Xi_{\xc}$ through Eq.~\eqref{eq:Xi_xc_parquet_complete}, whereas
repeated scattering associated with the direct Coulomb interaction is
resummed explicitly into $W$.

The remaining step in recovering the conventional Hedin equations is
therefore not another many-body approximation or channel elimination.
It is a reduction in representation that exploits the special
density-density structure of the Coulomb interaction.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{From 4-point to conventional Hedin equations}
\label{sec:four_to_three}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The equations obtained in the preceding section are already an exact
formulation of Hedin's theory.\cite{StarkeKresse2012,MarieAmmarLoos2024}
The remaining step is a reduction of representation.

This reduction relies on the local density structure of the Coulomb
interaction in the space-time representation.
It should not be confused with a reduction in the number of orbital
indices in an arbitrary one-particle basis.
In particular, a three-leg field-theoretical quantity may still possess
four orbital indices after projection onto a finite basis.


%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Screened interaction}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

In the space-time representation, the direct 4-point screened
interaction has the structure
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\mathcal W(12;1'2')
	=
	\delta(11')
	\delta(22')
	W(12).
	}
	\label{eq:W4_W2}
\end{equation}
The nontrivial screened interaction is therefore the usual two-point
quantity $W(12)$.

The irreducible polarizability is obtained from the equal-coordinate
projection
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	P(12)
	=
	-\ii
	\widetilde L(12;1^+2^+).
	}
	\label{eq:P_projection}
\end{equation}

Applying Eqs.~\eqref{eq:W4_W2} and
\eqref{eq:P_projection} to the 4-point Dyson equation
\eqref{eq:W4} yields
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	W(12)
	=
	v(12)
	+
	v(13)
	P(34)
	W(42).
	}
	\label{eq:W_standard}
\end{equation}


%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{3-point vertex}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The conventional 3-point Hedin vertex is obtained by identifying one
incoming and outgoing coordinate of the 4-point vertex,
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\gamma(123)
	=
	\widetilde\Gamma(13;23).
	}
	\label{eq:gamma_projection}
\end{equation}

The zeroth-order term provides an immediate consistency check,
\begin{equation}
	\widetilde\Gamma_0(13;23)
	=
	\delta(13)
	\delta(23)
	=
	\delta(12)
	\delta(13),
\end{equation}
which is precisely the bare 3-point Hedin vertex.

Applying the projection
Eq.~\eqref{eq:gamma_projection} to
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Gamma4} gives
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\gamma(123)
	=
	\delta(12)
	\delta(13)
	+
	\Xi_{\xc}(12;45)
	G(46)
	G(75)
	\gamma(673).
	}
	\label{eq:gamma_standard}
\end{equation}

This is the conventional Hedin vertex equation.


%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Polarizability}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

Combining Eqs.~\eqref{eq:Ltilde_Gamma4},
\eqref{eq:P_projection}, and
\eqref{eq:gamma_projection} gives
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	P(12)
	=
	-\ii
	G(13)
	G(41)
	\gamma(342).
	}
	\label{eq:P_standard}
\end{equation}

Thus the conventional irreducible polarizability is simply the
equal-coordinate projection of the 4-point irreducible response.


%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Self-energy}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The same projections reduce
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Sigma_xc_fourpoint} to
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\Sigma_{\xc}(12)
	=
	\ii
	G(14)
	W(1^+3)
	\gamma(423).
	}
	\label{eq:Sigma_standard}
\end{equation}

The infinitesimal positive time shift in $W(1^+3)$ specifies the
appropriate ordering at the interaction vertex.


%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Standard Hedin equations}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

Collecting the preceding results gives the conventional Hedin equations,
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\begin{aligned}
	\gamma(123)
	&=
	\delta(12)\delta(13)
	+
	\Xi_{\xc}(12;45)
	G(46)G(75)
	\gamma(673),
	\\[1mm]
	P(12)
	&=
	-\ii
	G(13)G(41)
	\gamma(342),
	\\[1mm]
	W(12)
	&=
	v(12)
	+
	v(13)P(34)W(42),
	\\[1mm]
	\Sigma_{\xc}(12)
	&=
	\ii
	G(14)W(1^+3)
	\gamma(423),
	\\[1mm]
	G(12)
	&=
	G_H(12)
	+
	G_H(13)
	\Sigma_{\xc}(34)
	G(42).
	\end{aligned}
	}
	\label{eq:Hedin_standard}
\end{equation}

The kernel appearing in the vertex equation is
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\Xi_{\xc}(12;1'2')
	=
	\frac{
		\delta\Sigma_{\xc}(11')
	}{
		\delta G(2'2)
	}.
	}
	\label{eq:Hedin_kernel_standard}
\end{equation}

The complete route developed in these notes can therefore be summarized as
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\begin{array}{ccccc}
	\text{parquet}
	&
	\longrightarrow
	&
	\text{4-point Hedin}
	&
	\longrightarrow
	&
	\text{conventional Hedin},
	\\[1mm]
	\Lambda,\Phi^{\teh},\Phi^\pp
	&
	\longrightarrow
	&
	\Xi=\Gamma^\eh
	&
	\longrightarrow
	&
	W,P,\gamma.
	\end{array}
	}
	\label{eq:complete_route}
\end{equation}

The two arrows have different meanings.
The first is a channel downfolding:
the fully irreducible, transverse electron-hole, and particle-particle
contributions are combined into the single $\eh$-irreducible kernel
$\Xi$.
The second performs no additional channel elimination.
It is an equal-coordinate projection that exploits the local
density-density structure of the Coulomb interaction in the space-time
representation.


%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{Parquet as a channel-resolved Hedin theory}
\label{sec:interpretation}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The preceding derivation establishes an exact connection between parquet
theory and Hedin's equations.
It is useful to pause at this stage and examine what this connection means
for the organization of the two-particle problem.

The central identity is
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\Xi
	=
	\Gamma^\eh
	=
	\Lambda
	+
	\Phi^{\teh}
	+
	\Phi^\pp
	}.
	\label{eq:interpretation_central}
\end{equation}
The 4-point Hedin kernel is therefore nothing but the vertex irreducible
in the distinguished electron-hole channel.

This result immediately clarifies an apparent difference between Hedin
and parquet theories.
Parquet treats the different two-particle scattering channels as explicit
objects, whereas Hedin combines the channels that are irreducible with
respect to the selected electron-hole propagation into a single kernel.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Channel downfolding}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The $\eh$-irreducible parquet vertex satisfies
\begin{equation}
	\Gamma^\eh
	=
	\Lambda
	+
	\Phi^{\teh}
	+
	\Phi^\pp.
	\label{eq:Gamma_eh_interpretation}
\end{equation}
Thus, passing from parquet to the 4-point Hedin representation amounts
to replacing the three explicit quantities
\begin{equation}
	\Lambda,
	\qquad
	\Phi^{\teh},
	\qquad
	\Phi^\pp,
\end{equation}
by their sum,
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\left\{
	\Lambda,
	\Phi^{\teh},
	\Phi^\pp
	\right\}
	\longrightarrow
	\Gamma^\eh.
	}
	\label{eq:channel_downfolding_interpretation}
\end{equation}

The term \emph{downfolding} is used here only to describe this change of
representation.
No approximation is implied.
The full transverse electron-hole and particle-particle dynamics remain
present in $\Gamma^\eh$.

Conversely, parquet theory may be regarded as an \emph{upfolding} of the
Hedin kernel in which its internal two-particle structure is resolved
according to reducibility in the other scattering channels,
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\Gamma^\eh
	\longrightarrow
	\Lambda
	+
	\Phi^{\teh}
	+
	\Phi^\pp.
	}
	\label{eq:channel_upfolding_interpretation}
\end{equation}

The advantage of the parquet representation is that the different
scattering processes obey separate Bethe--Salpeter equations.
The price is an enlarged set of coupled 4-point quantities.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{What is resummed into the screened interaction?}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

A second reorganization occurs when the full Hedin kernel is separated
into its direct Hartree contribution and its exchange-correlation part,
\begin{equation}
	\Xi
	=
	-\ii V
	+
	\Xi_{\xc}.
\end{equation}
Using Eq.~\eqref{eq:interpretation_central},
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\Xi_{\xc}
	=
	\Lambda
	+
	\Phi^{\teh}
	+
	\Phi^\pp
	+
	\ii V.
	}
	\label{eq:Xi_xc_interpretation}
\end{equation}

The direct Coulomb interaction is therefore treated differently from the
remaining pieces of the $\eh$-irreducible vertex.
Repeated insertions of $V$ are resummed explicitly into the screened
interaction $W$, whereas the remaining vertex corrections stay inside
$\Xi_{\xc}$.

Schematically,
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\begin{aligned}
	\text{direct $\eh$ Coulomb scattering}
	&\longrightarrow
	W,
	\\
	\Lambda
	+
	\Phi^{\teh}
	+
	\Phi^\pp
	+\ii V
	&\longrightarrow
	\Xi_{\xc}.
	\end{aligned}
	}
	\label{eq:two_downfoldings}
\end{equation}

This provides a useful interpretation of the conventional Hedin
organization.
One particular part of the electron-hole channel---the repeated direct
Coulomb interaction---is represented explicitly by a bosonic screened
interaction.
The remaining two-particle structure is retained in the
three-frequency kernel $\Xi_{\xc}$.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Frequency structure}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

A general 4-point vertex depends on three independent frequencies.
Parquet theory exploits the fact that the three scattering channels are
naturally described using different transfer frequencies.

For the direct electron-hole channel one may use variables of the form
\begin{equation}
	(\nu,\nu',\omega_\eh),
\end{equation}
whereas the transverse electron-hole and particle-particle channels are
naturally associated with
\begin{equation}
	(\nu,\nu',\omega_{\teh})
	\qquad\text{and}\qquad
	(\nu,\nu',\omega_\pp),
\end{equation}
respectively.

These are different parametrizations of the same three-frequency
4-point object.
In parquet theory, each reducible contribution is described in the
frequency variables naturally adapted to its own scattering channel.

After downfolding to the Hedin representation, the contributions
$\Phi^{\teh}$ and $\Phi^\pp$ are contained in
\begin{equation}
	\Xi_{\xc}
	(
		\nu,
		\nu',
		\omega_\eh
	).
\end{equation}
Their natural transfer-frequency dependence is therefore encoded in the
full three-frequency structure of the Hedin kernel.

By contrast, the repeated direct electron-hole screening is isolated into
the simpler bosonic quantity
\begin{equation}
	W(\omega_\eh).
\end{equation}

Thus the conventional Hedin representation may be viewed schematically as
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\text{three channel-resolved frequency structures}
	\qq{$\longrightarrow$}
	W(\omega_\eh)
	+
	\Xi_{\xc}(\nu,\nu',\omega_\eh).
	}
	\label{eq:frequency_downfolding}
\end{equation}

This is another way of seeing that Hedin theory has not discarded the
other parquet channels.
Their frequency dependence has simply been absorbed into the
multifrequency vertex kernel.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Explicit versus implicit channel coupling}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The different organization of the two theories also affects the way their
nonlinearity appears.

For a fixed channel-irreducible vertex, each parquet Bethe--Salpeter
equation is linear in the full vertex.
For example,
\begin{equation}
	F
	=
	\Gamma^\eh
	+
	\Gamma^\eh
	L_0
	F
\end{equation}
is a linear integral equation for $F$ if $\Gamma^\eh$ is known.

The complete parquet problem is nevertheless nonlinear because the
channel-irreducible vertices depend on the reducible contributions in the
other channels,
\begin{equation}
	\Gamma^{r}
	=
	\Lambda
	+
	\sum_{r'\neq r}
	\Phi^{r'}.
\end{equation}
The nonlinearity therefore appears as an explicit feedback between
different channel equations.

In Hedin theory, these channel variables are no longer retained
separately.
Instead, their coupled dynamics are compressed into the functional kernel
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\Xi_{\xc}
	=
	\frac{\delta\Sigma_{\xc}}{\delta G}.
	}
	\label{eq:implicit_channel_coupling}
\end{equation}

This suggests the qualitative distinction
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\begin{array}{ll}
	\text{parquet:}
	&
	\text{explicit channel coupling in an enlarged variable space},
	\\[1mm]
	\text{Hedin:}
	&
	\text{compact but functionally entangled channel dependence}.
	\end{array}
	}
	\label{eq:nonlinearity_comparison}
\end{equation}
This statement should not be interpreted as defining an invariant
mathematical ``degree of nonlinearity.''
The exact theories contain the same information.
Rather, the nonlinearity is distributed differently among the variables.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Two distinct transformations}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The complete parquet-to-Hedin construction contains two conceptually
different steps.

The first,
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\text{parquet}
	\longrightarrow
	\text{4-point Hedin},
	}
\end{equation}
changes the organization of the two-particle physics by replacing the
explicit channel decomposition with the single irreducible kernel
$\Gamma^\eh$.

The second,
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\text{4-point Hedin}
	\longrightarrow
	\text{conventional Hedin},
	}
\end{equation}
does not perform any additional channel elimination.
The second transformation does not involve any further channel
downfolding.
In the space-time representation, the density-density structure of the
Coulomb interaction permits equal-coordinate projections of the covariant
4-point quantities,
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	P(12)
	=
	-\ii\widetilde L(12;1^+2^+),
	\qquad
	\gamma(123)
	=
	\widetilde\Gamma(13;23).
	}
\end{equation}
These projections yield the conventional two-point polarizability and
3-point Hedin vertex.
In a generic orbital representation, the corresponding objects may still
carry four orbital indices.
The complete construction can therefore be summarized as
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\begin{array}{ccccc}
	\Lambda,\Phi^{\teh},\Phi^\pp
	&
	\longrightarrow
	&
	\Gamma^\eh=\Xi
	&
	\longrightarrow
	&
	W,\gamma
	\\[1mm]
	\text{parquet}
	&&
	\text{4-point Hedin}
	&&
	\text{conventional Hedin}.
	\end{array}
	}
	\label{eq:complete_conceptual_map}
\end{equation}

The first arrow is a channel downfolding.
The second is a reduction of representation.
This distinction provides a useful conceptual separation between the
parquet and Hedin organizations of the exact many-body problem.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{Particle-particle analogue}
\label{sec:pp_analogue}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The preceding construction singled out the $\eh$ channel and led naturally
to the conventional Hedin equations.\cite{MarieRomanielloLoos2024,MarieRomanielloBlaseLoos2025}
The same reasoning can be applied to the particle-particle channel.
This provides a useful complementary example of the channel-downfolding
picture developed above.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Particle-particle Bethe--Salpeter equation}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The parquet Bethe--Salpeter equation in the $\pp$ channel reads
\begin{align}
	F(12;34)
	={}&
	\Gamma^\pp(12;34)
	\nonumber
	\\
	&-
	\frac{1}{2}
	\Gamma^\pp(12;1'2')
	G(1'3')
	G(2'4')
	F(3'4';34).
	\label{eq:BSE_pp_again}
\end{align}
The corresponding $\pp$ propagator satisfies
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\begin{aligned}
	K(12;1'2')
	={}&
	K_0(12;1'2')
	\\
	&-
	\frac{1}{2}
	K(12;44')
	\Gamma^\pp(44';33')
	K_0(33';1'2'),
	\end{aligned}
	}
	\label{eq:K_parquet}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	K_0(12;1'2')
	=
	\frac{1}{2}
	\left[
		G(11')G(22')
		-
		G(21')G(12')
	\right].
	}
	\label{eq:K0_def}
\end{equation}

The factor $1/2$ reflects the antisymmetry of the fermionic pair space.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Particle-particle Hedin kernel}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The same $\pp$ response can be obtained from an anomalous functional
derivative.\cite{MarieRomanielloBlaseLoos2025}
Introducing a pairing field and taking the number-conserving limit after
differentiation gives
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	K
	=
	K_0
	-
	K
	\Xi^\pp
	K_0,
	}
	\label{eq:K_pp_Hedin}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\Xi^\pp(12;1'2')
	=
	\left.
	\frac{
		\delta\Sigma^{ee}(1'2')
	}{
		\delta G^{ee}(12)
	}
	\right|_{U=0}.
	}
	\label{eq:Xi_pp_def}
\end{equation}

Comparison of Eqs.~\eqref{eq:K_parquet} and
\eqref{eq:K_pp_Hedin} immediately yields
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\Gamma^\pp
	=
	2\Xi^\pp.
	}
	\label{eq:Gamma_pp_Xi_pp}
\end{equation}
The factor of two is purely conventional and originates from the
normalization of the antisymmetric pair space.

Using the parquet decomposition,
\begin{equation}
	\Gamma^\pp
	=
	\Lambda
	+
	\Phi^\eh
	+
	\Phi^{\teh},
\end{equation}
we therefore obtain
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	2\Xi^\pp
	=
	\Lambda
	+
	\Phi^\eh
	+
	\Phi^{\teh}.
	}
	\label{eq:Xi_pp_parquet}
\end{equation}

Equation~\eqref{eq:Xi_pp_parquet} is the particle-particle counterpart of
the central relation
\begin{equation}
	\Xi
	=
	\Gamma^\eh
	=
	\Lambda
	+
	\Phi^{\teh}
	+
	\Phi^\pp.
\end{equation}
In the $\pp$ representation, the two electron-hole channels are downfolded
into the $\pp$-irreducible kernel.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Particle-particle Hedin equations}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

To resum repeated particle-particle scattering, one introduces the
4-point effective interaction $T$.\cite{MarieRomanielloLoos2024}
In the number-conserving limit, the particle-particle Hedin equations can
be written schematically as
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\begin{aligned}
	G
	&=
	G_0
	+
	G_0\Sigma G,
	\\[1mm]
	\Sigma
	&=
	\ii G T \widetilde\Gamma^\pp,
	\\[1mm]
	T
	&=
	-\bar v
	-
	T\widetilde K v,
	\\[1mm]
	\widetilde K
	&=
	\ii GG\widetilde\Gamma^\pp,
	\\[1mm]
	\widetilde\Gamma^\pp
	&=
	\mathcal A
	-
	\Xi^\pp GG\widetilde\Gamma^\pp,
	\end{aligned}
	}
	\label{eq:pp_Hedin_system}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
	\mathcal A(12;1'2')
	=
	\frac{1}{2}
	\left[
		\delta(1'2)\delta(2'1)
		-
		\delta(1'1)\delta(2'2)
	\right]
	\label{eq:pair_identity}
\end{equation}
is the identity in the antisymmetric pair space.

The quantity $T$ plays a role analogous to the screened interaction $W$
in the conventional Hedin equations.
Whereas $W$ resums repeated direct electron-hole screening processes,
$T$ resums repeated particle-particle scattering processes.
Unlike $W$, however, $T$ remains a genuine 4-point interaction.

The simplest approximation is obtained by neglecting the vertex-correction
term in the last line of Eq.~\eqref{eq:pp_Hedin_system},
\begin{equation}
	\widetilde\Gamma^\pp
	\approx
	\mathcal A.
\end{equation}
The corresponding effective interaction reduces to the familiar
particle-particle $T$ matrix, and the associated self-energy is the
standard $T$-matrix self-energy.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Role of anomalous propagators}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

There is an important formal difference between the conventional and
particle-particle Hedin constructions.

In the conventional case, the kernel
\begin{equation}
	\Xi_{\xc}
	=
	\frac{\delta\Sigma_{\xc}}{\delta G}
\end{equation}
is defined entirely within the normal one-particle sector.

By contrast,
\begin{equation}
	\Xi^\pp
	=
	\left.
	\frac{\delta\Sigma^{ee}}
	     {\delta G^{ee}}
	\right|_{U=0}
\end{equation}
involves anomalous quantities.
Although $G^{ee}$ and $\Sigma^{ee}$ vanish in the normal phase, their
functional derivative does not.
A pairing field must therefore be introduced during the derivation and
the number-conserving limit taken only afterwards.

Consequently, the normal-state equations
Eq.~\eqref{eq:pp_Hedin_system} should be regarded as the physical limit of
a more general Nambu--Gorkov construction.
Beyond the lowest-order vertex approximation, the anomalous sector is
required to generate $\Xi^\pp$ consistently.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{A common channel-downfolding principle}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The conventional and particle-particle Hedin formulations can now be
placed side by side:
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\begin{aligned}
	\Gamma^\eh
	&=
	\Lambda
	+
	\Phi^{\teh}
	+
	\Phi^\pp,
	\\[1mm]
	\Gamma^\pp
	&=
	\Lambda
	+
	\Phi^\eh
	+
	\Phi^{\teh}.
	\end{aligned}
	}
	\label{eq:two_Hedin_channels}
\end{equation}

In each case, one parquet channel is selected as the explicit response
channel while the other two channels are incorporated into its
irreducible kernel.

Schematically,
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\begin{array}{ccc}
	\text{parquet}
	&
	\longrightarrow
	&
	\text{$\eh$-Hedin}
	\\[1mm]
	\Phi^{\teh},\Phi^\pp
	&&
	\Gamma^\eh
	\\[4mm]
	\text{parquet}
	&
	\longrightarrow
	&
	\text{$\pp$-Hedin}
	\\[1mm]
	\Phi^\eh,\Phi^{\teh}
	&&
	\Gamma^\pp.
	\end{array}
	}
	\label{eq:channel_Hedin_summary}
\end{equation}

The difference between the two formulations lies in the channel that is
retained explicitly and in the effective interaction used to resum its
repeated scattering:
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\begin{array}{lll}
	eh\text{ channel:}
	&
	W
	&
	\text{screening},
	\\[1mm]
	pp\text{ channel:}
	&
	T
	&
	\text{pair scattering}.
	\end{array}
	}
	\label{eq:W_T_comparison}
\end{equation}

This parallel construction supports the interpretation of Hedin-type
equations as channel-centered reductions of the more symmetric parquet
description.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{Exact equivalence and approximate closures}
\label{sec:approximations}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The connection established in the preceding sections is exact.\cite{KuglerVonDelft2018}
In particular,
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\Gamma^\eh
	=
	\Xi
	=
	\frac{\delta\Sigma}{\delta G}
	}
	\label{eq:exact_kernel_identity}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\Gamma^\eh
	=
	\Lambda
	+
	\Phi^{\teh}
	+
	\Phi^\pp
	}
	\label{eq:exact_parquet_identity}
\end{equation}
are simultaneously satisfied by the exact theory.

Consequently, the Hedin and parquet formulations contain identical
information before any approximation is introduced.
This exact equivalence does not, however, imply that independently
truncated Hedin and parquet theories remain equivalent.\cite{KuglerVonDelft2018,Baym_1961,Baym_1962}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Two different closure strategies}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The distinction can be understood by considering how the hierarchy is
closed in the two formulations.

A Hedin-type approximation naturally starts from an approximation to the
self-energy,
\begin{equation}
	\Sigma
	\longrightarrow
	\Sigma^{\rm app},
\end{equation}
and determines the corresponding two-particle kernel by functional
differentiation,
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\Xi^{\rm app}
	=
	\frac{\delta\Sigma^{\rm app}}{\delta G}.
	}
	\label{eq:Hedin_closure}
\end{equation}
The vertex equation then uses this kernel to construct the corresponding
response vertex.

Parquet theory proceeds in the opposite direction.
One first chooses an approximation to the fully two-particle-irreducible
vertex,
\begin{equation}
	\Lambda
	\longrightarrow
	\Lambda^{\rm app},
\end{equation}
and solves
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\begin{aligned}
	F^{\rm app}
	&=
	\Lambda^{\rm app}
	+
	\Phi^\eh_{\rm app}
	+
	\Phi^{\teh}_{\rm app}
	+
	\Phi^\pp_{\rm app},
	\\
	\Gamma^{r}_{\rm app}
	&=
	\Lambda^{\rm app}
	+
	\sum_{r'\neq r}
	\Phi^{r'}_{\rm app},
	\end{aligned}
	}
	\label{eq:parquet_closure}
\end{equation}
together with the three Bethe--Salpeter equations.
The resulting full vertex is subsequently inserted into the
Schwinger--Dyson equation to obtain
\begin{equation}
	\Sigma^{\rm app}_{\rm P}
	=
	\Sigma[F^{\rm app}].
	\label{eq:parquet_selfenergy}
\end{equation}

Thus the two closure strategies may be summarized as
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\begin{array}{lll}
	\text{Hedin closure:}
	&
	\Sigma^{\rm app}
	&
	\longrightarrow
	\displaystyle
	\frac{\delta\Sigma^{\rm app}}{\delta G},
	\\[4mm]
	\text{parquet closure:}
	&
	\Lambda^{\rm app}
	&
	\longrightarrow
	F^{\rm app}
	\longrightarrow
	\Sigma^{\rm app}_{\rm P}.
	\end{array}
	}
	\label{eq:two_closures}
\end{equation}

These operations are equivalent only when the underlying quantities are
exact.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Loss of the functional identity after truncation}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

Suppose that an approximate fully irreducible vertex
$\Lambda^{\rm app}$ is used to solve the parquet equations and the
corresponding self-energy is evaluated from the Schwinger--Dyson equation.

The $\eh$-irreducible parquet vertex is then
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\Gamma^\eh_{\rm P}
	=
	\Lambda^{\rm app}
	+
	\Phi^{\teh}_{\rm app}
	+
	\Phi^\pp_{\rm app}.
	}
	\label{eq:Gamma_parquet_approx}
\end{equation}

One may independently differentiate the resulting parquet self-energy,
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\Xi_{\rm FD}
	=
	\frac{
		\delta\Sigma^{\rm app}_{\rm P}
	}{
		\delta G
	}.
	}
	\label{eq:Xi_functional_derivative}
\end{equation}

In general,
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\Xi_{\rm FD}
	\neq
	\Gamma^\eh_{\rm P}.
	}
	\label{eq:approx_mismatch}
\end{equation}

The reason is that functional differentiation acts on every propagator
appearing implicitly and explicitly in the approximate parquet
self-energy.
It therefore generates contributions that are not necessarily contained
in the independently truncated parquet kernel
$\Gamma^\eh_{\rm P}$.

Equation~\eqref{eq:approx_mismatch} is not a contradiction with the exact
identity
\begin{equation}
	\frac{\delta\Sigma}{\delta G}
	=
	\Gamma^\eh.
\end{equation}
Rather, it shows that the operations
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\text{truncate}
	\qquad\text{and}\qquad
	\text{take a functional derivative}
	}
\end{equation}
do not generally commute.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Parquet approximation}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The distinction is particularly transparent for the parquet approximation,
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\Lambda^{\rm PA}
	=
	-\ii\bar v.
	}
	\label{eq:PA_again}
\end{equation}
The coupled parquet equations then generate a crossing-consistent full
vertex containing all diagrams obtained by repeated two-particle
scattering from the bare fully irreducible interaction.

The corresponding self-energy,
\begin{equation}
	\Sigma^{\rm PA}
	=
	\Sigma[F^{\rm PA}],
\end{equation}
satisfies the Schwinger--Dyson relation by construction.

However, the functional derivative
\begin{equation}
	\frac{\delta\Sigma^{\rm PA}}{\delta G}
\end{equation}
generates a new two-particle kernel that is, in general, different from
the $\eh$-irreducible parquet vertex obtained directly from the parquet
equations.
Functional differentiation can therefore be used to construct a
two-particle vertex consistent with the approximate self-energy, but this
new vertex no longer coincides with the vertex of the original parquet
closure.\cite{KuglerVonDelft2018}

This illustrates a general tension between two desirable forms of
self-consistency:
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\begin{aligned}
	\text{parquet consistency:}
	&\qquad
	F
	=
	\Lambda+\sum_r\Phi^r,
	\\[1mm]
	\text{functional consistency:}
	&\qquad
	\Gamma^\eh
	=
	\frac{\delta\Sigma}{\delta G}.
	\end{aligned}
	}
	\label{eq:two_consistencies}
\end{equation}

At the exact level both conditions hold simultaneously.
For an approximate closure, enforcing one does not in general guarantee
the other.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Implication for the Hedin--parquet connection}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

This distinction is particularly important for the interpretation
developed in these notes.

At the exact level,
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\Xi
	=
	\Gamma^\eh
	=
	\Lambda
	+
	\Phi^{\teh}
	+
	\Phi^\pp
	}
\end{equation}
provides a reversible map between Hedin and parquet representations.

After approximation, however, there are two different possible objects:
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
	\begin{aligned}
	\Xi_{\rm Hedin}^{\rm app}
	&=
	\frac{\delta\Sigma^{\rm app}}{\delta G},
	\\[2mm]
	\Xi_{\rm parquet}^{\rm app}
	&=
	\Lambda^{\rm app}
	+
	\Phi^{\teh}_{\rm app}
	+
	\Phi^\pp_{\rm app}.
	\end{aligned}
	}
	\label{eq:two_approx_kernels}
\end{equation}
There is no general reason for these two approximations to coincide.

Thus the statement that parquet is a channel-resolved or upfolded version
of Hedin should be understood as an exact structural relation.
It does not imply a one-to-one correspondence between arbitrary
approximations constructed independently in the two formalisms.

This observation also suggests two complementary strategies for developing
approximate many-body theories:
one may approximate the self-energy and obtain its response kernel by
functional differentiation, or approximate the fully irreducible vertex
and reconstruct the different scattering channels through the parquet
equations.
The two routes organize the missing diagrams differently and should not
be expected to generate identical finite-order approximations.
