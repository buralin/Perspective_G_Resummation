\title{Multiresolvent Representation of Frequency-Dependent Bethe--Salpeter Kernels}

\author{Pierre-Fran\c{c}ois \surname{Loos}}
	\email{loos@irsamc.ups-tlse.fr}
	\affiliation{\LCPQ}

\begin{abstract}
A general four-point vertex depends on three independent frequencies and cannot, in general, be represented by the familiar single-frequency resolvent structure encountered in one-body Green's-function, self-energy, and polarization-propagator theories, including the algebraic-diagrammatic construction (ADC).
In these notes, we develop a channel-resolved multiresolvent representation of the parquet vertex through second order in the bare interaction, taking the electron-hole ($\eh$) and particle-particle ($\pp$) reducible vertices as primitive objects and generating the transverse electron-hole ($\teh$) contribution by crossing symmetry.
Integrating over one fermionic frequency of the three-frequency non-interacting two-particle propagator generates two-frequency external coupling vertices whose poles lie in the $(N\pm1)$-electron sectors.
A second frequency integration yields the corresponding single-frequency $\eh$ and $\pp$ kernels associated with second-order Green's function theory (GF2).
We then apply the same frequency-reduction procedure to the conventional dynamical $GW$ kernel, retaining the kernel to first order in the screened interaction $W$, and independently recover the known dynamical $\eh$ and $\pp$ Bethe--Salpeter kernels.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

A general four-point quantity contains substantially more analytic
structure than a one-particle Green's function or a linear-response
propagator.
For a time-independent Hamiltonian, time-translation invariance removes
one overall time variable, leaving three independent time differences or,
equivalently, three independent frequencies.
The full two-particle vertex can therefore be written as
$F(\nu,\nu',\omega)$.

This three-frequency dependence makes a direct extension of conventional
spectral or algebraic-diagrammatic construction (ADC) non-trivial.
A one-particle self-energy can be represented by resolvents in the
$(N\pm1)$-electron sectors, while a polarization propagator is naturally
expressed through a neutral $N$-electron resolvent.
For a four-point vertex, several propagation sectors and several independent
frequencies coexist.
The objective is therefore not to introduce a single formal resolvent
carrying all three frequencies, but to organize the vertex into ordinary
resolvents associated with well-defined intermediate-state spaces.

Parquet theory provides the natural channel decomposition for this purpose.
We denote the electron-hole, transverse electron-hole, and particle-particle
channels by $\eh$, $\teh$, and $\pp$, respectively.
The full vertex is decomposed as\cite{DeDominicis_1964b,Bickers_2004,MarieLoos2025}
\begin{equation}
	F
	=
	\Lambda
	+
	\Phi^\eh
	+
	\Phi^{\teh}
	+
	\Phi^\pp
	\label{eq:parquet_decomposition}
\end{equation}
where $\Lambda$ is fully two-particle irreducible.
The direct $\eh$ reducible vertex $\Phi^\eh$ is associated with neutral
$N$-electron propagation, whereas $\Phi^\pp$ is associated with the
$(N\pm2)$-electron sectors.
The transverse contribution $\Phi^{\teh}$ follows from $\Phi^\eh$ by
fermionic crossing.
Consequently, only two primitive multiresolvent constructions are required:
$\Phi^\eh$ and $\Phi^\pp$.

In each primitive channel, the channel-reducible four-point vertex is
represented as a left external coupling vertex, a channel-specific central resolvent,
and a right external coupling vertex.
The central resolvent carries the natural bosonic transfer of the channel,
while the two external coupling vertices retain the remaining fermionic-frequency
dependence.

This construction also provides a direct route to the four-point Hedin
kernel.
The vertex irreducible in the direct $\eh$ channel is
\begin{equation}
	\Gamma^\eh
	=
	\Lambda
	+
	\Phi^{\teh}
	+
	\Phi^\pp
	\label{eq:Gamma_eh_parquet}
\end{equation}
At the exact level, this is the same four-point kernel that enters Hedin's
Bethe--Salpeter equation (BSE) through the functional derivative
$\Xi=\fdv*{\Sigma}{G}$, once a common index convention is adopted.~\cite{Hedin1965,MarieAmmarLoos2024}
Thus, constructing $\Phi^\eh$ and $\Phi^\pp$ also yields a multiresolvent
representation of the direct $\eh$ Hedin kernel.

The present notes are deliberately restricted to the part of the construction
that is fully controlled at second order in the bare interaction.
We establish the channel frequency structure, primitive reducible vertices,
one-sided frequency integrations, the pole structure of the external coupling vertices, recovery of
the second-order Green's function (GF2) $\eh$ kernel, and parquet consistency.
A dedicated recipe summarizes these results.
We then consider the usual dynamical $GW$ kernel at first order in the
screened interaction, and show that it possesses the same
multiresolvent structure after a spectral decomposition of $W$.
Higher-order bare-interaction extensions and the kernel contribution that is
second order in $W$ are treated separately and are not part of the present
document.

Throughout the perturbative discussion, we use a Hartree--Fock (HF) zeroth-order
reference together with a M{\o}ller--Plesset (MP) partition.
Accordingly, $G_0$ denotes the non-interacting one-particle propagator and
$L_0=G_0G_0$ the corresponding non-interacting two-particle propagator throughout.
For compactness, all expressions are written for real-valued orbitals.
Left and right external coupling vertices are kept distinct whenever this distinction
matters, and all causal prescriptions are denoted by $0^+$.

\section{Frequency conventions and channel organization}
\label{sec:frequencies}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The direct $\eh$ convention is used throughout these notes.
The four external fermionic line energies are
\begin{equation}
	\nu
	\qquad
	\nu+\omega
	\qquad
	\nu'
	\qquad
	\nu'+\omega
	\label{eq:eh_frequencies}
\end{equation}
The natural bosonic transfer frequencies of the three parquet channels are
\begin{align}
	\Omega_\eh
	&=
	\omega
	\label{eq:Omega_eh}
	\\
	\Omega_{\teh}
	&=
	\nu'-\nu
	\label{eq:Omega_ehbar}
	\\
	\Omega_\pp
	&=
	\omega+\nu+\nu'
	\label{eq:Omega_pp}
\end{align}

For the transverse $\eh$ channel, exchanging the third and fourth external
legs gives the frequency map
\begin{equation}
	\left(
	\nu_{\teh},
	\nu'_{\teh},
	\Omega_{\teh}
	\right)
	=
	\left(
	\nu,
	\nu+\omega,
	\nu'-\nu
	\right)
	\label{eq:crossed_frequency_map}
\end{equation}
The natural $\pp$ variables are related to the direct $\eh$ convention by
\begin{equation}
	\left(
	\nu_\pp,
	\nu'_\pp,
	\Omega_\pp
	\right)
	=
	\left(
	-\omega-\nu',
	-\nu',
	\omega+\nu+\nu'
	\right)
	\label{eq:pp_frequency_map}
\end{equation}

These three combinations determine the characteristic frequency structure of
the parquet vertex.
At the lowest non-vanishing order, each reducible vertex depends only on the
bosonic transfer natural to its channel
\begin{align}
	\Phi^{\eh,(2)}
	&\equiv
	\Phi^{\eh,(2)}(\omega)
	\\
	\Phi^{\teh,(2)}
	&\equiv
	\Phi^{\teh,(2)}(\nu'-\nu)
	\\
	\Phi^{\pp,(2)}
	&\equiv
	\Phi^{\pp,(2)}(\nu+\nu'+\omega)
	\label{eq:Phi_frequency_second_order}
\end{align}
Beyond second order, the primitive reducible vertices are genuine
three-frequency objects.
The multiresolvent representation introduced below assigns the natural
bosonic transfer to the central resolvent and the two remaining fermionic
frequencies to the external coupling vertices.

The exact channel-irreducible vertices satisfy
\begin{align}
	\Gamma^\eh
	&=
	\Lambda
	+
	\Phi^{\teh}
	+
	\Phi^\pp
	\label{eq:Gamma_eh}
	\\
	\Gamma^\pp
	&=
	\Lambda
	+
	\Phi^\eh
	+
	\Phi^{\teh}
	\label{eq:Gamma_pp}
\end{align}
For a two-body interaction, the first correction to the fully irreducible
vertex beyond the bare interaction occurs at fourth order, so that
\begin{equation}
	\Lambda
	=
	\Lambda^{(1)}
	+
	O(v^4)
	\label{eq:Lambda_order}
\end{equation}
where $v$ denotes the two-electron interaction.

The second-order channel-irreducible vertices are therefore
\begin{align}
	\Gamma^{\eh,(2)}
	&=
	\Phi^{\teh,(2)}
	+
	\Phi^{\pp,(2)}
	\label{eq:Gamma_eh_second}
	\\
	\Gamma^{\pp,(2)}
	&=
	\Phi^{\eh,(2)}
	+
	\Phi^{\teh,(2)}
	\label{eq:Gamma_pp_second}
\end{align}

\section{Target multiresolvent representation}
\label{sec:target_representation}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

Having fixed the channel frequencies, we can state the algebraic form that we
seek before deriving any perturbative ingredient.

In conventional self-energy ADC, the exact Lehmann representation of the
one-particle Green's function motivates an auxiliary charged-particle space,
but the poles of the proper self-energy are not, in general, the exact
$(N\pm1)$ addition and removal energies themselves.
ADC instead constructs an intermediate-state representation of the proper
self-energy in an $(N\pm1)$-type auxiliary space and exploits the freedom
within that space to obtain a non-diagonal effective matrix representation.
For a general four-point vertex, no analogous single diagonal representation
exists because three independent frequencies cannot be carried by one
resolvent.
Our construction is therefore channel resolved.

For each primitive reducible vertex, the natural bosonic transfer is carried
by a central resolvent, while the remaining fermionic-frequency dependences
are carried by a left and a right external coupling vertex.
We denote these external coupling vertices by $U^r$ and $V^r$ (with $r = \eh$ or $\pp$), respectively, and keep
them as independent objects unless a symmetry relation is explicitly used.
The target forms are
\begin{align}
	\ii\Phi^\eh(\nu,\nu',\omega)
	&=
	U^\eh(\nu,\omega)
	R^\eh(\omega)
	V^\eh(\nu',\omega)
	\label{eq:target_eh}
	\\
	\ii\Phi^\pp(\nu_\pp,\nu'_\pp,\Omega_\pp)
	&=
	U^\pp(\nu_\pp,\Omega_\pp)
	R^\pp(\Omega_\pp)
	V^\pp(\nu'_\pp,\Omega_\pp)
	\label{eq:target_pp}
\end{align}
with
\begin{align}
	R^\eh(\omega)
	&=
	\left[
	\omega\eta^\eh-M^\eh
	\right]^{-1}
	\label{eq:target_R_eh}
	\\
	R^\pp(\Omega_\pp)
	&=
	\left[
	\Omega_\pp\eta^\pp-M^\pp
	\right]^{-1}
	\label{eq:target_R_pp}
\end{align}
Here $\eta^r$ is the channel metric, with eigenvalues $+1$ and $-1$ for
the two propagation sectors.
Antisymmetrized two-electron integrals are written directly as
$\mel{pq}{}{rs}$ throughout.
when specifying perturbative orders and must not be confused with the right
external coupling vertex $V^r$.

The corresponding particle-number structures are
\begin{align}
	\Phi^\eh
	&:\quad
	N\pm1
	\longrightarrow
	N
	\longrightarrow
	N\pm1
	\label{eq:target_sector_eh}
	\\
	\Phi^\pp
	&:\quad
	N\pm1
	\longrightarrow
	N\pm2
	\longrightarrow
	N\pm1
	\label{eq:target_sector_pp}
\end{align}
Thus, the central resolvent distinguishes $\eh$ and $\pp$ propagation,
whereas the external coupling vertices are associated with $(N\pm1)$-electron
propagation.
At second order, their non-trivial poles are the $\hhp$ and $\pph$ poles of
the $(N-1)$- and $(N+1)$-electron sectors.

The zeroth-order central resolvent is
\begin{equation}
	R^{r,(0)}(\Omega_r)
	=
	\left[
	\Omega_r\eta^r-M^{r,(0)}
	\right]^{-1}
	\label{eq:target_R_zero}
\end{equation}
and the second-order target is
\begin{equation}
	\ii\Phi^{r,(2)}
	=
	U^{r,(1)}
	R^{r,(0)}
	V^{r,(1)}
	\label{eq:target_second_order}
\end{equation}
The remainder of these notes establishes this representation completely
through second order and then applies the same frequency-reduction strategy to
the usual dynamical $GW$ kernel at first order in $W$.

\section{Three-frequency BSE and one-sided frequency reduction}
\label{sec:three_frequency_bse}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

At the exact level, the $\eh$ correlation function obeys the
four-point Bethe--Salpeter equation (BSE)
\begin{equation}
	L
	=
	L_0
	+
	L_0 \Gamma^\eh L
	\label{eq:BSE_symbolic}
\end{equation}
In the direct $\eh$ convention of Sec.~\ref{sec:frequencies},
\begin{multline}
	L(\nu,\nu',\omega)
	=
	L_0(\nu,\nu',\omega)
	\\
	+
	\frac{1}{(2\pi)^2}
	\int d\bar\nu\,d\bar\nu'\,
	L_0(\nu,\bar\nu,\omega)
	\Gamma^\eh(\bar\nu,\bar\nu',\omega)
	L(\bar\nu',\nu',\omega)
	\label{eq:BSE_three_frequency}
\end{multline}
Barred frequencies denote integration variables throughout these notes.

For occupied and virtual reference orbitals,
\begin{align}
	G_{0,i}(\nu)
	&=
	\frac{1}{\nu-\eps_i-\ii\eta}
	\\
	G_{0,a}(\nu)
	&=
	\frac{1}{\nu-\eps_a+\ii\eta}
	\label{eq:G0}
\end{align}
We use $+$ for forward propagation $i\to a$ and $-$ for backward propagation
$a\to i$, while keeping the orbital pair label $ia$ in both cases.
The corresponding three-frequency non-interacting pair propagators are
\begin{align}
	L_{0,ia}^{+}(\nu,\nu',\omega)
	&=
	2\pi\delta(\nu-\nu')
	G_{0,a}(\nu+\omega)G_{0,i}(\nu)
	\label{eq:L0_three_frequency_plus}
	\\
	L_{0,ia}^{-}(\nu,\nu',\omega)
	&=
	2\pi\delta(\nu-\nu')
	G_{0,i}(\nu+\omega)G_{0,a}(\nu)
	\label{eq:L0_three_frequency_minus}
\end{align}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Exact factorization of the non-interacting pair propagator}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The key observation is that each three-frequency pair propagator factorizes
exactly into a normalized two-frequency factor and the usual one-frequency
pair propagator.
Define
\begin{equation}
	\begin{aligned}
	(L_0^+)_{ia}(\omega)
	&=
	\frac{\ii}
	{\omega-(\eps_a-\eps_i)+\ii0^+}
	\\
	(L_0^-)_{ia}(\omega)
	&=
	-\frac{\ii}
	{\omega+(\eps_a-\eps_i)-\ii0^+}
	\end{aligned}
	\label{eq:L0_pm}
\end{equation}
and the corresponding one-sided factors
\begin{equation}
	\begin{aligned}
	Q^+_{ia}(\nu,\omega)
	&=
	-\ii
	\left[
	G_{0,i}(\nu)-G_{0,a}(\nu+\omega)
	\right]
	\\
	Q^-_{ia}(\nu,\omega)
	&=
	+\ii
	\left[
	G_{0,a}(\nu)-G_{0,i}(\nu+\omega)
	\right]
	\end{aligned}
	\label{eq:Q_pm}
\end{equation}
Partial fractioning gives the exact identities
\begin{align}
	L_{0,ia}^{+}(\nu,\nu',\omega)
	&=
	2\pi\delta(\nu-\nu')
	Q^+_{ia}(\nu,\omega)
	(L_0^+)_{ia}(\omega)
	\label{eq:L0_factorization_plus}
	\\
	L_{0,ia}^{-}(\nu,\nu',\omega)
	&=
	2\pi\delta(\nu-\nu')
	Q^-_{ia}(\nu,\omega)
	(L_0^-)_{ia}(\omega)
	\label{eq:L0_factorization_minus}
\end{align}
No approximation is involved in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:L0_factorization_plus} and
\eqref{eq:L0_factorization_minus}.


The one-sided factors satisfy
\begin{align}
	\frac{1}{2\pi}
	\int d\bar\nu\,
	Q^+_{ia}(\bar\nu,\omega)
	&=1
	\\
	\frac{1}{2\pi}
	\int d\bar\nu\,
	Q^-_{ia}(\bar\nu,\omega)
	&=1
	\label{eq:Q_normalization}
\end{align}
and pair reversal gives
\begin{equation}
	Q^-_{ia}(\nu,\omega)
	=
	Q^+_{ia}(\nu+\omega,-\omega)
	\label{eq:Q_reversal}
\end{equation}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{One-sided frequency integration}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

We now use the exact factorization above to remove only one fermionic
frequency from a BSE kernel insertion.
Let $\sigma,\sigma'=\pm$ denote the propagation directions of the left pair
$ia$ and right pair $jb$, respectively. Thus $++$ denotes forward propagation
on both pairs, whereas $+-$ denotes forward propagation on $ia$ and backward
propagation on $jb$.
In this pair-space discussion we suppress the channel superscript $\eh$ on
$U$ and $V$.
To match the convention $\ii\Phi^r=U^rR^rV^r$, the projected external coupling vertices are defined from $\ii\Gamma^\eh$.

Projecting the left pair gives
\begin{align}
	&\frac{1}{(2\pi)^2}
	\int d\bar\nu_1\,d\bar\nu_2\,
	L_{0,ia}^{\sigma}(\bar\nu_1,\bar\nu_2,\omega)
	\left[
	\ii\Gamma^\eh
	\right]^{\sigma\sigma'}_{ia,jb}(\bar\nu_2,\nu',\omega)
	\nonumber
	\\
	&\qquad=
	(L_0^\sigma)_{ia}(\omega)
	\frac{1}{2\pi}
	\int d\bar\nu\,
	Q^\sigma_{ia}(\bar\nu,\omega)
	\left[
	\ii\Gamma^\eh
	\right]^{\sigma\sigma'}_{ia,jb}(\bar\nu,\nu',\omega)
	\label{eq:BSE_right_factorization}
\end{align}
which defines the right external coupling vertex
\begin{equation}
	V^{\sigma\sigma'}_{ia,jb}(\nu',\omega)
	=
	\frac{1}{2\pi}
	\int d\bar\nu\,
	Q^\sigma_{ia}(\bar\nu,\omega)
	\left[
	\ii\Gamma^\eh
	\right]^{\sigma\sigma'}_{ia,jb}(\bar\nu,\nu',\omega)
	\label{eq:V_def}
\end{equation}
so that Eq.~\eqref{eq:BSE_right_factorization} becomes
\begin{equation}
	(L_0^\sigma)_{ia}(\omega)
	V^{\sigma\sigma'}_{ia,jb}(\nu',\omega)
	\label{eq:BSE_right_compact}
\end{equation}

Projecting the right pair gives analogously
\begin{align}
	&\frac{1}{(2\pi)^2}
	\int d\bar\nu'_1\,d\bar\nu'_2\,
	\left[
	\ii\Gamma^\eh
	\right]^{\sigma\sigma'}_{ia,jb}(\nu,\bar\nu'_1,\omega)
	L_{0,jb}^{\sigma'}(\bar\nu'_1,\bar\nu'_2,\omega)
	\nonumber
	\\
	&\qquad=
	\frac{1}{2\pi}
	\int d\bar\nu'\,
	\left[
	\ii\Gamma^\eh
	\right]^{\sigma\sigma'}_{ia,jb}(\nu,\bar\nu',\omega)
	Q^{\sigma'}_{jb}(\bar\nu',\omega)
	(L_0^{\sigma'})_{jb}(\omega)
	\label{eq:BSE_left_factorization}
\end{align}
which defines the left external coupling vertex
\begin{equation}
	U^{\sigma\sigma'}_{ia,jb}(\nu,\omega)
	=
	\frac{1}{2\pi}
	\int d\bar\nu'\,
	\left[
	\ii\Gamma^\eh
	\right]^{\sigma\sigma'}_{ia,jb}(\nu,\bar\nu',\omega)
	Q^{\sigma'}_{jb}(\bar\nu',\omega)
	\label{eq:U_def}
\end{equation}
and therefore
\begin{equation}
	U^{\sigma\sigma'}_{ia,jb}(\nu,\omega)
	(L_0^{\sigma'})_{jb}(\omega)
	\label{eq:BSE_left_compact}
\end{equation}

Thus $U$ retains the left fermionic frequency $\nu$, whereas $V$ retains the
right fermionic frequency $\nu'$.
Equations~\eqref{eq:U_def} and \eqref{eq:V_def} are definitions of exact
one-sided projections of the three-frequency irreducible vertex, not
additional approximations.
Perturbative approximations enter only when these objects and the central
resolvent are truncated in powers of the interaction.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{From three to two to one frequency}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

A second one-sided integration removes the remaining fermionic frequency.
We define the one-frequency projected eh kernel by
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\left[
	\ii\widetilde{\Gamma}^\eh
	\right]^{\sigma\sigma'}_{ia,jb}(\omega)
	={}&
	\frac{1}{(2\pi)^2}
	\int d\bar\nu\,d\bar\nu'\,
	Q^\sigma_{ia}(\bar\nu,\omega)
	\\
	&\times
	\left[
	\ii\Gamma^\eh
	\right]^{\sigma\sigma'}_{ia,jb}(\bar\nu,\bar\nu',\omega)
	Q^{\sigma'}_{jb}(\bar\nu',\omega)
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:Gamma_tilde_def}
\end{equation}
Using Eqs.~\eqref{eq:U_def} and \eqref{eq:V_def}, the same object is obtained
in either order
\begin{align}
	\left[
	\ii\widetilde{\Gamma}^\eh
	\right]^{\sigma\sigma'}_{ia,jb}(\omega)
	&=
	\frac{1}{2\pi}
	\int d\bar\nu'\,
	V^{\sigma\sigma'}_{ia,jb}(\bar\nu',\omega)
	Q^{\sigma'}_{jb}(\bar\nu',\omega)
	\label{eq:Gamma_tilde_from_V}
	\\
	&=
	\frac{1}{2\pi}
	\int d\bar\nu\,
	Q^\sigma_{ia}(\bar\nu,\omega)
	U^{\sigma\sigma'}_{ia,jb}(\bar\nu,\omega)
	\label{eq:Gamma_tilde_from_U}
\end{align}
The frequency reduction is therefore
\begin{equation}
	\Gamma^\eh(\nu,\nu',\omega)
	\longrightarrow
	\left\{
	U(\nu,\omega),V(\nu',\omega)
	\right\}
	\longrightarrow
	\widetilde{\Gamma}^\eh(\omega)
	\label{eq:frequency_reduction}
\end{equation}
The first arrow denotes one-sided frequency integration and the second the
integration over the remaining fermionic frequency.

\section{Second-order primitive vertices}
\label{sec:second_order_primitives}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

We now construct the second-order $\eh$ irreducible vertex from
the two channels that are irreducible with respect to direct $\eh$
propagation.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Direct eh reducible vertex}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

At first order, the $\eh$ irreducible interaction is the bare
antisymmetrized interaction
\begin{equation}
	\ii\Gamma^{\eh,(1)}_{pqrs}
	=
	\mel{pq}{}{rs}
	\label{eq:Gamma_eh_first}
\end{equation}
The second-order direct $\eh$ reducible vertex follows from the
non-interacting orbital transitions $i\to a$
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\ii\Phi^{\eh,(2)}_{pqrs}(\omega)
	={}&
	\sum_{ia}
	\frac{
	\mel{pa}{}{ri}
	\mel{qi}{}{sa}
	}
	{\omega-(\eps_a-\eps_i)+\ii0^+}
	\\
	&-
	\sum_{ia}
	\frac{
	\mel{pi}{}{ra}
	\mel{qa}{}{si}
	}
	{\omega+(\eps_a-\eps_i)-\ii0^+}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:Phi_eh_second}
\end{equation}
At this order the two end vertices are instantaneous and
$\Phi^{\eh,(2)}$ depends only on the direct $\eh$ transfer
frequency $\omega$.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Transverse eh contribution from crossing}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

Fermionic crossing gives
\begin{equation}
	\Phi^{\teh}_{pqrs}(\nu,\nu',\omega)
	=
	-
	\Phi^\eh_{pqsr}
	\left(
	\nu,
	\nu+\omega,
	\nu'-\nu
	\right)
	\label{eq:Phi_ehbar_crossing}
\end{equation}
Using Eq.~\eqref{eq:Phi_eh_second} therefore yields
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\ii\Phi^{\teh,(2)}_{pqrs}(\nu,\nu',\omega)
	={}&
	-
	\sum_{kc}
	\frac{
	\mel{pc}{}{sk}
	\mel{qk}{}{rc}
	}
	{\nu'-\nu-(\eps_c-\eps_k)+\ii0^+}
	\\
	&+
	\sum_{kc}
	\frac{
	\mel{pk}{}{sc}
	\mel{qc}{}{rk}
	}
	{\nu'-\nu+(\eps_c-\eps_k)-\ii0^+}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:Phi_ehbar_second}
\end{equation}
The characteristic transverse $\eh$ poles are therefore controlled
by $\nu'-\nu$.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{pp reducible vertex}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The $\pp$ channel contains $\pp$ and $\hh$
propagation.
Using unrestricted orbital sums, the second-order pair-reducible vertex
is
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\ii\Phi_P^{\pp,(2)}{}_{pqrs}(\Omega)
	={}&
	\frac{1}{2}
	\sum_{cd}
	\frac{
	\mel{pq}{}{cd}
	\mel{cd}{}{rs}
	}
	{\Omega-(\eps_c+\eps_d)+\ii0^+}
	\\
	&-
	\frac{1}{2}
	\sum_{kl}
	\frac{
	\mel{pq}{}{kl}
	\mel{kl}{}{rs}
	}
	{\Omega-(\eps_k+\eps_l)-\ii0^+}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:Phi_pp_second_native}
\end{equation}
The factors $1/2$ remove the double counting associated with unrestricted
antisymmetric pair sums.
They are absent when the sums are restricted to unique pairs.

Transforming Eq.~\eqref{eq:Phi_pp_second_native} to the direct
$\eh$ convention with Eq.~\eqref{eq:pp_frequency_map} gives
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\ii\Phi^{\pp,(2)}_{pqrs}(\nu,\nu',\omega)
	={}&
	\frac{1}{2}
	\sum_{cd}
	\frac{
	\mel{pq}{}{cd}
	\mel{cd}{}{rs}
	}
	{\nu+\nu'+\omega-\eps_c-\eps_d+\ii0^+}
	\\
	&-
	\frac{1}{2}
	\sum_{kl}
	\frac{
	\mel{pq}{}{kl}
	\mel{kl}{}{rs}
	}
	{\nu+\nu'+\omega-\eps_k-\eps_l-\ii0^+}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:Phi_pp_second_eh}
\end{equation}
The characteristic pair poles are therefore controlled by
$\nu+\nu'+\omega$.

\subsection{Second-order eh irreducible kernel}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

Combining the transverse $\eh$ and $\pp$
contributions gives
\begin{equation}
	\ii\Gamma^{\eh,(2)}
	=
	\ii\Phi^{\teh,(2)}
	+
	\ii\Phi^{\pp,(2)}
	\label{eq:Gamma_eh_second_i}
\end{equation}
Explicitly,
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\ii\Gamma^{\eh,(2)}_{pqrs}(\nu,\nu',\omega)
	={}&
	-
	\sum_{kc}
	\frac{
	\mel{pc}{}{sk}
	\mel{qk}{}{rc}
	}
	{\nu'-\nu-(\eps_c-\eps_k)+\ii0^+}
	\\
	&+
	\sum_{kc}
	\frac{
	\mel{pk}{}{sc}
	\mel{qc}{}{rk}
	}
	{\nu'-\nu+(\eps_c-\eps_k)-\ii0^+}
	\\
	&+
	\frac{1}{2}
	\sum_{cd}
	\frac{
	\mel{pq}{}{cd}
	\mel{cd}{}{rs}
	}
	{\nu+\nu'+\omega-\eps_c-\eps_d+\ii0^+}
	\\
	&-
	\frac{1}{2}
	\sum_{kl}

	\mel{pq}{}{kl}
	\mel{kl}{}{rs}

	\end{split}

\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:Gamma_eh_second_explicit} is the starting point for

\section{Multiresolvent representation at second order}
\label{sec:second_order_multiresolvent}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The primitive second-order vertices can now be written in the target form of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:target_second_order}.
At this order, the external coupling vertices are the bare interaction and the central
resolvent is the zeroth-order non-interacting resolvent.

For the $\eh$ channel,
\begin{align}
	\eta^\eh
	&=
	\begin{pmatrix}
	1&0
	\\
	0&-1
	\end{pmatrix}
	&
	M^{\eh,(0)}
	&=
	\begin{pmatrix}
	\Delta\eps&0
	\\
	0&\Delta\eps
	\end{pmatrix}
	\label{eq:eta_M_eh_zero}
\end{align}
with
\begin{equation}
	(\Delta\eps)_{ia,jb}
	=
	(\eps_a-\eps_i)\delta_{ij}\delta_{ab}
	\label{eq:Delta_eh}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
	R^{\eh,(0)}(\omega)
	=
	\left[
	\omega\eta^\eh-M^{\eh,(0)}
	\right]^{-1}
	\label{eq:R_eh_zero}
\end{equation}
The two blocks of the central resolvent correspond to forward ($+$) and
backward ($-$) eh propagation.
The corresponding first-order external-coupling-vertex components are
\begin{align}
	U^{\eh,+,(1)}_{pr,ia}
	&=
	\mel{pa}{}{ri}
	&
	V^{\eh,+,(1)}_{ia,sq}
	&=
	\mel{qi}{}{sa}
	\label{eq:UV_eh_first_plus}
	\\
	U^{\eh,-,(1)}_{pr,ia}
	&=
	\mel{pi}{}{ra}
	&
	V^{\eh,-,(1)}_{ia,sq}
	&=
	\mel{qa}{}{si}
	\label{eq:UV_eh_first_minus}
\end{align}
These are the two block components of $U^{\eh,(1)}$ and $V^{\eh,(1)}$, so that
\begin{equation}
	\ii\Phi^{\eh,(2)}
	=
	U^{\eh,(1)}R^{\eh,(0)}V^{\eh,(1)}
	\label{eq:Phi_eh_second_resolvent}
\end{equation}
which is identical to Eq.~\eqref{eq:Phi_eh_second}.

For the $\pp$ channel, we use a normalized antisymmetric pair basis with
unique particle pairs $a<b$ and hole pairs $i<j$.
The metric and zeroth-order pair matrix are
\begin{align}
	\eta^\pp
	&=
	\begin{pmatrix}
	1&0
	\\
	0&-1
	\end{pmatrix}
	&
	M^{\pp,(0)}
	&=
	\begin{pmatrix}
	\eps_a+\eps_b&0
	\\
	0&-(\eps_i+\eps_j)
	\end{pmatrix}
	\label{eq:eta_M_pp_zero}
\end{align}
with
\begin{equation}
	R^{\pp,(0)}(\Omega_\pp)
	=
	\left[
	\Omega_\pp\eta^\pp-M^{\pp,(0)}
	\right]^{-1}
	\label{eq:R_pp_zero}
\end{equation}
The first-order external coupling vertices are
\begin{align}
	U^{\pp,(1)}_{pq,ab}
	&=
	\mel{pq}{}{ab}
	&
	V^{\pp,(1)}_{ab,rs}
	&=
	\mel{ab}{}{rs}
	\label{eq:UV_pp_first_ee}
	\\
	U^{\pp,(1)}_{pq,ij}
	&=
	\mel{pq}{}{ij}
	&
	V^{\pp,(1)}_{ij,rs}
	&=
	\mel{ij}{}{rs}
	\label{eq:UV_pp_first_hh}
\end{align}
Therefore
\begin{equation}
	\ii\Phi_P^{\pp,(2)}
	=
	U^{\pp,(1)}R^{\pp,(0)}V^{\pp,(1)}
	\label{eq:Phi_pp_second_resolvent}
\end{equation}
which reproduces Eq.~\eqref{eq:Phi_pp_second_native}.
The opposite signs of the $\pp$ and $\hh$ denominators are encoded by the
$\pm1$ eigenvalues of $\eta^\pp$.

Equations~\eqref{eq:Phi_eh_second_resolvent} and
\eqref{eq:Phi_pp_second_resolvent} establish the primitive multiresolvent
representation completely through second order.

\section{Pair-space external coupling vertices at second order}
\label{sec:half_contraction_second}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

We now evaluate the one-sided projections of Sec.~\ref{sec:three_frequency_bse}
for the second-order direct-$\eh$ irreducible vertex.
It is sufficient to display the right external coupling vertex $V$; the left external coupling vertex
$U$ follows from Eq.~\eqref{eq:U_def} by the analogous integration on the
opposite side.

The only external-coupling-vertex pole energies are
\begin{align}
	E^-_{ij;a}
	&=
	\eps_i+\eps_j-\eps_a
	&
	E^+_{ab;i}
	&=
	\eps_a+\eps_b-\eps_i
	\label{eq:E_pm}
\end{align}
corresponding to $\hhp$ states in the $(N-1)$ sector and $\pph$ states in
the $(N+1)$ sector.
For a forward ($+$) pair $ia$, the four elementary contour integrations are
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	&\frac{1}{2\pi}
	\int d\bar\nu\,
	Q^+_{ia}(\bar\nu,\omega)
	\frac{1}{\nu'-\bar\nu+(\eps_c-\eps_k)-\ii0^+}
	\\
	&\qquad=
	\frac{1}{\nu'-E^-_{ik;c}-\ii0^+}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:contour_ehbar_hhp}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	&\frac{1}{2\pi}
	\int d\bar\nu\,
	Q^+_{ia}(\bar\nu,\omega)
	\frac{1}{\nu'-\bar\nu-(\eps_c-\eps_k)+\ii0^+}
	\\
	&\qquad=
	\frac{1}{\nu'+\omega-E^+_{ac;k}+\ii0^+}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:contour_ehbar_pph}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	&\frac{1}{2\pi}
	\int d\bar\nu\,
	Q^+_{ia}(\bar\nu,\omega)
	\frac{1}{\bar\nu+\nu'+\omega-\eps_c-\eps_d+\ii0^+}
	\\
	&\qquad=
	\frac{1}{\nu'+\omega-E^+_{cd;i}+\ii0^+}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:contour_pp_pph}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	&\frac{1}{2\pi}
	\int d\bar\nu\,
	Q^+_{ia}(\bar\nu,\omega)
	\frac{1}{\bar\nu+\nu'+\omega-\eps_k-\eps_l-\ii0^+}
	\\
	&\qquad=
	\frac{1}{\nu'-E^-_{kl;a}-\ii0^+}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:contour_pp_hhp}
\end{equation}
The signs multiplying these terms are inherited from
$\Gamma^{\eh,(2)}$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:Gamma_eh_second_explicit}; they do not
arise from the contour integration.

For the $++$ block,
\begin{equation}
	\left[\ii\Gamma^\eh\right]^{++}_{ia,jb}
	\equiv
	\ii\Gamma^\eh_{aj\,ib}
	\label{eq:Gamma_pp_block}
\end{equation}
so that
\begin{equation}
	V^{++,(1)}_{ia,jb}
	=
	\mel{aj}{}{ib}
	\label{eq:V_pp_first}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	V^{++,(2)}_{ia,jb}(\nu',\omega)
	={}&
	\sum_{kc}
	\frac{\mel{jc}{}{ik}\mel{ka}{}{cb}}
	{\nu'-E^-_{ik;c}-\ii0^+}
	\\
	&-
	\sum_{kc}
	\frac{\mel{jk}{}{ic}\mel{ca}{}{kb}}
	{\nu'+\omega-E^+_{ac;k}+\ii0^+}
	\\
	&-
	\frac{1}{2}
	\sum_{kl}
	\frac{\mel{aj}{}{kl}\mel{lk}{}{bi}}
	{\nu'-E^-_{kl;a}-\ii0^+}
	\\
	&+
	\frac{1}{2}
	\sum_{cd}
	\frac{\mel{aj}{}{cd}\mel{dc}{}{bi}}
	{\nu'+\omega-E^+_{cd;i}+\ii0^+}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:V_pp_second}
\end{equation}
For the $+-$ block,
\begin{equation}
	\left[\ii\Gamma^\eh\right]^{+-}_{ia,jb}
	\equiv
	\ii\Gamma^\eh_{ab\,ij}
	\label{eq:Gamma_pm_block}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
	V^{+-,(1)}_{ia,jb}
	=
	\mel{ab}{}{ij}
	\label{eq:V_pm_first}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	V^{+-,(2)}_{ia,jb}(\nu',\omega)
	={}&
	\sum_{kc}
	\frac{\mel{bc}{}{ik}\mel{ka}{}{cj}}
	{\nu'-E^-_{ik;c}-\ii0^+}
	\\
	&-
	\sum_{kc}
	\frac{\mel{bk}{}{ic}\mel{ca}{}{kj}}
	{\nu'+\omega-E^+_{ac;k}+\ii0^+}
	\\
	&-
	\frac{1}{2}
	\sum_{kl}
	\frac{\mel{ab}{}{kl}\mel{kl}{}{ij}}
	{\nu'-E^-_{kl;a}-\ii0^+}
	\\
	&+
	\frac{1}{2}
	\sum_{cd}
	\frac{\mel{ab}{}{cd}\mel{cd}{}{ij}}
	{\nu'+\omega-E^+_{cd;i}+\ii0^+}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:V_pm_second}
\end{equation}
The $--$ and $-+$ blocks follow by pair reversal
\begin{align}
	V^{--}_{ia,jb}(\nu',\omega)
	&=
	V^{++}_{ia,jb}(\nu'+\omega,-\omega)
	\label{eq:V_mm_reversal}
	\\
	V^{-+}_{ia,jb}(\nu',\omega)
	&=
	V^{+-}_{ia,jb}(\nu'+\omega,-\omega)
	\label{eq:V_mp_reversal}
\end{align}
for real orbitals.
No complex conjugation is applied to the complete time-ordered boundary
value: conjugating the denominators would reverse their causal prescriptions.
For complex orbitals the transformation of the orbital residues must be
stated separately from the transformation of the frequency denominators.
The fermionic frequency associated with each pole is summarized by
\begin{equation}
	\begin{array}{c|cc}
	& \hhp & \pph
	\\
	\hline
	+ & \nu' & \nu'+\omega
	\\
	- & \nu'+\omega & \nu'
	\end{array}
	\label{eq:external_frequency_table}
\end{equation}
The left external coupling vertex $U$ has the same pole spaces, with the surviving
fermionic frequency $\nu$ instead of $\nu'$.

\section{Residue-tensor representation}
\label{sec:residue_tensor}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The second-order external coupling vertices admit a compact spectral representation.
Using ordered intermediate configurations
\begin{align}
	I^-
	&=
	(\mu\nu;\alpha)
	\in\hhp
	&
	E^-_{\mu\nu;\alpha}
	&=
	\eps_\mu+\eps_\nu-\eps_\alpha
	\label{eq:E_minus_general}
	\\
	I^+
	&=
	(\alpha\beta;\mu)
	\in\pph
	&
	E^+_{\alpha\beta;\mu}
	&=
	\eps_\alpha+\eps_\beta-\eps_\mu
	\label{eq:E_plus_general}
\end{align}
the right external coupling vertex for a forward ($+$) first pair has the form
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	V^{+\sigma',(2)}_{ia,jb}(\nu',\omega)
	={}&
	\sum_{I^-}
	\frac{\mathcal R^{+\sigma',-}_{ia,jb;I^-}}
	{\nu'-E^-_{I^-}-\ii0^+}
	\\
	&+
	\sum_{I^+}
	\frac{\mathcal R^{+\sigma',+}_{ia,jb;I^+}}
	{\nu'+\omega-E^+_{I^+}+\ii0^+}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:V_residue_general}
\end{equation}
with $\sigma'=+$ or $-$.
The ordered-tuple convention keeps the $1/2$ factors of the pair channel
explicit.

For the $++$ block,
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\mathcal R^{++,-}_{ia,jb;\mu\nu;\alpha}
	={}&
	\delta_{\mu i}
	\mel{j\alpha}{}{\mu\nu}
	\mel{\nu a}{}{\alpha b}
	\\
	&-
	\frac{1}{2}
	\delta_{\alpha a}
	\mel{aj}{}{\mu\nu}
	\mel{\nu\mu}{}{bi}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:R_pp_minus}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\mathcal R^{++,+}_{ia,jb;\alpha\beta;\mu}
	={}&
	-
	\delta_{\alpha a}
	\mel{j\mu}{}{i\beta}
	\mel{\beta a}{}{\mu b}
	\\
	&+
	\frac{1}{2}
	\delta_{\mu i}
	\mel{aj}{}{\alpha\beta}
	\mel{\beta\alpha}{}{bi}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:R_pp_plus}
\end{equation}
For the $+-$ block,
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\mathcal R^{+-,-}_{ia,jb;\mu\nu;\alpha}
	={}&
	\delta_{\mu i}
	\mel{b\alpha}{}{\mu\nu}
	\mel{\nu a}{}{\alpha j}
	\\
	&-
	\frac{1}{2}
	\delta_{\alpha a}
	\mel{ab}{}{\mu\nu}
	\mel{\mu\nu}{}{ij}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:R_pm_minus}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\mathcal R^{+-,+}_{ia,jb;\alpha\beta;\mu}
	={}&
	-
	\delta_{\alpha a}
	\mel{b\mu}{}{i\beta}
	\mel{\beta a}{}{\mu j}
	\\
	&+
	\frac{1}{2}
	\delta_{\mu i}
	\mel{ab}{}{\alpha\beta}
	\mel{\alpha\beta}{}{ij}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:R_pm_plus}
\end{equation}
The $--$ and $-+$ blocks follow from pair reversal, and the left external coupling vertex
has the analogous residue structure.
The pole spaces are universal ($\hhp$ and $\pph$), while the residues retain
the topology of their parquet channel.
A further coupling--resolvent--coupling factorization of an external coupling vertex
is not generally minimal in the direct-$\eh$ pair grouping; the residue-tensor
form of Eq.~\eqref{eq:V_residue_general} is sufficient here.

\section{Second frequency integration: the eh GF2 kernel}
\label{sec:GF2_contraction}
\label{sec:second_contraction}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The remaining fermionic frequency can now be integrated with the
one-sided factor of the second external pair.
At second order, the projected kernel $\widetilde\Gamma^{\eh,(2)}$ defined in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Gamma_tilde_def} is the correlation part of the GF2 BSE kernel,
which we denote by $\widetilde\Xi_c^{(2)}$ below.
For a forward closure, the elementary $\hhp$ and $\pph$ frequency integrations are
\begin{equation}
	\frac{1}{2\pi}
	\int d\bar\nu\,
	Q^+_{jb}(\bar\nu,\omega)
	\frac{1}
	{\bar\nu-E^- -\ii0^+}
	=
	-\frac{1}
	{\omega-(\eps_b-E^-)+\ii0^+}
	\label{eq:second_contraction_minus}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
	\frac{1}{2\pi}
	\int d\bar\nu\,
	Q^+_{jb}(\bar\nu,\omega)
	\frac{1}
	{\bar\nu+\omega-E^+ +\ii0^+}
	=
	\frac{1}
	{\omega-(E^+-\eps_j)+\ii0^+}
	\label{eq:second_contraction_plus}
\end{equation}
Applying these identities to Eq.~\eqref{eq:V_pp_second} gives the
second-order resonant block of the effective kernel
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\left[\widetilde\Xi_c^{(2)}(\omega)\right]^{++}_{ia,jb}
	={}&
	-
	\sum_{kc}
	\frac{
	\mel{jc}{}{ik}
	\mel{ka}{}{cb}
	}
	{\omega-(\eps_b-\eps_i+\eps_c-\eps_k)}
	\\
	&-
	\sum_{kc}
	\frac{
	\mel{jk}{}{ic}
	\mel{ca}{}{kb}
	}
	{\omega-(\eps_a-\eps_j+\eps_c-\eps_k)}
	\\
	&+
	\frac{1}{2}
	\sum_{kl}
	\frac{
	\mel{aj}{}{kl}
	\mel{lk}{}{bi}
	}
	{\omega-(\eps_a+\eps_b-\eps_k-\eps_l)}
	\\
	&+
	\frac{1}{2}
	\sum_{cd}
	\frac{
	\mel{aj}{}{cd}
	\mel{dc}{}{bi}
	}
	{\omega-(\eps_c+\eps_d-\eps_i-\eps_j)}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:GF2_A}
\end{equation}

Closing the $+-$ right external coupling vertex with a backward ($-$) pair gives
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\left[\widetilde\Xi_c^{(2)}\right]^{+-}_{ia,jb}
	={}&
	-
	\sum_{kc}
	\frac{
	\mel{bc}{}{ik}
	\mel{ka}{}{cj}
	}
	{-(\eps_b-\eps_i+\eps_c-\eps_k)}
	\\
	&-
	\sum_{kc}
	\frac{
	\mel{bk}{}{ic}
	\mel{ca}{}{kj}
	}
	{-(\eps_a-\eps_j+\eps_c-\eps_k)}
	\\
	&+
	\frac{1}{2}
	\sum_{kl}
	\frac{
	\mel{ab}{}{kl}
	\mel{kl}{}{ij}
	}
	{-(\eps_a+\eps_b-\eps_k-\eps_l)}
	\\
	&+
	\frac{1}{2}
	\sum_{cd}
	\frac{
	\mel{ab}{}{cd}
	\mel{cd}{}{ij}
	}
	{-(\eps_c+\eps_d-\eps_i-\eps_j)}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:GF2_B}
\end{equation}
The external transfer frequency cancels in the coupling block.
This is a consequence of closing the resolvents entering the external coupling vertices with a backward
external pair rather than a new type of intermediate-state propagation.

The complete effective kernel therefore has the familiar Casida-like
structure
\begin{equation}
	\widetilde{\boldsymbol\Xi}^{(2)}(\omega)
	=
	\begin{pmatrix}
	A_c^{(2)}(\omega)
	&
	B_c^{(2)}
	\\
	B_c^{(2)\mathsf T}
	&
	A_c^{(2)\mathsf T}(-\omega)
	\end{pmatrix}
	\label{eq:GF2_matrix}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathsf T$ denotes transposition of the orbital-pair indices only.
It does not complex-conjugate the time-ordered frequency denominators.
This distinction is essential because pair reversal maps the analytic
boundary value to the corresponding expression at $-\omega$ without changing
the sign of its causal prescription.
Equations~\eqref{eq:GF2_A} and \eqref{eq:GF2_B} give the effective
GF2 correlation kernel in the present antisymmetrized pair convention.
A direct contour evaluation of the original three-frequency kernel provides
an independent check of every term.
In particular, the last two terms of Eq.~\eqref{eq:GF2_B} contain
$\mel{kl}{}{ij}$ and $\mel{cd}{}{ij}$, respectively.
Reversing only one of these antisymmetric pairs would introduce an
additional minus sign and is therefore inconsistent with the
three-frequency expression in Eq.~\eqref{eq:Gamma_eh_second_explicit}.
This check fixes the channel frequency maps, fermionic signs, and
pair-channel factors without relying on the already integrated kernel.

The two-sided frequency projection used here defines the conventional
one-frequency effective kernel and is employed only as an independent check of
the second-order three-frequency construction.
No equivalence with the fully unprojected BSE is assumed in this section.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{Second frequency integration in the pp channel}
\label{sec:pp_gf2_check}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The $\pp$ channel provides an especially stringent independent check because
the non-interacting pair propagator is antisymmetric under exchange of the two
members of each pair.
It is therefore important to distinguish a single oriented frequency routing
from the full antisymmetrized pair projection used in the pp BSE.
Let $x=\nu_\pp$ and $y=\nu'_\pp$ denote the native left and right fermionic
frequencies.
The four external orbital legs carry
\begin{equation}
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
	& p & q & r & s
	\\
\hline
\eh
	& \nu & \nu'+\omega & \nu+\omega & \nu'
	\\
\pp
	& \Omega+x & -x & \Omega+y & -y
\end{array}
\label{eq:pp_leg_frequency_table}
\end{equation}
which is consistent with Eq.~\eqref{eq:pp_frequency_map}.
Equivalently, with centered relative frequencies
\begin{equation}
	\widetilde\nu=x+\frac{\Omega}{2}
	\qquad
	\overline\nu=y+\frac{\Omega}{2}
	\label{eq:pp_centered_relative_frequencies}
\end{equation}
the two members of each pair carry $\Omega/2\pm\widetilde\nu$ or
$\Omega/2\pm\overline\nu$.

For an oriented external pair, the elementary one-sided factors are useful
for tracking the poles associated with the two individual legs.
However, the physical pp BSE is formulated in a normalized antisymmetric
pair basis.
The non-interacting pp propagator therefore contains both the direct and exchange
products of one-particle Green's functions,
\begin{equation}
	L_0^\pp
	\propto
	\frac{1}{2}
	\left(
	G_0G_0-G_0G_0\big|_{p\leftrightarrow q}
	\right)
	\label{eq:pp_free_antisym_reminder}
\end{equation}
and both orientations must be retained in each frequency projection.
Keeping only one oriented factor is sufficient to diagnose which external
orbital is attached to a given pole, but it does not generate the complete
antisymmetrized $C$, $B$, and $D$ blocks.
This observation resolves the apparent mismatch encountered in a
single-orientation closure.

We define the energy-valued projected correlation kernel by
\begin{equation}
	\mathcal K_c^{\pp,(2)}
	\equiv
	\ii\widetilde\Xi_c^{\pp,(2)}
	\label{eq:pp_Kc_definition}
\end{equation}
The full antisymmetric two-sided projection of
$\Gamma^{\pp,(2)}=\Phi^{\eh,(2)}+\Phi^{\teh,(2)}$ then yields the three
independent pp-GF2 blocks.
For the $\ee\to\ee$ block, introduce
\begin{align}
	\mathcal C^{(1)}_{ab,cd;me}
	&=
	\mel{am}{}{ce}\mel{eb}{}{md}
	-
	\mel{bm}{}{ce}\mel{ea}{}{md}
	\label{eq:pp_GF2_C_residue_1}
	\\
	\mathcal C^{(2)}_{ab,cd;me}
	&=
	\mel{ae}{}{cm}\mel{mb}{}{ed}
	-
	\mel{be}{}{cm}\mel{ma}{}{ed}
	\label{eq:pp_GF2_C_residue_2}
\end{align}
so that
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\left[\mathcal K_c^{\pp,(2)}(\Omega)\right]_{ab,cd}
={}&
\frac{1}{2}\sum_{me}
\frac{\mathcal C^{(1)}_{ab,cd;me}}
{\Omega-(\eps_a+\eps_c+\eps_e-\eps_m)+\ii0^+}
\\
&+
\frac{1}{2}\sum_{me}
\frac{\mathcal C^{(2)}_{ab,cd;me}}
{\Omega-(\eps_b+\eps_d+\eps_e-\eps_m)+\ii0^+}
\\
&+
\frac{1}{2}\sum_{me}
\frac{\mathcal C^{(1)}_{ab,cd;me}}
{\Omega-(\eps_b+\eps_c+\eps_e-\eps_m)+\ii0^+}
\\
&+
\frac{1}{2}\sum_{me}
\frac{\mathcal C^{(2)}_{ab,cd;me}}
{\Omega-(\eps_a+\eps_d+\eps_e-\eps_m)+\ii0^+}
\end{split}
\label{eq:pp_GF2_C}
\end{equation}

For the $\ee\leftrightarrow\hh$ coupling block, define
\begin{align}
	\mathcal B^{(1)}_{ab,ij;me}
	&=
	\mel{am}{}{ie}\mel{eb}{}{mj}
	-
	\mel{bm}{}{ie}\mel{ea}{}{mj}
	\label{eq:pp_GF2_B_residue_1}
	\\
	\mathcal B^{(2)}_{ab,ij;me}
	&=
	\mel{ae}{}{im}\mel{mb}{}{ej}
	-
	\mel{be}{}{im}\mel{ma}{}{ej}
	\label{eq:pp_GF2_B_residue_2}
\end{align}
which gives the static block
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\left[\mathcal K_c^{\pp,(2)}\right]_{ab,ij}
={}&
\frac{1}{2}\sum_{me}
\frac{\mathcal B^{(1)}_{ab,ij;me}}
{-(\eps_a-\eps_j+\eps_e-\eps_m)+\ii0^+}
\\
&+
\frac{1}{2}\sum_{me}
\frac{\mathcal B^{(2)}_{ab,ij;me}}
{-(\eps_b-\eps_i+\eps_e-\eps_m)+\ii0^+}
\\
&+
\frac{1}{2}\sum_{me}
\frac{\mathcal B^{(1)}_{ab,ij;me}}
{-(\eps_b-\eps_j+\eps_e-\eps_m)+\ii0^+}
\\
&+
\frac{1}{2}\sum_{me}
\frac{\mathcal B^{(2)}_{ab,ij;me}}
{-(\eps_a-\eps_i+\eps_e-\eps_m)+\ii0^+}
\end{split}
\label{eq:pp_GF2_B}
\end{equation}
The cancellation of the external pair frequency is therefore a consequence
of the complete $\ee$--$\hh$ closure, not an additional approximation.

For the $\hh\to\hh$ block, define
\begin{align}
	\mathcal D^{(1)}_{ij,kl;me}
	&=
	\mel{im}{}{ke}\mel{ej}{}{ml}
	-
	\mel{jm}{}{ke}\mel{ei}{}{ml}
	\label{eq:pp_GF2_D_residue_1}
	\\
	\mathcal D^{(2)}_{ij,kl;me}
	&=
	\mel{ie}{}{km}\mel{mj}{}{el}
	-
	\mel{je}{}{km}\mel{mi}{}{el}
	\label{eq:pp_GF2_D_residue_2}
\end{align}
and obtain
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\left[\mathcal K_c^{\pp,(2)}(-\Omega)\right]_{ij,kl}
={}&
\frac{1}{2}\sum_{me}
\frac{\mathcal D^{(1)}_{ij,kl;me}}
{\Omega-(-\eps_j-\eps_l+\eps_e-\eps_m)+\ii0^+}
\\
&+
\frac{1}{2}\sum_{me}
\frac{\mathcal D^{(2)}_{ij,kl;me}}
{\Omega-(-\eps_i-\eps_k+\eps_e-\eps_m)+\ii0^+}
\\
&+
\frac{1}{2}\sum_{me}
\frac{\mathcal D^{(1)}_{ij,kl;me}}
{\Omega-(-\eps_i-\eps_l+\eps_e-\eps_m)+\ii0^+}
\\
&+
\frac{1}{2}\sum_{me}
\frac{\mathcal D^{(2)}_{ij,kl;me}}
{\Omega-(-\eps_j-\eps_k+\eps_e-\eps_m)+\ii0^+}
\end{split}
\label{eq:pp_GF2_D}
\end{equation}

The complete ppBSE matrix therefore contains
\begin{align}
	C^{\rm GF2}_{ab,cd}(\Omega)
	&=
	(\eps_a+\eps_b)\delta_{ac}\delta_{bd}
	+
	\mel{ab}{}{cd}
	+
	\left[\mathcal K_c^{\pp,(2)}(\Omega)\right]_{ab,cd}
	\label{eq:pp_GF2_C_full}
	\\
	B^{\rm GF2}_{ab,ij}
	&=
	\mel{ab}{}{ij}
	+
	\left[\mathcal K_c^{\pp,(2)}\right]_{ab,ij}
	\label{eq:pp_GF2_B_full}
	\\
	D^{\rm GF2}_{ij,kl}(-\Omega)
	&=
	-(\eps_i+\eps_j)\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl}
	+
	\mel{ij}{}{kl}
	+
	\left[\mathcal K_c^{\pp,(2)}(-\Omega)\right]_{ij,kl}
	\label{eq:pp_GF2_D_full}
\end{align}
Equations~\eqref{eq:pp_GF2_C}--\eqref{eq:pp_GF2_D} coincide term by term
with the independently derived anomalous-GF2 pp kernel in the normalized
antisymmetric pair basis.\cite{MarieRomanielloBlaseLoos2025}
The agreement simultaneously checks the direct and exchange pieces of the
non-interacting pp propagator, the orbital-frequency routing, the relative signs of the
four attachments, and the pair normalization.
In particular, the overall factor $1/2$ in the correlation blocks is the
normalized-pair remnant of the direct-minus-exchange non-interacting pair propagator.
Thus all three independent blocks, $C(\Omega)$, $B$, and $D(-\Omega)$,
provide a complete second-order pp validation rather than only a routing
check.

The $\eh$ and $\pp$ reductions therefore exhibit the parallel structure
\begin{equation}
\begin{array}{ccc}
\eh & & \pp
\\[3pt]
A(\omega) & \longleftrightarrow & C(\Omega)
\\
B & \longleftrightarrow & B
\\
A^{\mathsf T}(-\omega) & \longleftrightarrow & D(-\Omega)
\end{array}
\label{eq:eh_pp_GF2_parallel}
\end{equation}
where $\mathsf T$ acts only on orbital-pair indices and does not conjugate
the time-ordered boundary value.


\section{Usual dynamical GW kernel: first order in $W$}
\label{sec:gw_first}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The second-order bare-interaction construction above suggests a natural
resummed analogue based on the screened interaction.
For the $GW$ self-energy,
\begin{equation}
	\Sigma_{\xc}^{GW}(12)
	=
	\ii G(12)W(1^+2)
	\label{eq:Sigma_GW}
\end{equation}
the functional derivative entering the direct-$\eh$ BSE kernel separates as
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\Xi^{GW}(36;45)
	={}&
	\Xi_H(36;45)
	+
	\ii\delta(35)\delta(46)W(3^+4)
	\\
	&+
	\ii G(34)\frac{\delta W(3^+4)}{\delta G(56)}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:Xi_GW_split}
\end{equation}
We organize the non-Hartree part as an expansion in powers of the screened
interaction $W$.
The usual $GW$ kernel is the term first order in $W$,
\begin{equation}
	K^{GW,(1)}=W
	\label{eq:GW_K_first_W}
\end{equation}
up to the index ordering and factors of $\ii$ associated with the present
four-point convention.
Using the screened-interaction Dyson equation,
\begin{equation}
	\frac{\delta W}{\delta G}
	=
	W\frac{\delta P}{\delta G}W
	\label{eq:deltaW_deltaG_order}
\end{equation}
the last term of Eq.~\eqref{eq:Xi_GW_split} is explicitly second order in
$W$ and is not considered here.
Thus, throughout this section ``the $GW$ kernel'' means the usual dynamical
first-order kernel $K^{GW,(1)}=W$, without a static-screening approximation.

It is important to distinguish the direct Coulomb matrix elements entering
$W$ from the antisymmetrized matrix elements used in the GF2 construction.
We denote a non-antisymmetrized Coulomb integral by $(pq|rs)$, whereas
$\mel{pq}{}{rs}$ remains antisymmetrized.
For real orbitals, the RPA spectral representation of the correlation part
$W_c$ is
\begin{equation}
	W^c_{pqrs}(\Omega)
	=
	\sum_{m>0}
	g^m_{pr}g^m_{qs}
	\left[
	\frac{1}{\Omega-\Omega_m+\ii0^+}
	-
	\frac{1}{\Omega+\Omega_m-\ii0^+}
	\right]
	\label{eq:Wc_spectral}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
	g^m_{pr}
	=
	\sum_{kc}
	(pc|rk)
	\left(
	X^m_{kc}+Y^m_{kc}
	\right)
	\label{eq:gw_transition_integral}
\end{equation}
The instantaneous bare part of $W$ reproduces the first-order interaction
and is omitted from the correlation formulas below whenever it is displayed
separately.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Electron-hole GW kernel}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

With the direct-$\eh$ frequency convention of Sec.~\ref{sec:frequencies},
the correlation part of the first-order-in-$W$ kernel is
\begin{equation}
	\left[
	\ii\Gamma_{W,c}^{GW}
	\right]_{pqrs}
	(\nu,\nu',\omega)
	=
	-
	W^c_{pqsr}(\nu'-\nu)
	\label{eq:GW_three_frequency}
\end{equation}
Thus,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
	\left[
	\ii\Gamma_{W,c}^{GW}
	\right]^{++}_{ia,jb}
	={}&
	-
	\sum_{m>0}
	\frac{g^m_{ab}g^m_{ji}}
	{\nu'-\nu-\Omega_m+\ii0^+}
	\\
	&+
	\sum_{m>0}
	\frac{g^m_{ab}g^m_{ji}}
	{\nu'-\nu+\Omega_m-\ii0^+}
\end{split}
\label{eq:GW_three_frequency_pp}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
	\left[
	\ii\Gamma_{W,c}^{GW}
	\right]^{+-}_{ia,jb}
	={}&
	-
	\sum_{m>0}
	\frac{g^m_{aj}g^m_{bi}}
	{\nu'-\nu-\Omega_m+\ii0^+}
	\\
	&+
	\sum_{m>0}
	\frac{g^m_{aj}g^m_{bi}}
	{\nu'-\nu+\Omega_m-\ii0^+}
\end{split}
\label{eq:GW_three_frequency_pm}
\end{equation}
The first one-sided integration gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
	V_{W,c}^{GW,++}{}_{ia,jb}(\nu',\omega)
	={}&
	\sum_{m>0}
	\frac{g^m_{ab}g^m_{ji}}
	{\nu'-(\eps_i-\Omega_m)-\ii0^+}
	\\
	&-
	\sum_{m>0}
	\frac{g^m_{ab}g^m_{ji}}
	{\nu'+\omega-(\eps_a+\Omega_m)+\ii0^+}
\end{split}
\label{eq:GW_external_pp}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
	V_{W,c}^{GW,+-}{}_{ia,jb}(\nu',\omega)
	={}&
	\sum_{m>0}
	\frac{g^m_{aj}g^m_{bi}}
	{\nu'-(\eps_i-\Omega_m)-\ii0^+}
	\\
	&-
	\sum_{m>0}
	\frac{g^m_{aj}g^m_{bi}}
	{\nu'+\omega-(\eps_a+\Omega_m)+\ii0^+}
\end{split}
\label{eq:GW_external_pm}
\end{equation}
with hole--mode and particle--mode pole energies
\begin{equation}
	E^-_{i;m}=\eps_i-\Omega_m
	\qquad
	E^+_{a;m}=\eps_a+\Omega_m
	\label{eq:GW_external_poles}
\end{equation}
The second integration yields the two independent $\eh$ blocks
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\left[
	\widetilde\Xi_{W,c}^{GW}(\omega)
	\right]^{++}_{ia,jb}
	={}&
	-
	\sum_{m>0}
	\frac{g^m_{ab}g^m_{ji}}
	{\omega-(\eps_b-\eps_i+\Omega_m)+\ii0^+}
	\\
	&-
	\sum_{m>0}
	\frac{g^m_{ab}g^m_{ji}}
	{\omega-(\eps_a-\eps_j+\Omega_m)+\ii0^+}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:GW_effective_A}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\left[
	\widetilde\Xi_{W,c}^{GW}
	\right]^{+-}_{ia,jb}
	={}&
	\sum_{m>0}
	\frac{g^m_{aj}g^m_{bi}}
	{\eps_b-\eps_i+\Omega_m}
	\\
	&+
	\sum_{m>0}
	\frac{g^m_{aj}g^m_{bi}}
	{\eps_a-\eps_j+\Omega_m}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:GW_effective_B}
\end{equation}
The $--$ and $-+$ blocks follow from the same pair-reversal operation as in
Sec.~\ref{sec:half_contraction_second}.
Equations~\eqref{eq:GW_effective_A} and \eqref{eq:GW_effective_B} are the
standard fully dynamical first-order-in-$W$ $\eh$ kernel obtained directly
from the $GW$ self-energy.\cite{MarieAmmarLoos2024}
Thus the one-sided reduction reproduces both independent $\eh$ blocks before
any static approximation is made.

The multiresolvent interpretation is immediate.
Introduce an auxiliary neutral pair $kc$ together with an RPA mode $m$ and
two attachment labels $\lambda=1,2$, with energy
\begin{equation}
	\mathcal E_{kc,m}
	=
	\eps_c-\eps_k+\Omega_m
	\label{eq:GW_aux_energy}
\end{equation}
and couplings
\begin{align}
	C^{GW,1}_{ia,kc;m}
	&=
	\delta_{ik}g^m_{ac}
	&
	D^{GW,1}_{kc;m,jb}
	&=
	\delta_{cb}g^m_{jk}
	\label{eq:GW_aux_coupling_1}
	\\
	C^{GW,2}_{ia,kc;m}
	&=
	\delta_{ac}g^m_{ki}
	&
	D^{GW,2}_{kc;m,jb}
	&=
	\delta_{jk}g^m_{cb}
	\label{eq:GW_aux_coupling_2}
\end{align}
so that the resonant block can be written as
\begin{equation}
	\left[
	\widetilde\Xi_{W,c}^{GW}(\omega)
	\right]^{++}
	=
	-
	\sum_{\lambda=1}^{2}
	C^{GW,\lambda}
	\left[
	\omega-M_{GW}^{(0)}
	\right]^{-1}
	D^{GW,\lambda}
	\label{eq:GW_multiresolvent}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
	\left[M_{GW}^{(0)}\right]_{kc;m,k'c';n}
	=
	\mathcal E_{kc,m}
	\delta_{kk'}\delta_{cc'}\delta_{mn}
	\label{eq:GW_aux_matrix}
\end{equation}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Particle-particle GW kernel}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The corresponding pp kernel is known independently from the anomalous
$GW$ self-energy.\cite{MarieRomanielloBlaseLoos2025}
In the normal limit,
\begin{equation}
	\Sigma^{ee,GW}(11')
	=
	-\ii\,G^{ee}(33')W(33';11')
	\label{eq:Sigma_ee_GW}
\end{equation}
and differentiation with respect to the anomalous propagator gives the
first-order-in-$W$ pp kernel
\begin{equation}
	\ii\Xi^{\pp,GW}(11';22')
	=
	\frac{1}{2}
	\left[
	W(11';22')-W(11;2'2)
	\right]
	\label{eq:Xi_pp_GW_firstW}
\end{equation}
The derivative of $W$ itself does not contribute in the normal limit because
it is multiplied by the anomalous propagator that is set to zero after the
functional derivative.
Hence Eq.~\eqref{eq:Xi_pp_GW_firstW} is precisely the pp counterpart of the
usual first-order-in-$W$ $\eh$ kernel considered above.

We define the energy-valued projected pp kernel by
\begin{equation}
	\mathcal K^{\pp,GW}
	\equiv
	\ii\widetilde\Xi^{\pp,GW}
	\label{eq:K_pp_GW_definition}
\end{equation}
For real orbitals, introduce the two antisymmetrized transition-density
combinations entering the $\ee\to\ee$ block,
\begin{align}
	\mathcal C^{GW,(1)}_{ab,cd;m}
	&=
	g^m_{ac}g^m_{db}-g^m_{bc}g^m_{da}
	\label{eq:pp_GW_C_residue_1}
	\\
	\mathcal C^{GW,(2)}_{ab,cd;m}
	&=
	g^m_{ca}g^m_{bd}-g^m_{cb}g^m_{ad}
	\label{eq:pp_GW_C_residue_2}
\end{align}
The complete dynamical $C$ block is
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\mathcal K^{\pp,GW}_{ab,cd}(\Omega)
={}&
\mel{ab}{}{cd}
+
\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m>0}
\frac{\mathcal C^{GW,(1)}_{ab,cd;m}}
{\Omega-(\eps_a+\eps_c+\Omega_m)+\ii0^+}
\\
&+
\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m>0}
\frac{\mathcal C^{GW,(2)}_{ab,cd;m}}
{\Omega-(\eps_b+\eps_d+\Omega_m)+\ii0^+}
\\
&+
\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m>0}
\frac{\mathcal C^{GW,(1)}_{ab,cd;m}}
{\Omega-(\eps_b+\eps_c+\Omega_m)+\ii0^+}
\\
&+
\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m>0}
\frac{\mathcal C^{GW,(2)}_{ab,cd;m}}
{\Omega-(\eps_a+\eps_d+\Omega_m)+\ii0^+}
\end{split}
\label{eq:pp_GW_C}
\end{equation}

For the $\ee\leftrightarrow\hh$ block, define
\begin{align}
	\mathcal B^{GW,(1)}_{ab,ij;m}
	&=
	g^m_{ai}g^m_{jb}-g^m_{bi}g^m_{ja}
	\label{eq:pp_GW_B_residue_1}
	\\
	\mathcal B^{GW,(2)}_{ab,ij;m}
	&=
	g^m_{ia}g^m_{bj}-g^m_{ib}g^m_{aj}
	\label{eq:pp_GW_B_residue_2}
\end{align}
which gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\mathcal K^{\pp,GW}_{ab,ij}
={}&
\mel{ab}{}{ij}
+
\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m>0}
\frac{\mathcal B^{GW,(1)}_{ab,ij;m}}
{-(\eps_a-\eps_j+\Omega_m)+\ii0^+}
\\
&+
\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m>0}
\frac{\mathcal B^{GW,(2)}_{ab,ij;m}}
{-(\eps_b-\eps_i+\Omega_m)+\ii0^+}
\\
&+
\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m>0}
\frac{\mathcal B^{GW,(1)}_{ab,ij;m}}
{-(\eps_b-\eps_j+\Omega_m)+\ii0^+}
\\
&+
\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m>0}
\frac{\mathcal B^{GW,(2)}_{ab,ij;m}}
{-(\eps_a-\eps_i+\Omega_m)+\ii0^+}
\end{split}
\label{eq:pp_GW_B}
\end{equation}
which is static with respect to the external pair frequency.

For the $\hh\to\hh$ block, define
\begin{align}
	\mathcal D^{GW,(1)}_{ij,kl;m}
	&=
	g^m_{ik}g^m_{lj}-g^m_{jk}g^m_{li}
	\label{eq:pp_GW_D_residue_1}
	\\
	\mathcal D^{GW,(2)}_{ij,kl;m}
	&=
	g^m_{ki}g^m_{jl}-g^m_{kj}g^m_{il}
	\label{eq:pp_GW_D_residue_2}
\end{align}
and obtain
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\mathcal K^{\pp,GW}_{ij,kl}(-\Omega)
={}&
\mel{ij}{}{kl}
+
\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m>0}
\frac{\mathcal D^{GW,(1)}_{ij,kl;m}}
{\Omega-(-\eps_j-\eps_l+\Omega_m)+\ii0^+}
\\
&+
\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m>0}
\frac{\mathcal D^{GW,(2)}_{ij,kl;m}}
{\Omega-(-\eps_i-\eps_k+\Omega_m)+\ii0^+}
\\
&+
\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m>0}
\frac{\mathcal D^{GW,(1)}_{ij,kl;m}}
{\Omega-(-\eps_i-\eps_l+\Omega_m)+\ii0^+}
\\
&+
\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m>0}
\frac{\mathcal D^{GW,(2)}_{ij,kl;m}}
{\Omega-(-\eps_j-\eps_k+\Omega_m)+\ii0^+}
\end{split}
\label{eq:pp_GW_D}
\end{equation}
The factor $1/2$ in each dynamical sum of Eq.~\eqref{eq:pp_GW_D} is essential;
it is the corrected expression of the $\hh$--$\hh$ dynamical ppBSE@$GW$
kernel.\cite{MarieRomanielloBlaseLoosErratum2026}

Equations~\eqref{eq:pp_GW_C}--\eqref{eq:pp_GW_D} provide an independent
first-order-in-$W$ validation of exactly the same antisymmetric pp frequency
projection used in Sec.~\ref{sec:pp_gf2_check}.
The check is genuinely independent of GF2 because the bare second-order
particle--hole intermediate state is replaced by an RPA neutral mode.
Nevertheless, the block topology is identical:
$C(\Omega)$ and $D(-\Omega)$ remain dynamical, whereas the coupling block
$B$ is static.
In the static-screening limit $W(\Omega)\to W(0)$ these expressions reduce to
\begin{align}
	\mathcal K^{\pp,GW}_{ab,cd}
	&=
	W_{abcd}(0)-W_{abdc}(0)
	\\
	\mathcal K^{\pp,GW}_{ab,ij}
	&=
	W_{abij}(0)-W_{abji}(0)
	\\
	\mathcal K^{\pp,GW}_{ij,kl}
	&=
	W_{ijkl}(0)-W_{ijlk}(0)
	\label{eq:pp_GW_static_limit}
\end{align}
which is the standard static ppBSE@$GW$ kernel.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Comparison of the GF2 and GW checks}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The complete validation pattern can now be summarized as
\begin{equation}
\begin{array}{c|cc}
	& \eh & \pp
	\\
\hline
\text{GF2}
	& A(\omega),\ B
	& C(\Omega),\ B,\ D(-\Omega)
	\\
GW[W]
	& A^{GW}(\omega),\ B^{GW}
	& C^{GW}(\Omega),\ B^{GW},\ D^{GW}(-\Omega)
\end{array}
\label{eq:complete_validation_table}
\end{equation}
The GF2 and $GW$ tests therefore probe the same frequency-reduction
machinery with two independent physical kernels.
For $GW$, the independent-particle limit of an RPA mode,
\begin{equation}
	\Omega_m\longrightarrow\eps_d-\eps_l
	\qquad
	X^m_{kc}+Y^m_{kc}
	\longrightarrow
	\delta_{kl}\delta_{cd}
	\label{eq:GW_IP_limit}
\end{equation}
reduces the RPA-mode denominators to the corresponding particle--hole-like
second-order pole manifold.
The residues do not become the complete GF2 residues because the screened
interaction resums the direct ring channel, while GF2 also contains exchange
and explicit pair-channel contributions.


\section{Channel consistency at second order}
\label{sec:channel_consistency}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The second-order construction can be checked in several independent ways.
The first check is crossing.
The complete transverse $\eh$ contribution must be obtained from
the direct $\eh$ reducible vertex through
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Phi_ehbar_crossing}.
This fixes both the orbital permutation and the frequency combination
$\nu'-\nu$.

The second check uses the $\pp$ convention.
The native pair propagator depends only on $\Omega_\pp$, and the exact
frequency transformation of Eq.~\eqref{eq:pp_frequency_map} fixes the
combination $\nu+\nu'+\omega$ in the direct $\eh$ convention.
One-sided frequency integration of this object must produce the same $\hhp$ and $\pph$
external-coupling-vertex poles obtained from the transverse $\eh$ contribution.

The third check is the recovery of the $\eh$ GF2 kernel after integrating
over the remaining fermionic frequency, as shown in
Sec.~\ref{sec:second_contraction}.
This check is independent of the initial channel-resolved derivation and is
sensitive to signs, orbital-index permutations, and pair-channel factors.
The pp reduction of Sec.~\ref{sec:pp_gf2_check} provides a second,
fully independent validation.
Using the complete antisymmetric non-interacting pair propagator, all three pp-GF2
blocks $C(\Omega)$, $B$, and $D(-\Omega)$ are recovered term by term.
The first-order-in-$W$ $GW$ construction provides the same overdetermined
check with independent RPA-mode residues in both the $\eh$ and $\pp$
channels.

Finally, the full second-order vertex must be independent of the channel
through which it is reconstructed.
Using the parquet relations,
\begin{align}
	\Gamma^{\eh,(2)}+\Phi^{\eh,(2)}
	&=
	\Phi^{\eh,(2)}+\Phi^{\teh,(2)}+\Phi^{\pp,(2)}
	\label{eq:F_second_eh}
	\\
	\Gamma^{\pp,(2)}+\Phi^{\pp,(2)}
	&=
	\Phi^{\eh,(2)}+\Phi^{\teh,(2)}+\Phi^{\pp,(2)}
	\label{eq:F_second_pp}
	\\
	F^{(2)}
	&=
	\Gamma^{\eh,(2)}+\Phi^{\eh,(2)}
	=
	\Gamma^{\pp,(2)}+\Phi^{\pp,(2)}
	\label{eq:channel_consistency_second}
\end{align}
The same channel organization reconstructs the direct $\eh$ Hedin
kernel through Eq.~\eqref{eq:Gamma_eh}.

\section{Recipe through second order}
\label{sec:recipe_second}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The construction is now complete through second order.
Using the channel transfers defined in Sec.~\ref{sec:frequencies}, construct
the two primitive reducible vertices $\Phi^{\eh,(2)}$ and
$\Phi^{\pp,(2)}$ in their natural channels and obtain
$\Phi^{\teh,(2)}$ by crossing.
The two primitive vertices are represented as
\begin{align}
	\ii\Phi^{\eh,(2)}
	&=
	U^{\eh,(1)}R^{\eh,(0)}V^{\eh,(1)}
	\label{eq:recipe_second_eh}
	\\
	\ii\Phi^{\pp,(2)}
	&=
	U^{\pp,(1)}R^{\pp,(0)}V^{\pp,(1)}
	\label{eq:recipe_second_pp}
\end{align}
where the central resolvents describe neutral $N$-electron and charged
$(N\pm2)$-electron propagation, respectively.

The second-order channel-irreducible vertices follow from the parquet
relations of Eqs.~\eqref{eq:Gamma_eh_second} and \eqref{eq:Gamma_pp_second}.
A one-sided frequency integration generates external coupling vertices whose only
non-trivial poles are of $\hhp$ and $\pph$ origin.

A second frequency integration yields the one-frequency $\eh$ GF2 BSE
kernel and all three independent blocks of the pp-GF2 kernel.
The same projection machinery applied to the usual $GW$
kernel reproduces the known dynamical $\eh$ blocks.
Finally,
\begin{equation}
	F^{(2)}
	=
	\Gamma^{\eh,(2)}+\Phi^{\eh,(2)}
	=
	\Gamma^{\pp,(2)}+\Phi^{\pp,(2)}
	\label{eq:recipe_second_F}
\end{equation}
