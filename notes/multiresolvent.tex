\title{Multiresolvent Representation of the Parquet Vertex through Third Order}

\author{Pierre-Fran\c{c}ois \surname{Loos}}
	\email{loos@irsamc.ups-tlse.fr}
	\affiliation{\LCPQ}

\begin{abstract}
A general four-point vertex depends on three independent frequencies and cannot, in general, be represented by the single resolvent familiar from linear-response theory or conventional algebraic-diagrammatic construction.
In these notes, we develop a channel-resolved multiresolvent representation of the parquet vertex through third order in the interaction.
The channel-reducible vertices $\Phi^\eh$ and $\Phi^\pp$ are chosen as the two primitive objects because their central propagation occurs naturally in the $N$- and $(N\pm2)$-electron sectors, respectively, while the transverse electron-hole contribution follows from crossing symmetry.
In each channel, the reducible vertex is represented as two frequency-dependent external vertices connected by a central resolvent.
The direct electron-hole irreducible vertex, and therefore the corresponding four-point Hedin kernel, is reconstructed from $\Gamma^\eh=\Lambda+\Phi^{\teh}+\Phi^\pp$.
At second order, integrating over one fermionic frequency of the exact three-frequency kernel with the corresponding noninteracting pair propagator produces the two-frequency external vertices. In both the electron-hole and particle-particle channels, their poles belong exclusively to the $(N\pm1)$-electron sectors.
Integrating over the remaining fermionic frequency recovers the known one-frequency GF2 Bethe--Salpeter kernels in both channels.
At third order, the direct electron-hole reducible vertex is generated by second-order corrections to the two external vertices together with a first-order correction to the central neutral resolvent.
The analogous particle-particle construction has the same external-vertex structure but a central resolvent associated with the $(N\pm2)$-electron sectors.
The resulting formulation provides a compact recipe for reconstructing the full parquet vertex and the Hedin kernel from ordinary resolvents associated with the $N$-, $(N\pm1)$-, and $(N\pm2)$-electron sectors.
\end{abstract}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

A general four-point quantity contains substantially more analytic
structure than a one-particle Green's function or a linear-response
propagator.
For a time-independent Hamiltonian, time-translation invariance removes
one overall time variable, leaving three independent time differences or,
equivalently, three independent frequencies.
The full two-particle vertex can therefore be written as
$F(\nu,\nu',\omega)$, where the three frequency variables are naturally
associated with different two-particle scattering channels.

This three-frequency dependence makes a direct extension of conventional
spectral or algebraic-diagrammatic representations non-trivial.
A one-particle self-energy can be written in terms of resolvents in the
$(N\pm1)$-particle sectors, while a polarization propagator is
naturally expressed through an $N$-electron neutral-excitation resolvent.
For a four-point vertex, these different propagation sectors coexist.
The natural objective is therefore not to introduce a single formal
resolvent depending simultaneously on three frequencies, but to organize
the vertex as a product or chain of ordinary resolvents, each associated
with a physically well-defined intermediate-state space.

Parquet theory provides a particularly natural starting point for such a
construction.
The full vertex is decomposed according to two-particle reducibility in
the electron-hole ($\eh$), transverse electron-hole ($\teh$), and
particle-particle ($\pp$) channels\cite{DeDominicis_1964b,Bickers_2004,MarieLoos2025}
\begin{equation}
	F
	=
	\Lambda
	+
	\Phi^\eh
	+
	\Phi^{\teh}
	+
	\Phi^\pp
	\label{eq:parquet_decomposition}
\end{equation}
where $\Lambda$ is fully two-particle irreducible.
Each reducible contribution has a preferred bosonic transfer frequency
and a natural propagation channel.
The direct electron-hole vertex $\Phi^\eh$ is associated with neutral
$N$-electron propagation, while $\Phi^\pp$ is associated with the
$(N\pm2)$-electron sectors.
The transverse electron-hole contribution is obtained from $\Phi^\eh$ by
fermionic crossing.
Consequently, only two primitive multiresolvent constructions are needed, $\Phi^\eh$ and $\Phi^\pp$.

In each channel, the channel-reducible four-point vertex $\Phi$ is represented as the product of a left external vertex, a channel-specific central resolvent, and a right external vertex (see below).
The central resolvent carries the natural bosonic transfer of the channel,
while the two external vertices retain the remaining fermionic-frequency
dependence.

This choice also provides a direct route to the four-point Hedin kernel.
The vertex irreducible in the direct electron-hole channel is
\begin{equation}
	\Gamma^\eh
	=
	\Lambda
	+
	\Phi^{\teh}
	+
	\Phi^\pp
	\label{eq:Gamma_eh_parquet}
\end{equation}
At the exact level, this is the same four-point kernel that appears in the
Hedin Bethe--Salpeter equation through the functional derivative
$\Xi=\fdv*{\Sigma}{G}$, once a common index convention is adopted.~\cite{Hedin1965,MarieAmmarLoos2024}
Thus, a multiresolvent construction of $\Phi^\eh$ and $\Phi^\pp$ also
provides a route to a multiresolvent representation of the Hedin kernel.

The purpose of the present notes is to establish this construction through
third order in the interaction.
We first fix the frequency conventions and state explicitly the
multiresolvent representation that we seek to construct.
We then introduce the exact three-frequency Bethe--Salpeter equation and
integrate over only one fermionic frequency to determine the
two-frequency external vertices.
At second order, the transverse electron-hole and particle-particle
contributions to $\Gamma^\eh$ can be transformed into the same direct
$\eh$ convention and partially integrated explicitly.
The resulting external vertices contain only $\hhp$ and $\pph$ poles, while
integrating over the remaining fermionic frequency recovers the known GF2
effective kernel.
At third order, second-order corrections to the external vertices combine
with the first-order correction to the central resolvent to
generate the complete $\eh$- and $\pp$-reducible vertices within the same
architecture.
Crossing and the parquet identities then reconstruct the full vertex and
the direct electron-hole Hedin kernel through the same perturbative order.

Throughout the perturbative discussion, we use a Hartree--Fock reference
and a M\o ller--Plesset partition.
For compactness, all expressions are written for real-valued orbitals.
Left and right vertices are kept distinct whenever this distinction matters,
and all causal prescriptions are denoted by $0^+$ because integer multiples
of the infinitesimal regulator carry no physical significance.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{Frequency conventions and channel organization}
\label{sec:frequencies}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The direct electron-hole convention will be used throughout these notes.
The four external fermionic line energies are chosen as
\begin{equation}
	\nu
	\qquad
	\nu+\omega
	\qquad
	\nu'
	\qquad
	\nu'+\omega
	\label{eq:eh_frequencies}
\end{equation}
The bosonic transfer in the direct electron-hole channel is therefore
\begin{equation}
	\Omega_\eh
	=
	\omega
	\label{eq:Omega_eh}
\end{equation}

The two other parquet channels are associated with different linear
combinations of the same three independent frequencies.
For the transverse electron-hole channel, exchanging the third and fourth
external legs gives
\begin{equation}
	\left(
	\nu_{\teh},
	\nu'_{\teh},
	\Omega_{\teh}
	\right)
	=
	\left(
	\nu,
	\nu+\omega,
	\nu'-\nu
	\right)
	\label{eq:crossed_frequency_map}
\end{equation}
so that the transverse transfer frequency is
\begin{equation}
	\Omega_{\teh}
	=
	\nu'-\nu
	\label{eq:Omega_ehbar}
\end{equation}

The particle-particle convention is related to the direct electron-hole
convention through
\begin{equation}
	\left(
	\nu_\pp,
	\nu'_\pp,
	\Omega_\pp
	\right)
	=
	\left(
	-\omega-\nu',
	-\nu',
	\omega+\nu+\nu'
	\right)
	\label{eq:pp_frequency_map}
\end{equation}
which identifies the pair transfer frequency as
\begin{equation}
	\Omega_\pp
	=
	\omega+\nu+\nu'
	\label{eq:Omega_pp}
\end{equation}

These three bosonic combinations are responsible for the characteristic
frequency structures of the parquet vertex.
At the lowest non-vanishing order, the reducible vertices depend only on
the natural bosonic transfer of their channel,
\begin{equation}
	\begin{array}{rcl}
	\Phi^{\eh,(2)} &\equiv& \Phi^{\eh,(2)}(\omega)
	\\[3pt]
	\Phi^{\teh,(2)} &\equiv& \Phi^{\teh,(2)}(\nu'-\nu)
	\\[3pt]
	\Phi^{\pp,(2)} &\equiv& \Phi^{\pp,(2)}(\nu+\nu'+\omega)
	\end{array}
	\label{eq:Phi_frequency_second_order}
\end{equation}
Beyond second order, however, the primitive reducible vertices are genuine
three-frequency objects.
The construction developed below assigns the natural bosonic transfer to a
central resolvent and the remaining fermionic-frequency dependence to two
external vertices.
Thus, the central resolvent remains a one-frequency object even when the
full reducible vertex depends on all three independent frequencies.

The exact channel-irreducible vertices satisfy
\begin{gather}
	\Gamma^\eh
	=
	\Lambda
	+
	\Phi^{\teh}
	+
	\Phi^\pp
	\label{eq:Gamma_eh}
	\\
	\Gamma^\pp
	=
	\Lambda
	+
	\Phi^\eh
	+
	\Phi^{\teh}
	\label{eq:Gamma_pp}
\end{gather}
For a two-body interaction, the first correction to the fully irreducible
vertex beyond the bare interaction occurs at fourth order.
Consequently, through third order one may use
\begin{equation}
	\Lambda
	=
	\Lambda^{(1)}
	+
	O(v^4)
	\label{eq:Lambda_order}
\end{equation}
At second order this gives
\begin{equation}
	\Gamma^{\eh,(2)}
	=
	\Phi^{\teh,(2)}
	+
	\Phi^{\pp,(2)}
	\label{eq:Gamma_eh_second}
\end{equation}

\section{Target multiresolvent representation}
\label{sec:target_representation}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

Having fixed the channel frequencies, we can now state precisely the
representation that we seek to construct before deriving any of its
perturbative ingredients.

In conventional algebraic-diagrammatic construction of the self-energy,
one starts from its diagonal spectral representation in the exact
$(N\pm1)$-electron eigenstate basis.
The unitary freedom within this intermediate-state space can then be used to
recast the diagonal resolvent into the non-diagonal ADC representation, whose
coupling and secular matrices are determined perturbatively.
For a general four-point vertex, there is no analogous single diagonal
spectral representation because three independent frequencies cannot be
carried by one resolvent.
There is therefore no direct analogue of the usual ADC transformation from
a diagonal three-frequency representation to a non-diagonal one.

Our strategy is instead to exploit the parquet decomposition before
constructing the algebraic representation.
For each primitive channel, we seek a factorization in which the natural
bosonic transfer is carried by a central resolvent and the two remaining
fermionic-frequency dependences are carried by external vertices.
For the direct electron-hole primitive, the target form is
\begin{equation}
	\ii\Phi^\eh(\nu,\nu',\omega)
	=
	U_L^\eh(\nu,\omega)
	R_\eh(\omega)
	U_R^\eh(\nu',\omega)
	\label{eq:target_eh}
\end{equation}
with the neutral central resolvent
\begin{equation}
	R_\eh(\omega)
	=
	\left[
	\omega\eta_\eh-M_\eh
	\right]^{-1}
	\label{eq:target_R_eh}
\end{equation}
The central resolvent depends only on the direct electron-hole transfer
$\omega$, while the left and right external vertices carry the remaining
dependence on $\nu$ and $\nu'$, respectively.
The transverse electron-hole contribution is not constructed independently but
follows from crossing, so its central frequency is $\nu'-\nu$.

For the particle-particle primitive, it is most transparent to use its
natural frequency variables and seek
\begin{equation}
	\ii\Phi_P^\pp(\nu_\pp,\nu'_\pp,\Omega_\pp)
	=
	U_L^\pp(\nu_\pp,\Omega_\pp)
	R_\pp(\Omega_\pp)
	U_R^\pp(\nu'_\pp,\Omega_\pp)
	\label{eq:target_pp}
\end{equation}
with the pair central resolvent
\begin{equation}
	R_\pp(\Omega_\pp)
	=
	\left[
	\Omega_\pp\eta_\pp-M_\pp
	\right]^{-1}
	\label{eq:target_R_pp}
\end{equation}
In the direct electron-hole convention, the corresponding bosonic transfer
is $\Omega_\pp=\nu+\nu'+\omega$.
In Eqs.~\eqref{eq:target_R_eh} and \eqref{eq:target_R_pp}, $\eta_r$ denotes
the metric that distinguishes the forward and backward sectors of the
corresponding two-particle propagation.
The symbols $U_L^r$ and $U_R^r$ denote the left and right external vertices
and should not be confused with the unitary rotation used in the conventional
ADC construction.
The corresponding particle-number sectors are
\begin{gather}
	\Phi^\eh
	\colon
	N\pm1
	\longrightarrow
	N
	\longrightarrow
	N\pm1
	\label{eq:target_sector_eh}
	\\
	\Phi^\pp
	\colon
	N\pm1
	\longrightarrow
	N\pm2
	\longrightarrow
	N\pm1
	\label{eq:target_sector_pp}
\end{gather}
The central propagation therefore distinguishes the two primitive channels,
whereas the external vertices are associated with $N\pm1$ propagation.
At the perturbative orders considered below, their non-trivial poles are the
$\hhp$ and $\pph$ poles of the $(N-1)$- and $(N+1)$-electron sectors.

The objective is to determine the ingredients of
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:target_eh} and \eqref{eq:target_pp} by matching their
perturbative expansions to the exact channel-resolved Bethe--Salpeter equations.
We write
\begin{equation}
	U_{L/R}^r
	=
	U_{L/R}^{r,(1)}
	+
	U_{L/R}^{r,(2)}
	+
	O(V^3)
	\label{eq:target_U_expansion}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
	M_r
	=
	M_r^{(0)}
	+
	M_r^{(1)}
	+
	O(V^2)
	\label{eq:target_M_expansion}
\end{equation}
where $r=\eh$ or $\pp$.
The central resolvent consequently has the perturbative expansion
\begin{equation}
	R_r
	=
	R_{r,0}
	+
	R_{r,0}
	M_r^{(1)}
	R_{r,0}
	+
	O(V^2)
	\label{eq:target_R_expansion}
\end{equation}
The proposed representation must therefore reproduce at second order
\begin{equation}
	\ii\Phi^{r,(2)}
	=
	U_L^{r,(1)}
	R_{r,0}
	U_R^{r,(1)}
	\label{eq:target_second_order}
\end{equation}
and at third order
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\ii\Phi^{r,(3)}
	={}&
	U_L^{r,(2)}
	R_{r,0}
	U_R^{r,(1)}
	\\
	&+
	U_L^{r,(1)}
	R_{r,0}
	U_R^{r,(2)}
	\\
	&+
	U_L^{r,(1)}
	R_{r,0}
	M_r^{(1)}
	R_{r,0}
	U_R^{r,(1)}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:target_third_order}
\end{equation}
Equations~\eqref{eq:target_second_order} and
\eqref{eq:target_third_order} define the constructive problem addressed in
the remainder of these notes.
The first-order external vertices are generated by the bare interaction.
The second-order external vertices and the first-order corrections to the
central matrices must be extracted from the exact perturbative vertex.

Unlike conventional ADC, we therefore do not obtain the desired
multiresolvent form by transforming a pre-existing diagonal
three-frequency representation.
Instead, the representation is constructed directly by perturbative matching
to the channel-resolved Bethe--Salpeter equations.
The second- and third-order derivations below show that this ansatz reproduces
the complete reducible vertices through third order.

\section{Three-frequency Bethe--Salpeter equation}
\label{sec:three_frequency_bse}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The exact electron-hole correlation function obeys a four-point
Bethe--Salpeter equation
\begin{equation}
	L
	=
	L_0
	+
	L_0\Gamma^\eh L
	\label{eq:BSE_symbolic}
\end{equation}
where the products imply integrations over internal space-spin-time
variables.
Before the instantaneous-pair approximation, all quantities depend on
three independent time differences.
In frequency space, the Bethe--Salpeter equation therefore contains two
internal fermionic-frequency integrations
\begin{multline}
	L(\nu,\nu',\omega)
	=
	L_0(\nu,\nu',\omega)
	\\
	+
	\frac{1}{(2\pi)^2}
	\int d\bar\nu d\bar\nu'
	L_0(\nu,\bar\nu,\omega)
	\Gamma^\eh(\bar\nu,\bar\nu',\omega)
	L(\bar\nu',\nu',\omega)
	\label{eq:BSE_three_frequency}
\end{multline}

For a non-interacting reference, the three-frequency electron-hole
propagator is diagonal in the fermionic frequency
\begin{equation}
	L_0(\nu,\nu',\omega)
	=
	2\pi
	\delta(\nu-\nu')
	G_0(\nu+\omega)
	G_0(\nu)
	\label{eq:L0_three_frequency}
\end{equation}
The ordering of the two one-particle Green's functions in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:L0_three_frequency} fixes the frequency convention adopted
below.
It is chosen such that a forward pair $i\to a$ propagates through
$G_i(\nu)$ and $G_a(\nu+\omega)$.

The reference one-particle Green's function is
\begin{equation}
	G_{0,p}(\nu)
	=
	\frac{1}
	{\nu-\eps_p+\ii\eta\operatorname{sgn}(\eps_p-\mu)}
	\label{eq:G0_orbital}
\end{equation}
For occupied and virtual orbitals this gives
\begin{gather}
	G_{0,i}(\nu)
	=
	\frac{1}{\nu-\eps_i-\ii\eta}
	\label{eq:G0_occ}
	\\
	G_{0,a}(\nu)
	=
	\frac{1}{\nu-\eps_a+\ii\eta}
	\label{eq:G0_vir}
\end{gather}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Forward and backward pair propagation}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

For a forward electron-hole pair, the two one-particle propagators are
\begin{equation}
	\ell^+_{ia}(\nu,\omega)
	=
	G_{0,a}(\nu+\omega)
	G_{0,i}(\nu)
	\label{eq:ell_plus}
\end{equation}
Partial fractioning gives
\begin{equation}
	\ell^+_{ia}(\nu,\omega)
	=
	\frac{
	G_{0,i}(\nu)-G_{0,a}(\nu+\omega)
	}
	{
	\omega-(\eps_a-\eps_i)+2\ii\eta
	}
	\label{eq:ell_plus_pf}
\end{equation}
The fully contracted forward propagator is therefore
\begin{equation}
	(L_0^+)_{ia}(\omega)
	=
	\frac{\ii}
	{\omega-(\eps_a-\eps_i)+2\ii\eta}
	\label{eq:L0_plus}
\end{equation}

For the backward pair
\begin{equation}
	\ell^-_{ia}(\nu,\omega)
	=
	G_{0,i}(\nu+\omega)
	G_{0,a}(\nu)
	\label{eq:ell_minus}
\end{equation}
with fully contracted form
\begin{equation}
	(L_0^-)_{ia}(\omega)
	=
	-\frac{\ii}
	{\omega+(\eps_a-\eps_i)-2\ii\eta}
	\label{eq:L0_minus}
\end{equation}

It is convenient to introduce the normalized one-sided factors
\begin{gather}
	Q^+_{ia}(\nu,\omega)
	=
	-\ii
	\left[
	G_{0,i}(\nu)
	-
	G_{0,a}(\nu+\omega)
	\right]
	\label{eq:Q_plus}
	\\
	Q^-_{ia}(\nu,\omega)
	=
	+\ii
	\left[
	G_{0,a}(\nu)
	-
	G_{0,i}(\nu+\omega)
	\right]
	\label{eq:Q_minus}
\end{gather}
With the infinitesimal time shifts implied in the time-ordered
frequency integrations, these satisfy
\begin{equation}
	\frac{1}{2\pi}
	\int d\nu
	Q^\pm_{ia}(\nu,\omega)
	=
	1
	\label{eq:Q_normalization}
\end{equation}
and pair reversal gives
\begin{equation}
	Q^-_{ia}(\nu,\omega)
	=
	Q^+_{ia}(\nu+\omega,-\omega)
	\label{eq:Q_reversal}
\end{equation}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Frequency-dependent external vertices}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The conventional frequency-dependent Bethe--Salpeter kernel is obtained
by integrating over both fermionic frequencies of the full three-frequency
kernel.
To construct the external vertices appearing in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:target_eh}, we instead integrate over only one fermionic
frequency and retain the other one explicitly.
It is convenient to absorb the factor of $\ii$ multiplying the
channel-irreducible vertex and write the right external vertex as
\begin{equation}
	U_R^{\sigma\sigma'}{}_{ia,jb}(\nu',\omega)
	=
	\frac{1}{2\pi}
	\int d\nu
	Q^\sigma_{ia}(\nu,\omega)
	\left[
	\ii\Gamma^\eh
	\right]^{\sigma\sigma'}_{ia,jb}
	(\nu,\nu',\omega)
	\label{eq:U_R_def}
\end{equation}
Similarly, the left external vertex is
\begin{equation}
	U_L^{\sigma\sigma'}{}_{ia,jb}(\nu,\omega)
	=
	\frac{1}{2\pi}
	\int d\nu'
	\left[
	\ii\Gamma^\eh
	\right]^{\sigma\sigma'}_{ia,jb}
	(\nu,\nu',\omega)
	Q^{\sigma'}_{jb}(\nu',\omega)
	\label{eq:U_L_def}
\end{equation}
The sequence of frequency integrations is therefore
\begin{equation}
	\Gamma^\eh(\nu,\nu',\omega)
	\longrightarrow
	U(\nu,\omega)
	\longrightarrow
	\widetilde\Gamma^\eh(\omega)
	\label{eq:contraction_hierarchy}
\end{equation}
The first step retains one fermionic frequency and defines the external
vertices of the present construction.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{Second-order primitive vertices}
\label{sec:second_order_primitives}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

We now construct the second-order electron-hole irreducible vertex from
the two channels that are irreducible with respect to direct electron-hole
propagation.
Antisymmetrized two-electron integrals are denoted as
\begin{equation}
	V_{pqrs}
	=
	\mel{pq}{}{rs}
	\label{eq:V_def}
\end{equation}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Direct electron-hole reducible vertex}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

At first order, the electron-hole irreducible interaction is the bare
antisymmetrized interaction
\begin{equation}
	\ii\Gamma^{\eh,(1)}_{pqrs}
	=
	V_{pqrs}
	\label{eq:Gamma_eh_first}
\end{equation}
The second-order direct electron-hole reducible vertex follows from the
zeroth-order neutral excitations $i\to a$
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\ii\Phi^{\eh,(2)}_{pqrs}(\omega)
	={}&
	\sum_{ia}
	\frac{
	V_{pa\,ri}
	V_{qi\,sa}
	}
	{\omega-(\eps_a-\eps_i)+\ii0^+}
	\\
	&-
	\sum_{ia}
	\frac{
	V_{pi\,ra}
	V_{qa\,si}
	}
	{\omega+(\eps_a-\eps_i)-\ii0^+}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:Phi_eh_second}
\end{equation}
At this order the two end vertices are instantaneous and
$\Phi^{\eh,(2)}$ depends only on the direct electron-hole transfer
frequency $\omega$.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Transverse electron-hole contribution from crossing}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

Fermionic crossing gives
\begin{equation}
	\Phi^{\teh}_{pqrs}(\nu,\nu',\omega)
	=
	-
	\Phi^\eh_{pqsr}
	\left(
	\nu,
	\nu+\omega,
	\nu'-\nu
	\right)
	\label{eq:Phi_ehbar_crossing}
\end{equation}
Using Eq.~\eqref{eq:Phi_eh_second} therefore yields
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\ii\Phi^{\teh,(2)}_{pqrs}(\nu,\nu',\omega)
	={}&
	-
	\sum_{kc}
	\frac{
	V_{pc\,sk}
	V_{qk\,rc}
	}
	{\nu'-\nu-(\eps_c-\eps_k)+\ii0^+}
	\\
	&+
	\sum_{kc}
	\frac{
	V_{pk\,sc}
	V_{qc\,rk}
	}
	{\nu'-\nu+(\eps_c-\eps_k)-\ii0^+}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:Phi_ehbar_second}
\end{equation}
The characteristic transverse electron-hole poles are therefore controlled
by $\nu'-\nu$.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Particle-particle reducible vertex}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The particle-particle channel contains particle-particle and hole-hole
propagation.
Using unrestricted orbital sums, the second-order pair-reducible vertex
is
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\ii\Phi_P^{\pp,(2)}{}_{pqrs}(\Omega)
	={}&
	\frac{1}{2}
	\sum_{cd}
	\frac{
	V_{pq\,cd}
	V_{cd\,rs}
	}
	{\Omega-(\eps_c+\eps_d)+\ii0^+}
	\\
	&-
	\frac{1}{2}
	\sum_{kl}
	\frac{
	V_{pq\,kl}
	V_{kl\,rs}
	}
	{\Omega-(\eps_k+\eps_l)-\ii0^+}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:Phi_pp_second_native}
\end{equation}
The factors $1/2$ remove the double counting associated with unrestricted
antisymmetric pair sums.
They are absent when the sums are restricted to unique pairs.

Transforming Eq.~\eqref{eq:Phi_pp_second_native} to the direct
$\eh$ convention with Eq.~\eqref{eq:pp_frequency_map} gives
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\ii\Phi^{\pp,(2)}_{pqrs}(\nu,\nu',\omega)
	={}&
	\frac{1}{2}
	\sum_{cd}
	\frac{
	V_{pq\,cd}
	V_{cd\,rs}
	}
	{\nu+\nu'+\omega-\eps_c-\eps_d+\ii0^+}
	\\
	&-
	\frac{1}{2}
	\sum_{kl}
	\frac{
	V_{pq\,kl}
	V_{kl\,rs}
	}
	{\nu+\nu'+\omega-\eps_k-\eps_l-\ii0^+}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:Phi_pp_second_eh}
\end{equation}
The characteristic pair poles are therefore controlled by
$\nu+\nu'+\omega$.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Second-order electron-hole irreducible kernel}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

Combining the transverse electron-hole and particle-particle
contributions gives
\begin{equation}
	\ii\Gamma^{\eh,(2)}
	=
	\ii\Phi^{\teh,(2)}
	+
	\ii\Phi^{\pp,(2)}
	\label{eq:Gamma_eh_second_i}
\end{equation}
Explicitly,
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\ii\Gamma^{\eh,(2)}_{pqrs}(\nu,\nu',\omega)
	={}&
	-
	\sum_{kc}
	\frac{
	V_{pc\,sk}
	V_{qk\,rc}
	}
	{\nu'-\nu-(\eps_c-\eps_k)+\ii0^+}
	\\
	&+
	\sum_{kc}
	\frac{
	V_{pk\,sc}
	V_{qc\,rk}
	}
	{\nu'-\nu+(\eps_c-\eps_k)-\ii0^+}
	\\
	&+
	\frac{1}{2}
	\sum_{cd}
	\frac{
	V_{pq\,cd}
	V_{cd\,rs}
	}
	{\nu+\nu'+\omega-\eps_c-\eps_d+\ii0^+}
	\\
	&-
	\frac{1}{2}
	\sum_{kl}
	\frac{
	V_{pq\,kl}
	V_{kl\,rs}
	}
	{\nu+\nu'+\omega-\eps_k-\eps_l-\ii0^+}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:Gamma_eh_second_explicit}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:Gamma_eh_second_explicit} is the starting point for
the one-sided frequency integration.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{External vertices at second order}
\label{sec:half_contraction_second}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The one-sided frequency integration removes one fermionic frequency while retaining
the other and the bosonic transfer frequency.
The resulting poles can be identified before considering the detailed
orbital numerators.
For a forward external pair $i\to a$, the relevant contour integrations
transform the transverse electron-hole poles according to
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	&\frac{1}{2\pi}
	\int d\nu
	Q^+_{ia}(\nu,\omega)
	\frac{1}
	{\nu'-\nu+(\eps_c-\eps_k)-\ii0^+}
	\\
	&\qquad=
	\frac{1}
	{\nu'-(\eps_i+\eps_k-\eps_c)-\ii0^+}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:contour_ehbar_hhp}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	&\frac{1}{2\pi}
	\int d\nu
	Q^+_{ia}(\nu,\omega)
	\frac{1}
	{\nu'-\nu-(\eps_c-\eps_k)+\ii0^+}
	\\
	&\qquad=
	\frac{1}
	{\nu'+\omega-(\eps_a+\eps_c-\eps_k)+\ii0^+}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:contour_ehbar_pph}
\end{equation}
The particle-particle poles give
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	&\frac{1}{2\pi}
	\int d\nu
	Q^+_{ia}(\nu,\omega)
	\frac{1}
	{\nu+\nu'+\omega-\eps_c-\eps_d+\ii0^+}
	\\
	&\qquad=
	\frac{1}
	{\nu'+\omega-(\eps_c+\eps_d-\eps_i)+\ii0^+}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:contour_pp_pph}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	&\frac{1}{2\pi}
	\int d\nu
	Q^+_{ia}(\nu,\omega)
	\frac{1}
	{\nu+\nu'+\omega-\eps_k-\eps_l-\ii0^+}
	\\
	&\qquad=
	\frac{1}
	{\nu'-(\eps_k+\eps_l-\eps_a)-\ii0^+}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:contour_pp_hhp}
\end{equation}

The signs of the corresponding contributions to the external vertices are
therefore inherited from the coefficients of
$\Gamma^{\eh,(2)}$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:Gamma_eh_second_explicit}.
In particular, the negative $\pph$ transverse contribution and the
negative $\hhp$ pair contribution do not originate from the contour
integration itself.

The only energies appearing as poles of the external vertices are therefore
\begin{equation}
	E^-_{ij a}
	=
	\eps_i+\eps_j-\eps_a
	\label{eq:E_minus}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
	E^+_{ab i}
	=
	\eps_a+\eps_b-\eps_i
	\label{eq:E_plus}
\end{equation}
which correspond to $\hhp$ states in the $N-1$ sector and $\pph$ states
in the $N+1$ sector.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Forward-forward block}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The forward-forward block is identified as
\begin{equation}
	\left[
	\ii\Gamma^\eh
	\right]^{++}_{ia,jb}
	=
	\ii\Gamma^\eh_{aj\,ib}
	\label{eq:Gamma_pp_block}
\end{equation}
At first order,
\begin{equation}
	U^{++,(1)}_{ia,jb}
	=
	V_{aj\,ib}
	\label{eq:U_pp_first}
\end{equation}
At second order, inserting Eq.~\eqref{eq:Gamma_eh_second_explicit} in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:U_R_def} gives
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	U^{++,(2)}_{ia,jb}(\nu,\omega)
	={}&
	\sum_{kc}
	\frac{
	V_{jc\,ik}
	V_{ka\,cb}
	}
	{\nu-(\eps_i+\eps_k-\eps_c)-\ii0^+}
	\\
	&-
	\sum_{kc}
	\frac{
	V_{jk\,ic}
	V_{ca\,kb}
	}
	{\nu+\omega-(\eps_a+\eps_c-\eps_k)+\ii0^+}
	\\
	&-
	\frac{1}{2}
	\sum_{kl}
	\frac{
	V_{aj\,kl}
	V_{lk\,bi}
	}
	{\nu-(\eps_k+\eps_l-\eps_a)-\ii0^+}
	\\
	&+
	\frac{1}{2}
	\sum_{cd}
	\frac{
	V_{aj\,cd}
	V_{dc\,bi}
	}
	{\nu+\omega-(\eps_c+\eps_d-\eps_i)+\ii0^+}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:U_pp_second}
\end{equation}
The first two terms originate from the transverse electron-hole channel,
while the last two originate from the hole-hole and particle-particle
branches of $\Phi^\pp$.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Forward-backward block}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The forward-backward block is
\begin{equation}
	\left[
	\ii\Gamma^\eh
	\right]^{+-}_{ia,jb}
	=
	\ii\Gamma^\eh_{ab\,ij}
	\label{eq:Gamma_pm_block}
\end{equation}
At first order,
\begin{equation}
	U^{+-,(1)}_{ia,jb}
	=
	V_{ab\,ij}
	\label{eq:U_pm_first}
\end{equation}
At second order,
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	U^{+-,(2)}_{ia,jb}(\nu,\omega)
	={}&
	\sum_{kc}
	\frac{
	V_{bc\,ik}
	V_{ka\,cj}
	}
	{\nu-(\eps_i+\eps_k-\eps_c)-\ii0^+}
	\\
	&-
	\sum_{kc}
	\frac{
	V_{bk\,ic}
	V_{ca\,kj}
	}
	{\nu+\omega-(\eps_a+\eps_c-\eps_k)+\ii0^+}
	\\
	&-
	\frac{1}{2}
	\sum_{kl}
	\frac{
	V_{ab\,kl}
	V_{lk\,ij}
	}
	{\nu-(\eps_k+\eps_l-\eps_a)-\ii0^+}
	\\
	&+
	\frac{1}{2}
	\sum_{cd}
	\frac{
	V_{ab\,cd}
	V_{dc\,ij}
	}
	{\nu+\omega-(\eps_c+\eps_d-\eps_i)+\ii0^+}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:U_pm_second}
\end{equation}
The same $\hhp$ and $\pph$ pole spaces therefore appear in both upper
pair-orientation blocks.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Backward blocks}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

For real orbitals, pair reversal relates the remaining two blocks to the
upper blocks
\begin{equation}
	U^{--}_{ai,bj}(\nu,\omega)
	=
	\left[
	U^{++}_{ia,jb}(\nu+\omega,-\omega)
	\right]^*
	\label{eq:U_mm_reversal}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
	U^{-+}_{ai,jb}(\nu,\omega)
	=
	\left[
	U^{+-}_{ia,jb}(\nu+\omega,-\omega)
	\right]^*
	\label{eq:U_mp_reversal}
\end{equation}
The fermionic frequency carried by a given particle-number sector is therefore
controlled by the orientation of the external pair
\begin{equation}
	\begin{array}{c|cc}
	& \hhp & \pph
	\\
	\hline
	+ & \nu & \nu+\omega
	\\
	- & \nu+\omega & \nu
	\end{array}
	\label{eq:side_frequency_table}
\end{equation}
A derivation for complex orbitals requires the corresponding explicit
Hermitian and crossing relations and will not be needed below.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{Residue-tensor representation}
\label{sec:residue_tensor}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The second-order external vertices admit a compact spectral representation.
We first use ordered intermediate configurations
\begin{equation}
	I^-
	=
	(\mu\nu;\alpha)
	\in
	\hhp
	\label{eq:I_minus}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
	I^+
	=
	(\alpha\beta;\mu)
	\in
	\pph
	\label{eq:I_plus}
\end{equation}
with zeroth-order energies
\begin{equation}
	E^-_{\mu\nu;\alpha}
	=
	\eps_\mu+\eps_\nu-\eps_\alpha
	\label{eq:E_minus_general}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
	E^+_{\alpha\beta;\mu}
	=
	\eps_\alpha+\eps_\beta-\eps_\mu
	\label{eq:E_plus_general}
\end{equation}
The ordered-tuple convention is used here only to keep the $1/2$ factors
of the pair channel explicit.
A transformation to normalized antisymmetric ADC intermediate states can
be performed afterwards.

For a forward pair, the general second-order structure is
\begin{equation}
	U^{\sigma\sigma',(2)}_{AB}(\nu,\omega)
	=
	\sum_{I^-}
	\frac{
	\mathcal R^{\sigma\sigma',-}_{AB;I^-}
	}
	{\nu-E^-_{I^-}-\ii0^+}
	+
	\sum_{I^+}
	\frac{
	\mathcal R^{\sigma\sigma',+}_{AB;I^+}
	}
	{\nu+\omega-E^+_{I^+}+\ii0^+}
	\label{eq:U_residue_general}
\end{equation}

For the forward-forward block, the $\hhp$ residue is
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\mathcal R^{++,-}_{ia,jb;\mu\nu;\alpha}
	={}&
	\delta_{\mu i}
	V_{j\alpha\,\mu\nu}
	V_{\nu a\,\alpha b}
	\\
	&-
	\frac{1}{2}
	\delta_{\alpha a}
	V_{aj\,\mu\nu}
	V_{\nu\mu\,bi}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:R_pp_minus}
\end{equation}
and the $\pph$ residue is
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\mathcal R^{++,+}_{ia,jb;\alpha\beta;\mu}
	={}&
	-
	\delta_{\alpha a}
	V_{j\mu\,i\beta}
	V_{\beta a\,\mu b}
	\\
	&+
	\frac{1}{2}
	\delta_{\mu i}
	V_{aj\,\alpha\beta}
	V_{\beta\alpha\,bi}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:R_pp_plus}
\end{equation}

For the forward-backward block, the $\hhp$ residue is
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\mathcal R^{+-,-}_{ia,jb;\mu\nu;\alpha}
	={}&
	\delta_{\mu i}
	V_{b\alpha\,\mu\nu}
	V_{\nu a\,\alpha j}
	\\
	&-
	\frac{1}{2}
	\delta_{\alpha a}
	V_{ab\,\mu\nu}
	V_{\nu\mu\,ij}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:R_pm_minus}
\end{equation}
and the $\pph$ residue is
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\mathcal R^{+-,+}_{ia,jb;\alpha\beta;\mu}
	={}&
	-
	\delta_{\alpha a}
	V_{b\mu\,i\beta}
	V_{\beta a\,\mu j}
	\\
	&+
	\frac{1}{2}
	\delta_{\mu i}
	V_{ab\,\alpha\beta}
	V_{\beta\alpha\,ij}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:R_pm_plus}
\end{equation}
The lower-block residues follow from pair reversal.

Equations~\eqref{eq:R_pp_minus}--\eqref{eq:R_pm_plus} reveal an important
feature of the multiresolvent construction.
The pole spaces of the external vertices are universally the $\hhp$ and $\pph$ sectors, but
the residues retain the topology of the parquet channel from which they
originate.
The transverse electron-hole terms factor naturally in the transverse
pairing of the external indices, while the particle-particle terms factor
naturally in the pair channel.
Consequently, a representation of the form
$U=CRD$ in the direct electron-hole pair grouping is not generally the
minimal representation of the exact residue structure.
The safer second-order statement is the matrix-valued spectral form of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:U_residue_general}.

A simple vertex--resolvent--vertex factorization can still be recovered
channel by channel in the native pairing of each contribution or by
introducing a larger auxiliary space containing the required spectator
labels.
Neither construction is required at second order and the residue-tensor
form will be retained below.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{Second frequency integration and the GF2 kernel}
\label{sec:GF2_contraction}
\label{sec:second_contraction}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The remaining fermionic frequency can now be integrated with the
one-sided propagator of the second external pair.
For a forward closure, the elementary $\hhp$ and $\pph$ frequency integrations are
\begin{equation}
	\frac{1}{2\pi}
	\int d\nu
	Q^+_{jb}(\nu,\omega)
	\frac{1}
	{\nu-E^- -\ii0^+}
	=
	-\frac{1}
	{\omega-(\eps_b-E^-)+\ii0^+}
	\label{eq:second_contraction_minus}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
	\frac{1}{2\pi}
	\int d\nu
	Q^+_{jb}(\nu,\omega)
	\frac{1}
	{\nu+\omega-E^+ +\ii0^+}
	=
	\frac{1}
	{\omega-(E^+-\eps_j)+\ii0^+}
	\label{eq:second_contraction_plus}
\end{equation}
Applying these identities to Eq.~\eqref{eq:U_pp_second} gives the
second-order resonant block of the effective kernel
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	(\widetilde\Xi_c^{(2)})^{jb}_{ia}(\omega)
	={}&
	-
	\sum_{kc}
	\frac{
	V_{jc\,ik}
	V_{ka\,cb}
	}
	{\omega-(\eps_b-\eps_i+\eps_c-\eps_k)}
	\\
	&-
	\sum_{kc}
	\frac{
	V_{jk\,ic}
	V_{ca\,kb}
	}
	{\omega-(\eps_a-\eps_j+\eps_c-\eps_k)}
	\\
	&+
	\frac{1}{2}
	\sum_{kl}
	\frac{
	V_{aj\,kl}
	V_{lk\,bi}
	}
	{\omega-(\eps_a+\eps_b-\eps_k-\eps_l)}
	\\
	&+
	\frac{1}{2}
	\sum_{cd}
	\frac{
	V_{aj\,cd}
	V_{dc\,bi}
	}
	{\omega-(\eps_c+\eps_d-\eps_i-\eps_j)}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:GF2_A}
\end{equation}

Closing the forward-backward external vertex with a backward pair gives
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	(\widetilde\Xi_c^{(2)})^{bj}_{ia}
	={}&
	-
	\sum_{kc}
	\frac{
	V_{bc\,ik}
	V_{ka\,cj}
	}
	{-(\eps_b-\eps_i+\eps_c-\eps_k)}
	\\
	&-
	\sum_{kc}
	\frac{
	V_{bk\,ic}
	V_{ca\,kj}
	}
	{-(\eps_a-\eps_j+\eps_c-\eps_k)}
	\\
	&+
	\frac{1}{2}
	\sum_{kl}
	\frac{
	V_{ab\,kl}
	V_{lk\,ij}
	}
	{-(\eps_a+\eps_b-\eps_k-\eps_l)}
	\\
	&+
	\frac{1}{2}
	\sum_{cd}
	\frac{
	V_{ab\,cd}
	V_{dc\,ij}
	}
	{-(\eps_c+\eps_d-\eps_i-\eps_j)}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:GF2_B}
\end{equation}
The external transfer frequency cancels in the coupling block.
This is a consequence of closing the resolvents entering the external vertices with a backward
external pair rather than a new type of intermediate-state propagation.

The complete effective kernel therefore has the familiar Casida-like
structure
\begin{equation}
	\widetilde{\boldsymbol\Xi}^{(2)}(\omega)
	=
	\begin{pmatrix}
	A_c^{(2)}(\omega)
	&
	B_c^{(2)}
	\\
	B_c^{(2)\dagger}
	&
	A_c^{(2)\dagger}(-\omega)
	\end{pmatrix}
	\label{eq:GF2_matrix}
\end{equation}
Equations~\eqref{eq:GF2_A} and \eqref{eq:GF2_B} reproduce the effective
dynamic kernel obtained independently by differentiating the GF2
self-energy and performing the conventional two-frequency integration.
This agreement provides a stringent check of the channel frequency maps,
fermionic signs, and pair-channel factors.

The conventional effective kernel involves an additional approximation at
the level of the exact three-frequency Bethe--Salpeter equation, where the
full correlation function appearing inside the frequency integration is replaced by
$L_0$.
The comparison made here is therefore with this $L_0$-projected dynamic
kernel rather than with the exact uncontracted three-frequency kernel.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{Channel consistency at second order}
\label{sec:channel_consistency}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The second-order construction can be checked in several independent ways.
The first check is crossing.
The complete transverse electron-hole contribution must be obtained from
the direct electron-hole reducible vertex through
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Phi_ehbar_crossing}.
This fixes both the orbital permutation and the frequency combination
$\nu'-\nu$.

The second check uses the particle-particle convention.
The native pair propagator depends only on $\Omega_\pp$, and the exact
frequency transformation of Eq.~\eqref{eq:pp_frequency_map} fixes the
combination $\nu+\nu'+\omega$ in the direct electron-hole convention.
One-sided frequency integration of this object must produce the same $\hhp$ and $\pph$
external-vertex poles obtained from the transverse electron-hole contribution.

The third check is the recovery of the effective GF2 kernel after integrating
over the remaining fermionic frequency, as shown in Sec.~\ref{sec:second_contraction}.
This check is independent of the initial channel-resolved derivation and
is sensitive to the signs, orbital-index permutations, and the $1/2$
factors in the pair channel.

Finally, the full second-order vertex must be independent of the channel
through which it is reconstructed.
Using the parquet relations,
\begin{equation}
	\Gamma^{\eh,(2)}
	+
	\Phi^{\eh,(2)}
	=
	\Phi^{\eh,(2)}
	+
	\Phi^{\teh,(2)}
	+
	\Phi^{\pp,(2)}
	\label{eq:F_second_eh}
\end{equation}
while
\begin{equation}
	\Gamma^{\pp,(2)}
	+
	\Phi^{\pp,(2)}
	=
	\Phi^{\eh,(2)}
	+
	\Phi^{\teh,(2)}
	+
	\Phi^{\pp,(2)}
	\label{eq:F_second_pp}
\end{equation}
Hence
\begin{equation}
	F^{(2)}
	=
	\Gamma^{\eh,(2)}
	+
	\Phi^{\eh,(2)}
	=
	\Gamma^{\pp,(2)}
	+
	\Phi^{\pp,(2)}
	\label{eq:channel_consistency_second}
\end{equation}
The same channel organization reconstructs the direct electron-hole Hedin
kernel through Eq.~\eqref{eq:Gamma_eh}.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{Multiresolvent architecture through third order}
\label{sec:multiresolvent_third}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The second-order analysis establishes the complete analytic structure of
the external vertices.
For a forward external pair,
\begin{equation}
	U^{(2)}_{AB}(\nu,\omega)
	=
	\sum_{I^-}
	\frac{
	\mathcal R^-_{AB;I^-}
	}
	{\nu-E^-_{I^-}-\ii0^+}
	+
	\sum_{I^+}
	\frac{
	\mathcal R^+_{AB;I^+}
	}
	{\nu+\omega-E^+_{I^+}+\ii0^+}
	\label{eq:U_second_summary}
\end{equation}
The $N-1$ propagation entering the external vertices is therefore carried entirely by $\hhp$
states and the $N+1$ propagation entering the external vertices entirely by $\pph$ states.
Pair reversal exchanges which external fermionic energy enters the two
particle-number sectors.

The central direct electron-hole propagation is associated with neutral
$N$-electron excitations.
We introduce
\begin{equation}
	R_\eh(\omega)
	=
	\left[
	\omega\eta_\eh-M_\eh
	\right]^{-1}
	\label{eq:R_eh}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
	\eta_\eh
	=
	\begin{pmatrix}
	1&0
	\\
	0&-1
	\end{pmatrix}
	\label{eq:eta_eh}
\end{equation}
The master direct electron-hole representation is written for the reduced
vertex as
\begin{equation}
	\ii\boldsymbol\Phi^\eh(\nu,\nu',\omega)
	=
	\mathbf U_L^\eh(\nu,\omega)
	R_\eh(\omega)
	\mathbf U_R^\eh(\nu',\omega)
	\label{eq:master_eh}
\end{equation}
The factor of $\ii$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:master_eh} follows from the exact
closed Bethe--Salpeter form
$\Phi^\eh=-(\ii\Gamma^\eh)L(\ii\Gamma^\eh)$ together with
$L=\ii R_\eh$.

At second order,
$\Phi^{\eh,(2)}$ contains the first-order external vertices and the zeroth-order
central propagator.
The second-order external vertices derived above enter the reducible vertex
for the first time at third order.
This is the first perturbative order at which the three-frequency
multiresolvent structure becomes explicit.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{Direct electron-hole vertex through third order}
\label{sec:eh_third}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

It is useful to distinguish two closely related partially integrated objects.
The quantities $U^{++}_{ia,jb}$ and $U^{+-}_{ia,jb}$ derived in
Sec.~\ref{sec:half_contraction_second} are partially integrated pair-space
kernels and were introduced to test the reduction to the one-frequency
GF2 kernel.
The external vertices entering Eq.~\eqref{eq:master_eh} have instead one
general external index pair and one central neutral pair.
Both are obtained from the same three-frequency
$\Gamma^{\eh,(2)}$ and therefore possess the same $\hhp$ and $\pph$
pole spaces, but their external index grouping is different.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Perturbative Bethe--Salpeter expansion}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

Define the reduced quantities
\begin{equation}
	\mathcal G^\eh
	=
	\ii\Gamma^\eh
	\qquad
	\mathcal P^\eh
	=
	\ii\Phi^\eh
	\label{eq:reduced_vertices}
\end{equation}
Before one-sided frequency integration, products of frequency-dependent vertices and pair
propagators are frequency convolutions.
We denote these convolutions by the symbol $\circ$.
The complete third-order direct electron-hole reducible vertex is
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\mathcal P^{\eh,(3)}
	={}&
	\mathcal G^{\eh,(2)}
	\circ L_0
	\circ \mathcal G^{\eh,(1)}
	\\
	&+
	\mathcal G^{\eh,(1)}
	\circ L_0
	\circ \mathcal G^{\eh,(2)}
	\\
	&+
	\mathcal G^{\eh,(1)}
	\circ L_0
	\circ \mathcal G^{\eh,(1)}
	\circ L_0
	\circ \mathcal G^{\eh,(1)}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:Phi_eh_third_convolution}
\end{equation}
No $\Gamma^{\eh,(3)}$ contribution appears in
$\Phi^{\eh,(3)}$ because an electron-hole reducible diagram contains at
least one interaction on each side of the cut.
A third-order irreducible vertex would therefore first enter
$\Phi^\eh$ at fourth order.

The fully irreducible vertex also has no second- or third-order
contribution beyond the bare interaction,
\begin{equation}
	\Lambda
	=
	V
	+
	\mathcal O(V^4)
	\label{eq:Lambda_through_third}
\end{equation}
so that the second-order irreducible kernel entering
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Phi_eh_third_convolution} is exactly the object derived in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Gamma_eh_second_explicit}.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Zeroth-order neutral resolvent and first-order external vertices}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The zeroth-order neutral matrix is
\begin{equation}
	M_\eh^{(0)}
	=
	\begin{pmatrix}
	\Delta\eps&0
	\\
	0&\Delta\eps
	\end{pmatrix}
	\label{eq:M_eh_zero}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
	(\Delta\eps)_{ia,jb}
	=
	(\eps_a-\eps_i)
	\delta_{ij}\delta_{ab}
	\label{eq:Delta_eh}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
	R_{\eh,0}(\omega)
	=
	\left[
	\omega\eta_\eh-M_\eh^{(0)}
	\right]^{-1}
	\label{eq:R_eh_zero}
\end{equation}

For a forward central pair, the first-order left and right external vertices
are
\begin{equation}
	(U_L^{\eh,(1),+})_{pr,ia}
	=
	V_{pa\,ri}
	\label{eq:UL_eh_first_plus}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
	(U_R^{\eh,(1),+})_{ia,sq}
	=
	V_{qi\,sa}
	\label{eq:UR_eh_first_plus}
\end{equation}
For a backward central pair,
\begin{equation}
	(U_L^{\eh,(1),-})_{pr,ai}
	=
	V_{pi\,ra}
	\label{eq:UL_eh_first_minus}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
	(U_R^{\eh,(1),-})_{ai,sq}
	=
	V_{qa\,si}
	\label{eq:UR_eh_first_minus}
\end{equation}
With these definitions,
\begin{equation}
	\ii\Phi^{\eh,(2)}
	=
	U_L^{\eh,(1)}
	R_{\eh,0}
	U_R^{\eh,(1)}
	\label{eq:Phi_eh_second_resolvent}
\end{equation}
which reproduces Eq.~\eqref{eq:Phi_eh_second}.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Second-order corrections to the external vertices}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The left forward external-vertex correction is obtained by integrating over one
fermionic frequency of the second-order irreducible kernel on its central neutral pair,
\begin{equation}
	(U_L^{\eh,(2),+})_{pr,ia}(\nu,\omega)
	=
	\frac{1}{2\pi}
	\int d\bar\nu
	\left[
	\ii\Gamma^{\eh,(2)}_{pa\,ri}
	(\nu,\bar\nu,\omega)
	\right]
	Q^+_{ia}(\bar\nu,\omega)
	\label{eq:UL_eh_second_def}
\end{equation}
The explicit result is
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	(U_L^{\eh,(2),+})_{pr,ia}(\nu,\omega)
	={}&
	\sum_{jb}
	\frac{
	V_{pb\,ij}
	V_{aj\,rb}
	}
	{\nu-(\eps_i+\eps_j-\eps_b)-\ii0^+}
	\\
	&-
	\sum_{jb}
	\frac{
	V_{pj\,ib}
	V_{ab\,rj}
	}
	{\nu+\omega-(\eps_a+\eps_b-\eps_j)+\ii0^+}
	\\
	&-
	\frac{1}{2}
	\sum_{jk}
	\frac{
	V_{pa\,jk}
	V_{jk\,ri}
	}
	{\nu-(\eps_j+\eps_k-\eps_a)-\ii0^+}
	\\
	&+
	\frac{1}{2}
	\sum_{bc}
	\frac{
	V_{pa\,bc}
	V_{bc\,ri}
	}
	{\nu+\omega-(\eps_b+\eps_c-\eps_i)+\ii0^+}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:UL_eh_second_explicit}
\end{equation}
The first two terms originate from the transverse electron-hole channel
and the last two from the hole-hole and particle-particle branches.

The right forward external-vertex correction is
\begin{equation}
	(U_R^{\eh,(2),+})_{ia,sq}(\nu',\omega)
	=
	\frac{1}{2\pi}
	\int d\bar\nu
	Q^+_{ia}(\bar\nu,\omega)
	\left[
	\ii\Gamma^{\eh,(2)}_{sa\,qi}
	(\bar\nu,\nu',\omega)
	\right]
	\label{eq:UR_eh_second_def}
\end{equation}
which gives
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	(U_R^{\eh,(2),+})_{ia,sq}(\nu',\omega)
	={}&
	\sum_{kc}
	\frac{
	V_{sk\,ic}
	V_{ac\,qk}
	}
	{\nu'-(\eps_i+\eps_k-\eps_c)-\ii0^+}
	\\
	&-
	\sum_{kc}
	\frac{
	V_{sc\,ik}
	V_{ak\,qc}
	}
	{\nu'+\omega-(\eps_a+\eps_c-\eps_k)+\ii0^+}
	\\
	&-
	\frac{1}{2}
	\sum_{kl}
	\frac{
	V_{sa\,kl}
	V_{kl\,qi}
	}
	{\nu'-(\eps_k+\eps_l-\eps_a)-\ii0^+}
	\\
	&+
	\frac{1}{2}
	\sum_{cd}
	\frac{
	V_{sa\,cd}
	V_{cd\,qi}
	}
	{\nu'+\omega-(\eps_c+\eps_d-\eps_i)+\ii0^+}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:UR_eh_second_explicit}
\end{equation}
The backward external vertices follow from the same pair-reversal operation as
the pair-space external vertices.
The explicit formulas are not required for the third-order matching.

Equations~\eqref{eq:UL_eh_second_explicit} and
\eqref{eq:UR_eh_second_explicit} demonstrate that the external vertices of the
reducible direct electron-hole object contain the same $\hhp$ and $\pph$
resolvents as the partially integrated Hedin kernel.
The difference lies only in the grouping of the external indices.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{First-order correction to the neutral resolvent}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The first-order neutral interaction matrix is
\begin{equation}
	M_\eh^{(1)}
	=
	\begin{pmatrix}
	A^{(1)}&B^{(1)}
	\\
	B^{(1)\dagger}&A^{(1)\dagger}
	\end{pmatrix}
	\label{eq:M_eh_first}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
	A^{(1)}_{ia,jb}
	=
	V_{aj\,ib}
	\label{eq:A_eh_first}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
	B^{(1)}_{ia,bj}
	=
	V_{ab\,ij}
	\label{eq:B_eh_first}
\end{equation}
The interacting neutral resolvent is
\begin{equation}
	R_\eh(\omega)
	=
	\left[
	\omega\eta_\eh
	-
	M_\eh^{(0)}
	-
	M_\eh^{(1)}
	-
	\cdots
	\right]^{-1}
	\label{eq:R_eh_interacting}
\end{equation}
whose first-order expansion is
\begin{equation}
	R_\eh
	=
	R_{\eh,0}
	+
	R_{\eh,0}
	M_\eh^{(1)}
	R_{\eh,0}
	+
	\mathcal O(V^2)
	\label{eq:R_eh_expansion}
\end{equation}
No additional metric matrix appears between the resolvents and
$M_\eh^{(1)}$ because the metric is already included in the definition
of $R_\eh$.

In the Tamm--Dancoff sector, the corresponding central third-order
contribution is
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\left[
	\ii\Phi_{\rm cent}^{\eh,(3)}
	\right]_{pqrs}
	={}&
	\sum_{ia,jb}
	\frac{
	V_{pa\,ri}
	V_{aj\,ib}
	V_{qj\,sb}
	}
	{
	[\omega-(\eps_a-\eps_i)+\ii0^+]
	[\omega-(\eps_b-\eps_j)+\ii0^+]
	}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:Phi_eh_third_central_tda}
\end{equation}
The full forward-backward expression is generated automatically by the
matrix product
\begin{equation}
	U_L^{\eh,(1)}
	R_{\eh,0}
	M_\eh^{(1)}
	R_{\eh,0}
	U_R^{\eh,(1)}
	\label{eq:Phi_eh_third_central_matrix}
\end{equation}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Complete direct electron-hole result through third order}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

After the one-sided frequency integrations, the frequency convolutions in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Phi_eh_third_convolution} reduce to ordinary matrix products.
The complete third-order result is
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\ii\Phi^{\eh,(3)}
	={}&
	U_L^{\eh,(2)}
	R_{\eh,0}
	U_R^{\eh,(1)}
	\\
	&+
	U_L^{\eh,(1)}
	R_{\eh,0}
	U_R^{\eh,(2)}
	\\
	&+
	U_L^{\eh,(1)}
	R_{\eh,0}
	M_\eh^{(1)}
	R_{\eh,0}
	U_R^{\eh,(1)}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:Phi_eh_third_final}
\end{equation}
Together with Eq.~\eqref{eq:Phi_eh_second_resolvent}, this reproduces the
target representation of Eq.~\eqref{eq:target_eh} through third order.

The three terms in Eq.~\eqref{eq:Phi_eh_third_final} have a transparent
interpretation.
The first term corrects the left external vertex, the second corrects the right
external vertex, and the third corrects the central neutral resolvent.
These exhaust the third-order direct electron-hole reducible diagrams.
Consequently, through third order all contributions are absorbed into
corrections to the two external vertices and the central neutral resolvent.

The explicit products contain genuine multiresolvent structures.
For example, a correction to the left external vertex contains terms of the form
\begin{equation}
	\frac{1}{\nu-E^-_{I^-}-\ii0^+}
	\frac{1}{\omega-\Delta\eps_{ia}+\ii0^+}
	\label{eq:multi_resolvent_left_minus}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
	\frac{1}{\nu+\omega-E^+_{I^+}+\ii0^+}
	\frac{1}{\omega-\Delta\eps_{ia}+\ii0^+}
	\label{eq:multi_resolvent_left_plus}
\end{equation}
while the central correction contains two neutral resolvents separated by
the first-order interaction matrix.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{Particle-particle primitive through third order}
\label{sec:pp_third}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The particle-particle primitive is most transparent in the normalized
antisymmetric pair basis.
Unique particle pairs are denoted by $A=(ab)$ with $a<b$ and unique hole
pairs by $I=(ij)$ with $i<j$.
In this basis the explicit pair double-counting factors are absorbed in
the basis normalization.

The pair metric is
\begin{equation}
	\eta_\pp
	=
	\begin{pmatrix}
	1&0
	\\
	0&-1
	\end{pmatrix}
	\label{eq:eta_pp}
\end{equation}
and the central pair resolvent is
\begin{equation}
	R_\pp(\Omega)
	=
	\left[
	\Omega\eta_\pp-M_\pp
	\right]^{-1}
	\label{eq:R_pp}
\end{equation}
At zeroth order,
\begin{equation}
	M_\pp^{(0)}
	=
	\begin{pmatrix}
	\eps_a+\eps_b&0
	\\
	0&-(\eps_i+\eps_j)
	\end{pmatrix}
	\label{eq:M_pp_zero}
\end{equation}
where the diagonal notation is understood in the corresponding unique
pair spaces.

The first-order pair interaction matrix is
\begin{equation}
	M_\pp^{(1)}
	=
	\begin{pmatrix}
	V_{ab\,cd}&V_{ab\,ij}
	\\
	V_{ij\,cd}&V_{ij\,kl}
	\end{pmatrix}
	\label{eq:M_pp_first}
\end{equation}
The upper-left block acts in the $N+2$ sector, the lower-right block in the
$N-2$ sector, and the off-diagonal blocks couple the two pair sectors.

The particle-particle irreducible vertex obeys the parquet relation
\begin{equation}
	\ii\Gamma^{\pp,(2)}
	=
	\ii\Phi^{\eh,(2)}
	+
	\ii\Phi^{\teh,(2)}
	\label{eq:Gamma_pp_second}
\end{equation}
so the second-order corrections to the pp external vertices are generated by
electron-hole intermediate propagation.
One-sided frequency integration with the one-sided pp pair propagator produces
$N+1$ $\pph$ and $N-1$ $\hhp$ external-vertex poles.
For the two-particle central sector,
\begin{equation}
	U_{L,\ee}^{\pp,(2)}
	=
	\sum_{\ell}
	\sum_{I^+}
	\frac{
	\mathcal S^{+,\ell}_{I^+}
	}
	{z_\ell-E^+_{I^+}+\ii0^+}
	\label{eq:UL_pp_ee_second}
\end{equation}
while for the two-hole central sector,
\begin{equation}
	U_{L,\hh}^{\pp,(2)}
	=
	\sum_{\ell}
	\sum_{I^-}
	\frac{
	\mathcal S^{-,\ell}_{I^-}
	}
	{z_\ell-E^-_{I^-}-\ii0^+}
	\label{eq:UL_pp_hh_second}
\end{equation}
Here $z_\ell$ denotes the one-particle energy carried by either member of
the external pair and $\ell$ labels the two antisymmetrically related
attachments.
The corresponding right external vertices have the same pole spaces.
The detailed residues are not required for the third-order parquet
closure and are therefore left in channel-adapted form.

In the antisymmetric pair basis, the target representation of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:target_pp} gives at second order
\begin{equation}
	\ii\Phi_P^{\pp,(2)}
	=
	U_L^{\pp,(1)}
	R_{\pp,0}
	U_R^{\pp,(1)}
	\label{eq:Phi_pp_second_resolvent}
\end{equation}
At third order,
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\ii\Phi_P^{\pp,(3)}
	={}&
	U_L^{\pp,(2)}
	R_{\pp,0}
	U_R^{\pp,(1)}
	\\
	&+
	U_L^{\pp,(1)}
	R_{\pp,0}
	U_R^{\pp,(2)}
	\\
	&+
	U_L^{\pp,(1)}
	R_{\pp,0}
	M_\pp^{(1)}
	R_{\pp,0}
	U_R^{\pp,(1)}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:Phi_pp_third_final}
\end{equation}
The pure two-particle part of the central correction is the third-order
pair ladder
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\left[
	\ii\Phi_{P,\rm cent}^{\pp,(3)}
	\right]_{pqrs}^{\ee}
	={}&
	\sum_{\substack{a<b\\c<d}}
	\frac{
	V_{pq\,ab}
	V_{ab\,cd}
	V_{cd\,rs}
	}
	{
	[\Omega-(\eps_a+\eps_b)+\ii0^+]
	[\Omega-(\eps_c+\eps_d)+\ii0^+]
	}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:Phi_pp_third_central_ee}
\end{equation}
The remaining $\ee\leftrightarrow\hh$ and $\hh\to\hh$ contributions are
generated by the other blocks of $M_\pp^{(1)}$.

Equation~\eqref{eq:Phi_pp_third_final} exhausts the third-order
particle-particle reducible diagrams for the same reason as in the direct
electron-hole channel.
The corrections to the external vertices contain the second-order pp-irreducible kernel and
the central correction contains one first-order ppRPA interaction.
No additional third-order remainder is required.

When expressed in the direct electron-hole frequency convention, the
central pair transfer is
\begin{equation}
	\Omega_\pp
	=
	\nu+\nu'+\omega
	\label{eq:Omega_pp_repeated}
\end{equation}
which generates the characteristic anti-diagonal pair structure of the
three-frequency vertex.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Second frequency integration and the particle-particle GF2 kernel}
\label{sec:pp_gf2_check}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The particle-particle construction admits an independent check that is
fully analogous to the electron-hole GF2 test of
Sec.~\ref{sec:GF2_contraction}.
Starting from the second-order pp-irreducible kernel in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Gamma_pp_second}, integrating over one fermionic frequency with
the corresponding noninteracting pair propagator produces the two-frequency
external vertex $U^{\pp,(2)}$.
Contracting the remaining relative fermionic frequency must then recover
the one-frequency second-order pp kernel obtained independently from the
GF2 anomalous self-energy.
This comparison provides a stringent test because it probes simultaneously
the $\pph$ and $\hhp$ external-vertex poles, the antisymmetry of the pair space, and
the relative signs of the four orbital attachments.

Consider first an incoming two-particle pair $ab$.
After integrating over one fermionic frequency, the upper external vertex can be written as
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
U_{ab,rs}^{\pp,(2)}(\bar\nu,\Omega)
={}&
\sum_{me}
\frac{
V_{rm\,ae}V_{se\,bm}
}
{\bar\nu-(\eps_a+\eps_e-\eps_m-\Omega/2)+\ii0^+}
\\
&+
\sum_{me}
\frac{
V_{re\,bm}V_{sm\,ae}
}
{\bar\nu-(\eps_m-\eps_a-\eps_e+\Omega/2)-\ii0^+}
\\
&-
\sum_{me}
\frac{
V_{re\,am}V_{sm\,be}
}
{\bar\nu-(\eps_m-\eps_e-\eps_b+\Omega/2)-\ii0^+}
\\
&-
\sum_{me}
\frac{
V_{rm\,be}V_{se\,am}
}
{\bar\nu-(\eps_b+\eps_e-\eps_m-\Omega/2)+\ii0^+}
\end{split}
\label{eq:pp_half_coupling_explicit}
\end{equation}
The two individual fermionic energies of the outgoing pair are
\begin{equation}
 z_1=\frac{\Omega}{2}+\bar\nu
 \qquad
 z_2=\frac{\Omega}{2}-\bar\nu
 \label{eq:pp_individual_energies}
\end{equation}
so the poles of Eq.~\eqref{eq:pp_half_coupling_explicit} occur at $\pph$
energies.
The two terms associated with $z_1$ and $z_2$ correspond to attaching the
particle-hole correction to either member of the antisymmetric pair.
The hole-hole external vertex is obtained in the same manner and contains the
corresponding $\hhp$ energies.

Projecting the remaining pair onto a two-particle state $cd$ yields the
dynamic upper-left block of the effective pp kernel
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\ii\widetilde\Xi_{ab,cd}^{\pp,(2)}(\Omega)
={}&
\ii\sum_{me}
\frac{
V_{cm\,ae}V_{de\,bm}
}
{\Omega-(\eps_a+\eps_c+\eps_e-\eps_m)+\ii0^+}
\\
&-
\ii\sum_{me}
\frac{
V_{ce\,bm}V_{dm\,ae}
}
{\Omega-(\eps_a+\eps_d+\eps_e-\eps_m)+\ii0^+}
\\
&-
\ii\sum_{me}
\frac{
V_{cm\,be}V_{de\,am}
}
{\Omega-(\eps_b+\eps_c+\eps_e-\eps_m)+\ii0^+}
\\
&+
\ii\sum_{me}
\frac{
V_{ce\,am}V_{dm\,be}
}
{\Omega-(\eps_b+\eps_d+\eps_e-\eps_m)+\ii0^+}
\end{split}
\label{eq:pp_GF2_C}
\end{equation}
This is the correlation contribution to the dynamic $C$ block of the GF2
particle-particle BSE matrix.
The four residues appear with the relative pattern $+,-,-,+$, exactly as
required by antisymmetry under interchange of either external pair.

Projecting instead onto a two-hole state $ij$ produces the coupling block
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\ii\widetilde\Xi_{ab,ij}^{\pp,(2)}
={}&
\ii\sum_{me}
\frac{
V_{ie\,bm}V_{jm\,ae}
}
{-(\eps_i-\eps_a+\eps_m-\eps_e)}
\\
&+
\ii\sum_{me}
\frac{
V_{im\,ae}V_{je\,bm}
}
{-(\eps_a-\eps_j+\eps_e-\eps_m)}
\\
&-
\ii\sum_{me}
\frac{
V_{ie\,am}V_{jm\,be}
}
{-(\eps_i-\eps_b+\eps_m-\eps_e)}
\\
&-
\ii\sum_{me}
\frac{
V_{im\,be}V_{je\,am}
}
{-(\eps_b-\eps_j+\eps_e-\eps_m)}
\end{split}
\label{eq:pp_GF2_B}
\end{equation}
The explicit dependence on the bosonic pair frequency cancels in this
block.
The static character of the $\ee$--$\hh$ coupling is therefore not an
additional approximation but follows directly from the analytic structure
of the partially integrated kernel.
This is the precise particle-particle analogue of the static $B$ block in
the electron-hole GF2 kernel.

Starting from the two-hole external vertex and integrating the remaining fermionic frequency with
a two-hole projector gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\ii\widetilde\Xi_{ij,kl}^{\pp,(2)}(-\Omega)
={}&
\ii\sum_{me}
\frac{
V_{ke\,im}V_{lm\,je}
}
{-\Omega-(-\eps_i-\eps_k+\eps_e-\eps_m)-\ii0^+}
\\
&-
\ii\sum_{me}
\frac{
V_{ke\,jm}V_{lm\,ie}
}
{-\Omega-(-\eps_j-\eps_k+\eps_e-\eps_m)-\ii0^+}
\\
&-
\ii\sum_{me}
\frac{
V_{km\,je}V_{le\,im}
}
{-\Omega-(-\eps_i-\eps_l+\eps_e-\eps_m)-\ii0^+}
\\
&+
\ii\sum_{me}
\frac{
V_{km\,ie}V_{le\,jm}
}
{-\Omega-(-\eps_j-\eps_l+\eps_e-\eps_m)-\ii0^+}
\end{split}
\label{eq:pp_GF2_D}
\end{equation}
which is the dynamic $D$ block of the GF2 ppBSE matrix.
Equations~\eqref{eq:pp_GF2_C}, \eqref{eq:pp_GF2_B}, and
\eqref{eq:pp_GF2_D} reproduce term by term the second-order pp kernel
derived independently from the anomalous GF2 self-energy.
The agreement includes the orbital permutations, relative signs, energy
denominators, and the static character of the coupling block.

This comparison also fixes the pair normalization.
In unrestricted orbital sums the pp Bethe--Salpeter equation contains an
explicit factor $1/2$ because the ordered pairs $(p,q)$ and $(q,p)$ are
both present.
In the normalized antisymmetric basis with $p<q$, this double counting is
removed and no additional $1/2$ appears in the multiresolvent expression
\begin{equation}
 \ii\Phi_P^\pp
 =
 U_L^\pp R_\pp U_R^\pp
 \label{eq:pp_master_normalization_check}
\end{equation}
The successful recovery of all three independent pp-GF2 blocks provides
an independent verification of this normalization.

The two primitive channels therefore exhibit a completely parallel
frequency-integration structure
\begin{equation}
\begin{array}{ccc}
\eh & & \pp
\\[3pt]
A(\omega) & \longleftrightarrow & C(\Omega)
\\
B & \longleftrightarrow & B
\\
A^\dagger(-\omega) & \longleftrightarrow & D(-\Omega)
\end{array}
\label{eq:eh_pp_GF2_parallel}
\end{equation}
In both channels the diagonal blocks retain the bosonic frequency while
the off-diagonal coupling block becomes static after the second
frequency integration.
This provides one of the strongest independent validations of the complete
multiresolvent construction.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{Consistency checks through third order}
\label{sec:checks_third}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The construction is deliberately overdetermined.
Several independent checks can therefore be applied before using the
multiresolvent expressions as an ADC starting point.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Second frequency integration and the one-frequency kernel}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

At second order, integrating the remaining fermionic frequency of
$U^{++,(2)}$ and $U^{+-,(2)}$ reproduces the known electron-hole GF2
effective Bethe--Salpeter kernel in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:GF2_A} and
\eqref{eq:GF2_B}.
The analogous frequency integration of the pp external vertices reproduces all
three independent pp-GF2 blocks in
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:pp_GF2_C}--\eqref{eq:pp_GF2_D}.
Together, these two tests fix the frequency assignment, orbital permutations,
relative signs, pair-channel factors, and antisymmetric-pair normalization
independently of the initial parquet derivation.

At third order, integrating over the remaining fermionic frequencies in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Phi_eh_third_final} produces
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\ii\widetilde\Phi^{\eh,(3)}
	={}&
	\widetilde\Gamma^{\eh,(2)}
	R_{\eh,0}
	\Gamma^{\eh,(1)}
	\\
	&+
	\Gamma^{\eh,(1)}
	R_{\eh,0}
	\widetilde\Gamma^{\eh,(2)}
	\\
	&+
	\Gamma^{\eh,(1)}
	R_{\eh,0}
	M_\eh^{(1)}
	R_{\eh,0}
	\Gamma^{\eh,(1)}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:third_one_frequency_check}
\end{equation}
which is precisely the third-order expansion of the one-frequency BSE
with a second-order dynamic kernel.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Crossing}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The transverse electron-hole primitive is never constructed
independently.
At every perturbative order,
\begin{equation}
	\Phi^{\teh,(n)}_{pqrs}(\nu,\nu',\omega)
	=
	-
	\Phi^{\eh,(n)}_{pqsr}
	\left(
	\nu,
	\nu+\omega,
	\nu'-\nu
	\right)
	\label{eq:crossing_order_n}
\end{equation}
This maps each left, right, and central third-order direct electron-hole
contribution into the corresponding transverse topology and preserves the
fermionic crossing symmetry order by order.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Parquet reconstruction}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

Because the fully irreducible vertex has no second- or third-order
correction,
\begin{equation}
	F^{(n)}
	=
	\Phi^{\eh,(n)}
	+
	\Phi^{\teh,(n)}
	+
	\Phi^{\pp,(n)}
	\qquad
	n=2,3
	\label{eq:F_n_parquet}
\end{equation}
The corresponding irreducible kernels are
\begin{equation}
	\Gamma^{\eh,(n)}
	=
	\Phi^{\teh,(n)}
	+
	\Phi^{\pp,(n)}
	\qquad
	n=2,3
	\label{eq:Gamma_eh_n}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
	\Gamma^{\pp,(n)}
	=
	\Phi^{\eh,(n)}
	+
	\Phi^{\teh,(n)}
	\qquad
	n=2,3
	\label{eq:Gamma_pp_n}
\end{equation}
Hence
\begin{equation}
	F^{(n)}
	=
	\Gamma^{\eh,(n)}
	+
	\Phi^{\eh,(n)}
	=
	\Gamma^{\pp,(n)}
	+
	\Phi^{\pp,(n)}
	\qquad
	n=2,3
	\label{eq:channel_consistency_n}
\end{equation}
The direct electron-hole Hedin kernel is therefore reconstructed from the
two primitive multiresolvent objects through
$\Gamma^\eh=\Lambda+\Phi^{\teh}+\Phi^\pp$.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{High-frequency structure}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The second-order corrections to the external vertices decay as the inverse of their fermionic frequency,
\begin{equation}
	U_L^{(2)}
	=
	\mathcal O(\nu^{-1})
	\qquad
	U_R^{(2)}
	=
	\mathcal O({\nu'}^{-1})
	\label{eq:side_high_frequency}
\end{equation}
When both fermionic frequencies are large, only the central
$\omega$-dependent contribution survives at third order.
The resulting hierarchy is therefore consistent with the standard
parquet asymptotic decomposition into a purely bosonic part and left and
right one-fermionic-frequency corrections.

The same argument applies in the pp channel with the central pair
frequency $\Omega_\pp$ held fixed.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Completeness and absence of double counting}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

Every third-order diagram reducible in the direct electron-hole channel
belongs to one of three disjoint classes,
\begin{equation}
	\Gamma^{\eh,(2)}L_0\Gamma^{\eh,(1)}
	\qquad
	\Gamma^{\eh,(1)}L_0\Gamma^{\eh,(2)}
	\qquad
	\Gamma^{\eh,(1)}L_0\Gamma^{\eh,(1)}L_0\Gamma^{\eh,(1)}
	\label{eq:third_classes_eh}
\end{equation}
The first two contain a second-order block irreducible in the direct
electron-hole channel, while the last is generated by repeated direct
electron-hole scattering.
The corresponding statement holds in the particle-particle channel.

The parquet classification assigns each two-particle reducible diagram to
one and only one reducibility channel.
The left external-vertex, right external-vertex, and central-resolvent classes above therefore do not
overlap.
Together with Eq.~\eqref{eq:Lambda_through_third}, this proves
perturbative completeness of the two primitive multiresolvent
constructions through third order.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{Recipe through third order}
\label{sec:recipe}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The complete construction through third order can be organized into a
short sequence.

First, choose a Hartree--Fock reference and fix the three-frequency
conventions.
The natural transfer frequencies are
\begin{equation}
	\Omega_\eh
	=
	\omega
	\qquad
	\Omega_{\teh}
	=
	\nu'-\nu
	\qquad
	\Omega_\pp
	=
	\nu+\nu'+\omega
	\label{eq:recipe_transfers}
\end{equation}

Second, construct the two primitive second-order reducible vertices
$\Phi^{\eh,(2)}$ and $\Phi_P^{\pp,(2)}$ in their natural channels.
Generate $\Phi^{\teh,(2)}$ exclusively by crossing and transform the pair
primitive to the direct electron-hole convention when reconstructing
$\Gamma^{\eh,(2)}$.

Third, integrate over one fermionic frequency of $\Gamma^{\eh,(2)}$ to construct the external vertices.
The only intermediate states entering the external vertices are
\begin{equation}
	N-1
	\quad
	\hhp
	\qquad\qquad
	N+1
	\quad
	\pph
	\label{eq:recipe_side_spaces}
\end{equation}
The resulting residues retain the topology of the transverse
electron-hole and pair channels.

Fourth, construct the central first-order BSE matrices
$M_\eh^{(1)}$ and $M_\pp^{(1)}$ and form the neutral and pair resolvents
\begin{equation}
	R_\eh
	=
	[
	\omega\eta_\eh-M_\eh
	]^{-1}
	\qquad
	R_\pp
	=
	[
	\Omega_\pp\eta_\pp-M_\pp
	]^{-1}
	\label{eq:recipe_central_resolvents}
\end{equation}

Fifth, assemble the direct electron-hole primitive through third order,
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\ii\Phi^{\eh,(2)}
	={}&
	U_L^{\eh,(1)}
	R_{\eh,0}
	U_R^{\eh,(1)}
	\\
	\ii\Phi^{\eh,(3)}
	={}&
	U_L^{\eh,(2)}
	R_{\eh,0}
	U_R^{\eh,(1)}
	+
	U_L^{\eh,(1)}
	R_{\eh,0}
	U_R^{\eh,(2)}
	\\
	&+
	U_L^{\eh,(1)}
	R_{\eh,0}
	M_\eh^{(1)}
	R_{\eh,0}
	U_R^{\eh,(1)}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:recipe_eh_through_third}
\end{equation}
and the particle-particle primitive by the analogous expression
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\ii\Phi_P^{\pp,(2)}
	={}&
	U_L^{\pp,(1)}
	R_{\pp,0}
	U_R^{\pp,(1)}
	\\
	\ii\Phi_P^{\pp,(3)}
	={}&
	U_L^{\pp,(2)}
	R_{\pp,0}
	U_R^{\pp,(1)}
	+
	U_L^{\pp,(1)}
	R_{\pp,0}
	U_R^{\pp,(2)}
	\\
	&+
	U_L^{\pp,(1)}
	R_{\pp,0}
	M_\pp^{(1)}
	R_{\pp,0}
	U_R^{\pp,(1)}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:recipe_pp_through_third}
\end{equation}

Finally, obtain the transverse electron-hole contribution by crossing and
reconstruct
\begin{equation}
	\Gamma^\eh
	=
	\Lambda
	+
	\Phi^{\teh}
	+
	\Phi^\pp
	\label{eq:recipe_Gamma_final}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
	F
	=
	\Lambda
	+
	\Phi^\eh
	+
	\Phi^{\teh}
	+
	\Phi^\pp
	\label{eq:recipe_F_final}
\end{equation}
with $\Lambda=V+\mathcal O(V^4)$.

The resulting multiresolvent picture can be summarized by the
particle-number sectors traversed by the two primitive channels,
\begin{equation}
	\Phi^\eh
	\colon
	N\pm1
	\longrightarrow
	N
	\longrightarrow
	N\pm1
	\label{eq:sector_eh}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
	\Phi^\pp
	\colon
	N\pm1
	\longrightarrow
	N\pm2
	\longrightarrow
	N\pm1
	\label{eq:sector_pp}
\end{equation}
The $N\pm1$ resolvents entering the external vertices are therefore common
to the two constructions, while the central propagation distinguishes the
neutral and pair channels.

Through third order, the construction is perturbatively complete in both
primitive channels and respects crossing and parquet channel consistency.
All contributions are absorbed into corrections to the two external vertices
and the corresponding central resolvent.
This provides a closed stopping point for the present project and a
well-defined starting point for a future algebraic-diagrammatic
implementation.
