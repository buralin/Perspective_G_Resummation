\title{Multiresolvent Representation of the Parquet Vertex: Second-Order Construction and Third-Order Extension}

\author{Pierre-Fran\c{c}ois \surname{Loos}}
	\email{loos@irsamc.ups-tlse.fr}
	\affiliation{\LCPQ}

\begin{abstract}
A general four-point vertex depends on three independent frequencies and cannot, in general, be represented by the single resolvent familiar from linear-response theory or conventional algebraic-diagrammatic construction (ADC).
In these notes, we develop a channel-resolved multiresolvent organization of the parquet vertex using the electron-hole ($\eh$) and particle-particle ($\pp$) reducible vertices as primitive objects, while the transverse electron-hole ($\teh$) contribution follows from crossing symmetry.
At second order, the construction is complete: integrating over one fermionic frequency of the three-frequency HF reference kernel produces external vertices with $(N\pm1)$-electron poles, and a second integration yields the corresponding one-frequency $\eh$ kernel.
The analogous $\pp$ reduction is derived in the same frequency convention, although its detailed identification with the published anomalous-GF2 kernel is kept separate pending a convention-by-convention comparison.
At third order, the familiar external-vertex and central-resolvent terms are recovered in the usual $1h1p/1p1h$ central space.
A direct audit of the unrestricted frequency convolution shows, however, that frequency-dependent second-order vertices also activate same-occupancy internal orbital sectors ($vv$ and $oo$ in the $\eh$ channel, and mixed sectors in the $\pp$ channel) whose isolated free bubbles vanish but whose convolutions with a dynamic vertex do not.
We therefore retain these contributions as explicit third-order remainders rather than claiming completeness of the restricted central-resolvent ansatz.
As a resummed extension, the dynamical screened-interaction contribution of the $GW$ kernel is shown to obey the same one-sided reduction, with explicit particle--hole excitations replaced by RPA neutral modes.
The resulting framework separates the parts that admit ordinary resolvent representations from the additional frequency-dependent sectors that must be included in a complete higher-order construction.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

A general four-point quantity contains substantially more analytic
structure than a one-particle Green's function or a linear-response
propagator.
For a time-independent Hamiltonian, time-translation invariance removes
one overall time variable, leaving three independent time differences or,
equivalently, three independent frequencies.
The full two-particle vertex can therefore be written as
$F(\nu,\nu',\omega)$.

This three-frequency dependence makes a direct extension of conventional
spectral or algebraic-diagrammatic construction (ADC) non-trivial.
A one-particle self-energy can be represented by resolvents in the
$(N\pm1)$-electron sectors, while a polarization propagator is naturally
expressed through a neutral $N$-electron resolvent.
For a four-point vertex, several propagation sectors and several independent
frequencies coexist.
The objective is therefore not to introduce a single formal resolvent
carrying all three frequencies, but to organize the vertex into ordinary
resolvents associated with well-defined intermediate-state spaces.

Parquet theory provides the natural channel decomposition for this purpose.
We denote the electron-hole, transverse electron-hole, and particle-particle
channels by $\eh$, $\teh$, and $\pp$, respectively.
The full vertex is decomposed as\cite{DeDominicis_1964b,Bickers_2004,MarieLoos2025}
\begin{equation}
	F
	=
	\Lambda
	+
	\Phi^\eh
	+
	\Phi^{\teh}
	+
	\Phi^\pp
	\label{eq:parquet_decomposition}
\end{equation}
where $\Lambda$ is fully two-particle irreducible.
The direct $\eh$ reducible vertex $\Phi^\eh$ is associated with neutral
$N$-electron propagation, whereas $\Phi^\pp$ is associated with the
$(N\pm2)$-electron sectors.
The transverse contribution $\Phi^{\teh}$ follows from $\Phi^\eh$ by
fermionic crossing.
Consequently, only two primitive multiresolvent constructions are required:
$\Phi^\eh$ and $\Phi^\pp$.

In each primitive channel, the channel-reducible four-point vertex is
represented as a left external vertex, a channel-specific central resolvent,
and a right external vertex
\begin{equation}
	\text{left external vertex}
	\;\times\;
	\text{central resolvent}
	\;\times\;
	\text{right external vertex}
	\label{eq:architecture}
\end{equation}
The central resolvent carries the natural bosonic transfer of the channel,
while the two external vertices retain the remaining fermionic-frequency
dependence.

This construction also provides a direct route to the four-point Hedin
kernel.
The vertex irreducible in the direct $\eh$ channel is
\begin{equation}
	\Gamma^\eh
	=
	\Lambda
	+
	\Phi^{\teh}
	+
	\Phi^\pp
	\label{eq:Gamma_eh_parquet}
\end{equation}
At the exact level, this is the same four-point kernel that enters Hedin's
Bethe--Salpeter equation (BSE) through the functional derivative
$\Xi=\fdv*{\Sigma}{G}$, once a common index convention is adopted\cite{Hedin1965,MarieAmmarLoos2024}.
Thus, constructing $\Phi^\eh$ and $\Phi^\pp$ also yields a multiresolvent
representation of the direct $\eh$ Hedin kernel.

The presentation is deliberately separated by perturbative order.
We first establish a complete second-order construction: channel frequency
structure, primitive reducible vertices, one-sided frequency integrations,
external-vertex pole spaces, recovery of the second-order Green's-function
(GF2) BSE kernels, and parquet consistency.
A dedicated recipe then summarizes the complete result through second order.
Only after this point do we introduce the third-order extension.
Besides second-order corrections to the two external vertices and the
first-order correction to the central resolvent, the unrestricted frequency
convolution activates additional internal orbital sectors that are absent at
second order; these are retained explicitly as third-order remainders.

Throughout the perturbative discussion, we use a Hartree--Fock (HF) reference
and a M\o ller--Plesset (MP) partition.
For compactness, all expressions are written for real-valued orbitals.
Left and right external vertices are kept distinct whenever this distinction
matters, and all causal prescriptions are denoted by $0^+$ because integer
multiples of the infinitesimal regulator carry no physical significance.

\section{Frequency conventions and channel organization}
\label{sec:frequencies}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The direct $\eh$ convention is used throughout these notes.
The four external fermionic line energies are
\begin{equation}
	\nu
	\qquad
	\nu+\omega
	\qquad
	\nu'
	\qquad
	\nu'+\omega
	\label{eq:eh_frequencies}
\end{equation}
The natural bosonic transfer frequencies of the three parquet channels are
\begin{align}
	\Omega_\eh
	&=
	\omega
	\label{eq:Omega_eh}
	\\
	\Omega_{\teh}
	&=
	\nu'-\nu
	\label{eq:Omega_ehbar}
	\\
	\Omega_\pp
	&=
	\omega+\nu+\nu'
	\label{eq:Omega_pp}
\end{align}

For the transverse $\eh$ channel, exchanging the third and fourth external
legs gives the frequency map
\begin{equation}
	\left(
	\nu_{\teh},
	\nu'_{\teh},
	\Omega_{\teh}
	\right)
	=
	\left(
	\nu,
	\nu+\omega,
	\nu'-\nu
	\right)
	\label{eq:crossed_frequency_map}
\end{equation}
The natural $\pp$ variables are related to the direct $\eh$ convention by
\begin{equation}
	\left(
	\nu_\pp,
	\nu'_\pp,
	\Omega_\pp
	\right)
	=
	\left(
	-\omega-\nu',
	-\nu',
	\omega+\nu+\nu'
	\right)
	\label{eq:pp_frequency_map}
\end{equation}

These three combinations determine the characteristic frequency structure of
the parquet vertex.
At the lowest non-vanishing order, each reducible vertex depends only on the
bosonic transfer natural to its channel
\begin{align}
	\Phi^{\eh,(2)}
	&\equiv
	\Phi^{\eh,(2)}(\omega)
	\\
	\Phi^{\teh,(2)}
	&\equiv
	\Phi^{\teh,(2)}(\nu'-\nu)
	\\
	\Phi^{\pp,(2)}
	&\equiv
	\Phi^{\pp,(2)}(\nu+\nu'+\omega)
	\label{eq:Phi_frequency_second_order}
\end{align}
Beyond second order, the primitive reducible vertices are genuine
three-frequency objects.
The multiresolvent representation introduced below assigns the natural
bosonic transfer to the central resolvent and the two remaining fermionic
frequencies to the external vertices.

The exact channel-irreducible vertices satisfy
\begin{align}
	\Gamma^\eh
	&=
	\Lambda
	+
	\Phi^{\teh}
	+
	\Phi^\pp
	\label{eq:Gamma_eh}
	\\
	\Gamma^\pp
	&=
	\Lambda
	+
	\Phi^\eh
	+
	\Phi^{\teh}
	\label{eq:Gamma_pp}
\end{align}
For a two-body interaction, the first correction to the fully irreducible
vertex beyond the bare interaction occurs at fourth order, so that
\begin{equation}
	\Lambda
	=
	\Lambda^{(1)}
	+
	O(v^4)
	\label{eq:Lambda_order}
\end{equation}
The second-order channel-irreducible vertices are therefore
\begin{align}
	\Gamma^{\eh,(2)}
	&=
	\Phi^{\teh,(2)}
	+
	\Phi^{\pp,(2)}
	\label{eq:Gamma_eh_second}
	\\
	\Gamma^{\pp,(2)}
	&=
	\Phi^{\eh,(2)}
	+
	\Phi^{\teh,(2)}
	\label{eq:Gamma_pp_second}
\end{align}

\section{Target multiresolvent representation}
\label{sec:target_representation}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

Having fixed the channel frequencies, we can state the algebraic form that we
seek before deriving any perturbative ingredient.

In conventional ADC for the self-energy, one starts from a diagonal spectral
representation in the exact $(N\pm1)$-electron eigenstate basis and exploits
the freedom within the intermediate-state space to obtain a non-diagonal ADC
representation.
For a general four-point vertex, no analogous single diagonal representation
exists because three independent frequencies cannot be carried by one
resolvent.
Our construction is therefore channel resolved.

For each primitive reducible vertex, the natural bosonic transfer is carried
by a central resolvent, while the remaining fermionic-frequency dependences
are carried by a left and a right external vertex.
We denote these external vertices by $U^r$ and $V^r$, respectively, and keep
them as independent objects unless a symmetry relation is explicitly used.
The target forms are
\begin{align}
	\ii\Phi^\eh(\nu,\nu',\omega)
	&=
	U^\eh(\nu,\omega)
	R^\eh(\omega)
	V^\eh(\nu',\omega)
	\label{eq:target_eh}
	\\
	\ii\Phi_P^\pp(\nu_\pp,\nu'_\pp,\Omega_\pp)
	&=
	U^\pp(\nu_\pp,\Omega_\pp)
	R^\pp(\Omega_\pp)
	V^\pp(\nu'_\pp,\Omega_\pp)
	\label{eq:target_pp}
\end{align}
with
\begin{align}
	R^\eh(\omega)
	&=
	\left[
	\omega\eta^\eh-M^\eh
	\right]^{-1}
	\label{eq:target_R_eh}
	\\
	R^\pp(\Omega_\pp)
	&=
	\left[
	\Omega_\pp\eta^\pp-M^\pp
	\right]^{-1}
	\label{eq:target_R_pp}
\end{align}
Here $\eta^r$ is the channel metric, with eigenvalues $+1$ and $-1$ for
the two propagation sectors.
Antisymmetrized two-electron integrals are written directly as
$\mel{pq}{}{rs}$ throughout.
The lowercase symbol $v$ is used only to denote the two-electron interaction
when specifying perturbative orders and must not be confused with the right
external vertex $V^r$.

The corresponding particle-number structures are
\begin{align}
	\Phi^\eh
	&:\quad
	N\pm1
	\longrightarrow
	N
	\longrightarrow
	N\pm1
	\label{eq:target_sector_eh}
	\\
	\Phi^\pp
	&:\quad
	N\pm1
	\longrightarrow
	N\pm2
	\longrightarrow
	N\pm1
	\label{eq:target_sector_pp}
\end{align}
Thus, the central resolvent distinguishes neutral and pair propagation,
whereas the external vertices are associated with $(N\pm1)$-electron
propagation.
At second order, their non-trivial poles are the $\hhp$ and $\pph$ poles of
the $(N-1)$- and $(N+1)$-electron sectors.

The zeroth-order central resolvent is
\begin{equation}
	R^{r,(0)}(\Omega_r)
	=
	\left[
	\Omega_r\eta^r-M^{r,(0)}
	\right]^{-1}
	\label{eq:target_R_zero}
\end{equation}
and the second-order target is
\begin{equation}
	\ii\Phi^{r,(2)}
	=
	U^{r,(1)}
	R^{r,(0)}
	V^{r,(1)}
	\label{eq:target_second_order}
\end{equation}
The first part of these notes establishes this representation completely
through second order.
The third-order extension is introduced only after the second-order recipe in
Sec.~\ref{sec:recipe_second}.

\section{Three-frequency BSE and one-sided frequency reduction}
\label{sec:three_frequency_bse}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

At the exact level, the electron-hole (eh) correlation function obeys the
four-point Bethe--Salpeter equation (BSE)
\begin{equation}
	L
	=
	L_{\rm d}
	+
	L_{\rm d}\Gamma^\eh L
	\label{eq:BSE_symbolic}
\end{equation}
where $L_{\rm d}=GG$ denotes the disconnected pair propagator constructed
from dressed one-particle Green's functions and products imply integrations
over internal space-spin-time variables.
In the direct eh convention of Sec.~\ref{sec:frequencies},
\begin{multline}
	L(\nu,\nu',\omega)
	=
	L_{\rm d}(\nu,\nu',\omega)
	\\
	+
	\frac{1}{(2\pi)^2}
	\int d\bar\nu\,d\bar\nu'\,
	L_{\rm d}(\nu,\bar\nu,\omega)
	\Gamma^\eh(\bar\nu,\bar\nu',\omega)
	L(\bar\nu',\nu',\omega)
	\label{eq:BSE_three_frequency}
\end{multline}
Barred frequencies denote integration variables throughout these notes.

The perturbative construction below uses a HF/MP reference and replaces
$L_{\rm d}$ by the zeroth-order pair propagator $L_0=G_0G_0$ inside the
order-by-order vertex expansion.
Since $G-G_0=\mathcal O(v^2)$ for this partition, replacing a dressed line by
its correlation correction inside the second-order reducible structure first
changes the reducible vertex at fourth order.
This statement concerns the perturbative vertex construction; it should not
be read as a claim that physical excitation energies obtained with $G_0G_0$
are exact through third order.

For occupied and virtual HF orbitals,
\begin{align}
	G_{0,i}(\nu)
	&=
	\frac{1}{\nu-\eps_i-\ii\eta}
	&
	G_{0,a}(\nu)
	&=
	\frac{1}{\nu-\eps_a+\ii\eta}
	\label{eq:G0}
\end{align}
We use $+$ for forward propagation $i\to a$ and $-$ for backward propagation
$a\to i$, while keeping the orbital pair label $ia$ in both cases.
The corresponding three-frequency noninteracting pair propagators are
\begin{align}
	L_{0,ia}^{+}(\nu,\nu',\omega)
	&=
	2\pi\delta(\nu-\nu')
	G_{0,a}(\nu+\omega)G_{0,i}(\nu)
	\label{eq:L0_three_frequency_plus}
	\\
	L_{0,ia}^{-}(\nu,\nu',\omega)
	&=
	2\pi\delta(\nu-\nu')
	G_{0,i}(\nu+\omega)G_{0,a}(\nu)
	\label{eq:L0_three_frequency_minus}
\end{align}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Exact factorization of the noninteracting pair propagator}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The key observation is that each three-frequency pair propagator factorizes
exactly into a normalized two-frequency factor and the usual one-frequency
pair propagator.
Define
\begin{equation}
	\begin{aligned}
	(L_0^+)_{ia}(\omega)
	&=
	\frac{\ii}
	{\omega-(\eps_a-\eps_i)+2\ii\eta}
	\\
	(L_0^-)_{ia}(\omega)
	&=
	-\frac{\ii}
	{\omega+(\eps_a-\eps_i)-2\ii\eta}
	\end{aligned}
	\label{eq:L0_pm}
\end{equation}
and the corresponding one-sided factors
\begin{equation}
	\begin{aligned}
	Q^+_{ia}(\nu,\omega)
	&=
	-\ii
	\left[
	G_{0,i}(\nu)-G_{0,a}(\nu+\omega)
	\right]
	\\
	Q^-_{ia}(\nu,\omega)
	&=
	+\ii
	\left[
	G_{0,a}(\nu)-G_{0,i}(\nu+\omega)
	\right]
	\end{aligned}
	\label{eq:Q_pm}
\end{equation}
Partial fractioning gives the exact identities
\begin{align}
	L_{0,ia}^{+}(\nu,\nu',\omega)
	&=
	2\pi\delta(\nu-\nu')
	Q^+_{ia}(\nu,\omega)
	(L_0^+)_{ia}(\omega)
	\label{eq:L0_factorization_plus}
	\\
	L_{0,ia}^{-}(\nu,\nu',\omega)
	&=
	2\pi\delta(\nu-\nu')
	Q^-_{ia}(\nu,\omega)
	(L_0^-)_{ia}(\omega)
	\label{eq:L0_factorization_minus}
\end{align}
No approximation is involved in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:L0_factorization_plus} and
\eqref{eq:L0_factorization_minus}.
The explicit factors $2\eta$ are retained here because they are required for
the identities to hold exactly at finite $\eta$.
In the remainder of the notes, integer multiples of $\eta$ are denoted by the
generic causal prescription $0^+$.

The one-sided factors satisfy
\begin{align}
	\frac{1}{2\pi}
	\int d\bar\nu\,
	Q^+_{ia}(\bar\nu,\omega)
	&=1
	&
	\frac{1}{2\pi}
	\int d\bar\nu\,
	Q^-_{ia}(\bar\nu,\omega)
	&=1
	\label{eq:Q_normalization}
\end{align}
and pair reversal gives
\begin{equation}
	Q^-_{ia}(\nu,\omega)
	=
	Q^+_{ia}(\nu+\omega,-\omega)
	\label{eq:Q_reversal}
\end{equation}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{One-sided frequency integration}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

We now use the exact factorization above to remove only one fermionic
frequency from a BSE kernel insertion.
Let $\sigma,\sigma'=\pm$ denote the propagation directions of the left pair
$ia$ and right pair $jb$, respectively. Thus $++$ denotes forward propagation
on both pairs, whereas $+-$ denotes forward propagation on $ia$ and backward
propagation on $jb$.
In this pair-space discussion we suppress the channel superscript $\eh$ on
$U$ and $V$.
To match the convention $\ii\Phi^r=U^rR^rV^r$, the projected external
vertices are defined from $\ii\Gamma^\eh$.

Projecting the left pair gives
\begin{align}
	&\frac{1}{(2\pi)^2}
	\int d\bar\nu_1\,d\bar\nu_2\,
	L_{0,ia}^{\sigma}(\bar\nu_1,\bar\nu_2,\omega)
	\left[
	\ii\Gamma^\eh
	\right]^{\sigma\sigma'}_{ia,jb}(\bar\nu_2,\nu',\omega)
	\nonumber
	\\
	&\qquad=
	(L_0^\sigma)_{ia}(\omega)
	\frac{1}{2\pi}
	\int d\bar\nu\,
	Q^\sigma_{ia}(\bar\nu,\omega)
	\left[
	\ii\Gamma^\eh
	\right]^{\sigma\sigma'}_{ia,jb}(\bar\nu,\nu',\omega)
	\label{eq:BSE_right_factorization}
\end{align}
which defines the right external vertex
\begin{equation}
	V^{\sigma\sigma'}_{ia,jb}(\nu',\omega)
	=
	\frac{1}{2\pi}
	\int d\bar\nu\,
	Q^\sigma_{ia}(\bar\nu,\omega)
	\left[
	\ii\Gamma^\eh
	\right]^{\sigma\sigma'}_{ia,jb}(\bar\nu,\nu',\omega)
	\label{eq:V_def}
\end{equation}
so that Eq.~\eqref{eq:BSE_right_factorization} becomes
\begin{equation}
	(L_0^\sigma)_{ia}(\omega)
	V^{\sigma\sigma'}_{ia,jb}(\nu',\omega)
	\label{eq:BSE_right_compact}
\end{equation}

Projecting the right pair gives analogously
\begin{align}
	&\frac{1}{(2\pi)^2}
	\int d\bar\nu'_1\,d\bar\nu'_2\,
	\left[
	\ii\Gamma^\eh
	\right]^{\sigma\sigma'}_{ia,jb}(\nu,\bar\nu'_1,\omega)
	L_{0,jb}^{\sigma'}(\bar\nu'_1,\bar\nu'_2,\omega)
	\nonumber
	\\
	&\qquad=
	\frac{1}{2\pi}
	\int d\bar\nu'\,
	\left[
	\ii\Gamma^\eh
	\right]^{\sigma\sigma'}_{ia,jb}(\nu,\bar\nu',\omega)
	Q^{\sigma'}_{jb}(\bar\nu',\omega)
	(L_0^{\sigma'})_{jb}(\omega)
	\label{eq:BSE_left_factorization}
\end{align}
which defines the left external vertex
\begin{equation}
	U^{\sigma\sigma'}_{ia,jb}(\nu,\omega)
	=
	\frac{1}{2\pi}
	\int d\bar\nu'\,
	\left[
	\ii\Gamma^\eh
	\right]^{\sigma\sigma'}_{ia,jb}(\nu,\bar\nu',\omega)
	Q^{\sigma'}_{jb}(\bar\nu',\omega)
	\label{eq:U_def}
\end{equation}
and therefore
\begin{equation}
	U^{\sigma\sigma'}_{ia,jb}(\nu,\omega)
	(L_0^{\sigma'})_{jb}(\omega)
	\label{eq:BSE_left_compact}
\end{equation}

Thus $U$ retains the left fermionic frequency $\nu$, whereas $V$ retains the
right fermionic frequency $\nu'$.
Equations~\eqref{eq:U_def} and \eqref{eq:V_def} are definitions of exact
one-sided projections of the three-frequency irreducible vertex, not
additional approximations.
Perturbative approximations enter only when these objects and the central
resolvent are truncated in powers of the interaction.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{From three to two to one frequency}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

A second one-sided integration removes the remaining fermionic frequency.
We define the one-frequency projected eh kernel by
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\left[
	\ii\widetilde{\Gamma}^\eh
	\right]^{\sigma\sigma'}_{ia,jb}(\omega)
	={}&
	\frac{1}{(2\pi)^2}
	\int d\bar\nu\,d\bar\nu'\,
	Q^\sigma_{ia}(\bar\nu,\omega)
	\\
	&\times
	\left[
	\ii\Gamma^\eh
	\right]^{\sigma\sigma'}_{ia,jb}(\bar\nu,\bar\nu',\omega)
	Q^{\sigma'}_{jb}(\bar\nu',\omega)
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:Gamma_tilde_def}
\end{equation}
Using Eqs.~\eqref{eq:U_def} and \eqref{eq:V_def}, the same object is obtained
in either order
\begin{align}
	\left[
	\ii\widetilde{\Gamma}^\eh
	\right]^{\sigma\sigma'}_{ia,jb}(\omega)
	&=
	\frac{1}{2\pi}
	\int d\bar\nu'\,
	V^{\sigma\sigma'}_{ia,jb}(\bar\nu',\omega)
	Q^{\sigma'}_{jb}(\bar\nu',\omega)
	\label{eq:Gamma_tilde_from_V}
	\\
	&=
	\frac{1}{2\pi}
	\int d\bar\nu\,
	Q^\sigma_{ia}(\bar\nu,\omega)
	U^{\sigma\sigma'}_{ia,jb}(\bar\nu,\omega)
	\label{eq:Gamma_tilde_from_U}
\end{align}
The frequency reduction is therefore
\begin{equation}
	\Gamma^\eh(\nu,\nu',\omega)
	\longrightarrow
	\left\{
	U(\nu,\omega),V(\nu',\omega)
	\right\}
	\longrightarrow
	\widetilde{\Gamma}^\eh(\omega)
	\label{eq:frequency_reduction}
\end{equation}
The first arrow denotes one-sided frequency integration and the second the
integration over the remaining fermionic frequency.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

\section{Second-order primitive vertices}
\label{sec:second_order_primitives}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

We now construct the second-order $\eh$ irreducible vertex from
the two channels that are irreducible with respect to direct $\eh$
propagation.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Direct eh reducible vertex}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

At first order, the $\eh$ irreducible interaction is the bare
antisymmetrized interaction
\begin{equation}
	\ii\Gamma^{\eh,(1)}_{pqrs}
	=
	\mel{pq}{}{rs}
	\label{eq:Gamma_eh_first}
\end{equation}
The second-order direct $\eh$ reducible vertex follows from the
zeroth-order neutral excitations $i\to a$
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\ii\Phi^{\eh,(2)}_{pqrs}(\omega)
	={}&
	\sum_{ia}
	\frac{
	\mel{pa}{}{ri}
	\mel{qi}{}{sa}
	}
	{\omega-(\eps_a-\eps_i)+\ii0^+}
	\\
	&-
	\sum_{ia}
	\frac{
	\mel{pi}{}{ra}
	\mel{qa}{}{si}
	}
	{\omega+(\eps_a-\eps_i)-\ii0^+}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:Phi_eh_second}
\end{equation}
At this order the two end vertices are instantaneous and
$\Phi^{\eh,(2)}$ depends only on the direct $\eh$ transfer
frequency $\omega$.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Transverse eh contribution from crossing}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

Fermionic crossing gives
\begin{equation}
	\Phi^{\teh}_{pqrs}(\nu,\nu',\omega)
	=
	-
	\Phi^\eh_{pqsr}
	\left(
	\nu,
	\nu+\omega,
	\nu'-\nu
	\right)
	\label{eq:Phi_ehbar_crossing}
\end{equation}
Using Eq.~\eqref{eq:Phi_eh_second} therefore yields
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\ii\Phi^{\teh,(2)}_{pqrs}(\nu,\nu',\omega)
	={}&
	-
	\sum_{kc}
	\frac{
	\mel{pc}{}{sk}
	\mel{qk}{}{rc}
	}
	{\nu'-\nu-(\eps_c-\eps_k)+\ii0^+}
	\\
	&+
	\sum_{kc}
	\frac{
	\mel{pk}{}{sc}
	\mel{qc}{}{rk}
	}
	{\nu'-\nu+(\eps_c-\eps_k)-\ii0^+}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:Phi_ehbar_second}
\end{equation}
The characteristic transverse $\eh$ poles are therefore controlled
by $\nu'-\nu$.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{pp reducible vertex}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The $\pp$ channel contains $\pp$ and $\hh$
propagation.
Using unrestricted orbital sums, the second-order pair-reducible vertex
is
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\ii\Phi_P^{\pp,(2)}{}_{pqrs}(\Omega)
	={}&
	\frac{1}{2}
	\sum_{cd}
	\frac{
	\mel{pq}{}{cd}
	\mel{cd}{}{rs}
	}
	{\Omega-(\eps_c+\eps_d)+\ii0^+}
	\\
	&-
	\frac{1}{2}
	\sum_{kl}
	\frac{
	\mel{pq}{}{kl}
	\mel{kl}{}{rs}
	}
	{\Omega-(\eps_k+\eps_l)-\ii0^+}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:Phi_pp_second_native}
\end{equation}
The factors $1/2$ remove the double counting associated with unrestricted
antisymmetric pair sums.
They are absent when the sums are restricted to unique pairs.

Transforming Eq.~\eqref{eq:Phi_pp_second_native} to the direct
$\eh$ convention with Eq.~\eqref{eq:pp_frequency_map} gives
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\ii\Phi^{\pp,(2)}_{pqrs}(\nu,\nu',\omega)
	={}&
	\frac{1}{2}
	\sum_{cd}
	\frac{
	\mel{pq}{}{cd}
	\mel{cd}{}{rs}
	}
	{\nu+\nu'+\omega-\eps_c-\eps_d+\ii0^+}
	\\
	&-
	\frac{1}{2}
	\sum_{kl}
	\frac{
	\mel{pq}{}{kl}
	\mel{kl}{}{rs}
	}
	{\nu+\nu'+\omega-\eps_k-\eps_l-\ii0^+}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:Phi_pp_second_eh}
\end{equation}
The characteristic pair poles are therefore controlled by
$\nu+\nu'+\omega$.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Second-order eh irreducible kernel}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

Combining the transverse $\eh$ and $\pp$
contributions gives
\begin{equation}
	\ii\Gamma^{\eh,(2)}
	=
	\ii\Phi^{\teh,(2)}
	+
	\ii\Phi^{\pp,(2)}
	\label{eq:Gamma_eh_second_i}
\end{equation}
Explicitly,
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\ii\Gamma^{\eh,(2)}_{pqrs}(\nu,\nu',\omega)
	={}&
	-
	\sum_{kc}
	\frac{
	\mel{pc}{}{sk}
	\mel{qk}{}{rc}
	}
	{\nu'-\nu-(\eps_c-\eps_k)+\ii0^+}
	\\
	&+
	\sum_{kc}
	\frac{
	\mel{pk}{}{sc}
	\mel{qc}{}{rk}
	}
	{\nu'-\nu+(\eps_c-\eps_k)-\ii0^+}
	\\
	&+
	\frac{1}{2}
	\sum_{cd}
	\frac{
	\mel{pq}{}{cd}
	\mel{cd}{}{rs}
	}
	{\nu+\nu'+\omega-\eps_c-\eps_d+\ii0^+}
	\\
	&-
	\frac{1}{2}
	\sum_{kl}
	\frac{
	\mel{pq}{}{kl}
	\mel{kl}{}{rs}
	}
	{\nu+\nu'+\omega-\eps_k-\eps_l-\ii0^+}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:Gamma_eh_second_explicit}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:Gamma_eh_second_explicit} is the starting point for
the one-sided frequency integration.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{Multiresolvent representation at second order}
\label{sec:second_order_multiresolvent}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The primitive second-order vertices can now be written in the target form of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:target_second_order}.
At this order, the external vertices are the bare interaction and the central
resolvent is noninteracting.

For the $\eh$ channel,
\begin{align}
	\eta^\eh
	&=
	\begin{pmatrix}
	1&0
	\\
	0&-1
	\end{pmatrix}
	&
	M^{\eh,(0)}
	&=
	\begin{pmatrix}
	\Delta\eps&0
	\\
	0&\Delta\eps
	\end{pmatrix}
	\label{eq:eta_M_eh_zero}
\end{align}
with
\begin{equation}
	(\Delta\eps)_{ia,jb}
	=
	(\eps_a-\eps_i)\delta_{ij}\delta_{ab}
	\label{eq:Delta_eh}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
	R^{\eh,(0)}(\omega)
	=
	\left[
	\omega\eta^\eh-M^{\eh,(0)}
	\right]^{-1}
	\label{eq:R_eh_zero}
\end{equation}
The two blocks of the central resolvent correspond to forward ($+$) and
backward ($-$) eh propagation.
The corresponding first-order external-vertex components are
\begin{align}
	U^{\eh,+,(1)}_{pr,ia}
	&=
	\mel{pa}{}{ri}
	&
	V^{\eh,+,(1)}_{ia,sq}
	&=
	\mel{qi}{}{sa}
	\label{eq:UV_eh_first_plus}
	\\
	U^{\eh,-,(1)}_{pr,ia}
	&=
	\mel{pi}{}{ra}
	&
	V^{\eh,-,(1)}_{ia,sq}
	&=
	\mel{qa}{}{si}
	\label{eq:UV_eh_first_minus}
\end{align}
These are the two block components of $U^{\eh,(1)}$ and $V^{\eh,(1)}$, so that
\begin{equation}
	\ii\Phi^{\eh,(2)}
	=
	U^{\eh,(1)}R^{\eh,(0)}V^{\eh,(1)}
	\label{eq:Phi_eh_second_resolvent}
\end{equation}
which is identical to Eq.~\eqref{eq:Phi_eh_second}.

For the $\pp$ channel, we use a normalized antisymmetric pair basis with
unique particle pairs $a<b$ and hole pairs $i<j$.
The metric and zeroth-order pair matrix are
\begin{align}
	\eta^\pp
	&=
	\begin{pmatrix}
	1&0
	\\
	0&-1
	\end{pmatrix}
	&
	M^{\pp,(0)}
	&=
	\begin{pmatrix}
	\eps_a+\eps_b&0
	\\
	0&-(\eps_i+\eps_j)
	\end{pmatrix}
	\label{eq:eta_M_pp_zero}
\end{align}
with
\begin{equation}
	R^{\pp,(0)}(\Omega_\pp)
	=
	\left[
	\Omega_\pp\eta^\pp-M^{\pp,(0)}
	\right]^{-1}
	\label{eq:R_pp_zero}
\end{equation}
The first-order external vertices are
\begin{align}
	U^{\pp,(1)}_{pq,ab}
	&=
	\mel{pq}{}{ab}
	&
	V^{\pp,(1)}_{ab,rs}
	&=
	\mel{ab}{}{rs}
	\label{eq:UV_pp_first_ee}
	\\
	U^{\pp,(1)}_{pq,ij}
	&=
	\mel{pq}{}{ij}
	&
	V^{\pp,(1)}_{ij,rs}
	&=
	\mel{ij}{}{rs}
	\label{eq:UV_pp_first_hh}
\end{align}
Therefore
\begin{equation}
	\ii\Phi_P^{\pp,(2)}
	=
	U^{\pp,(1)}R^{\pp,(0)}V^{\pp,(1)}
	\label{eq:Phi_pp_second_resolvent}
\end{equation}
which reproduces Eq.~\eqref{eq:Phi_pp_second_native}.
The opposite signs of the $\pp$ and $\hh$ denominators are encoded by the
$\pm1$ eigenvalues of $\eta^\pp$.

Equations~\eqref{eq:Phi_eh_second_resolvent} and
\eqref{eq:Phi_pp_second_resolvent} establish the primitive multiresolvent
representation completely through second order.

\section{Pair-space external vertices at second order}
\label{sec:half_contraction_second}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

We now evaluate the one-sided projections of Sec.~\ref{sec:three_frequency_bse}
for the second-order direct-$\eh$ irreducible vertex.
It is sufficient to display the right external vertex $V$; the left vertex
$U$ follows from Eq.~\eqref{eq:U_def} by the analogous integration on the
opposite side.

The only external-vertex pole energies are
\begin{align}
	E^-_{ij;a}
	&=
	\eps_i+\eps_j-\eps_a
	&
	E^+_{ab;i}
	&=
	\eps_a+\eps_b-\eps_i
	\label{eq:E_pm}
\end{align}
corresponding to $\hhp$ states in the $(N-1)$ sector and $\pph$ states in
the $(N+1)$ sector.
For a forward ($+$) pair $ia$, the four elementary contour integrations are
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	&\frac{1}{2\pi}
	\int d\bar\nu\,
	Q^+_{ia}(\bar\nu,\omega)
	\frac{1}{\nu'-\bar\nu+(\eps_c-\eps_k)-\ii0^+}
	\\
	&\qquad=
	\frac{1}{\nu'-E^-_{ik;c}-\ii0^+}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:contour_ehbar_hhp}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	&\frac{1}{2\pi}
	\int d\bar\nu\,
	Q^+_{ia}(\bar\nu,\omega)
	\frac{1}{\nu'-\bar\nu-(\eps_c-\eps_k)+\ii0^+}
	\\
	&\qquad=
	\frac{1}{\nu'+\omega-E^+_{ac;k}+\ii0^+}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:contour_ehbar_pph}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	&\frac{1}{2\pi}
	\int d\bar\nu\,
	Q^+_{ia}(\bar\nu,\omega)
	\frac{1}{\bar\nu+\nu'+\omega-\eps_c-\eps_d+\ii0^+}
	\\
	&\qquad=
	\frac{1}{\nu'+\omega-E^+_{cd;i}+\ii0^+}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:contour_pp_pph}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	&\frac{1}{2\pi}
	\int d\bar\nu\,
	Q^+_{ia}(\bar\nu,\omega)
	\frac{1}{\bar\nu+\nu'+\omega-\eps_k-\eps_l-\ii0^+}
	\\
	&\qquad=
	\frac{1}{\nu'-E^-_{kl;a}-\ii0^+}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:contour_pp_hhp}
\end{equation}
The signs multiplying these terms are inherited from
$\Gamma^{\eh,(2)}$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:Gamma_eh_second_explicit}; they do not
arise from the contour integration.

For the $++$ block,
\begin{equation}
	\left[\ii\Gamma^\eh\right]^{++}_{ia,jb}
	\equiv
	\ii\Gamma^\eh_{aj\,ib}
	\label{eq:Gamma_pp_block}
\end{equation}
so that
\begin{equation}
	V^{++,(1)}_{ia,jb}
	=
	\mel{aj}{}{ib}
	\label{eq:V_pp_first}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	V^{++,(2)}_{ia,jb}(\nu',\omega)
	={}&
	\sum_{kc}
	\frac{\mel{jc}{}{ik}\mel{ka}{}{cb}}
	{\nu'-E^-_{ik;c}-\ii0^+}
	\\
	&-
	\sum_{kc}
	\frac{\mel{jk}{}{ic}\mel{ca}{}{kb}}
	{\nu'+\omega-E^+_{ac;k}+\ii0^+}
	\\
	&-
	\frac{1}{2}
	\sum_{kl}
	\frac{\mel{aj}{}{kl}\mel{lk}{}{bi}}
	{\nu'-E^-_{kl;a}-\ii0^+}
	\\
	&+
	\frac{1}{2}
	\sum_{cd}
	\frac{\mel{aj}{}{cd}\mel{dc}{}{bi}}
	{\nu'+\omega-E^+_{cd;i}+\ii0^+}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:V_pp_second}
\end{equation}
For the $+-$ block,
\begin{equation}
	\left[\ii\Gamma^\eh\right]^{+-}_{ia,jb}
	\equiv
	\ii\Gamma^\eh_{ab\,ij}
	\label{eq:Gamma_pm_block}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
	V^{+-,(1)}_{ia,jb}
	=
	\mel{ab}{}{ij}
	\label{eq:V_pm_first}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	V^{+-,(2)}_{ia,jb}(\nu',\omega)
	={}&
	\sum_{kc}
	\frac{\mel{bc}{}{ik}\mel{ka}{}{cj}}
	{\nu'-E^-_{ik;c}-\ii0^+}
	\\
	&-
	\sum_{kc}
	\frac{\mel{bk}{}{ic}\mel{ca}{}{kj}}
	{\nu'+\omega-E^+_{ac;k}+\ii0^+}
	\\
	&-
	\frac{1}{2}
	\sum_{kl}
	\frac{\mel{ab}{}{kl}\mel{kl}{}{ij}}
	{\nu'-E^-_{kl;a}-\ii0^+}
	\\
	&+
	\frac{1}{2}
	\sum_{cd}
	\frac{\mel{ab}{}{cd}\mel{cd}{}{ij}}
	{\nu'+\omega-E^+_{cd;i}+\ii0^+}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:V_pm_second}
\end{equation}
The $--$ and $-+$ blocks follow by pair reversal
\begin{align}
	V^{--}_{ia,jb}(\nu',\omega)
	&=
	V^{++}_{ia,jb}(\nu'+\omega,-\omega)
	\label{eq:V_mm_reversal}
	\\
	V^{-+}_{ia,jb}(\nu',\omega)
	&=
	V^{+-}_{ia,jb}(\nu'+\omega,-\omega)
	\label{eq:V_mp_reversal}
\end{align}
for real orbitals.
No complex conjugation is applied to the complete time-ordered boundary
value: conjugating the denominators would reverse their causal prescriptions.
For complex orbitals the transformation of the orbital residues must be
stated separately from the transformation of the frequency denominators.
The fermionic frequency associated with each pole is summarized by
\begin{equation}
	\begin{array}{c|cc}
	& \hhp & \pph
	\\
	\hline
	+ & \nu' & \nu'+\omega
	\\
	- & \nu'+\omega & \nu'
	\end{array}
	\label{eq:external_frequency_table}
\end{equation}
The left external vertex $U$ has the same pole spaces, with the surviving
fermionic frequency $\nu$ instead of $\nu'$.

\section{Residue-tensor representation}
\label{sec:residue_tensor}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The second-order external vertices admit a compact spectral representation.
Using ordered intermediate configurations
\begin{align}
	I^-
	&=
	(\mu\nu;\alpha)
	\in\hhp
	&
	E^-_{\mu\nu;\alpha}
	&=
	\eps_\mu+\eps_\nu-\eps_\alpha
	\label{eq:E_minus_general}
	\\
	I^+
	&=
	(\alpha\beta;\mu)
	\in\pph
	&
	E^+_{\alpha\beta;\mu}
	&=
	\eps_\alpha+\eps_\beta-\eps_\mu
	\label{eq:E_plus_general}
\end{align}
the right external vertex for a forward ($+$) first pair has the form
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	V^{+\sigma',(2)}_{ia,jb}(\nu',\omega)
	={}&
	\sum_{I^-}
	\frac{\mathcal R^{+\sigma',-}_{ia,jb;I^-}}
	{\nu'-E^-_{I^-}-\ii0^+}
	\\
	&+
	\sum_{I^+}
	\frac{\mathcal R^{+\sigma',+}_{ia,jb;I^+}}
	{\nu'+\omega-E^+_{I^+}+\ii0^+}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:V_residue_general}
\end{equation}
with $\sigma'=+$ or $-$.
The ordered-tuple convention keeps the $1/2$ factors of the pair channel
explicit.

For the $++$ block,
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\mathcal R^{++,-}_{ia,jb;\mu\nu;\alpha}
	={}&
	\delta_{\mu i}
	\mel{j\alpha}{}{\mu\nu}
	\mel{\nu a}{}{\alpha b}
	\\
	&-
	\frac{1}{2}
	\delta_{\alpha a}
	\mel{aj}{}{\mu\nu}
	\mel{\nu\mu}{}{bi}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:R_pp_minus}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\mathcal R^{++,+}_{ia,jb;\alpha\beta;\mu}
	={}&
	-
	\delta_{\alpha a}
	\mel{j\mu}{}{i\beta}
	\mel{\beta a}{}{\mu b}
	\\
	&+
	\frac{1}{2}
	\delta_{\mu i}
	\mel{aj}{}{\alpha\beta}
	\mel{\beta\alpha}{}{bi}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:R_pp_plus}
\end{equation}
For the $+-$ block,
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\mathcal R^{+-,-}_{ia,jb;\mu\nu;\alpha}
	={}&
	\delta_{\mu i}
	\mel{b\alpha}{}{\mu\nu}
	\mel{\nu a}{}{\alpha j}
	\\
	&-
	\frac{1}{2}
	\delta_{\alpha a}
	\mel{ab}{}{\mu\nu}
	\mel{\mu\nu}{}{ij}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:R_pm_minus}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\mathcal R^{+-,+}_{ia,jb;\alpha\beta;\mu}
	={}&
	-
	\delta_{\alpha a}
	\mel{b\mu}{}{i\beta}
	\mel{\beta a}{}{\mu j}
	\\
	&+
	\frac{1}{2}
	\delta_{\mu i}
	\mel{ab}{}{\alpha\beta}
	\mel{\alpha\beta}{}{ij}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:R_pm_plus}
\end{equation}
The $--$ and $-+$ blocks follow from pair reversal, and the left external vertex
has the analogous residue structure.
The pole spaces are universal ($\hhp$ and $\pph$), while the residues retain
the topology of their parquet channel.
A further coupling--resolvent--coupling factorization of an external vertex
is not generally minimal in the direct-$\eh$ pair grouping; the residue-tensor
form of Eq.~\eqref{eq:V_residue_general} is sufficient here.

\section{Second frequency integration: the eh GF2 kernel}
\label{sec:GF2_contraction}
\label{sec:second_contraction}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The remaining fermionic frequency can now be integrated with the
one-sided factor of the second external pair.
At second order, the projected kernel $\widetilde\Gamma^{\eh,(2)}$ defined in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Gamma_tilde_def} is the correlation part of the GF2 BSE kernel,
which we denote by $\widetilde\Xi_c^{(2)}$ below.
For a forward closure, the elementary $\hhp$ and $\pph$ frequency integrations are
\begin{equation}
	\frac{1}{2\pi}
	\int d\bar\nu\,
	Q^+_{jb}(\bar\nu,\omega)
	\frac{1}
	{\bar\nu-E^- -\ii0^+}
	=
	-\frac{1}
	{\omega-(\eps_b-E^-)+\ii0^+}
	\label{eq:second_contraction_minus}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
	\frac{1}{2\pi}
	\int d\bar\nu\,
	Q^+_{jb}(\bar\nu,\omega)
	\frac{1}
	{\bar\nu+\omega-E^+ +\ii0^+}
	=
	\frac{1}
	{\omega-(E^+-\eps_j)+\ii0^+}
	\label{eq:second_contraction_plus}
\end{equation}
Applying these identities to Eq.~\eqref{eq:V_pp_second} gives the
second-order resonant block of the effective kernel
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\left[\widetilde\Xi_c^{(2)}(\omega)\right]^{++}_{ia,jb}
	={}&
	-
	\sum_{kc}
	\frac{
	\mel{jc}{}{ik}
	\mel{ka}{}{cb}
	}
	{\omega-(\eps_b-\eps_i+\eps_c-\eps_k)}
	\\
	&-
	\sum_{kc}
	\frac{
	\mel{jk}{}{ic}
	\mel{ca}{}{kb}
	}
	{\omega-(\eps_a-\eps_j+\eps_c-\eps_k)}
	\\
	&+
	\frac{1}{2}
	\sum_{kl}
	\frac{
	\mel{aj}{}{kl}
	\mel{lk}{}{bi}
	}
	{\omega-(\eps_a+\eps_b-\eps_k-\eps_l)}
	\\
	&+
	\frac{1}{2}
	\sum_{cd}
	\frac{
	\mel{aj}{}{cd}
	\mel{dc}{}{bi}
	}
	{\omega-(\eps_c+\eps_d-\eps_i-\eps_j)}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:GF2_A}
\end{equation}

Closing the $+-$ right external vertex with a backward ($-$) pair gives
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\left[\widetilde\Xi_c^{(2)}\right]^{+-}_{ia,jb}
	={}&
	-
	\sum_{kc}
	\frac{
	\mel{bc}{}{ik}
	\mel{ka}{}{cj}
	}
	{-(\eps_b-\eps_i+\eps_c-\eps_k)}
	\\
	&-
	\sum_{kc}
	\frac{
	\mel{bk}{}{ic}
	\mel{ca}{}{kj}
	}
	{-(\eps_a-\eps_j+\eps_c-\eps_k)}
	\\
	&+
	\frac{1}{2}
	\sum_{kl}
	\frac{
	\mel{ab}{}{kl}
	\mel{kl}{}{ij}
	}
	{-(\eps_a+\eps_b-\eps_k-\eps_l)}
	\\
	&+
	\frac{1}{2}
	\sum_{cd}
	\frac{
	\mel{ab}{}{cd}
	\mel{cd}{}{ij}
	}
	{-(\eps_c+\eps_d-\eps_i-\eps_j)}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:GF2_B}
\end{equation}
The external transfer frequency cancels in the coupling block.
This is a consequence of closing the resolvents entering the external vertices with a backward
external pair rather than a new type of intermediate-state propagation.

The complete effective kernel therefore has the familiar Casida-like
structure
\begin{equation}
	\widetilde{\boldsymbol\Xi}^{(2)}(\omega)
	=
	\begin{pmatrix}
	A_c^{(2)}(\omega)
	&
	B_c^{(2)}
	\\
	B_c^{(2)\dagger}
	&
	A_c^{(2)\dagger}(-\omega)
	\end{pmatrix}
	\label{eq:GF2_matrix}
\end{equation}
Equations~\eqref{eq:GF2_A} and \eqref{eq:GF2_B} give the effective
GF2 correlation kernel in the present antisymmetrized pair convention.
A direct contour evaluation of the original three-frequency kernel provides
an independent check of every term.
In particular, the last two terms of Eq.~\eqref{eq:GF2_B} contain
$\mel{kl}{}{ij}$ and $\mel{cd}{}{ij}$, respectively.
Reversing only one of these antisymmetric pairs would introduce an
additional minus sign and is therefore inconsistent with the
three-frequency expression in Eq.~\eqref{eq:Gamma_eh_second_explicit}.
This check fixes the channel frequency maps, fermionic signs, and
pair-channel factors without relying on the already integrated kernel.

At the exact level, the two-sided frequency projection is not by itself an
identity for the full three-frequency BSE because the transition amplitude can
contain frequency shapes orthogonal to the $Q^\pm$ factors.
Within the retained $1h1p/1p1h$ orbital-pair sector, a centered
one-sided-vertex analysis shows that the feedback of these complementary
frequency shapes starts at fourth order, provided no $\hhp$, $\pph$, or mixed
denominator becomes perturbatively small.
This frequency-shape statement does not eliminate the same-occupancy orbital
sectors that become active when a dynamic second-order vertex is inserted at
third order; those sectors are analyzed explicitly below.
The detailed frequency-shape algebra is developed separately in the companion notes on the
algebraic reduction and linearization of the three-frequency BSE.

\section{Second frequency integration in the pp channel}
\label{sec:pp_gf2_check}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The $\pp$ construction admits an independent second-order check analogous
to the $\eh$ GF2 test of Sec.~\ref{sec:GF2_contraction}.
Starting from the second-order $\pp$-irreducible kernel in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Gamma_pp_second}, integrating over one fermionic frequency with
the corresponding noninteracting pair propagator produces a two-frequency
pair-space right external vertex $V^{\pp,(2)}$.
Contracting the remaining relative fermionic frequency must then recover
the one-frequency second-order pp kernel obtained independently from the
GF2 anomalous self-energy.
This comparison provides a stringent test because it probes simultaneously
the $\pph$ and $\hhp$ external-vertex poles, the antisymmetry of the pair space, and
the relative signs of the four orbital attachments.

Consider first a central two-particle pair $ab$ and an external pair $rs$.
It is convenient in this subsection to use the centered relative frequency
$\bar{\nu}_\pp$ for the external pair rather than the native right frequency
$\nu'_\pp$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:target_pp}.
They are related by
\begin{equation}
	\bar{\nu}_\pp
	=
	\nu'_\pp+\frac{\Omega}{2}
	\label{eq:pp_centered_frequency}
\end{equation}
After integrating over one fermionic frequency, the upper right external vertex can be written as
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
V_{ab,rs}^{\pp,(2)}(\bar{\nu}_\pp,\Omega)
={}&
\sum_{me}
\frac{
\mel{rm}{}{ae}\mel{se}{}{bm}
}
{\bar{\nu}_\pp-(\eps_a+\eps_e-\eps_m-\Omega/2)+\ii0^+}
\\
&+
\sum_{me}
\frac{
\mel{re}{}{bm}\mel{sm}{}{ae}
}
{\bar{\nu}_\pp-(\eps_m-\eps_a-\eps_e+\Omega/2)-\ii0^+}
\\
&-
\sum_{me}
\frac{
\mel{re}{}{am}\mel{sm}{}{be}
}
{\bar{\nu}_\pp-(\eps_m-\eps_e-\eps_b+\Omega/2)-\ii0^+}
\\
&-
\sum_{me}
\frac{
\mel{rm}{}{be}\mel{se}{}{am}
}
{\bar{\nu}_\pp-(\eps_b+\eps_e-\eps_m-\Omega/2)+\ii0^+}
\end{split}
\label{eq:pp_V_explicit}
\end{equation}
The two individual fermionic energies of the external pair are
\begin{equation}
	z_1
	=
	\frac{\Omega}{2}+\bar{\nu}_\pp
	=
	\Omega+\nu'_\pp
	\qquad
	z_2
	=
	\frac{\Omega}{2}-\bar{\nu}_\pp
	=
	-\nu'_\pp
	\label{eq:pp_individual_energies}
\end{equation}
Using the direct-$\eh$ frequency map of Eq.~\eqref{eq:pp_frequency_map},
\begin{equation}
	z_1
	=
	\nu+\omega
	\qquad
	z_2
	=
	\nu'
	\label{eq:pp_individual_energies_eh}
\end{equation}
so the poles of Eq.~\eqref{eq:pp_V_explicit} occur at $\pph$
energies.
The two terms associated with $z_1$ and $z_2$ correspond to attaching the
particle-hole correction to either member of the antisymmetric pair.
The $\hh$ external vertex is obtained in the same manner and contains the
corresponding $\hhp$ energies.

Projecting the remaining pair onto a two-particle state $cd$ yields the
dynamic upper-left block of the effective pp kernel
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\ii\widetilde\Xi_{ab,cd}^{\pp,(2)}(\Omega)
={}&
\ii\sum_{me}
\frac{
\mel{cm}{}{ae}\mel{de}{}{bm}
}
{\Omega-(\eps_a+\eps_c+\eps_e-\eps_m)+\ii0^+}
\\
&-
\ii\sum_{me}
\frac{
\mel{ce}{}{bm}\mel{dm}{}{ae}
}
{\Omega-(\eps_a+\eps_d+\eps_e-\eps_m)+\ii0^+}
\\
&-
\ii\sum_{me}
\frac{
\mel{cm}{}{be}\mel{de}{}{am}
}
{\Omega-(\eps_b+\eps_c+\eps_e-\eps_m)+\ii0^+}
\\
&+
\ii\sum_{me}
\frac{
\mel{ce}{}{am}\mel{dm}{}{be}
}
{\Omega-(\eps_b+\eps_d+\eps_e-\eps_m)+\ii0^+}
\end{split}
\label{eq:pp_GF2_C}
\end{equation}
This is the correlation contribution to the dynamic $C$ block of the GF2
$\pp$ BSE matrix.
The four residues appear with the relative pattern $+,-,-,+$, exactly as
required by antisymmetry under interchange of either external pair.

Projecting instead onto a two-hole state $ij$ produces the coupling block
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\ii\widetilde\Xi_{ab,ij}^{\pp,(2)}
={}&
\ii\sum_{me}
\frac{
\mel{ie}{}{bm}\mel{jm}{}{ae}
}
{-(\eps_i-\eps_a+\eps_m-\eps_e)}
\\
&+
\ii\sum_{me}
\frac{
\mel{im}{}{ae}\mel{je}{}{bm}
}
{-(\eps_a-\eps_j+\eps_e-\eps_m)}
\\
&-
\ii\sum_{me}
\frac{
\mel{ie}{}{am}\mel{jm}{}{be}
}
{-(\eps_i-\eps_b+\eps_m-\eps_e)}
\\
&-
\ii\sum_{me}
\frac{
\mel{im}{}{be}\mel{je}{}{am}
}
{-(\eps_b-\eps_j+\eps_e-\eps_m)}
\end{split}
\label{eq:pp_GF2_B}
\end{equation}
The explicit dependence on the bosonic pair frequency cancels in this
block.
The static character of the $\ee$--$\hh$ coupling is therefore not an
additional approximation but follows directly from the analytic structure
of the partially integrated kernel.
This is the precise $\pp$ analogue of the static $B$ block in
the $\eh$ GF2 kernel.

Starting from the two-hole external vertex and integrating the remaining fermionic frequency with
a two-hole projector gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\ii\widetilde\Xi_{ij,kl}^{\pp,(2)}(-\Omega)
={}&
\ii\sum_{me}
\frac{
\mel{ke}{}{im}\mel{lm}{}{je}
}
{-\Omega-(-\eps_i-\eps_k+\eps_e-\eps_m)-\ii0^+}
\\
&-
\ii\sum_{me}
\frac{
\mel{ke}{}{jm}\mel{lm}{}{ie}
}
{-\Omega-(-\eps_j-\eps_k+\eps_e-\eps_m)-\ii0^+}
\\
&-
\ii\sum_{me}
\frac{
\mel{km}{}{je}\mel{le}{}{im}
}
{-\Omega-(-\eps_i-\eps_l+\eps_e-\eps_m)-\ii0^+}
\\
&+
\ii\sum_{me}
\frac{
\mel{km}{}{ie}\mel{le}{}{jm}
}
{-\Omega-(-\eps_j-\eps_l+\eps_e-\eps_m)-\ii0^+}
\end{split}
\label{eq:pp_GF2_D}
\end{equation}
which is the dynamic $D$ block of the GF2 pp BSE matrix.
Equations~\eqref{eq:pp_GF2_C}, \eqref{eq:pp_GF2_B}, and
\eqref{eq:pp_GF2_D} are the result of the present two-step frequency
projection in the antisymmetric pair convention used here.
They should not presently be identified term by term with the published
anomalous-GF2 pp kernel: in the standard reduced-kernel convention the
published dynamic block contains two antisymmetrized products at a given
denominator, whereas Eq.~\eqref{eq:pp_GF2_C} contains one.
This difference cannot be removed by a global phase or pair-normalization
factor alone and requires an explicit convention-by-convention derivation.
Accordingly, the pp reduction is retained here as a useful analytic result
but is not used as an independent validation of the second-order construction.

The pair normalization itself follows directly from antisymmetry.
In unrestricted orbital sums the pp BSE contains an explicit factor $1/2$
because the ordered pairs $(p,q)$ and $(q,p)$ are both present.
In a normalized antisymmetric basis with $p<q$, this double counting is
removed and no additional $1/2$ appears in
\begin{equation}
 \ii\Phi_P^\pp
 =
 U^\pp R^\pp V^\pp
 \label{eq:pp_master_normalization_check}
\end{equation}

The two primitive channels therefore exhibit a completely parallel
frequency-integration structure
\begin{equation}
\begin{array}{ccc}
\eh & & \pp
\\[3pt]
A(\omega) & \longleftrightarrow & C(\Omega)
\\
B & \longleftrightarrow & B
\\
A^\dagger(-\omega) & \longleftrightarrow & D(-\Omega)
\end{array}
\label{eq:eh_pp_GF2_parallel}
\end{equation}
In both channels the diagonal blocks retain the bosonic frequency while
the off-diagonal coupling block becomes static after the second
frequency integration.
This parallel analytic structure is useful, but the pp formulas are not
used here as an independent GF2 validation until their projection convention
has been mapped explicitly onto the anomalous-GF2 one.

\section{Screened-interaction resummation: the first GW kernel term}
\label{sec:gw_first}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The second-order construction above can be extended naturally from a bare
particle--hole intermediate state to a screened interaction.
For the $GW$ self-energy,
\begin{equation}
	\Sigma_{\xc}^{GW}(12)
	=
	\ii G(12)W(1^+2)
	\label{eq:Sigma_GW}
\end{equation}
the functional derivative entering the direct-$\eh$ BSE kernel separates as
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\Xi^{GW}(36;45)
	={}&
	\Xi_H(36;45)
	+
	\ii\delta(35)\delta(46)W(3^+4)
	\\
	&+
	\ii G(34)\frac{\delta W(3^+4)}{\delta G(56)}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:Xi_GW_split}
\end{equation}
The present section considers only the middle term of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Xi_GW_split}, namely the dynamical screened-interaction
contribution.
The last term, which contains $\delta W/\delta G$, is a distinct second
contribution to the full $GW$ kernel and is not included here.

It is important to distinguish the direct Coulomb matrix elements entering
$W$ from the antisymmetrized matrix elements used elsewhere in these notes.
We denote a non-antisymmetrized Coulomb integral by $(pq|rs)$, whereas
$\mel{pq}{}{rs}$ remains antisymmetrized.
For real orbitals, the RPA spectral representation of the correlation part
$W_c$, obtained by subtracting the instantaneous bare Coulomb interaction
from $W$, can be written as
\begin{equation}
	W^c_{pqrs}(\Omega)
	=
	\sum_{m>0}
	g^m_{pr}g^m_{qs}
	\left[
	\frac{1}{\Omega-\Omega_m+\ii0^+}
	-
	\frac{1}{\Omega+\Omega_m-\ii0^+}
	\right]
	\label{eq:Wc_spectral}
\end{equation}
For later use, define the mode propagator
\begin{equation}
	\mathcal W_m(x)
	=
	\frac{1}{x-\Omega_m+\ii0^+}
	-
	\frac{1}{x+\Omega_m-\ii0^+}
	\label{eq:GW_mode_propagator}
\end{equation}
where $\Omega_m>0$ is an RPA neutral-excitation energy and
\begin{equation}
	g^m_{pr}
	=
	\sum_{kc}
	(pc|rk)
	\left(
	X^m_{kc}+Y^m_{kc}
	\right)
	\label{eq:gw_transition_integral}
\end{equation}
is the screened transition integral.
The instantaneous bare part of $W$ reproduces the first-order exchange
kernel and is therefore not included again in the correlation expressions
below.

With the direct-$\eh$ frequency convention of Sec.~\ref{sec:frequencies},
the correlation part of the first $GW$ kernel term is
\begin{equation}
	\left[
	\ii\Gamma_{W,c}^{GW}
	\right]_{pqrs}
	(\nu,\nu',\omega)
	=
	-
	W^c_{pqsr}(\nu'-\nu)
	\label{eq:GW_three_frequency}
\end{equation}
Thus, the $++$ block is
\begin{equation}
	\left[
	\ii\Gamma_{W,c}^{GW}
	\right]^{++}_{ia,jb}
	(\nu,\nu',\omega)
	=
	-
	\sum_{m>0}
	g^m_{ab}g^m_{ji}
	\mathcal W_m(\nu'-\nu)
	\label{eq:GW_three_frequency_pp}
\end{equation}
while the $+-$ block is
\begin{equation}
	\left[
	\ii\Gamma_{W,c}^{GW}
	\right]^{+-}_{ia,jb}
	(\nu,\nu',\omega)
	=
	-
	\sum_{m>0}
	g^m_{aj}g^m_{bi}
	\mathcal W_m(\nu'-\nu)
	\label{eq:GW_three_frequency_pm}
\end{equation}

The first one-sided frequency integration is therefore identical to the
transverse-$\eh$ contour calculation of Sec.~\ref{sec:half_contraction_second}.
For the $++$ block,
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	V_{W,c}^{GW,++}{}_{ia,jb}(\nu',\omega)
	={}&
	\sum_{m>0}
	\frac{g^m_{ab}g^m_{ji}}
	{\nu'-(\eps_i-\Omega_m)-\ii0^+}
	\\
	&-
	\sum_{m>0}
	\frac{g^m_{ab}g^m_{ji}}
	{\nu'+\omega-(\eps_a+\Omega_m)+\ii0^+}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:GW_external_pp}
\end{equation}
while
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	V_{W,c}^{GW,+-}{}_{ia,jb}(\nu',\omega)
	={}&
	\sum_{m>0}
	\frac{g^m_{aj}g^m_{bi}}
	{\nu'-(\eps_i-\Omega_m)-\ii0^+}
	\\
	&-
	\sum_{m>0}
	\frac{g^m_{aj}g^m_{bi}}
	{\nu'+\omega-(\eps_a+\Omega_m)+\ii0^+}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:GW_external_pm}
\end{equation}
The two external-vertex pole families are therefore
\begin{equation}
	E^-_{i;m}
	=
	\eps_i-\Omega_m
	\qquad
	E^+_{a;m}
	=
	\eps_a+\Omega_m
	\label{eq:GW_external_poles}
\end{equation}
which may be viewed as hole--RPA-mode and particle--RPA-mode satellite
energies.

Performing the second frequency integration gives the resonant block
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\left[
	\widetilde\Xi_{W,c}^{GW}(\omega)
	\right]^{++}_{ia,jb}
	={}&
	-
	\sum_{m>0}
	\frac{g^m_{ab}g^m_{ji}}
	{\omega-(\eps_b-\eps_i+\Omega_m)+\ii0^+}
	\\
	&-
	\sum_{m>0}
	\frac{g^m_{ab}g^m_{ji}}
	{\omega-(\eps_a-\eps_j+\Omega_m)+\ii0^+}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:GW_effective_A}
\end{equation}
and the coupling block
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\left[
	\widetilde\Xi_{W,c}^{GW}
	\right]^{+-}_{ia,jb}
	={}&
	\sum_{m>0}
	\frac{g^m_{aj}g^m_{bi}}
	{\eps_b-\eps_i+\Omega_m}
	\\
	&+
	\sum_{m>0}
	\frac{g^m_{aj}g^m_{bi}}
	{\eps_a-\eps_j+\Omega_m}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:GW_effective_B}
\end{equation}
The $--$ and $-+$ blocks follow from pair reversal.
Equations~\eqref{eq:GW_effective_A} and \eqref{eq:GW_effective_B} reproduce
the usual dynamical first $GW$ kernel contribution without making the
static-screening approximation $W(\Omega)\to W(0)$.

The multiresolvent interpretation is especially simple.
Introduce an auxiliary neutral pair $kc$ together with an RPA mode $m$ and
two attachment labels $\lambda=1,2$, with zeroth-order energy
\begin{equation}
	\mathcal E_{kc,m}
	=
	\eps_c-\eps_k+\Omega_m
	\label{eq:GW_aux_energy}
\end{equation}
and couplings
\begin{align}
	C^{GW,1}_{ia,kc;m}
	&=
	\delta_{ik}g^m_{ac}
	&
	D^{GW,1}_{kc;m,jb}
	&=
	\delta_{cb}g^m_{jk}
	\label{eq:GW_aux_coupling_1}
	\\
	C^{GW,2}_{ia,kc;m}
	&=
	\delta_{ac}g^m_{ki}
	&
	D^{GW,2}_{kc;m,jb}
	&=
	\delta_{jk}g^m_{cb}
	\label{eq:GW_aux_coupling_2}
\end{align}
Then the complete resonant contribution in Eq.~\eqref{eq:GW_effective_A}
can be written as
\begin{equation}
	\left[
	\widetilde\Xi_{W,c}^{GW}(\omega)
	\right]^{++}
	=
	-
	\sum_{\lambda=1}^{2}
	C^{GW,\lambda}
	\left[
	\omega-M_{GW}^{(0)}
	\right]^{-1}
	D^{GW,\lambda}
	\label{eq:GW_multiresolvent}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
	\left[M_{GW}^{(0)}\right]_{kc;m,k'c';n}
	=
	\mathcal E_{kc,m}
	\delta_{kk'}\delta_{cc'}\delta_{mn}
	\label{eq:GW_aux_matrix}
\end{equation}
Thus dynamical screening replaces the explicit particle--hole excitation
inside the GF2 external vertex by an RPA neutral mode, while preserving an
ordinary one-frequency resolvent.

In the independent-particle limit of the RPA spectrum, a mode
$m\equiv(ld)$ obeys
\begin{equation}
	\Omega_m
	\longrightarrow
	\eps_d-\eps_l
	\qquad
	X^m_{kc}+Y^m_{kc}
	\longrightarrow
	\delta_{kl}\delta_{cd}
	\label{eq:GW_IP_limit}
\end{equation}
the auxiliary pole energies become ordinary $2h2p$ energies
\begin{equation}
	\mathcal E_{kc,m}
	\longrightarrow
	\eps_c+\eps_d-\eps_k-\eps_l
	\label{eq:GW_IP_poles}
\end{equation}
This identifies the same neutral-double pole manifold that appears in the
particle--hole-like terms of the GF2 kernel.
The residues should not, however, be identified with the complete GF2
residues: the present $\ii W$ term is only the first contribution generated
by $\delta\Sigma^{GW}/\delta G$, whereas
$\ii G\,\delta W/\delta G$ contributes at the same bare perturbative order.
Moreover, GF2 contains exchange as well as explicit $\pp$ and $\hh$ pole
classes that are not generated by the screened-interaction term alone.

The construction in this section concerns the ordinary direct-$\eh$ Hedin
kernel.
A particle-particle screened-interaction kernel would require the
corresponding anomalous/Gorkov $GW$ functional derivative; that explicit
kernel is not part of the present derivation and should not be obtained by
the formal replacement $\mel{pq}{}{rs}\to W_{pqrs}$ in the $\pp$ primitive.

\section{Channel consistency at second order}
\label{sec:channel_consistency}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The second-order construction can be checked in several independent ways.
The first check is crossing.
The complete transverse $\eh$ contribution must be obtained from
the direct $\eh$ reducible vertex through
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Phi_ehbar_crossing}.
This fixes both the orbital permutation and the frequency combination
$\nu'-\nu$.

The second check uses the $\pp$ convention.
The native pair propagator depends only on $\Omega_\pp$, and the exact
frequency transformation of Eq.~\eqref{eq:pp_frequency_map} fixes the
combination $\nu+\nu'+\omega$ in the direct $\eh$ convention.
One-sided frequency integration of this object must produce the same $\hhp$ and $\pph$
external-vertex poles obtained from the transverse $\eh$ contribution.

The third check is the recovery of the $\eh$ GF2 kernel after integrating
over the remaining fermionic frequency, as shown in
Sec.~\ref{sec:second_contraction}.
This check is independent of the initial channel-resolved derivation and is
sensitive to signs, orbital-index permutations, and pair-channel factors.
The analogous pp reduction of Sec.~\ref{sec:pp_gf2_check} has the expected
frequency and antisymmetry structure but is not used as a term-by-term GF2
validation pending an explicit mapping to the anomalous-GF2 projection
convention.

Finally, the full second-order vertex must be independent of the channel
through which it is reconstructed.
Using the parquet relations,
\begin{align}
	\Gamma^{\eh,(2)}+\Phi^{\eh,(2)}
	&=
	\Phi^{\eh,(2)}+\Phi^{\teh,(2)}+\Phi^{\pp,(2)}
	\label{eq:F_second_eh}
	\\
	\Gamma^{\pp,(2)}+\Phi^{\pp,(2)}
	&=
	\Phi^{\eh,(2)}+\Phi^{\teh,(2)}+\Phi^{\pp,(2)}
	\label{eq:F_second_pp}
	\\
	F^{(2)}
	&=
	\Gamma^{\eh,(2)}+\Phi^{\eh,(2)}
	=
	\Gamma^{\pp,(2)}+\Phi^{\pp,(2)}
	\label{eq:channel_consistency_second}
\end{align}
The same channel organization reconstructs the direct $\eh$ Hedin
kernel through Eq.~\eqref{eq:Gamma_eh}.

\section{Recipe through second order}
\label{sec:recipe_second}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The construction is now complete through second order.
Using the channel transfers defined in Sec.~\ref{sec:frequencies}, construct
the two primitive reducible vertices $\Phi^{\eh,(2)}$ and
$\Phi_P^{\pp,(2)}$ in their natural channels and obtain
$\Phi^{\teh,(2)}$ by crossing.
The two primitive vertices are represented as
\begin{align}
	\ii\Phi^{\eh,(2)}
	&=
	U^{\eh,(1)}R^{\eh,(0)}V^{\eh,(1)}
	\label{eq:recipe_second_eh}
	\\
	\ii\Phi_P^{\pp,(2)}
	&=
	U^{\pp,(1)}R^{\pp,(0)}V^{\pp,(1)}
	\label{eq:recipe_second_pp}
\end{align}
where the central resolvents describe neutral $N$-electron and pair
$(N\pm2)$-electron propagation, respectively.

The second-order channel-irreducible vertices follow from the parquet
relations of Eqs.~\eqref{eq:Gamma_eh_second} and \eqref{eq:Gamma_pp_second}.
A one-sided frequency integration generates external vertices whose only
non-trivial poles are
\begin{equation}
	N-1:\ \hhp
	\qquad\qquad
	N+1:\ \pph
	\label{eq:recipe_second_external_spaces}
\end{equation}
A second frequency integration yields the one-frequency $\eh$ GF2 BSE
kernel and, in the $\pp$ channel, the corresponding projected pair kernel.
The latter has the expected frequency and antisymmetry structure but is not
identified term by term with the published anomalous-GF2 kernel without an
explicit mapping of projection conventions.
Finally,
\begin{equation}
	F^{(2)}
	=
	\Gamma^{\eh,(2)}+\Phi^{\eh,(2)}
	=
	\Gamma^{\pp,(2)}+\Phi^{\pp,(2)}
	\label{eq:recipe_second_F}
\end{equation}

\section{Third-order multiresolvent extension}
\label{sec:multiresolvent_third}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The construction up to Sec.~\ref{sec:recipe_second} is complete through
second order.
We now examine the extension of the same multiresolvent ansatz by one
perturbative order.
Within the conventional central pair spaces, the third-order terms are
organized by corrections to the two external vertices and to the central
resolvent.
The unrestricted frequency convolution will show that additional orbital
sectors are activated at this order; these are introduced explicitly below
rather than being hidden inside the restricted ansatz.

For either primitive channel $r=\eh,\pp$, we expand
\begin{align}
	U^r
	&=
	U^{r,(1)}+U^{r,(2)}+\mathcal O(v^3)
	&
	V^r
	&=
	V^{r,(1)}+V^{r,(2)}+\mathcal O(v^3)
	\label{eq:target_UV_expansion}
	\\
	M^r
	&=
	M^{r,(0)}+M^{r,(1)}+\mathcal O(v^2)
	\label{eq:target_M_expansion}
\end{align}
The corresponding central resolvent becomes
\begin{align}
	R^r
	&=
	R^{r,(0)}
	+
	R^{r,(1)}
	+
	\mathcal O(v^2)
	\label{eq:target_R_expansion}
	\\
	R^{r,(1)}
	&=
	R^{r,(0)}M^{r,(1)}R^{r,(0)}
	\label{eq:target_R_first}
\end{align}
The third-order contribution captured by this restricted ansatz is
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\ii\Phi_{\rm proj}^{r,(3)}
	={}&
	U^{r,(2)}R^{r,(0)}V^{r,(1)}
	+
	U^{r,(1)}R^{r,(0)}V^{r,(2)}
	\\
	&+
	U^{r,(1)}R^{r,(1)}V^{r,(1)}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:target_third_order}
\end{equation}
The three terms correspond, respectively, to a second-order correction to
the left external vertex, a second-order correction to the right external
vertex, and a first-order correction to the central resolvent.
They exhaust the contribution obtained after projection onto the usual
central pair spaces, but not the unrestricted third-order convolution.
The complementary orbital-sector remainders are derived and kept explicitly
in the following sections.

\section{Direct eh vertex through third order}
\label{sec:eh_third}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

It is useful to distinguish two closely related partially integrated objects.
The pair-space vertices $V^{++}_{ia,jb}$ and $V^{+-}_{ia,jb}$ derived in
Sec.~\ref{sec:half_contraction_second} were introduced to validate the
reduction to the one-frequency GF2 kernel.
The external vertices entering Eq.~\eqref{eq:target_eh} have instead one
general external index pair and one central neutral pair.
Both constructions are one-sided projections of the same three-frequency
$\Gamma^{\eh,(2)}$ and therefore possess the same $\hhp$ and $\pph$ pole
spaces; only the external index grouping differs.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Perturbative BSE expansion}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

Define the reduced vertices and reduced reference pair propagator
\begin{equation}
	\mathcal G^\eh
	=
	\ii\Gamma^\eh
	\qquad
	\mathcal P^\eh
	=
	\ii\Phi^\eh
	\qquad
	\mathcal L_0
	=
	-\ii L_0
	\label{eq:reduced_vertices}
\end{equation}
The last definition removes the explicit phase carried by the time-ordered
pair propagator. In the forward/backward $1h1p$ space its one-frequency
form is precisely the zeroth-order metric resolvent $R^{\eh,(0)}$.
Before one-sided frequency integration, products of frequency-dependent
vertices and pair propagators are frequency convolutions, denoted by $\circ$.
With the reduced quantities the third-order direct $\eh$ reducible vertex is
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\mathcal P^{\eh,(3)}
	={}&
	\mathcal G^{\eh,(2)}
	\circ \mathcal L_0
	\circ \mathcal G^{\eh,(1)}
	\\
	&+
	\mathcal G^{\eh,(1)}
	\circ \mathcal L_0
	\circ \mathcal G^{\eh,(2)}
	\\
	&+
	\mathcal G^{\eh,(1)}
	\circ \mathcal L_0
	\circ \mathcal G^{\eh,(1)}
	\circ \mathcal L_0
	\circ \mathcal G^{\eh,(1)}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:Phi_eh_third_convolution}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, if the unreduced $L_0$ is used instead, the first two terms
carry a prefactor $-\ii$ and the three-interaction term a prefactor $-1$.
No $\Gamma^{\eh,(3)}$ contribution appears in
$\Phi^{\eh,(3)}$ because an $\eh$ reducible diagram contains at least one
interaction on each side of the cut.

The fully irreducible vertex has no second- or third-order contribution
beyond the bare interaction. In the present convention the unambiguous
statement is
\begin{equation}
	\ii\Lambda^{(1)}_{pqrs}
	=
	\mel{pq}{}{rs}
	\qquad
	\Lambda
	=
	\Lambda^{(1)}+\mathcal O(v^4)
	\label{eq:Lambda_through_third}
\end{equation}
so that the second-order irreducible kernel entering
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Phi_eh_third_convolution} is exactly the object derived in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Gamma_eh_second_explicit}.

The zeroth-order neutral resolvent $R^{\eh,(0)}$ and the first-order external vertices were fixed already in Sec.~\ref{sec:second_order_multiresolvent}.
The new ingredients required at third order are therefore the second-order external-vertex corrections and $R^{\eh,(1)}$.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Orbital sectors in the third-order convolution}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The orbital restriction implicit in the ordinary neutral resolvent requires
special care at third order.
For a frequency-independent interaction, same-occupancy reference bubbles
vanish,
\begin{equation}
	\int\frac{dx}{2\pi}
	G_{0,a}(x+\omega)G_{0,b}(x)
	=
	0
	\qquad
	\int\frac{dx}{2\pi}
	G_{0,i}(x+\omega)G_{0,j}(x)
	=
	0
	\label{eq:same_occupancy_bubbles_zero}
\end{equation}
because the two one-particle poles lie in the same half-plane.
This is why the second-order direct $\eh$ vertex may be represented entirely
in the $1h1p/1p1h$ neutral space.

A frequency-dependent second-order vertex changes this conclusion.
For example, with $a,b,c$ virtual, $k$ occupied, and
$\Delta_{ck}=\eps_c-\eps_k$,
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	&\int\frac{dx}{2\pi}
	\frac{1}{x+\omega-\eps_a+\ii0^+}
	\frac{1}{x-\eps_b+\ii0^+}
	\frac{1}{x-\nu+\Delta_{ck}-\ii0^+}
	\\
	&\qquad=
	\frac{\ii}
	{[\nu+\omega-(\eps_a+\Delta_{ck})+\ii0^+]
	 [\nu-(\eps_b+\Delta_{ck})+\ii0^+]}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:vv_dynamic_counterexample}
\end{equation}
which is nonzero.
The analogous $oo$ contributions are also nonzero when the dynamic kernel
provides a pole in the opposite half-plane.

It is therefore useful to decompose the reduced reference pair propagator as
\begin{equation}
	\mathcal L_0
	=
	\mathcal L_{0,\ph}
	+
	\mathcal L_{0,\rm so}
	\label{eq:L0_sector_decomposition}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal L_{0,\ph}$ contains the $ia$ and $ai$ sectors and
$\mathcal L_{0,\rm so}$ the same-occupancy $ab$ and $ij$ sectors.
The one-sided external vertices derived below evaluate the
$\mathcal L_{0,\ph}$ part exactly.
The remaining third-order contributions are
\begin{align}
	\Delta_L^{\eh,(3)}
	&=
	\mathcal G^{\eh,(2)}
	\circ\mathcal L_{0,\rm so}
	\circ\mathcal G^{\eh,(1)}
	\label{eq:Delta_eh_left_same_occ}
	\\
	\Delta_R^{\eh,(3)}
	&=
	\mathcal G^{\eh,(1)}
	\circ\mathcal L_{0,\rm so}
	\circ\mathcal G^{\eh,(2)}
	\label{eq:Delta_eh_right_same_occ}
\end{align}
The purely first-order ladder does not acquire such a remainder because the
interactions are frequency independent and the same-occupancy bubble then
vanishes before the next scattering event.
Consequently, the ordinary neutral-resolvent construction below should be
understood as the complete $\ph$-projected part of the third-order vertex;
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:Delta_eh_left_same_occ} and
\eqref{eq:Delta_eh_right_same_occ} must be retained for the unrestricted
three-frequency result.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Second-order corrections to the external vertices}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The left external-vertex correction for forward ($+$) central propagation through the pair $ia$ is obtained by integrating over one
fermionic frequency of the second-order irreducible kernel,
\begin{equation}
	U^{\eh,+,(2)}_{pr,ia}(\nu,\omega)
	=
	\frac{1}{2\pi}
	\int d\bar\nu
	\left[
	\ii\Gamma^{\eh,(2)}_{pa\,ri}
	(\nu,\bar\nu,\omega)
	\right]
	Q^+_{ia}(\bar\nu,\omega)
	\label{eq:U_eh_second_def}
\end{equation}
The explicit result is
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	U^{\eh,+,(2)}_{pr,ia}(\nu,\omega)
	={}&
	\sum_{jb}
	\frac{
	\mel{pb}{}{ij}
	\mel{aj}{}{rb}
	}
	{\nu-(\eps_i+\eps_j-\eps_b)-\ii0^+}
	\\
	&-
	\sum_{jb}
	\frac{
	\mel{pj}{}{ib}
	\mel{ab}{}{rj}
	}
	{\nu+\omega-(\eps_a+\eps_b-\eps_j)+\ii0^+}
	\\
	&-
	\frac{1}{2}
	\sum_{jk}
	\frac{
	\mel{pa}{}{jk}
	\mel{jk}{}{ri}
	}
	{\nu-(\eps_j+\eps_k-\eps_a)-\ii0^+}
	\\
	&+
	\frac{1}{2}
	\sum_{bc}
	\frac{
	\mel{pa}{}{bc}
	\mel{bc}{}{ri}
	}
	{\nu+\omega-(\eps_b+\eps_c-\eps_i)+\ii0^+}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:U_eh_second_explicit}
\end{equation}
The first two terms originate from the transverse $\eh$ channel
and the last two from the $\hh$ and $\pp$ branches.

The right external-vertex correction for forward ($+$) central propagation through the pair $ia$ is
\begin{equation}
	V^{\eh,+,(2)}_{ia,sq}(\nu',\omega)
	=
	\frac{1}{2\pi}
	\int d\bar\nu
	Q^+_{ia}(\bar\nu,\omega)
	\left[
	\ii\Gamma^{\eh,(2)}_{sa\,qi}
	(\bar\nu,\nu',\omega)
	\right]
	\label{eq:V_eh_second_def}
\end{equation}
which gives
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	V^{\eh,+,(2)}_{ia,sq}(\nu',\omega)
	={}&
	\sum_{kc}
	\frac{
	\mel{sk}{}{ic}
	\mel{ac}{}{qk}
	}
	{\nu'-(\eps_i+\eps_k-\eps_c)-\ii0^+}
	\\
	&-
	\sum_{kc}
	\frac{
	\mel{sc}{}{ik}
	\mel{ak}{}{qc}
	}
	{\nu'+\omega-(\eps_a+\eps_c-\eps_k)+\ii0^+}
	\\
	&-
	\frac{1}{2}
	\sum_{kl}
	\frac{
	\mel{sa}{}{kl}
	\mel{kl}{}{qi}
	}
	{\nu'-(\eps_k+\eps_l-\eps_a)-\ii0^+}
	\\
	&+
	\frac{1}{2}
	\sum_{cd}
	\frac{
	\mel{sa}{}{cd}
	\mel{cd}{}{qi}
	}
	{\nu'+\omega-(\eps_c+\eps_d-\eps_i)+\ii0^+}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:V_eh_second_explicit}
\end{equation}
The backward ($-$) external-vertex components follow from the same pair-reversal operation as
the pair-space external vertices.
The explicit formulas are not required for the third-order matching.

Equations~\eqref{eq:U_eh_second_explicit} and
\eqref{eq:V_eh_second_explicit} demonstrate that the external vertices of the
reducible direct $\eh$ object contain the same $\hhp$ and $\pph$
resolvents as the partially integrated Hedin kernel.
The difference lies only in the grouping of the external indices.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{First-order correction to the neutral resolvent}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The first-order neutral interaction matrix is
\begin{equation}
	M^{\eh,(1)}
	=
	\begin{pmatrix}
	A^{(1)}&B^{(1)}
	\\
	B^{(1)\dagger}&A^{(1)\dagger}
	\end{pmatrix}
	\label{eq:M_eh_first}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{align}
	A^{(1)}_{ia,jb}
	&=
	\mel{aj}{}{ib}
	\label{eq:A_eh_first}
	\\
	B^{(1)}_{ia,jb}
	&=
	\mel{ab}{}{ij}
	\label{eq:B_eh_first}
\end{align}
Using the general expansion of Eq.~\eqref{eq:target_R_first}, the first-order neutral-resolvent correction is
\begin{equation}
	R^{\eh,(1)}
	=
	R^{\eh,(0)}
	M^{\eh,(1)}
	R^{\eh,(0)}
	\label{eq:R_eh_first}
\end{equation}
No additional metric matrix appears in Eq.~\eqref{eq:R_eh_first} because the
metric is already included in the definition of the resolvent.

In the Tamm--Dancoff approximation (TDA), the corresponding central third-order
contribution is
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\left[
	\ii\Phi_{\rm cent}^{\eh,(3)}
	\right]_{pqrs}
	={}&
	\sum_{ia,jb}
	\frac{
	\mel{pa}{}{ri}
	\mel{aj}{}{ib}
	\mel{qj}{}{sb}
	}
	{
	[\omega-(\eps_a-\eps_i)+\ii0^+]
	[\omega-(\eps_b-\eps_j)+\ii0^+]
	}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:Phi_eh_third_central_tda}
\end{equation}
The full forward-backward expression is generated automatically by the
first-order correction to the neutral resolvent
\begin{equation}
	U^{\eh,(1)}
	R^{\eh,(1)}
	V^{\eh,(1)}
	=
	U^{\eh,(1)}
	R^{\eh,(0)}
	M^{\eh,(1)}
	R^{\eh,(0)}
	V^{\eh,(1)}
	\label{eq:Phi_eh_third_central_matrix}
\end{equation}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Direct eh result through third order}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

After the one-sided frequency integrations, the $\ph$ part of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Phi_eh_third_convolution} reduces to ordinary matrix products.
The unrestricted third-order result is therefore
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\ii\Phi^{\eh,(3)}
	={}&
	U^{\eh,(2)}R^{\eh,(0)}V^{\eh,(1)}
	+
	U^{\eh,(1)}R^{\eh,(0)}V^{\eh,(2)}
	\\
	&+
	U^{\eh,(1)}R^{\eh,(1)}V^{\eh,(1)}
	+
	\Delta_L^{\eh,(3)}
	+
	\Delta_R^{\eh,(3)}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:Phi_eh_third_final}
\end{equation}
The first three terms are the part captured by the ordinary neutral
$1h1p/1p1h$ resolvent: they correct the left external vertex, right external
vertex, and central neutral resolvent, respectively.
The last two terms contain the same-occupancy internal sectors activated by
the dynamic second-order kernel.
Thus the simple $URV$ ansatz is complete only after those sectors are either
represented in an enlarged auxiliary space or retained explicitly as the
remainders above.

The explicit products contain genuine multiresolvent structures.
For example, a correction to the left external vertex contains terms of the form
\begin{equation}
	\frac{1}{\nu-E^-_{I^-}-\ii0^+}
	\frac{1}{\omega-\Delta\eps_{ia}+\ii0^+}
	\label{eq:multi_resolvent_left_minus}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
	\frac{1}{\nu+\omega-E^+_{I^+}+\ii0^+}
	\frac{1}{\omega-\Delta\eps_{ia}+\ii0^+}
	\label{eq:multi_resolvent_left_plus}
\end{equation}
while the central correction contains two neutral resolvents separated by
the first-order interaction matrix.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{pp vertex through third order}
\label{sec:pp_third}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The zeroth-order $\pp$ metric, pair matrix, central resolvent, and first-order
external vertices were established in
Sec.~\ref{sec:second_order_multiresolvent}.
The third-order extension requires the first-order correction to the pair
matrix and the second-order corrections to the external vertices.

The first-order pair interaction matrix is
\begin{equation}
	M^{\pp,(1)}
	=
	\begin{pmatrix}
	\mel{ab}{}{cd}&\mel{ab}{}{ij}
	\\
	\mel{ij}{}{cd}&\mel{ij}{}{kl}
	\end{pmatrix}
	\label{eq:M_pp_first}
\end{equation}
The upper-left block acts in the $N+2$ sector, the lower-right block in the
$N-2$ sector, and the off-diagonal blocks couple the two pair sectors.
Accordingly,
\begin{equation}
	R^\pp
	=
	R^{\pp,(0)}
	+
	R^{\pp,(1)}
	+
	\mathcal O(v^2)
	\label{eq:R_pp_expansion}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
	R^{\pp,(1)}
	=
	R^{\pp,(0)}
	M^{\pp,(1)}
	R^{\pp,(0)}
	\label{eq:R_pp_first}
\end{equation}

From Eq.~\eqref{eq:Gamma_pp_second}, the second-order corrections to the $\pp$ external vertices are generated by $\eh$ intermediate propagation.
One-sided frequency integration with the one-sided pp pair propagator produces
$N+1$ $\pph$ and $N-1$ $\hhp$ external-vertex poles.
For the two-particle central sector,
\begin{equation}
	U_{\ee}^{\pp,(2)}
	=
	\sum_{\ell}
	\sum_{I^+}
	\frac{
	\mathcal S^{+,\ell}_{I^+}
	}
	{z_\ell-E^+_{I^+}+\ii0^+}
	\label{eq:U_pp_ee_second}
\end{equation}
while for the two-hole central sector,
\begin{equation}
	U_{\hh}^{\pp,(2)}
	=
	\sum_{\ell}
	\sum_{I^-}
	\frac{
	\mathcal S^{-,\ell}_{I^-}
	}
	{z_\ell-E^-_{I^-}-\ii0^+}
	\label{eq:U_pp_hh_second}
\end{equation}
Here $z_\ell$ denotes the one-particle energy carried by either member of
the external pair and $\ell$ labels the two antisymmetrically related
attachments.
The corresponding right external vertices $V^{\pp,(2)}$ have the same pole spaces.
The detailed residues are not required for the third-order parquet
closure and are therefore left in channel-adapted form.

The same orbital-sector issue appears in the $\pp$ channel.
The standard pair resolvent contains the $\ee$ and $\hh$ sectors, whereas a
frequency-dependent $\Gamma^{\pp,(2)}$ can activate mixed occupied--virtual
internal pairs whose isolated free pair integral vanishes.
Writing $\mathcal L_{0,\rm mix}^{\pp}$ for the mixed occupied--virtual
part of the reduced pair propagator and
$\mathcal G^{\pp}=\ii\Gamma^{\pp}$, define
\begin{align}
	\Delta_L^{\pp,(3)}
	&=
	\mathcal G^{\pp,(2)}
	\circ\mathcal L_{0,\rm mix}^{\pp}
	\circ\mathcal G^{\pp,(1)}
	\\
	\Delta_R^{\pp,(3)}
	&=
	\mathcal G^{\pp,(1)}
	\circ\mathcal L_{0,\rm mix}^{\pp}
	\circ\mathcal G^{\pp,(2)}
\end{align}
The unrestricted third-order result can therefore be organized as
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\ii\Phi_P^{\pp,(3)}
	={}&
	U^{\pp,(2)}R^{\pp,(0)}V^{\pp,(1)}
	+
	U^{\pp,(1)}R^{\pp,(0)}V^{\pp,(2)}
	\\
	&+
	U^{\pp,(1)}R^{\pp,(1)}V^{\pp,(1)}
	+
	\Delta_L^{\pp,(3)}
	+
	\Delta_R^{\pp,(3)}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:Phi_pp_third_final}
\end{equation}
The pure two-particle part of the central correction is the third-order
pair ladder
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\left[
	\ii\Phi_{P,\rm cent}^{\pp,(3)}
	\right]_{pqrs}^{\ee}
	={}&
	\sum_{\substack{a<b\\c<d}}
	\frac{
	\mel{pq}{}{ab}
	\mel{ab}{}{cd}
	\mel{cd}{}{rs}
	}
	{
	[\Omega-(\eps_a+\eps_b)+\ii0^+]
	[\Omega-(\eps_c+\eps_d)+\ii0^+]
	}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:Phi_pp_third_central_ee}
\end{equation}
The remaining $\ee\leftrightarrow\hh$ and $\hh\to\hh$ contributions are
generated by the other blocks of $M^{\pp,(1)}$.

The first three terms of Eq.~\eqref{eq:Phi_pp_third_final} contain the
second-order pp-irreducible kernel in the external vertices and one
first-order pp random-phase-approximation (ppRPA) interaction in the central
resolvent.
They give the complete contribution within the standard $\ee/\hh$ pair
space.
The mixed-sector remainders must be included, or represented by an enlarged
auxiliary space, before the unrestricted third-order $\pp$ vertex can be
called complete.

With $\Omega_\pp$ given by Eq.~\eqref{eq:Omega_pp}, the central pair
resolvent generates the characteristic anti-diagonal pair structure of the
three-frequency vertex.
\section{Consistency checks at third order}
\label{sec:checks_third}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The third-order extension is deliberately overdetermined.
Several independent checks can therefore be applied to the new third-order
terms, while the second-order checks have already been completed before
Sec.~\ref{sec:recipe_second}.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Second frequency integration}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

Applying the same two-sided projection as in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:Gamma_tilde_def} to the retained $\ph$ sector, define
$\widetilde{\mathcal G}^{\eh,(2)}=\ii\widetilde\Gamma^{\eh,(2)}$ and
$\widetilde{\mathcal P}^{\eh,(3)}=\ii\widetilde\Phi^{\eh,(3)}$.
The projected $URV$ part obeys
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\widetilde{\mathcal P}^{\eh,(3)}_{\ph}
	={}&
	\widetilde{\mathcal G}^{\eh,(2)}R^{\eh,(0)}\mathcal G^{\eh,(1)}
	+
	\mathcal G^{\eh,(1)}R^{\eh,(0)}\widetilde{\mathcal G}^{\eh,(2)}
	\\
	&+
	\mathcal G^{\eh,(1)}R^{\eh,(1)}\mathcal G^{\eh,(1)}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:third_one_frequency_check}
\end{equation}
which is the third-order expansion of the one-frequency BSE within the
standard neutral pair space.
The projections of $\Delta_L^{\eh,(3)}$ and
$\Delta_R^{\eh,(3)}$ are additional contributions and must be treated
separately in an unrestricted third-order reduction.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Crossing}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The transverse $\eh$ primitive is never constructed
independently.
At every perturbative order,
\begin{equation}
	\Phi^{\teh,(n)}_{pqrs}(\nu,\nu',\omega)
	=
	-
	\Phi^{\eh,(n)}_{pqsr}
	\left(
	\nu,
	\nu+\omega,
	\nu'-\nu
	\right)
	\label{eq:crossing_order_n}
\end{equation}
This maps each left, right, and central third-order direct $\eh$
contribution into the corresponding transverse topology and preserves the
fermionic crossing symmetry order by order.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Parquet reconstruction}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

Because the fully irreducible vertex has no third-order correction,
\begin{align}
	F^{(3)}
	&=
	\Phi^{\eh,(3)}+\Phi^{\teh,(3)}+\Phi^{\pp,(3)}
	\label{eq:F_n_parquet}
	\\
	\Gamma^{\eh,(3)}
	&=
	\Phi^{\teh,(3)}+\Phi^{\pp,(3)}
	\label{eq:Gamma_eh_n}
	\\
	\Gamma^{\pp,(3)}
	&=
	\Phi^{\eh,(3)}+\Phi^{\teh,(3)}
	\label{eq:Gamma_pp_n}
	\\
	F^{(3)}
	&=
	\Gamma^{\eh,(3)}+\Phi^{\eh,(3)}
	=
	\Gamma^{\pp,(3)}+\Phi^{\pp,(3)}
	\label{eq:channel_consistency_n}
\end{align}
The direct $\eh$ Hedin kernel is therefore reconstructed from the
two primitive multiresolvent objects through
$\Gamma^\eh=\Lambda+\Phi^{\teh}+\Phi^\pp$.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{High-frequency structure}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The second-order corrections to the external vertices decay as the inverse of their fermionic frequency,
\begin{equation}
	U^{(2)}
	=
	\mathcal O(\nu^{-1})
	\qquad
	V^{(2)}
	=
	\mathcal O({\nu'}^{-1})
	\label{eq:external_high_frequency}
\end{equation}
When both fermionic frequencies are large, only the central
$\omega$-dependent contribution survives at third order.
The resulting hierarchy is therefore consistent with the standard
parquet asymptotic decomposition into a purely bosonic part and left and
right one-fermionic-frequency corrections.

The same argument applies in the pp channel with the central pair
frequency $\Omega_\pp$ held fixed.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Diagram classes and orbital-sector completeness}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

Every third-order diagram reducible in the direct $\eh$ channel belongs to
one of three diagrammatic classes,
\begin{equation}
	\Gamma^{\eh,(2)}L_0\Gamma^{\eh,(1)}
	\qquad
	\Gamma^{\eh,(1)}L_0\Gamma^{\eh,(2)}
	\qquad
	\Gamma^{\eh,(1)}L_0\Gamma^{\eh,(1)}L_0\Gamma^{\eh,(1)}
	\label{eq:third_classes_eh}
\end{equation}
and the corresponding statement holds in the $\pp$ channel.
This topological classification avoids double counting between parquet
channels, but it does not by itself justify restricting the internal orbital
sums of the first two classes to the conventional central pair spaces.
Because $\Gamma^{(2)}$ is frequency dependent, the same-occupancy $vv/oo$
sectors in the $\eh$ channel and mixed sectors in the $\pp$ channel can be
nonzero, as demonstrated by Eq.~\eqref{eq:vv_dynamic_counterexample}.

Consequently, the $URV$ expressions derived above are the complete
contributions within the standard $\ph$ or $\ee/\hh$ central spaces, while
the unrestricted third-order vertex additionally contains the explicit
remainders $\Delta_{L/R}^{r,(3)}$.
Parquet crossing and reconstruction remain valid once these remainders are
included; they cannot recover terms that have already been discarded by an
orbital-space projection inside a primitive channel.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{Recipe through third order}
\label{sec:recipe}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

Starting from the complete second-order construction summarized in
Sec.~\ref{sec:recipe_second}, the third-order analysis proceeds in four
steps for each primitive channel $r=\eh,\pp$.

First, obtain the second-order corrections to the left and right external
vertices from the corresponding second-order channel-irreducible kernel.
Their poles remain confined to the $(N-1)$ $\hhp$ and $(N+1)$ $\pph$
sectors when the central pair is projected onto the conventional channel
space.

Second, construct the first-order correction to the central matrix,
$M^{r,(1)}$, and hence
\begin{equation}
	R^{r,(1)}
	=
	R^{r,(0)}M^{r,(1)}R^{r,(0)}
	\label{eq:recipe_R_first}
\end{equation}
The central resolvent describes $N$-electron propagation for $r=\eh$ and
$(N\pm2)$-electron propagation for $r=\pp$.

Third, evaluate the complementary internal orbital sectors before carrying
out any orbital-space restriction: $vv$ and $oo$ for the direct $\eh$
convolution, and mixed occupied--virtual pairs for the $\pp$ convolution.
Collect them in $\Delta_L^{r,(3)}$ and $\Delta_R^{r,(3)}$.

Fourth, assemble
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
	\ii\Phi^{r,(3)}
	={}&
	U^{r,(2)}R^{r,(0)}V^{r,(1)}
	+
	U^{r,(1)}R^{r,(0)}V^{r,(2)}
	\\
	&+
	U^{r,(1)}R^{r,(1)}V^{r,(1)}
	+
	\Delta_L^{r,(3)}
	+
	\Delta_R^{r,(3)}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:recipe_third_general}
\end{equation}
Generate $\Phi^{\teh,(3)}$ by crossing and reconstruct
\begin{align}
	\Gamma^{\eh,(3)}
	&=
	\Phi^{\teh,(3)}+\Phi^{\pp,(3)}
	\\
	F^{(3)}
	&=
	\Phi^{\eh,(3)}+\Phi^{\teh,(3)}+\Phi^{\pp,(3)}
\end{align}
The resulting expressions are complete only when the complementary-sector
remainders are retained or represented in an enlarged auxiliary space.

The two primitive channels retain the common particle-number architecture
\begin{align}
	\Phi^\eh
	&:\quad
	N\pm1\longrightarrow N\longrightarrow N\pm1
	\label{eq:sector_eh}
	\\
	\Phi^\pp
	&:\quad
	N\pm1\longrightarrow N\pm2\longrightarrow N\pm1
	\label{eq:sector_pp}
\end{align}
with common $(N\pm1)$ external-vertex pole spaces and channel-specific
central resolvents.
The $\Delta_{L/R}^{r,(3)}$ terms quantify precisely where this restricted
architecture must be enlarged to represent the unrestricted third-order
three-frequency vertex.
