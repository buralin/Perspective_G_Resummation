\title{From Parquet Theory to Non-Mosaic Coupled-Cluster Doubles}

%\author{Pierre-Fran\c{c}ois %\surname{Loos}}
 %   \email{loos@irsamc.ups-tlse.fr}
 %   \affiliation{\LCPQ}


\begin{abstract}
Starting from the correlation-channel organization of parquet theory, we
construct a static bare-kernel representation whose prescribed
common-amplitude combination reproduces the non-mosaic coupled-cluster
doubles (CCD) residual. We first isolate the fixed-kernel vertex problem
and resum repeated scattering within each channel.
For a canonical Hartree--Fock reference, the individual static channel
blocks yield Bethe--Salpeter matrices and associated Riccati equations.
We evaluate the residuals of these equations using three matrix
arrangements of one antisymmetric doubles tensor, retain the common source
and free term once, and sum the antisymmetrized scattering contributions.
Their explicit orbital expansion reproduces the ring, crossed-ring, and
ladder terms with the CCD signs and coefficients. This equality holds for
an arbitrary antisymmetric doubles tensor under the stated assumptions.
The result establishes a residual identity for the specified construction;
it does not derive the common-amplitude prescription from the coupled
vertex equations.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Parquet theory and coupled-cluster doubles (CCD) organize two-body correlation
through different quantities. The former decomposes a four-point vertex into
three correlation channels, whereas the latter determines a single
antisymmetric doubles amplitude. The parquet formulation and its static
kernel approximation have recently been developed for molecular
systems.\cite{Marie2025} Relating its channel blocks to CCD requires specifying
both their matrix representation and their combination in a common
fermionic amplitude space.

Connections between individual correlation channels and CCD are well
established. Scuseria, Henderson, and Sorensen demonstrated the relation
between particle-hole random-phase approximation (RPA) and ring
CCD.\cite{Scuseria2008} The corresponding connection between particle-particle
RPA and ladder CCD was established by Scuseria, Henderson, and Bulik and
independently by Peng \textit{et al.}\cite{Scuseria2013,Peng2013}
In Secs.~III.C and III.D of Ref.~\onlinecite{Scuseria2013}, the ring,
ladder, and crossed-ring contributions are combined through common
fermionic amplitudes. That analysis explicitly addresses the different
composite-index arrangements and repeated driving terms, with mosaic
contributions incorporated into an effective one-body Hamiltonian.
The interpretation of CCD through these three channels therefore provides
the starting point for the present comparison. Related work connects RPA
excitation energies to approximate equation-of-motion ring
CCD,\cite{Berkelbach2018} and recasts the $GW$ and Bethe--Salpeter equations
as nonlinear coupled-cluster-like equations.\cite{Quintero2022} These
connections provide context for the matrix and residual representations
used here; our comparison concerns the combined non-mosaic doubles residual.

Here, we organize this correspondence from the fixed bare-kernel channel
blocks of the parquet vertex equations, using a canonical Hartree--Fock (HF)
reference. We isolate repeated scattering within each channel and construct
its static Bethe--Salpeter equation (BSE) matrix and Riccati equation. The
specific contribution is to formulate and verify the maps and counting
conventions that express the established three-channel interpretation of
CCD in a common doubles space. Index-arrangement maps specify the channel amplitudes,
antisymmetrization maps enforce the doubles symmetries, and a
common/scattering split identifies the source and free term retained once.
This formulation separates the pair-contraction factors, the normalization
of the antisymmetrization maps, and the counting of the common residual.
The comparison uses HF orbital energies and excludes mosaic terms.

An explicit orbital expansion establishes that this prescribed combination
reproduces the non-mosaic CCD residual for an arbitrary antisymmetric doubles
tensor. The result connects the static channel blocks to the CCD signs and
coefficients while distinguishing the exact fixed-kernel rearrangement from
the subsequent amplitude-level prescription. Equivalence of the coupled
vertex and amplitude solution sets is not established.

The remainder of this work is organized as follows.
Section~\ref{sec:parquet} introduces the channel equations and their
fixed-kernel rearrangement. Section~\ref{sec:multifrequency_static} gives
the static BSE matrices and the Riccati decoupling condition.
Section~\ref{sec:common_t} constructs the common-amplitude residual, whose
orbital expansion is compared with non-mosaic CCD in
Sec.~\ref{sec:ccd_recovery}. Conclusions are drawn in Sec.~\ref{sec:scope}.
Appendices~\ref{app:nonlinear_parquet} and \ref{app:weighted_projection}
collect the direct-sum block forms of the channel equations and the
weighted-projection representation, respectively.

\section{Fixed-kernel channel equations}
\label{sec:parquet}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

We first introduce the three-channel vertex decomposition and distinguish
its self-consistent kernel relations from the fixed-kernel problem used
below. Throughout this work, $i,j,k,l$ denote occupied spin orbitals,
$a,b,c,d$ virtual spin orbitals, and $p,q,r,s$ arbitrary spin orbitals.
The Greek indices $\alpha,\beta\in\{\eh,\teh,\pp\}$ label correlation
channels. The explicit orbital expressions use a finite basis of real
canonical HF spin orbitals and real amplitudes. Antisymmetrized
electron-repulsion integrals (ERIs) are written as $\mel{pq}{}{rs}$. The doubles tensor $\bt$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
 t_{ij}^{ab}=-t_{ji}^{ab}=-t_{ij}^{ba}=t_{ji}^{ba}
 \label{eq:t_antisymmetry}
\end{equation}
Channel matrices and doubles tensors are bold, their components are plain,
and direct-sum operators are blackboard bold. Four-point vertices and
abstract index-arrangement maps retain plain symbols. We use $\cdot$ for
matrix multiplication or map application, $\dagger$ for an adjoint, and
$\mathsf T$ for a transpose. Identity matrices are denoted by
$\bI$; $1$ denotes a vertex-space identity and $\mathbb I_{\oplus}$ a
direct-sum identity. We write $\ii^2=-1$ and use $\delta_{pq}$ for the
Kronecker delta. The inner products defining adjoints of the
index-arrangement maps are specified in Appendix~\ref{app:weighted_projection}.

\subsection{Vertex decomposition}

A connected four-point diagram that is two-particle reducible belongs to one
of three channels: direct electron-hole ($\eh$), transverse electron-hole
($\teh$), or particle-particle ($\pp$). Let $F$ be the full four-point vertex. Its
parquet decomposition reads\cite{Marie2025}
\begin{equation}
    F
    =
    \Lambda
    +
    \Phi^{\eh}
    +
    \Phi^{\teh}
    +
    \Phi^{\pp}
    \label{eq:parquet_decomposition}
\end{equation}
where $\Lambda$ is fully two-particle irreducible and $\Phi^{\alpha}$ contains all
diagrams reducible in channel $\alpha$. At fixed external labels and frequencies,
these reducibility classes are disjoint. Crossing relates the two
electron-hole classes by permuting external arguments; it does not count
the same labelled diagram twice in Eq.~\eqref{eq:parquet_decomposition}.
This distinction holds also when the interaction is
antisymmetrized.\cite{Marie2025} The corresponding channel-irreducible vertices are
\begin{align}
    \Gamma^{\eh}
    &=\Lambda+\Phi^{\teh}+\Phi^{\pp}
    \\
    \Gamma^{\teh}
    &=\Lambda+\Phi^{\eh}+\Phi^{\pp}
    \\
    \Gamma^{\pp}
    &=\Lambda+\Phi^{\eh}+\Phi^{\teh}
    \label{eq:parquet_kernels}
\end{align}
Let $L_0^{\alpha}$ denote the product of two one-particle Green's functions in
channel $\alpha$, before repeated two-particle scattering is included. In the exact
parquet equations these one-particle propagators are dressed; the subscript
$0$ does not here imply an HF approximation. Their HF specialization is
introduced in Sec.~\ref{sec:multifrequency_static}. The reducible component
is generated by its BSE,
\begin{equation}
    \Phi^{\alpha}
    =
    \Gamma^{\alpha} \circ L_0^{\alpha} \circ F
    \label{eq:Phi_BSE_channel}
\end{equation}
Here $\circ$ denotes the convolution in the channel fixed by its operands.
It includes the frequency integration, external-leg permutation, fermionic
sign, and pair-space metric. In particular, the
transverse-$\eh$ convolution carries the crossing sign and the $\pp$
convolution carries the antisymmetric-pair factor $1/2$.

\subsection{Fixed bare channel kernels}
\label{sec:bare_kernel_stage}

We now hold both the channel kernels and the one-particle propagators fixed.
The parquet approximation sets $\Lambda=v$, where $v$ is the bare
antisymmetrized interaction, while retaining the self-consistent kernel
relations. Here we additionally replace those relations by
\begin{equation}
    \Gamma^{\eh}=\Gamma^{\teh}=\Gamma^{\pp}=v
    \label{eq:bare_channel_kernels}
\end{equation}
This defines a fixed bare-kernel three-channel model obtained from the
parquet channel equations. It replaces the channel-irreducible vertex
relations above; it is not the self-consistent parquet approximation.
The exact parquet classification alone therefore does not establish the
diagrammatic completeness or multiplicities of this modified model.
The reconstruction and component BSEs give
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
    F&=v+\Phi^{\eh}+\Phi^{\teh}+\Phi^{\pp}
    \\
    \Phi^{\alpha}&=v\circ L_0^{\alpha}\circ F
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:bare_kernel_components}
\end{equation}
The three components share the reconstructed vertex $F$, so
Eq.~\eqref{eq:bare_kernel_components} remains a coupled problem and retains
repeated scattering to all orders. It differs from solving three independent
equations $F_{\alpha}=v+v\circ L_0^{\alpha}\circ F_{\alpha}$. The direct-sum block notation is
collected in Appendix~\ref{app:nonlinear_parquet}.

\subsection{Same-channel resummation}
\label{sec:faddeev_rearrangement}

To isolate repeated scattering within a given channel, define the map
acting on a four-point test vertex $X$ by
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal K_{\alpha}\cdot X
    =
    \Gamma^{\alpha}\circ L_0^{\alpha}\circ X
    \label{eq:K_r_def}
\end{equation}
Using $F=\Lambda+\Phi^{\alpha}+\sum_{\beta\ne\alpha}\Phi^{\beta}$ in
$\Phi^{\alpha}=\mathcal K_{\alpha}\cdot F$ gives
\begin{equation}
    (1-\mathcal K_{\alpha})\cdot\Phi^{\alpha}
    =
    \mathcal K_{\alpha}
    \cdot\qty(
        \Lambda+\sum_{\beta\ne\alpha}\Phi^{\beta}
    )
    \label{eq:faddeev_before_resum}
\end{equation}
Whenever $1-\mathcal K_{\alpha}$ is invertible, define the resummed channel map
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal R_{\alpha}
    =
    (1-\mathcal K_{\alpha})^{-1}\cdot\mathcal K_{\alpha}
    \label{eq:channel_resummed_map}
\end{equation}
The expansion $\mathcal R_{\alpha}=\mathcal K_{\alpha}+\mathcal K_{\alpha}^2+\cdots$ is
a formal scattering series; equality to its sum requires convergence of the
Neumann series, not merely invertibility of $1-\mathcal K_{\alpha}$.
The component equations become
\begin{equation}
    \Phi^{\alpha}
    =
    \mathcal R_{\alpha}
    \cdot\qty(
        \Lambda+\sum_{\beta\ne\alpha}\Phi^{\beta}
    )
    \label{eq:faddeev_component}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:faddeev_component} is an exact rearrangement of the
fixed-kernel component BSE, subject to the stated invertibility condition.
Repeated scattering in channel $\alpha$ is contained in $\mathcal R_{\alpha}$, while
$\Lambda+\sum_{\beta\ne\alpha}\Phi^{\beta}$ supplies its source and coupling to the other
channels. This separation has the algebraic structure of a Faddeev
rearrangement; the corresponding direct-sum form is given in
Appendix~\ref{app:nonlinear_parquet}.

For the bare-kernel problem,
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal K_{\alpha}^{\rm bk}\cdot X
    =
    v\circ L_0^{\alpha}\circ X
    \qquad
    \mathcal R_{\alpha}^{\rm bk}
    =
    (1-\mathcal K_{\alpha}^{\rm bk})^{-1}\cdot\mathcal K_{\alpha}^{\rm bk}
    \label{eq:bare_channel_resolvent}
\end{equation}
The following static construction represents these same-channel blocks.
The interchannel source in Eq.~\eqref{eq:faddeev_component} remains part of
the coupled vertex problem. The common-amplitude prescription introduced
later is specified separately, rather than derived from this source.

\section{Static channel reduction and Riccati decoupling}
\label{sec:multifrequency_static}

For a static kernel and an HF reference, the internal fermionic frequency
in a same-channel block can be integrated analytically. We give the resulting
BSE matrices for the direct and transverse electron-hole channels, followed
by the particle-particle channel, and derive their common Riccati form.

The coupled equation~\eqref{eq:faddeev_component} retains the natural
bosonic frequencies $\Omega_{\eh}$, $\Omega_{\teh}$, and $\Omega_{\pp}$
of its three channel blocks. The following construction gives a matrix
representation for each block separately; it does not provide a single
one-frequency Riccati matrix for the full four-point problem.

Let $\epsilon_p$ be the HF orbital energies and $G_0$ the diagonal
reference Green's function. Its occupied and virtual entries are
\begin{align}
    G_i(\omega)
    &=
    \frac{1}{\omega-\epsilon_i-\ii 0^+}
    \\
    G_a(\omega)
    &=
    \frac{1}{\omega-\epsilon_a+\ii 0^+}
    \label{eq:HF_G_static_reduction}
\end{align}

Here $\omega$ and $\nu$ are fermionic frequencies, $\Omega_{\alpha}$ is the
bosonic channel frequency, and $0^+$ is a positive infinitesimal specifying
time ordering. We write $\Omega$ when the channel is fixed. In the matrix
resolvents below, the causal prescription inherited from the free propagators
is understood. The amplitude matrix $\bT^{\alpha}$ maps the forward sector into the
backward sector; its Riccati condition is derived in
Sec.~\ref{sec:generic_decoupling}.

\subsection{Static channel contraction and propagator}
\label{sec:static_contraction}

We make the reduced same-channel contraction explicit before evaluating
its orbital blocks. Let $\bV^{\alpha}$ be the bare interaction matrix in the
channel's forward/backward representation, $\bL_0^{\alpha}(\Omega)$ its free
pair propagator, and $\bcalV^{\alpha}(\Omega)$ the vertex obtained by repeated
scattering in that channel alone. The entries of $\bV^{\alpha}$ and $\bL_0^{\alpha}$
are specified below. For channel matrices $\bX$ and $\bY$, we use the
following reduced contraction convention after the internal frequency
integration:
\begin{equation}
 \bX\circ\bL_0^{\alpha}\circ\bY
 =-\ii\sigma_{\alpha}\,\bX\cdot\bL_0^{\alpha}\cdot\bY,
 \qquad
 \sigma_{\eh}=\sigma_{\teh}=1,\quad \sigma_{\pp}=-1
 \label{eq:static_channel_contraction}
\end{equation}
Here $\sigma_{\alpha}$ accounts for the opposite overall phases of the free
electron-hole and particle-particle propagators in our time-ordered
convention. External-leg ordering and crossing signs are included in
$\bV^{\alpha}$; each unrestricted $\pp$ pair contraction in the matrix product
carries the factor $1/2$ defined in Eq.~\eqref{eq:pair_product}. This
convention, together with the orbital blocks below, specifies the static
same-channel representation used here. The full coupled vertex problem
retains the changes of external frequency variables described in
Sec.~\ref{sec:parquet}.

With the bare source, the same-channel vertex BSE becomes
\begin{equation}
 \bcalV^{\alpha}
 =\bV^{\alpha}-\ii\sigma_{\alpha}\,\bV^{\alpha}\cdot\bL_0^{\alpha}\cdot\bcalV^{\alpha}
 \label{eq:static_vertex_BSE}
\end{equation}
The frequency argument is suppressed here and below when unambiguous.
This equation resums the same insertions as
Eq.~\eqref{eq:bare_channel_resolvent}: on a channel test matrix $\bX$,
$\mathcal R_{\alpha}^{\rm bk}$ is represented by
$\bX\mapsto-\ii\sigma_{\alpha}\bcalV^{\alpha}\cdot\bL_0^{\alpha}\cdot\bX$.
Attaching the two free propagators gives the interacting pair propagator,
\begin{align}
 \bL^{\alpha}
 &=\bL_0^{\alpha}-\ii\sigma_{\alpha}\,\bL_0^{\alpha}\cdot\bcalV^{\alpha}\cdot\bL_0^{\alpha}
 \nonumber\\
 &=\bL_0^{\alpha}-\ii\sigma_{\alpha}\,\bL_0^{\alpha}\cdot\bV^{\alpha}\cdot\bL^{\alpha}
 \label{eq:static_propagator_BSE}
\end{align}
Consequently, wherever the inverses exist,
\begin{equation}
 (\bL^{\alpha})^{-1}=(\bL_0^{\alpha})^{-1}+\ii\sigma_{\alpha}\bV^{\alpha}
 \label{eq:static_inverse_BSE}
\end{equation}
The frequency integration below gives
$\bL_0^{\alpha}=\ii\sigma_{\alpha}(\Omega\bEta^{\alpha}-\bcalH_0^{\alpha})^{-1}$,
where $\bEta^{\alpha}=\operatorname{diag}(\bI,-\bI)$ is the sector metric and
$\bcalH_0^{\alpha}$ contains the free orbital-energy blocks. Thus
Eq.~\eqref{eq:static_inverse_BSE} yields
\begin{equation}
 \bL^{\alpha}=\ii\sigma_{\alpha}(\Omega\bEta^{\alpha}-\bcalH^{\alpha})^{-1},
 \qquad \bcalH^{\alpha}=\bcalH_0^{\alpha}+\bV^{\alpha}
 \label{eq:static_interacting_resolvent}
\end{equation}
Multiplying the homogeneous pole equation by $\bEta^{\alpha}$ gives the BSE
generator $\bH^{\alpha}=\bEta^{\alpha}\cdot\bcalH^{\alpha}$. Its Riccati equation follows
from the block transformation in Sec.~\ref{sec:generic_decoupling}.

\subsection{Direct electron-hole channel}

The forward sector represents particle-hole excitations and the backward
sector their deexcitations. Both sectors use occupied--virtual composite
labels; the block position specifies the direction. The corresponding
independent propagators are
\begin{align}
    L_{0,ia}^{\eh}(\Omega)
    &=
    \int\frac{\dd{\nu}}{2\pi}
    G_i(\nu)G_a(\nu+\Omega)
    =
    \frac{\ii}{\Omega-(\epsilon_a-\epsilon_i)+\ii 0^+}
    \\
    L_{0,ai}^{\eh}(\Omega)
    &=
    \int\frac{\dd{\nu}}{2\pi}
    G_a(\nu)G_i(\nu+\Omega)
    =
    -\frac{\ii}{\Omega+(\epsilon_a-\epsilon_i)-\ii 0^+}
    \label{eq:L0_eh_integrated}
\end{align}
so that the two sectors contain poles at $\pm(\epsilon_a-\epsilon_i)$.
Defining
\begin{equation*}
    (\bA_0^{\eh})_{ia,jb}
    =
    (\epsilon_a-\epsilon_i)\delta_{ij}\delta_{ab}
    \qquad
    \bEta^{\eh}
    =
    \begin{pmatrix}\bI&0\\0&-\bI\end{pmatrix}
\end{equation*}
the free propagator can be written as
\begin{equation}
    \bL_0^{\eh}(\Omega)
    =
    \ii
    \qty(
        \Omega\bEta^{\eh}-\bcalH_0^{\eh}
    )^{-1}
    \qquad
    \bcalH_0^{\eh}
    =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        \bA_0^{\eh}&0\\
        0&(\bA_0^{\eh})^{\dagger}
    \end{pmatrix}
    \label{eq:L0_eh_resolvent}
\end{equation}
For a bare static kernel, define
\begin{align}
    (\bK_A^{\eh})_{ia,jb}
    &=\mel{aj}{}{ib}
    \\
    (\bB^{\eh})_{ia,jb}
    &=\mel{ab}{}{ij}
    \\
    \bA^{\eh}
    &=\bA_0^{\eh}+\bK_A^{\eh}
\end{align}
These entries give the bare interaction block
\begin{equation}
 \bV^{\eh}=\begin{pmatrix}
 \bK_A^{\eh}&\bB^{\eh}\\
 (\bB^{\eh})^{\dagger}&(\bK_A^{\eh})^{\dagger}
 \end{pmatrix}
 \label{eq:bare_static_eh_block}
\end{equation}
Substitution into Eq.~\eqref{eq:static_inverse_BSE}, with
$\sigma_{\eh}=1$, gives the interacting propagator and its matrix,
\begin{equation}
    \bL^{\eh}(\Omega)
    =
    \ii
    \qty(
        \Omega\bEta^{\eh}-\bcalH^{\eh}
    )^{-1}
    \qquad
    \bcalH^{\eh}
    =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        \bA^{\eh}&\bB^{\eh}\\
        (\bB^{\eh})^{\dagger}&(\bA^{\eh})^{\dagger}
    \end{pmatrix}
    \label{eq:L_eh_resolvent}
\end{equation}
The corresponding BSE generator is
\begin{equation}
    \bH^{\eh}
    =
    \bEta^{\eh}\cdot\bcalH^{\eh}
    =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        \bA^{\eh}&\bB^{\eh}\\
        -(\bB^{\eh})^{\dagger}&-(\bA^{\eh})^{\dagger}
    \end{pmatrix}
    \label{eq:H_eh_from_frequency}
\end{equation}
Together, the integrated free propagator and the specified interaction
blocks define the static BSE matrix. The transfer frequency $\Omega$ has
not been set to zero.
The free part of the Riccati equation is
$(\bA_0^{\eh})^{\dagger}\cdot\bT^{\eh}
+\bT^{\eh}\cdot \bA_0^{\eh}$. Its relation to the usual doubles energy
difference follows directly after arranging the indices as described in
Sec.~\ref{sec:common_t}.

\subsection{Transverse electron-hole channel}

The transverse channel is another electron-hole problem with crossed
external labels. Using the composite ordering $(ib,ja)$, define
\begin{align}
    (\bA_0^{\teh})_{ib,kc}
    &=(\epsilon_b-\epsilon_i)\delta_{ik}\delta_{bc}
    \\
    (\bK_A^{\teh})_{ib,kc}
    &=\mel{bk}{}{ic}
    \\
    (\bB^{\teh})_{ib,ja}
    &=\mel{ba}{}{ij}
    =-\mel{ab}{}{ij}
    \\
    \bA^{\teh}
    &=\bA_0^{\teh}+\bK_A^{\teh}
\end{align}
The forward/backward sectors retain the excitation/deexcitation assignment
of the direct channel; crossing changes the external pairing, not this
sector assignment. In this ordering the bare block is
\begin{equation}
 \bV^{\teh}=\begin{pmatrix}
 \bK_A^{\teh}&-\bB^{\teh}\\
 -(\bB^{\teh})^{\dagger}&(\bK_A^{\teh})^{\dagger}
 \end{pmatrix}
 \label{eq:bare_static_teh_block}
\end{equation}
The off-diagonal signs implement the fermionic exchange discussed in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:crossed_operator_sign}. With $\sigma_{\teh}=1$ and
$\bcalH_0^{\teh}=\operatorname{diag}(\bA_0^{\teh},
(\bA_0^{\teh})^{\dagger})$, the same propagator construction gives
\begin{equation}
    \bH^{\teh}
    =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        \bA^{\teh}&-\bB^{\teh}\\
        (\bB^{\teh})^{\dagger}&-(\bA^{\teh})^{\dagger}
    \end{pmatrix}
    \label{eq:H_teh_from_crossing}
\end{equation}
The free Riccati combination is
$(\bA_0^{\teh})^{\dagger}\cdot\bT^{\teh}
+\bT^{\teh}\cdot \bA_0^{\teh}$. After arranging the crossed indices as
described in Sec.~\ref{sec:common_t}, both the free term and the bare driver
have the same fermionic doubles orientation as in the other two channels.

\subsection{Particle-particle channel}

Similarly, the virtual--virtual and occupied--occupied independent
particle-particle propagators are
\begin{align}
    L_{0,ab}^{\pp}(\Omega)
    &=
    \int\frac{\dd{\nu}}{2\pi}
    G_a(\nu)G_b(\Omega-\nu)
    =
    -\frac{\ii}{\Omega-(\epsilon_a+\epsilon_b)+\ii 0^+}
    \\
    L_{0,ij}^{\pp}(\Omega)
    &=
    \int\frac{\dd{\nu}}{2\pi}
    G_i(\nu)G_j(\Omega-\nu)
    =
    \frac{\ii}{\Omega-(\epsilon_i+\epsilon_j)-\ii 0^+}
    \label{eq:L0_pp_integrated}
\end{align}
The forward sector is the $vv$ pair space and the backward sector the $oo$
pair space.
We use unrestricted antisymmetric pair labels.  The antisymmetric identity is
\begin{equation*}
    \delta^{\rm A}_{pq,rs}
    =
    \delta_{pr}\delta_{qs}-\delta_{ps}\delta_{qr}
\end{equation*}
and matrix multiplication over an unrestricted pair space contains a factor
$1/2$,
\begin{equation}
    [\bX\cdot\bY]_{pq,tu}
    =
    \frac12\sum_{rs}[\bX]_{pq,rs}[\bY]_{rs,tu}
    \label{eq:pair_product}
\end{equation}
With this convention, $\delta^{\rm A}$ is the identity operator on
antisymmetric tensors.

Equivalently, one may work with ordered pairs $a<b$ and $i<j$; then the
explicit $1/2$ factors disappear from the pair-space matrix products.  The
unrestricted convention is retained here because it maps directly onto the
standard spin-orbital CCD sums below.

The free and interaction blocks are
\begin{align}
    (\bC_0^{\pp})_{ab,cd}
    &=(\epsilon_a+\epsilon_b)\delta^{\rm A}_{ab,cd}
    \\
    (\bD_0^{\pp})_{ij,kl}
    &=-(\epsilon_i+\epsilon_j)\delta^{\rm A}_{ij,kl}
    \\
    (\bK_C^{\pp})_{ab,cd}
    &=\mel{ab}{}{cd}
    \\
    (\bK_D^{\pp})_{ij,kl}
    &=\mel{ij}{}{kl}
    \\
    (\bB^{\pp})_{ab,ij}
    &=\mel{ab}{}{ij}
\end{align}
We then write
\begin{equation*}
    \bC^{\pp}=\bC_0^{\pp}+\bK_C^{\pp}
    \qquad
    \bD^{\pp}=\bD_0^{\pp}+\bK_D^{\pp}
    \qquad
    \bEta^{\pp}=\begin{pmatrix}\bI&0\\0&-\bI\end{pmatrix}
\end{equation*}
The bare interaction matrix is
\begin{equation}
 \bV^{\pp}=\begin{pmatrix}
 \bK_C^{\pp}&\bB^{\pp}\\
 (\bB^{\pp})^{\dagger}&\bK_D^{\pp}
 \end{pmatrix}
 \label{eq:bare_static_pp_block}
\end{equation}
Using $\sigma_{\pp}=-1$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:static_inverse_BSE} gives
$(\bL^{\pp})^{-1}=(\bL_0^{\pp})^{-1}-\ii\bV^{\pp}$.
The free and interacting propagators therefore have the resolvent forms
\begin{align}
    \bL_0^{\pp}(\Omega)
    &=-\ii\qty(\Omega\bEta^{\pp}-\bcalH_0^{\pp})^{-1}
    \\
    \bL^{\pp}(\Omega)
    &=-\ii\qty(\Omega\bEta^{\pp}-\bcalH^{\pp})^{-1}
\end{align}
with
\begin{equation*}
    \bcalH_0^{\pp}
    =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        \bC_0^{\pp}&0\\
        0&\bD_0^{\pp}
    \end{pmatrix}
    \qquad
    \bcalH^{\pp}
    =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        \bC^{\pp}&\bB^{\pp}\\
        (\bB^{\pp})^{\dagger}&\bD^{\pp}
    \end{pmatrix}
\end{equation*}
The corresponding BSE generator is
\begin{equation}
    \bH^{\pp}
    =
    \bEta^{\pp}\cdot\bcalH^{\pp}
    =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        \bC^{\pp}&\bB^{\pp}\\
        -(\bB^{\pp})^{\dagger}&-\bD^{\pp}
    \end{pmatrix}
    \label{eq:H_pp_from_frequency}
\end{equation}
The diagonal blocks are Hermitian under our real-orbital assumptions.
The free Riccati combination is
$\bD_0^{\pp}\cdot\bT^{\pp}+\bT^{\pp}\cdot \bC_0^{\pp}$.
After arranging the indices as described in Sec.~\ref{sec:common_t}, it gives
the same doubles energy difference as in the electron-hole channels. Each
$\pp$ pair contraction contributes a factor $1/2$; two such contractions
contribute $1/4$. The separate amplitude-counting weight is defined in
Appendix~\ref{app:weighted_projection}.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Riccati equation as a block-decoupling condition}
\label{sec:generic_decoupling}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

Consider a generic static BSE matrix partitioned into forward ($P$) and
backward ($Q$) sectors,
\begin{equation*}
    \bH^{\alpha}=
    \begin{pmatrix}
        \bH_{PP}^{\alpha}&\bH_{PQ}^{\alpha}\\
        \bH_{QP}^{\alpha}&\bH_{QQ}^{\alpha}
    \end{pmatrix}
\end{equation*}
Let $\bP_{\alpha}=(\bI\;0)^{\mathsf T}$ include a forward vector into the
full channel space and $\bQ_{\alpha}=(0\;\bI)$ extract its backward part.
Thus $\bQ_{\alpha}\cdot\bM\cdot\bP_{\alpha}$ is the $QP$ block of a channel matrix
$\bM$. Identity blocks have the dimensions of their respective sectors;
in the $\pp$ channel all products use Eq.~\eqref{eq:pair_product}.
For an arbitrary forward-to-backward matrix $\bT^{\alpha}$ define
\begin{equation}
    \bcalT_{\alpha}[\bT^{\alpha}]=
    \begin{pmatrix}
        0&0\\
        \bT^{\alpha}&0
    \end{pmatrix}
    \label{eq:nilpotent_generator}
\end{equation}
We suppress the argument of $\bcalT_{\alpha}$ when it is clear. Because $\bcalT_{\alpha}^2=0$,
\begin{equation}
    e^{\bcalT_{\alpha}}
    =\bI+\bcalT_{\alpha}
    =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        \bI&0\\
        \bT^{\alpha}&\bI
    \end{pmatrix}
    \label{eq:S_exp}
\end{equation}
With $[\bX,\bY]=\bX\cdot\bY-\bY\cdot\bX$, the resulting channel transformation contains only
constant, linear, and quadratic powers of the channel amplitude,
\begin{equation}
    e^{-\bcalT_{\alpha}}\cdot \bH^{\alpha}\cdot e^{\bcalT_{\alpha}}
    =
    \bH^{\alpha}+\comm{\bH^{\alpha}}{\bcalT_{\alpha}}
    +\frac12\comm{\comm{\bH^{\alpha}}{\bcalT_{\alpha}}}{\bcalT_{\alpha}}
    \label{eq:channel_BCH}
\end{equation}

Substituting this transformation into the $QP$ block gives
\begin{align}
    \bQ_{\alpha}\cdot e^{-\bcalT_{\alpha}}\cdot \bH^{\alpha}\cdot e^{\bcalT_{\alpha}}\cdot \bP_{\alpha}
    ={}&
    \bH_{QP}^{\alpha}
    +\bH_{QQ}^{\alpha}\cdot \bT^{\alpha}
    -\bT^{\alpha}\cdot \bH_{PP}^{\alpha}
    \nonumber\\
    &-\bT^{\alpha}\cdot \bH_{PQ}^{\alpha}\cdot \bT^{\alpha}
    \label{eq:generic_QP}
\end{align}
We call the polynomial expression in Eq.~\eqref{eq:generic_QP} the
channel Riccati residual. Setting it to zero gives the Riccati equation,
\begin{equation}
    \bQ_{\alpha}\cdot e^{-\bcalT_{\alpha}}\cdot \bH^{\alpha}\cdot e^{\bcalT_{\alpha}}\cdot \bP_{\alpha}=0
\label{eq:BSE_decoupling}
\end{equation}
A spectral interpretation requires that the selected invariant subspace
have an invertible projection onto the forward sector. The following
comparison uses the algebraic residual and does not select a spectral branch.

For comparison, let $\ket{\Phi_0}$ be the HF reference determinant,
$P=\ket{\Phi_0}\bra{\Phi_0}$ its projector, $Q_2$ the projector onto its
double excitations, and $\hat H$ the many-electron Hamiltonian. With fermionic
creation and annihilation operators $\cre{p}$ and $\ani{p}$, define
\begin{equation*}
 \hat T_2=\frac14\sum_{ijab}t_{ij}^{ab}\cre{a}\cre{b}\ani{j}\ani{i}
\end{equation*}
The full CCD doubles condition is\cite{Shavitt2009}
\begin{equation}
 Q_2\cdot e^{-\hat T_2}\cdot\hat H\cdot e^{\hat T_2}\cdot P=0
 \label{eq:parallel_decoupling}
\end{equation}
Both constructions project a similarity-transformed operator. They act on
different spaces: the BSE channel space and the many-electron space,
respectively. Equation~\eqref{eq:parallel_decoupling} is a structural comparison;
only the non-mosaic residual is recovered below.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{Common-amplitude construction}
\label{sec:common_t}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

We now specify how the channel Riccati residuals are combined. We evaluate
them using matrix arrangements of the same antisymmetric doubles tensor,
separate the common and scattering terms, and sum the antisymmetrized
scattering contributions while retaining the common term once. The amplitude
sought is a solution of this combined doubles equation; it is not required
to solve each isolated channel Riccati equation separately. Since the
static matrices are frequency independent, this identification concerns
orbital amplitudes and does not equate the distinct transfer frequencies
$\Omega_{\alpha}$ of the vertex problem.

\subsection{Index arrangements}

Let $\mathscr D$ denote the space of doubles tensors satisfying
Eq.~\eqref{eq:t_antisymmetry}. We represent the three channel amplitudes
using one $\bt\in\mathscr D$.

Let $\mathscr T_{\alpha}$ be the space of forward-to-backward channel matrices.
For the electron-hole channels these have occupied--virtual composite row and
column indices; for $\pp$ they have occupied-pair rows and virtual-pair
columns, with antisymmetry within each pair. The map
$J_{\alpha}:\mathscr D\to\mathscr T_{\alpha}$ arranges the four indices of $\bt$ into
the matrix form appropriate to channel $\alpha$,
\begin{equation}
    \bT^{\alpha}=J_{\alpha}\cdot\bt
    \label{eq:T_J_t}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{align}
    [\bT^{\eh}]_{ia,jb}
    &=[J_{\eh}\cdot\bt]_{ia,jb}
    =t_{ij}^{ab}
    \\
    [\bT^{\teh}]_{ib,ja}
    &=[J_{\teh}\cdot\bt]_{ib,ja}
    =t_{ij}^{ab}
    \\
    [\bT^{\pp}]_{ij,ab}
    &=[J_{\pp}\cdot\bt]_{ij,ab}
    =t_{ij}^{ab}
    \label{eq:common_t_maps}
\end{align}
Thus $J_{\alpha}$ changes how the four indices of $\bt$ are grouped into the
two matrix indices of $\bT^{\alpha}$.


\subsection{Mapping to doubles space}

Given a channel matrix, $U_{\alpha}$ arranges its entries in doubles order and
antisymmetrizes them. It is a left inverse of $J_{\alpha}$, not an inverse on
arbitrary channel matrices. With unrestricted orbital labels,
\begin{align}
    [U_{\eh}\cdot\bX]_{ij}^{ab}
    ={}&\frac14
    \qty(
        [\bX]_{ia,jb}+[\bX]_{jb,ia}-[\bX]_{ib,ja}-[\bX]_{ja,ib}
    )
    \label{eq:U_eh_map}
    \\
    [U_{\teh}\cdot\bX]_{ij}^{ab}
    ={}&\frac14
    \qty(
        [\bX]_{ib,ja}+[\bX]_{ja,ib}-[\bX]_{ia,jb}-[\bX]_{jb,ia}
    )
    \\
    [U_{\pp}\cdot\bX]_{ij}^{ab}
    ={}&\frac14
    \qty(
        [\bX]_{ij,ab}-[\bX]_{ji,ab}-[\bX]_{ij,ba}+[\bX]_{ji,ba}
    )
    \label{eq:U_maps}
\end{align}
Each parenthesized expression equals four times the original tensor when
the input is $J_{\alpha}\cdot\bt$. The factor $1/4$ is therefore fixed by requiring
that antisymmetrization leave an already antisymmetric amplitude unchanged.
It is a normalization of the specified four-term map, not an adjustable
channel weight. In particular,
\begin{equation}
    U_{\alpha}\cdot J_{\alpha}=\bI_{\mathscr D}
    \label{eq:left_inverse_identity}
\end{equation}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Common and scattering terms}
\label{sec:source_scattering}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The explicit maps above are sufficient for combining the channel residuals.
Their representation as weighted adjoints is given in
Appendix~\ref{app:weighted_projection}.

We prescribe a split $\bH^{\alpha}=\bH_c^{\alpha}+\bH_s^{\alpha}$: the common block
contains the free diagonal blocks and the bare $QP$ source, while the
scattering block contains the remaining interaction blocks. Retaining the
common residual once is motivated by the single source in the parquet
reconstruction, but is an additional prescription at the amplitude level.

The placement of the off-diagonal blocks follows their roles in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:generic_QP}. The $QP$ block supplies the amplitude-independent
source, whereas the $PQ$ block enters quadratically as
$-\bT^{\alpha}\cdot\bH_{PQ}^{\alpha}\cdot\bT^{\alpha}$. The free diagonal blocks supply the
orbital-energy difference; the interaction parts of the diagonal blocks
supply linear scattering. The split therefore isolates the source and free
term from the interaction-dependent terms involving amplitudes. Placing part
of the $PQ$ interaction in the common block would also place a quadratic
scattering term there and would change the stated common-term prescription.
This rationale fixes the block allocation used here without asserting
uniqueness among all possible amplitude-level constructions.

For a channel amplitude $\bT\in\mathscr T_{\alpha}$, define the scattering residual
\begin{equation}
 \bcalQ_{s,\alpha}[\bT]
 =-\bQ_{\alpha}\cdot e^{-\bcalT_{\alpha}[\bT]}\cdot\bH_s^{\alpha}
 \cdot e^{\bcalT_{\alpha}[\bT]}\cdot\bP_{\alpha}
 \label{eq:channel_scattering_residual}
\end{equation}
Its entries in doubles-index order, before antisymmetrization, define
$\bchi^{\alpha}[\bt]$ by
\begin{align}
 [\bchi^{\eh}]_{ij}^{ab}
 &=[\bcalQ_{s,\eh}[J_{\eh}\cdot\bt]]_{ia,jb}
 \\
 [\bchi^{\teh}]_{ij}^{ab}
 &=[\bcalQ_{s,\teh}[J_{\teh}\cdot\bt]]_{ib,ja}
 \\
 [\bchi^{\pp}]_{ij}^{ab}
 &=[\bcalQ_{s,\pp}[J_{\pp}\cdot\bt]]_{ij,ab}
 \label{eq:raw_channel_contributions}
\end{align}
These tensors need not individually belong to $\mathscr D$. Below,
$\bT^{\alpha}=J_{\alpha}\cdot\bt$ throughout.

\subsubsection{Direct electron-hole channel}

Split the direct-$\eh$ matrix of Eq.~\eqref{eq:H_eh_from_frequency} as
\begin{equation}
    \bH_c^{\eh}
    =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        \bA_0^{\eh}&0\\
        -(\bB^{\eh})^{\dagger}&-(\bA_0^{\eh})^{\dagger}
    \end{pmatrix}
    \qquad
    \bH_s^{\eh}
    =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        \bK_A^{\eh}&\bB^{\eh}\\
        0&-(\bK_A^{\eh})^{\dagger}
    \end{pmatrix}
    \label{eq:split_eh}
\end{equation}
The sign convention is chosen so that minus the transformed $QP$ block is the
usual Riccati residual.  The common block gives
\begin{equation}
    -\bQ_{\eh}\cdot e^{-\bcalT_{\eh}}
    \cdot \bH_c^{\eh}
    \cdot e^{\bcalT_{\eh}}\cdot \bP_{\eh}
    =(\bB^{\eh})^{\dagger}
    +(\bA_0^{\eh})^{\dagger}\cdot\bT^{\eh}
    +\bT^{\eh}\cdot \bA_0^{\eh}
    \label{eq:common_eh}
\end{equation}
whose entries in doubles-index order are
\begin{equation}
    \mel{ab}{}{ij}
    +
    (\epsilon_a+\epsilon_b-\epsilon_i-\epsilon_j)t_{ij}^{ab}
    \label{eq:common_tensor}
\end{equation}
The scattering block gives
\begin{equation}
    \bcalQ_{s,\eh}[\bT^{\eh}]
    =(\bK_A^{\eh})^{\dagger}\cdot\bT^{\eh}
    +\bT^{\eh}\cdot \bK_A^{\eh}
    +\bT^{\eh}\cdot \bB^{\eh}\cdot\bT^{\eh}
    \label{eq:Xi_eh_matrix}
\end{equation}
In doubles-index order, before antisymmetrization,
\begin{align}
    [\bchi^{\eh}]_{ij}^{ab}
    ={}&
    \sum_{kc}\mel{ak}{}{ic}t_{kj}^{cb}
    +
    \sum_{kc}\mel{bk}{}{jc}t_{ki}^{ca}
    \nonumber\\
    &+
    \sum_{klcd}
    t_{ki}^{ca}\mel{kl}{}{cd}t_{lj}^{db}
    \label{eq:Xi_eh_explicit}
\end{align}

\subsubsection{Transverse electron-hole channel}

The relative sign of the crossed channel is fixed by fermionic operator
algebra. With $E_{ai}=\cre{a}\ani{i}$ and the reference
$\ket{\Phi_0}$ defined above,
\begin{align}
    E_{ai}E_{bj}\ket{\Phi_0}
    &=
    \cre{a}\cre{b}\ani{j}\ani{i}\ket{\Phi_0}
    \\
    E_{bi}E_{aj}\ket{\Phi_0}
    &=
    -\cre{a}\cre{b}\ani{j}\ani{i}\ket{\Phi_0}
    \label{eq:crossed_operator_sign}
\end{align}
The transverse pairing therefore differs by one fermionic exchange.  In the
matrix convention below this crossing is represented jointly by the crossed
composite ordering and the explicit signs of the off-diagonal BSE blocks; no
additional sign is introduced when the result is mapped back to doubles space.

For the crossed matrix of Eq.~\eqref{eq:H_teh_from_crossing}, the split is
\begin{equation}
    \bH_c^{\teh}
    =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        \bA_0^{\teh}&0\\
        (\bB^{\teh})^{\dagger}&-(\bA_0^{\teh})^{\dagger}
    \end{pmatrix}
    \qquad
    \bH_s^{\teh}
    =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        \bK_A^{\teh}&-\bB^{\teh}\\
        0&-(\bK_A^{\teh})^{\dagger}
    \end{pmatrix}
    \label{eq:split_teh}
\end{equation}
With the crossed ordering, the transformed common block again gives
Eq.~\eqref{eq:common_tensor}. The crossed ordering and the explicit BSE sign
therefore give the same bare doubles source as in the direct channel. The
channel-scattering block gives
\begin{equation}
    \bcalQ_{s,\teh}[\bT^{\teh}]
    =
    (\bK_A^{\teh})^{\dagger}\cdot\bT^{\teh}
    +\bT^{\teh}\cdot \bK_A^{\teh}
    -\bT^{\teh}\cdot \bB^{\teh}\cdot\bT^{\teh}
    \label{eq:Xi_teh_matrix}
\end{equation}
which becomes
\begin{align}
    [\bchi^{\teh}]_{ij}^{ab}
    ={}&
    -\sum_{kc}\mel{kb}{}{ic}t_{kj}^{ac}
    -\sum_{kc}\mel{ka}{}{jc}t_{ki}^{bc}
    \nonumber\\
    &-
    \sum_{klcd}
    t_{ki}^{bc}\mel{kl}{}{cd}t_{lj}^{ad}
    \label{eq:Xi_teh_explicit}
\end{align}

\subsubsection{Particle-particle channel}

Using the $\pp$ orientation in Eq.~\eqref{eq:common_t_maps} and the
matrix in Eq.~\eqref{eq:H_pp_from_frequency}, the split is
\begin{equation}
    \bH_c^{\pp}
    =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        \bC_0^{\pp}&0\\
        -(\bB^{\pp})^{\dagger}&-\bD_0^{\pp}
    \end{pmatrix}
    \qquad
    \bH_s^{\pp}
    =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        \bK_C^{\pp}&\bB^{\pp}\\
        0&-\bK_D^{\pp}
    \end{pmatrix}
    \label{eq:split_pp}
\end{equation}
The common block gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:common_tensor}.  The
scattering block gives
\begin{equation}
    \bcalQ_{s,\pp}[\bT^{\pp}]
    =\bK_D^{\pp}\cdot\bT^{\pp}
    +\bT^{\pp}\cdot \bK_C^{\pp}
    +\bT^{\pp}\cdot \bB^{\pp}\cdot\bT^{\pp}
    \label{eq:Xi_pp_matrix}
\end{equation}
Using Eq.~\eqref{eq:pair_product},
\begin{align}
    [\bchi^{\pp}]_{ij}^{ab}
    ={}&
    \frac12\sum_{cd}\mel{ab}{}{cd}t_{ij}^{cd}
    +
    \frac12\sum_{kl}\mel{kl}{}{ij}t_{kl}^{ab}
    \nonumber\\
    &+
    \frac14\sum_{klcd}
    t_{ij}^{cd}\mel{cd}{}{kl}t_{kl}^{ab}
    \label{eq:Xi_pp_explicit}
\end{align}
The linear terms contain one pair contraction, giving $1/2$, and the
quadratic term contains two, giving $1/4$.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Combined doubles residual}
\label{sec:combined_decoupling}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

Applying $U_{\alpha}$ to the scattering residual gives its antisymmetrized
contribution to doubles space,
\begin{equation}
 \bXi^{\alpha}[\bt]=U_{\alpha}\cdot\bcalQ_{s,\alpha}[J_{\alpha}\cdot\bt]
 \label{eq:Xi_weighted_map}
\end{equation}
To see how this operation acts on the electron-hole terms, consider the
direct channel. Equation~\eqref{eq:U_eh_map} gives
\begin{align}
 [\bXi^{\eh}]_{ij}^{ab}
 ={}&\frac14\qty([\bchi^{\eh}]_{ij}^{ab}
                  +[\bchi^{\eh}]_{ji}^{ba})
 \nonumber\\
 &-\frac14\qty([\bchi^{\eh}]_{ij}^{ba}
                  +[\bchi^{\eh}]_{ji}^{ab})
 \label{eq:direct_antisymmetrization}
\end{align}
In Eq.~\eqref{eq:Xi_eh_explicit}, exchanging both occupied and virtual
indices leaves the contribution unchanged, whereas either single exchange
gives minus the crossed contribution of Eq.~\eqref{eq:Xi_teh_explicit}.
Consequently, the two electron-hole channels and the already antisymmetric
$\pp$ channel yield
\begin{align}
 \bXi^{\eh}&=\tfrac12(\bchi^{\eh}+\bchi^{\teh})
 \label{eq:projected_eh_identity}
 \\
 \bXi^{\teh}&=\tfrac12(\bchi^{\eh}+\bchi^{\teh})
 \\
 \bXi^{\pp}&=\bchi^{\pp}
 \label{eq:projected_channel_identity}
\end{align}
Antisymmetrization therefore mixes the two electron-hole contributions
while preserving their sum. Under the stated assumptions, the two projected
contributions are equal, so their total can also be written as
$2\bXi^{\eh}=\bchi^{\eh}+\bchi^{\teh}$. Each projected electron-hole
contribution thus supplies one half of the combined ring and crossed-ring
terms established in Sec.~\ref{sec:ccd_recovery}. Retaining only one of
them with unit weight would halve those terms. We retain both channel
labels to record their origins; they do not supply independent contributions
after projection. This equality concerns the projected common-amplitude
residuals. It does not identify the two frequency-dependent vertex
functions at the same external arguments or establish double counting in
the parquet decomposition. The multiplicities of the combined doubles
residual are verified by its orbital expansion below.
The common source and free contribution is
\begin{equation}
    \bcalQ_c[\bt]
    =
    \boldsymbol{v}+\Delta[\bt]
    \qquad
    \Delta_{ij}^{ab}[\bt]
    =
    (\epsilon_a+\epsilon_b-\epsilon_i-\epsilon_j)t_{ij}^{ab}
    \label{eq:common_residual_weight}
\end{equation}
Here $\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathscr D$ is the doubles source,
$[\boldsymbol{v}]_{ij}^{ab}=\mel{ab}{}{ij}$, and $\Delta$ maps a doubles tensor to
its free energy-difference term. We retain this common contribution once.
Summing the three complete mapped-back channel residuals would instead give
$3\bcalQ_c+\sum_{\alpha}\bXi^{\alpha}$; the common-amplitude condition alone does not
remove this overcounting.

We define the combined doubles equation by retaining the common term once
and adding the three antisymmetrized scattering contributions,
\begin{equation}
    \Delta[\bt]+\boldsymbol{v}
    +\bXi^{\eh}[\bt]
    +\bXi^{\teh}[\bt]
    +\bXi^{\pp}[\bt]
    =0
    \label{eq:residual_channel_sum}
\end{equation}
Equations~\eqref{eq:projected_eh_identity}--\eqref{eq:projected_channel_identity}
allow the sum in Eq.~\eqref{eq:residual_channel_sum} to be evaluated directly
from the raw tensors $\bchi^{\alpha}$. Appendix~\ref{app:weighted_projection}
expresses this same combination as a weighted projection with the stated
component normalization. Figure~\ref{fig:construction} summarizes the three
stages of the construction. We next establish the residual identity by an
explicit orbital expansion.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{Recovery of the non-mosaic CCD equation}
\label{sec:ccd_recovery}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

\paragraph{Residual identity.}
Consider a finite basis of real canonical HF spin orbitals, the static
bare-kernel channel matrices of Sec.~\ref{sec:multifrequency_static}, and any
real $\bt\in\mathscr D$. Evaluate the channel residuals at
$\bT^{\alpha}=J_{\alpha}\cdot\bt$ using the common/scattering split of
Sec.~\ref{sec:source_scattering}. Retain $\bcalQ_c[\bt]$ once and sum
$\bXi^{\alpha}[\bt]=U_{\alpha}\cdot\bcalQ_{s,\alpha}[J_{\alpha}\cdot\bt]$ over the three channels.
Then the left-hand side of Eq.~\eqref{eq:residual_channel_sum} equals the
standard non-mosaic CCD residual, whose orbital expression is given in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:nonmosaic_ccd}. This identity holds before an amplitude
solution is chosen.

To establish the identity, we expand the contributions in orbital indices.
The ring and crossed-ring terms combine through the antisymmetrizers
\begin{equation}
    P(ij)=1-P_{ij}
    \qquad
    P(ab)=1-P_{ab}
    \label{eq:permutation_operators}
\end{equation}
where $P_{ij}$ and $P_{ab}$ exchange the indicated indices. We treat the
linear and quadratic contributions separately before adding the ladder terms.

\subsection{Linear ring and crossed-ring terms}

Using the antisymmetry of the ERIs and $\bt$, the linear parts of
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:Xi_eh_explicit} and \eqref{eq:Xi_teh_explicit} give
\begin{align}
    [\bchi^{\eh}_{\rm lin}+\bchi^{\teh}_{\rm lin}]_{ij}^{ab}
    ={}&
    \sum_{kc}\mel{kb}{}{cj}t_{ik}^{ac}
    -\sum_{kc}\mel{ka}{}{cj}t_{ik}^{bc}
    \nonumber\\
    &-
    \sum_{kc}\mel{kb}{}{ci}t_{jk}^{ac}
    +\sum_{kc}\mel{ka}{}{ci}t_{jk}^{bc}
    \nonumber\\
    ={}&
    P(ij)P(ab)
    \sum_{kc}\mel{kb}{}{cj}t_{ik}^{ac}
    \label{eq:ring_linear_audit}
\end{align}
This recovers the linear ring and crossed-ring terms with their CCD signs.

\subsection{Quadratic ring and crossed-ring terms}

The quadratic direct-$\eh$ contribution is
\begin{equation*}
    R_{ij}^{ab}
    =[\bchi^{\eh}_{\rm quad}]_{ij}^{ab}
    =
    \sum_{klcd}\mel{kl}{}{cd}t_{ik}^{ac}t_{jl}^{bd}
\end{equation*}
whereas the crossed channel gives
\begin{equation*}
    [\bchi^{\teh}_{\rm quad}]_{ij}^{ab}
    =
    \sum_{klcd}\mel{kl}{}{cd}t_{ik}^{bd}t_{jl}^{ac}
\end{equation*}
The separate exchanges $i\leftrightarrow j$ and $a\leftrightarrow b$ both
map $R_{ij}^{ab}$ to minus the crossed term, whereas applying both
exchanges returns $R_{ij}^{ab}$.  Hence
\begin{equation*}
    P(ij)P(ab)R_{ij}^{ab}
    =
    2\qty(
        [\bchi^{\eh}_{\rm quad}]_{ij}^{ab}
        +[\bchi^{\teh}_{\rm quad}]_{ij}^{ab}
    )
\end{equation*}
and therefore
\begin{equation}
    [\bchi^{\eh}_{\rm quad}+\bchi^{\teh}_{\rm quad}]_{ij}^{ab}
    =
    \frac12P(ij)P(ab)
    \sum_{klcd}\mel{kl}{}{cd}t_{ik}^{ac}t_{jl}^{bd}
    \label{eq:ring_quadratic_audit}
\end{equation}
Combining Eqs.~\eqref{eq:ring_linear_audit} and
\eqref{eq:ring_quadratic_audit} gives
\begin{align}
    [\bchi^{\eh}+\bchi^{\teh}]_{ij}^{ab}
    ={}&
    P(ij)P(ab)
    \sum_{kc}\mel{kb}{}{cj}t_{ik}^{ac}
    \nonumber\\
    &+
    \frac12P(ij)P(ab)
    \sum_{klcd}
    \mel{kl}{}{cd}t_{ik}^{ac}t_{jl}^{bd}
    \label{eq:ring_combined}
\end{align}

\subsection{Canonical non-mosaic residual}

Adding the $\pp$ contribution in Eq.~\eqref{eq:Xi_pp_explicit}, the combined
equation~\eqref{eq:residual_channel_sum} becomes
\begin{align}
    0={}&
    \mel{ab}{}{ij}
    +
    (\epsilon_a+\epsilon_b-\epsilon_i-\epsilon_j)t_{ij}^{ab}
    \nonumber\\
    &+
    \frac12\sum_{cd}\mel{ab}{}{cd}t_{ij}^{cd}
    +
    \frac12\sum_{kl}\mel{kl}{}{ij}t_{kl}^{ab}
    \nonumber\\
    &+
    P(ij)P(ab)\sum_{kc}\mel{kb}{}{cj}t_{ik}^{ac}
    \nonumber\\
    &+
    \frac14\sum_{klcd}
    \mel{kl}{}{cd}t_{ij}^{cd}t_{kl}^{ab}
    \nonumber\\
    &+
    \frac12P(ij)P(ab)
    \sum_{klcd}
    \mel{kl}{}{cd}t_{ik}^{ac}t_{jl}^{bd}
    \label{eq:nonmosaic_ccd}
\end{align}
The right-hand side of Eq.~\eqref{eq:nonmosaic_ccd} is the standard
spin-orbital CCD doubles residual restricted to the ring, crossed-ring,
and ladder contributions.\cite{Scuseria2013} The common source and
energy-difference term occur once, and all linear and quadratic scattering
terms have their CCD coefficients. This establishes the claimed residual
identity for the specified common-amplitude construction. Consequently,
the combined equation and non-mosaic CCD have the same roots in the stated
real doubles space. This statement neither requires those roots to solve
each isolated channel Riccati equation nor identifies them with solutions
of the original coupled vertex problem. No spectral branch is selected by
the residual identity.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:scope}

The static bare-kernel representation constructed from the channel
organization of parquet theory reproduces the non-mosaic CCD residual
under the specified common-amplitude prescription. Evaluating the channel Riccati residuals using one antisymmetric
doubles tensor, retaining the common source and free term once, and summing
the antisymmetrized scattering contributions recovers all ring,
crossed-ring, and ladder terms with their CCD signs and coefficients.

The formulation clarifies how the channel description is organized in a
common fermionic amplitude space. The index-arrangement maps specify the
matrix representations, while the antisymmetrization maps mix the two
electron-hole contributions and preserve their sum. The pair-contraction
factors and map normalization have distinct roles, and neither replaces
the prescription to count the common residual once. Keeping these
operations explicit explains how the channel contributions assemble into
the CCD residual and provides a way to track their signs and multiplicities.

The common-amplitude relation, common/scattering split, and residual
combination are specified at the amplitude level. The result therefore
establishes an algebraic connection through the static channel blocks without
identifying the solution sets of the coupled parquet and CCD equations.
No equivalence of correlation-energy expressions is established. Dressed
channel kernels, the full frequency dependence, and the additional terms
of full CCD remain outside the scope of this work.

\section{Block forms of the channel equations}
\label{app:nonlinear_parquet}

We collect the direct-sum notation for the component equations of
Sec.~\ref{sec:parquet}, including the nonlinear kernel closure and the
fixed-kernel rearrangement.

\subsection{Channel representation}

Let $\mathscr F_4$ denote the vector space of four-point vertex functions.
For each channel $\alpha\in\{\eh,\teh,\pp\}$, let $\mathscr F_{\alpha}$ denote a
channel-labelled copy of $\mathscr F_4$ in which the external legs and
frequency variables are arranged naturally for channel $\alpha$. The three copies are isomorphic,
\begin{equation*}
    \mathscr F_{\eh}
    \simeq
    \mathscr F_{\teh}
    \simeq
    \mathscr F_{\pp}
    \simeq
    \mathscr F_4
\end{equation*}
and retain their channel labels to specify the corresponding convolutions
and frequency variables. Their direct sum is
\begin{equation}
    \mathscr F_{\oplus}
    =
    \mathscr F_{\eh}
    \oplus
    \mathscr F_{\teh}
    \oplus
    \mathscr F_{\pp}
    \label{eq:channel_direct_sum}
\end{equation}
whose elements are triples of channel representations of four-point vertex
objects. The reducible and channel-irreducible vertices are collected into the
column vectors
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\Phi}
    =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        \Phi^{\eh}\\
        \Phi^{\teh}\\
        \Phi^{\pp}
    \end{pmatrix}
    \qquad
    \boldsymbol{\Gamma}
    =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        \Gamma^{\eh}\\
        \Gamma^{\teh}\\
        \Gamma^{\pp}
    \end{pmatrix}
    \label{eq:channel_supervectors}
\end{equation}
In the following block formulas, the channel copies are identified by
expressing their vertices in a common external-leg and frequency convention.
An entry $1$ denotes this identification, including the change of variables
when natural channel coordinates are used; it is not an instruction to equate
different transfer frequencies. The signs and pair factors remain in the
channel convolutions. We define
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf e
    =
    \begin{pmatrix}1\\1\\1\end{pmatrix}
    \qquad
    \mathbf s^{\mathsf T}
    =
    \begin{pmatrix}1&1&1\end{pmatrix}
    \qquad
    \mathbb A
    =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        0&1&1\\
        1&0&1\\
        1&1&0
    \end{pmatrix}
    \label{eq:channel_incidence}
\end{equation}
The vertex reconstruction and kernel relations become
\begin{equation}
    F
    =
    \Lambda
    +
    \mathbf s^{\mathsf T}\cdot\boldsymbol{\Phi}
    \label{eq:super_reconstruction}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\Gamma}
    =
    \mathbf e\Lambda
    +
    \mathbb A\cdot\boldsymbol{\Phi}
    \label{eq:super_Gamma}
\end{equation}
The zero diagonal of $\mathbb A$ is the parquet irreducibility condition in
matrix form: $\Gamma^{\alpha}$ contains the reducible contributions from the other
two channels but not from channel $\alpha$ itself.

\subsection{Nonlinear kernel closure}

For a given full vertex $F$, define the block-diagonal BSE operator
\begin{equation}
    \mathbb B[F]
    =
    \operatorname{diag}
    \qty(
        \mathcal B_{\eh}[F],
        \mathcal B_{\teh}[F],
        \mathcal B_{\pp}[F]
    )
    \label{eq:B_operator}
\end{equation}
with a generic four-point test vertex $X$,
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal B_{\alpha}[F]\cdot X
    =
    X\circ L_0^{\alpha}\circ F
    \label{eq:B_channel_map}
\end{equation}
The three BSEs become the single direct-sum equation
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\Phi}
    =
    \mathbb B[F]\cdot\boldsymbol{\Gamma}
    \label{eq:super_BSE}
\end{equation}
Equations~\eqref{eq:super_reconstruction}, \eqref{eq:super_Gamma}, and
\eqref{eq:super_BSE} are exactly equivalent to the parquet vertex equations
at specified $\Lambda$ and one-particle propagators. Eliminating $F$ and
$\boldsymbol{\Gamma}$ gives a single
nonlinear equation in the direct-sum channel space,
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\Phi}
    =
    \mathbb B
    \qty[
        \Lambda+\mathbf s^{\mathsf T}\cdot\boldsymbol{\Phi}
    ]
    \cdot\qty(
        \mathbf e\Lambda+\mathbb A\cdot\boldsymbol{\Phi}
    )
    \label{eq:parquet_super_nonlinear}
\end{equation}
This form makes explicit that parquet is naturally a coupled problem on the
direct sum of the three correlation-channel spaces.


\subsection{Fixed-kernel rearrangement}

For fixed channel kernels and propagators, define
\begin{equation*}
    \mathbb R
    =
    \operatorname{diag}
    (\mathcal R_{\eh},\mathcal R_{\teh},\mathcal R_{\pp})
\end{equation*}
Eq.~\eqref{eq:faddeev_component} is
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\Phi}
    =
    \mathbb R
    \cdot\qty(
        \mathbf e\Lambda+\mathbb A\cdot\boldsymbol{\Phi}
    )
    \label{eq:faddeev_supervector}
\end{equation}
Equivalently,
\begin{equation}
    (\mathbb I_{\oplus}-\mathbb R\cdot\mathbb A)\cdot\boldsymbol{\Phi}
    =
    \mathbb R\cdot\mathbf e\Lambda
    \label{eq:faddeev_supermatrix}
\end{equation}
These equations express Eq.~\eqref{eq:faddeev_component} in direct-sum
notation. The resummed map is diagonal in channel space, and $\mathbb A$
couples each component to the other two.

\section{Weighted representation of the doubles residual}
\label{app:weighted_projection}

The explicit combination in Eq.~\eqref{eq:residual_channel_sum} can be
written as one weighted projection. We first collect the channel amplitude
spaces and index-arrangement maps, then specify the inner products that
fix the adjoints and weights.

For compactness, define
\begin{equation}
    \mathscr T_{\oplus}
    =
    \mathscr T_{\eh}
    \oplus
    \mathscr T_{\teh}
    \oplus
    \mathscr T_{\pp}
    \label{eq:amplitude_direct_sum}
\end{equation}
and collect the three matrix arrangements as
\begin{equation}
    J\cdot\bt
    =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        J_{\eh}\cdot\bt\\
        J_{\teh}\cdot\bt\\
        J_{\pp}\cdot\bt
    \end{pmatrix}
    =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        \bT^{\eh}\\
        \bT^{\teh}\\
        \bT^{\pp}
    \end{pmatrix}
    \label{eq:J_channel_array}
\end{equation}
To define the adjoints of $J_{\alpha}$ and $J$, choose the component inner products
\begin{align}
 \langle\bt,\boldsymbol{u}\rangle_{\mathscr D}
 &=\frac14\sum_{ijab}(t_{ij}^{ab})^*u_{ij}^{ab}
 \label{eq:doubles_inner_product}
 \\
 \langle\bX,\bY\rangle_{\alpha}
 &=\sum_{\mu\nu}X_{\mu\nu}^*Y_{\mu\nu}
 \label{eq:map_inner_products}
\end{align}
Here $\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathscr D$, and $\mu,\nu$ run over all unrestricted
composite indices of the channel matrix. The direct sum uses the sum of
these inner products. In particular, the channel-array inner product has no
pair-contraction factors; those belong to matrix multiplication in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:pair_product}. Let $\bI_{\alpha}$ be the identity on the array space
$\mathscr T_{\alpha}$ and set $\bW_{\alpha}=\tfrac14\bI_{\alpha}$. With these conventions the
counting is represented by
\begin{equation}
    \mathbb W
    =
    \frac14
    \operatorname{diag}(\bI_{\eh},\bI_{\teh},\bI_{\pp})
    \label{eq:channel_weight}
\end{equation}
and direct evaluation of the adjoints gives
\begin{equation}
    U_{\alpha}=J_{\alpha}^{\dagger}\cdot \bW_{\alpha}
    \qquad
    J_{\alpha}^{\dagger}\cdot \bW_{\alpha}\cdot J_{\alpha}=\bI_{\mathscr D}
    \qquad
    J^{\dagger}\cdot\mathbb W\cdot J=3\bI_{\mathscr D}
    \label{eq:J_weight_J}
\end{equation}

The $1/4$ weight above is only the counting factor associated with the four
unrestricted components of $t_{ij}^{ab}$. In the $\pp$ channel, a separate
factor $1/2$ appears for each contraction over one unrestricted
antisymmetric pair. These two factors have different origins.

On $\mathscr D\oplus\mathscr T_{\oplus}$, collect the four residual pieces
and the corresponding matrix arrangements,
\begin{equation*}
    \widetilde{\boldsymbol{\mathcal Q}}[\bt]
    =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        \bcalQ_c[\bt]\\
        \bcalQ_{s,\eh}[J_{\eh}\cdot\bt]\\
        \bcalQ_{s,\teh}[J_{\teh}\cdot\bt]\\
        \bcalQ_{s,\pp}[J_{\pp}\cdot\bt]
    \end{pmatrix}
    \qquad
    \widetilde J\cdot\bt
    =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        \bt\\
        J_{\eh}\cdot\bt\\
        J_{\teh}\cdot\bt\\
        J_{\pp}\cdot\bt
    \end{pmatrix}
\end{equation*}
with
\begin{equation*}
    \widetilde{\mathbb W}
    =
    \operatorname{diag}
    \qty(
        \bI_{\mathscr D},
        \frac14\bI_{\eh},
        \frac14\bI_{\teh},
        \frac14\bI_{\pp}
    )
\end{equation*}
With these definitions, the weighted projection is
\begin{equation}
    \widetilde J^{\dagger}
    \cdot\widetilde{\mathbb W}
    \cdot\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\mathcal Q}}[\bt]
    =0
    \label{eq:weighted_projection}
\end{equation}

Using $U_{\alpha}=J_{\alpha}^{\dagger}\cdot\bW_{\alpha}$, the first block contributes
$\bcalQ_c$ and the remaining blocks contribute $\sum_{\alpha}\bXi^{\alpha}$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:weighted_projection} is therefore the compact form of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:residual_channel_sum}; the weights express the stated component
normalization, while the common term is counted once by construction.
